@chapter 19 | The 30-Day Launch Plan

Thirty days from now, you can have a real business with a real offer, a real store or booking page, and real customers — or at least real conversations with them.

This plan turns everything in this book into daily actions. Each day has a single clear task and a measurable goal. Most tasks take one to two hours. If a day takes longer, keep going the next day. The point is momentum.

@chapter 20 | The First-Year Success Plan

A launch is a beginning, not a finish line. The first year is where a business learns to stand on its own.

This plan gives you a focus for each month. Focus is powerful. It is far better to do one important thing well each month than to do twelve things poorly.

@figure fig-year | Twelve months, twelve focused milestones.

@month 1 | Launch. | Complete the 30-Day Launch Plan and make your first sales.
@month 2 | Collect your first reviews. | Over-deliver for early customers and ask every one of them for an honest review.
@month 3 | Reach 25 sales or jobs. | Market consistently using your weekly rhythm and content calendar.
@month 4 | Improve quality and efficiency. | Use feedback and time studies to refine your offer and your process.
@month 5 | Add a second product or service. | Validate it first with a small test or pilot.
@month 6 | Reach 100 sales or jobs. | Review your mid-year numbers and adjust your prices.
@month 7 | Introduce bundles or packages. | Increase your average order value.
@month 8 | Create repeat customers. | Start an email list routine and a follow-up system; launch a recurring plan if you offer services.
@month 9 | Begin simple advertising. | Test a small budget on your best-performing offer and track the results.
@month 10 | Improve systems and automation. | Write SOPs, save templates, and adopt tools that save time.
@month 11 | Prepare for your busy season. | Plan holiday or seasonal offers, inventory, and scheduling.
@month 12 | Evaluate your year. | Review your numbers, celebrate progress, and decide whether to pursue full-time business.

## Recommended Resources

These organizations and tools can help you along the way. Services, fees, and features change over time, so always check current details on each provider's website.

### Free Business Help (United States)

- **U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA)** — guides to planning, launching, and managing a business at sba.gov.
- **Small Business Development Centers (SBDCs)** — free one-on-one advising in every state.
- **SCORE** — free mentoring from experienced business owners.
- **Internal Revenue Service (IRS)** — free EIN applications and small-business tax information at irs.gov.
- **Your state's Secretary of State and Department of Revenue** — business registration and sales tax permits.
- **Your local library** — many libraries offer free business databases, workshops, and meeting rooms.

### Tools by Category

- **Marketplaces:** Etsy, Amazon Handmade, eBay, Facebook Marketplace
- **Website and store builders:** Shopify, Squarespace, Wix, WooCommerce
- **Local and service platforms:** Google Business Profile, Nextdoor, Thumbtack, Angi, TaskRabbit
- **Freelance platforms:** Upwork, Fiverr
- **Bookkeeping:** QuickBooks, Xero, Wave, FreshBooks
- **Payments and invoicing:** Square, Stripe, PayPal
- **Design and photo editing:** Canva, Adobe Express, Snapseed
- **Email marketing:** Mailchimp, Kit, MailerLite
- **Scheduling:** Calendly, Square Appointments, Acuity Scheduling
