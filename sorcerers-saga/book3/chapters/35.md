# The Moth and the Flame, Part One

Lex saw his father's hand go slack.

He was on his knees beside the litter. On the black floor of the fallen hall. With the breach open in front of him, a long gold-lit gap in the floor where the scar had been, pouring light down into the deep and dark up into the world. With his mother on the other side of the litter, holding his father's other hand. With the little one rising past them out of the opening, soft and starred and enormous, spreading out across the circle like the first dark of evening.

And his father's arm, reaching down into the gap. Down through the light. Holding his brother's hand.

It went slack.

And slid back up out of the breach. Limp. And fell onto the black floor beside the litter. Palm up. Empty.

"*Father*—"

His mother was already there. Over him. Her face an inch from his. Her ear on his chest.

She didn't say anything for a long time.

"*He's breathing*," said Seren Vey. At last. In a whisper. "*Lex. He's breathing. Just. Just.*"

She didn't lift her head.

"*He held it as long as he could*," she said. "*He held the edges. He held Antho's hand. He said my name.*" Her voice cracked. "*And then he let go.*"

---

Lex couldn't move.

He knelt on the black floor with his father's empty hand in front of him and couldn't move. He'd been carrying things for so long. The hammer. The fire. The circle. Three hundred and fourteen miles of it. Fifty-one days. And now his father was a white wisp on a litter with his eyes shut and his mother's ear on his chest, listening for a heart that might stop.

And below him, in the breach—

*[LEX*.]

Antho. Through the Bloodbond. Not faint now. A shout. A scream.

*[Lex, the MOTHS. They're coming down. All of them. Past Kell. Past HER. Down the opening. To the middle.]* His brother's voice was shaking so hard it barely made words. *[To Mal. To the little lights. Lex, I can't move. I can't move. If I move the door shuts and it tears.]*

*[LEX*.]

---

He looked down into the breach.

Into the light. Into the gold pouring down from three hundred and fourteen miles of lanterns. Into the dark pouring up. Soft. Starred. Rising past the edges of the opening like water coming up a well.

And in the middle of the opening, halfway down, standing on the rising dark like a woman standing on a hill—

Kell.

Very straight. In her ruined robes. With her white hair. With a stick held up in both hands. Crosswise. Like a bar across a door.

And above her, filling the opening, enormous, glowing, with her wings spread from wall to wall of the breach and every painted eye turned down—

Maelis.

And between them, and past them, and *round* them, like smoke round a chimney-pot when the wind turns—

The moths.

Pouring down. Past Kell. Past the Moth Queen. Down the opening. A river of grey-gold glitter, going *down*. Into the deep. Against the rising dark. Toward the back of the little one. Toward the middle.

Toward two little lights, curled together, singing in tune.

---

*[...MAL...]*

The little one. All round him. Rising. Stuck. Half through.

*[...they're going for MAL...for the MIDDLE...]*

*[...I can't stop them I can't MOVE I can't*—]

---

He stood up.

His mother looked at him. Over his father's chest. With her face white and her eyes very bright.

"*Go*," said Seren Vey.

"*Father*—"

"*I've got him*." Her hand was flat on the old man's heart. "*I've got him. I've had him all summer. I'll keep him.*" She looked at Lex. Fierce. Small. Freckled. "*Your brother's in the door. Kell's in the door. Somebody has to go down.*"

He looked at her.

"*Go*," said his mother.

---

And from the rubble, behind him, through the broken wall, a scream.

Not a moth-scream. Not a battle-scream.

*Wren*.

---

He turned.

Through the gap where Duskhold's gate had been. Up the black slope of rubble. To the top. To the block of Gloam-stone at the foot of the gate-tower, where she'd been sitting all day on her folded blanket. Where she'd sat all last night. With her hands on her stomach.

She wasn't sitting.

She was on her knees. On the blanket. Doubled over. With both hands pressed to the great round of her stomach and her face screwed up and her red hair loose and her mouth open.

And Old Mag was beside her. Ninety-six. With her one bright eye. And Maren Ashdown on her other side. Holding her shoulders.

And over the three of them, round them, a bubble.

Wind. Wren's wind. A dome of it. Clear and hard and spinning. Over the top of the rubble. Over the gate-tower. Over the blanket. Holding the moths off. A storm the size of a room, with three women in the eye of it.

And it was *flickering*.

---

He ran.

Up the rubble. He didn't remember deciding to. He was running. Slipping on the black stone. Grabbing. Climbing. Over the broken blocks of Morvane's citadel. Toward the bubble of wind. Toward the women in it.

The moths were round the bubble. Thick. Pressing. Grey-gold. Like a swarm round a hive. Beating against the spinning wall of air. Getting thrown off. Coming back. Getting thrown off.

Coming back.

And every time Wren's face screwed up, every time she doubled over, the bubble *flickered*. And the moths pressed closer. And got thrown off again. Barely.

He came through the bubble.

It let him. It always did. It was her wind. It knew him.

---

"*Lex*." Wren looked up. Her face was grey. Wet. Twisted. "*Lex. Oh, gods. It's coming.*"

"*Now*?"

"*No, next week*," said Wren Ashdown. Through her teeth. "*Yes, now. Now. It started at sunset. When the lanterns. When they all said*—" She stopped. Doubled over. Gasped. The bubble flickered. "*When they all said come.*"

He knelt in front of her.

"*Babies don't read calendars*," said Old Mag. Calmly. With her old hand on Wren's back. "*I told you. I told all of you. Nobody listens to the herbwife.*"

"*How long*?"

"*Hours*," said Old Mag. "*First one. Could be all night. Could be till dawn.*" She looked at him with her one bright eye. "*Could be faster. This one's in a hurry. This one heard everyone say come and thought they meant it.*"

---

Wren grabbed his coat.

Both fists. The way she had on the West Road. The way she had on the slope of the Hollowmaw. Pulling him close. Her face an inch from his.

"*Listen to me*," said Wren. "*Hammer. Listen.*"

"*I'm listening*."

"*Antho's screaming*." Her eyes were wild. "*I can hear him. In the wind. Through the breach. He's screaming your name.*"

"*I know*."

"*Go*."

He stared at her.

"*Go*," said Wren Ashdown. "*Go down. Go and help him. Go and help the little one. That's what you're for. That's what all of this was for.*" Her fists were shaking on his coat. "*I'm having a baby. I'm not dying. Women have been having babies since before there were Wells. Mag's got me. Mam's got me.*"

"*Wren*—"

"*She's going to come*," said Wren. "*Up here. For this.*" Her hand went to her stomach. "*You know she is. When it's born. She's going to feel it. A new light. The newest one. Never anyone's.*" Her voice cracked. "*So you go down there and you finish it. Fast. Before she can.*"

She let go of his coat.

"*And then come back*," she said. "*Because if you're not here when this baby comes out, Lex Vey, I'll never forgive you. Never. Not ever.*"

She doubled over. Gasped. The bubble flickered.

"*GO*," said Wren.

---

He went.

He didn't remember going. He'd ask her, after. And she'd say *you kissed me and you went, you idiot, you kissed me on the forehead like a priest and ran down the rubble like a bear in a china shop*.

He didn't remember.

He remembered the bubble letting him out. He remembered the moths round it, pressing. He remembered burning them. Black heat. Pouring off him as he ran. Not because he decided to. Because he was so frightened and so angry and so far past thinking that the fire just came, the way it had come on the night of the gloamhounds, when he was nineteen, when the seal cracked.

He remembered the hall.

He remembered his mother, on her knees beside the litter, with her ear on his father's chest. Looking up as he came running back. Her face.

He remembered the breach.

He remembered jumping in.

---

The light hit him like water.

Gold. Warm. Thick. Pouring down from above. From three hundred and fourteen miles of lanterns. He fell through it.

And the dark caught him.

Rising. Soft. Starred. The little one. Coming up through the breach like a tide coming up a beach. It caught him the way the Stone had caught him in Fennel Street. Not softly. *Kindly*. Folded round his feet and his legs. Held him.

*[...SMITH...]*

*I'm here*.

*[...smith you came down...]*

*Where*?

*[...the MIDDLE...they're going for the middle...]*

He looked up.

---

Kell was above him.

In the light. In the middle of the opening. On the rising dark. Very straight. With her stick crosswise.

And above Kell, the Moth Queen.

She wasn't fighting. Lex saw that at once. She wasn't trying to get past. She hung in the opening with her wings spread wall to wall and her lit eyes on Kell, *watching*, the way you'd watch a dog you've told to sit.

And past them both, round them both, down the edges of the breach like rain down a chimney—

The moths.

Not at Kell. Not at Lex. *Past*. Down. A river of them. Grey-gold. Pouring down the inside walls of the opening, along the edges, where the light was thinnest. Into the deep. Into the dark below. Toward the back of the little one. Toward the part still down there. Still round Antho. Still in the door.

Toward the middle.

She wasn't trying to get through.

She was holding Kell's attention while her moths went round.

---

"*Smith*."

Maelis. From above. Not looking at him. Looking at Kell.

"*How nice of you to drop in*," she said. "*Your wife's in labor, isn't she? I can feel it. Up there. On the rubble. In that little wind.*" Her voice was very soft. "*A new light. Coming. Tonight.*"

Lex didn't answer.

"*You should be up there with her*," said the Moth Queen. "*Holding her hand. What are you doing down here?*"

"*Burning your moths*," said Lex.

And let go of the dark.

---

He fell.

Down. Through the opening. Past the edges. Past the moths pouring down the walls. Down through the rising dark of the little one, which parted for him, which let him through the way the bubble had let him through. Down. Toward the bottom. Toward the door.

Toward the deep.

He hit the warm dark at the bottom of the breach on his feet. Beside his brother.

---

Antho was in the door.

Standing. Under the opening. Exactly where he'd been. With his feet set and his free hand held up, reaching, toward the place where their father's hand had been. Empty. And his other hand round the clay lamp. Lit.

His face was white. Grey. Wet. His eyes—

Clear. Not black. Not dim. Clear. And terrified.

"*Lex*," said his brother.

"*I know*."

"*Father*—"

"*He's breathing*. *Mam's got him.*"

Antho shut his eyes. For one breath. And opened them.

"*The middle*," he said. "*Lex. The middle. Go.*"

---

He went.

Behind his brother. Into the dark. Into the back of the little one. The part still in the deep. Still unrisen. Still waiting its turn to go up through the door.

It was huge. Even this part. Even the back. Soft dark going away in every direction under Shade's stars. Folding. Trembling.

And full of moths.

They were everywhere. He'd come down into a blizzard. A snowstorm of grey-gold. So thick he couldn't see the stars. Pouring down out of the opening, round Antho in the door, past him, into the dark, spreading out, *searching*.

Not drinking. Not yet. *Searching*. Turning. Drifting. Like bees in a field looking for the one flower.

Looking for the middle.

---

*[...SMITH...]*

*Where is it*?

*[...I'm HIDING it...I folded it...small...like the lamp-lady...]*

*[...but I'm going UP...I can't fold and go up at the same time...I can't HOLD it...]*

*[...it's coming UNFOLDED...]*

And Lex saw it.

Ahead. In the blizzard. A hundred yards off. Low down. Where the dark was softest.

A glow. Faint. Silver-gold. Like very old candles.

Two little lights. Curled together. Side by side. Asleep. Coming out of the fold. Unfolding. Slowly. As the little one rose and couldn't hold it.

Singing.

*Little light, little light, burning low*.

In tune.

And the moths saw it too.

---

They turned.

All of them. All at once. The whole blizzard. Like a flock of starlings in the moment before it turns. Every painted eye on every wing. Toward the glow.

And *poured*.

---

Lex ran.

He'd never run so fast. Not on the night of the gloamhounds. Not in the Glass Desert. Not on the white mountain at the Heart. He ran through the blizzard with the black heat pouring off him so hard the moths in front of him didn't have time to curl, they just *went*, like dry leaves thrown on a forge, a flash and a puff of grey-gold ash and nothing.

It wasn't enough.

They were faster. They were lighter. They were thousands and he was one. They poured past him on every side, a river, a flood, going for the glow, going for the two little lights, the small one with the straw hat and the tall one with the hammer.

He was going to be too late.

He *knew* it. The way he knew a weld was bad before he struck it. He was going to get there and the moths would be there first, thousands of them, pressed against the little lights, drinking.

Drinking Malachi.

Drinking the song in tune.

Drinking the middle of the thing he'd promised a home.

---

*[Lex*.]

Ember. In his hearth. Very calm. Very clear.

*[Lex. Stop running.]*

*I can't*—

*[Stop. You won't get there first. You know you won't.]* The coal was glowing. *[Stop running and think. Like a smith. What do you do when the fire's in the wrong place?]*

He stopped.

In the middle of the blizzard. On the warm dark. With the moths pouring past him on every side.

*What do you do when the fire's in the wrong place*?

*You don't chase it*, he thought. *You don't run round the forge after it with a bucket.*

*You move the coals*.

---

He knelt.

On the warm dark. On the back of the little one. In the blizzard.

And put both hands flat on it.

And didn't ask it to go anywhere. Didn't ask it to fold. Didn't ask it to hide. It couldn't. It was rising. It was half through a door. It was doing the hardest thing it had ever done and it didn't have anything left for hiding.

He asked it for something else.

*Make room*, thought Lex.

*[...what...]*

*Make room. Round me. Here. Now. Not round the middle. Round* me.

*[...why...]*

*Because I'm brighter*, thought Lex Vey.

---

He let the fire up.

All of it.

Not black heat. Not hidden. Not banked. *Gold*. The way it had been at the Heart, on the white mountain, at the anvil, when he struck the seam and the hunger unfolded. The way it had been at nineteen, on the green at Ashbourne, when the seal cracked and the gloamhounds came. The way it was in the forge every morning, when he blew on the coals and said good morning and the fire woke up glad to see him.

Bright.

Brighter than anything in the deep. Brighter than the two little lights. Brighter than the lanterns. Brighter than the opening.

A Worldforger. Tier IV. Level ninety-five. Flame Commanding. With everything he had pouring out of him at once. Gold and red and white. Like a forge-door flung open. Like a sunrise. Like a lamp in a window at the end of a long lane on the coldest night of the year.

And the little one made room round him.

---

He felt it happen.

The dark drew back. From round him. In a circle. A room. Ten yards. Twenty. Fifty. A great round space in the soft dark of the little one with him in the middle of it, kneeling, *blazing*.

Empty. Except for him. Except for the light.

And the moths saw it.

---

They turned.

All of them. The whole blizzard. Away from the glow. Away from the two little lights, curled together, singing. Away from the middle.

Toward *him*.

The brightest thing in the deep.

A lamp. Lit. In a window. In an empty room.

They came.

---

He didn't fight them.

He knelt in the middle of the room the little one had made and blazed, and let them come. All of them. Pouring in. Off the walls of the room. Down out of the dark. Toward him. Toward the light.

And when they were all in—

When the room was full of them, every one, a storm of grey-gold so thick he couldn't see his own hands—

He put the fire out.

---

Not out.

*Down*.

All at once. The gold going into him. Under the skin. Hiding. The way it had hidden in the deep the first time, when Kestrel asked *what's fire that isn't light*.

Black heat.

In an instant. Everything he'd had, everything he'd been pouring out as light, turned inside out. Hidden. Dark. And *hot*.

Hotter than he'd ever been. Hotter than the Sunwell. Hotter than the Hearth in the Wellworld of Flame, where he'd nearly become fire and his brother had quenched him through the Bloodbond.

The most dangerous heat there is.

The kind with no light in it.

---

The room went silent.

All at once. Like a held breath. The roar of wings, gone. The glitter, gone. The storm of grey-gold, gone.

Lex knelt in the middle of the room with the black heat pouring off him and his eyes shut.

And when he opened them, the floor round him was grey.

Ash. Fine. Soft. Like snow. Lying in a great circle on the warm dark of the little one, fifty yards across. A drift of it. An inch deep. Every moth that had come to the light.

Not gathering. Not piling up. Not stirring.

Gone.

---

*[...oh...]*

The little one. All round him. The walls of the room it had made. Trembling.

*[...oh, smith...]*

*[...you made a lamp...in a room...and they all came in...]*

*[...like I used to...when I was the other thing...when I was HUNGRY...the worlds came in...]*

*[...you made a trap that looked like me...]*

Lex knelt in the ash.

*I'm sorry*, he thought. *I'm sorry. I didn't know how else.*

The little one was quiet for a long time.

*[...don't be sorry...]* it said at last. Very small. *[...they were going for Mal...]*

*[...you moved the coals...]*

---

He got up.

Slowly. His legs shaking. The black heat going down, back to nothing, back to the banked glow he lived with every day. He was so tired. He'd never been so tired. He looked down at his hands. Grey. With ash. With moth.

And past them, past the edge of the room, which was folding back now, softly, the little one closing it like a hand closing—

The glow.

Faint. Silver-gold. Like very old candles.

Two little lights. Curled together. Side by side. Asleep. Untouched.

Singing.

*Little light, little light, burning low*.

In tune.

Antho. Through the Bloodbond. Not screaming now. Something else.

Through the dark. Through the deep. Toward the opening. Toward the light.

Kell.

*Starving*.

The face of a woman who has knelt at the edge of a dry pool for nine hundred years, scraping, and has just seen, at the top of a hill, in the dark, a window light up that's never been lit before.

Being dragged up.

Toward the light.

Toward the rubble.

Up. Toward the opening. Through the dark. The little one lifting him. Folding round his feet. Carrying him up the way it carried the lamp-lady, the way it carried everyone. Fast. As fast as it could. While still rising. While still half through a door.

He came up through it like a man coming up out of deep water. Out of the breach. Into the fallen hall. Onto the black floor. Rolling.

His mother was there. On her knees. By the litter. With his father. Her face turned up. Toward the gap in the broken wall. Toward the rubble beyond it.

He followed her eyes.

---

The Moth Queen was on the rubble.

Halfway up. On the black slope of Duskhold's fallen gate. Standing. On the broken blocks. With her wings half-spread. Torn. Ragged. One of them bent wrong, at the root, where a bony fist had been.

And at her feet, on the black stone, a shape.

Small. Thin. Grey. In ruined robes that had been white once. Lying very still. With white hair. With empty hands.

Lex couldn't see her face.

He didn't need to.

---

And above the Moth Queen, at the top of the rubble, at the foot of the gate-tower, a bubble of wind.

Flickering.

Going out.

And in it, a woman on her knees on a folded blanket, with her mother holding her shoulders and an old herbwife holding her hands. Screaming.

Not in fear.

The other kind.

The kind that comes at the very end. When the light's almost here.

---

Maelis looked up at it.

At the bubble. At the women in it. At the one in the middle.

Her face was lit from inside. All the way through. Like a window. Like a lamp. Like a moth's wings when the candle's behind them.

She took a step. Up. Toward it.

And a voice behind her, from the bottom of the rubble, from the black floor of the fallen hall, said:

"*Maelis*."

She turned her head. Slowly. Over her shoulder. Down.

At a small woman. Barefoot. Grey-brown hair loose. Kneeling on the black floor beside an old man on a litter. With her square strong freckled hand flat on his chest.

Looking up at her.

In the front of her ruined robe.
