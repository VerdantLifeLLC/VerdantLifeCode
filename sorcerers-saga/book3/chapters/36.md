# The Moth and the Flame, Part Two

The Moth Queen looked at the lamp.

Lex saw her look. From the black floor of the fallen hall, on his hands and knees, soaked in gold light and grey ash, with the breach behind him and the little one pouring up out of it like night coming up out of the ground. He saw her stop on the rubble. Halfway up. With her torn wings half-spread and her face turned down over her shoulder.

At his mother.

Small. Barefoot. Standing beside the litter with a clay lamp held up in her square strong hand. A plain lamp. Brown. From a market stall in Ashbourne. With a little gold flame in it.

"*Come down*," said Seren Harrow. "*Aunt. Come down here. I've got something for you.*"

Maelis didn't move.

Above her, at the top of the rubble, the bubble of wind flickered. Out. In. Out. And in it, Wren screamed. Not in fear. The other kind. And the Moth Queen's head jerked toward it. Up. Like a moth jerks toward a candle.

And then back. Down. To the lamp.

Torn.

---

"*You can't give me anything*," said Maelis.

Her voice was very quiet. It carried anyway. Down the rubble. Across the fallen hall. Low and soft and hoarse.

"*You know that*," she said. "*You've always known. Your mother knew. Your mother's mother. All of you.*" Her lit eyes were on the lamp. "*Anything you give me goes straight through. Your clever boy heard it. With his little hammer. In the deep.*" She turned one long glowing hand over. Looked at it. "*Straight through. Like water through a sieve. Nothing stays.*"

"*I know*," said Seren.

"*Then why*—"

"*Take it anyway*."

---

The rubble was very quiet.

Lex was on his feet. He didn't remember standing. He was standing on the black floor at the foot of the slope with his hands at his sides and the black heat coming up in him, slow, banked, ready. And Aurelian's little hammer in his coat. Against his ribs. He could feel it there.

*[Lex*,] said Ember. *[Wait.]*

*She's going to go for Wren*—

*[Wait*,] said Ember. *[Look at her face.]*

He looked.

---

She was looking at the lamp.

Not the way she'd looked at the lanterns of Highcrest. Not the way she'd looked at the little one in the deep. Not hungry. Not starving.

*Puzzled*.

The way a child looks at something it's never seen before and doesn't have a word for. The way the little one had sounded, in the gold room, when it said *nobody ever asked me that*.

"*Take it*," said Seren Harrow. "*It's for you. From me.*" She held it higher. "*Not left on for you. Not waiting. I'm not waiting for you to come home. I don't want you to come home.*" Her voice was very steady. "*I'm giving it to you. Here. Now. A light that's yours. Because nobody ever gave you one.*"

Maelis stared at her.

"*Nine hundred years*," said Seren. "*You've been drinking. Taking. Lights left on for other people. Promises. Waiting. You've never once had a light that was* yours.*"* She swallowed. "*Your sister wouldn't. My mother wouldn't. I wouldn't. Nobody ever gave you anything. You only ever took.*"

She held out the lamp. In her square hand. Up the slope of black rubble.

"*So I'm giving you one*," said Seren Harrow. "*Take it.*"

---

And above them, at the top of the rubble, something moved.

Not in the bubble. Beside it. Out of the dark. Out of the moth-ash drifting on the night air. Big. Heavy. Broad-shouldered. With great grey-gold wings folded tight. With a miner's pick in one hand.

Silas Hask.

He was standing at the edge of Wren's wind. At the foot of the gate-tower. Where the bubble met the rubble. With his pick up. Not swinging. *Looking*. The way a miner looks at a seam. His lit eyes moving over the spinning wall of air. Reading it.

Looking for the fault.

---

Lex saw it a moment after he did.

The flicker. Every time Wren screamed. Every time her body took her. The wind went thin. For a breath. In one place. On the side nearest the gate-tower. Where it was hardest for her to hold. Where her back was turned.

*Fault*, thought Lex. *She's got a fault in her wind. And he's found it.*

Hask lifted the pick.

---

"*SILAS*!"

Lex was running. Up the rubble. He knew he wouldn't make it. It was fifty yards. Uphill. Black broken stone. Past the Moth Queen, who was standing in the middle of it.

He ran anyway.

And somebody got there first.

---

Kit.

Seventeen. Thin. White-faced. From a grey town in a grey valley. Coming up the far side of the rubble. From the east. From the trench. Where he'd been all night, with the Banners, at the rim, burning moths.

Climbing. Fast. Over the broken blocks. With one hand on the stone.

And in the other, held up, held out in front of him like a priest carries a candle into a church—

A lantern.

Iron and glass. *ARDENT* on the pane. *HOME* on the base. Lit.

He came over the last block. Onto the flat at the foot of the gate-tower. Between Silas Hask and the bubble of wind.

And stood there.

---

Hask stopped.

The pick was up. Over his head. Ready. A miner's swing. Short and hard and absolutely precise. Aimed at the fault in the wind.

Kit was in front of it.

"*Kit*," said Silas Hask.

The boy didn't move. He stood in front of the bubble with the lantern held up in front of his chest and his thin face set like stone.

"*Move, lad*."

"*No*."

"*Kit*. *She wants it. The tracker's baby. She's coming for it. She told me to break the wind and hold it open for her.*" Hask's voice was rough. Calm. Reasonable. And under it, something else. Something shaking. "*Move. I don't want to*—"

"*Then don't*," said Kit.

---

And the boy put two fingers in his mouth.

And whistled.

---

Two notes.

High. Sharp. Clear.

A bird call.

The one Wren had used in the Greyspine, five years ago, on the way up the mountain. The one she'd sent from Fennel Street, a hundred miles, to a forge. The one a girl of eight had taught a boy in a dame-school in Coldwater, before the grey came, in case of firedamp, in case you get lost in the dark.

*Stop. Danger. Come*.

The one she'd whistled at the window. Every night. When her brother came home from the pits. With coal all over him. And he'd stand in the street outside and whistle back. And everyone would laugh.

---

Silas Hask stood very still.

With his pick up. Over his head. And his lit eyes on the boy. And his wings folded. And his face—

Lex saw it.

He'd seen it once before. On Fennel Street. For one breath. When Wren whistled and the moths came down and Hask lowered the pick. An inch.

He saw it now.

All the way.

---

Kit came forward.

One step. Two. Until he was standing right in front of the big man. Close. Under the pick. With the lantern held up between them.

And he held it out.

Not left on. Not waiting.

*Given*.

"*Silas Hask*," said Kit. His voice was shaking. He didn't let it stop him. "*Of Coldwater. Hewer. Delvebreaker. Level whatever it is. I don't care.*"

The pick didn't move.

"*Nell whistled at the window*," said Kit. "*Every night. When you came home from the pits. Two notes. And you'd stop in the street. With coal all over you. With your lamp on your helmet. Big as a house.*" His voice cracked. "*And you'd whistle back. Every night. And the whole street would laugh. Because you were so big and she was so little.*"

Hask's face was doing something.

"*She was my friend*," said Kit. "*She sat next to me in dame-school. She taught me the whistle. She said every pit-kid should know it.*" He was crying. "*She said, in case you get lost in the dark, Kit. In case you can't find your way home.*"

He pushed the lantern forward. Into Hask's chest.

"*She'd be so angry with you*," said Kit. "*She'd be so angry. Look at you. Wings. Lit up. Breaking things.*" His voice broke completely. "*You're lost, Silas. You're lost in the dark. And she's not at the window anymore.*"

He whistled again.

Two notes.

"*So I'm doing it*," said Kit. "*For her. Come home.*"

---

The pick came down.

Not on the wind. Not on the boy.

Down. Slowly. To Hask's side. The way a man lowers a pick at the end of a shift. When the coal's all cut. When there's nothing left to do but go home.

He let go of it.

It fell on the black stone with a clang.

And Silas Hask, Level eighty-eight, Mothborn Champion, the first of the Uncapped, the biggest man Lex had ever seen except Brannoc, put out both his enormous scarred hands. Glowing at the knuckles.

And took the lantern.

---

Lex saw it happen. From the middle of the rubble. Thirty yards off. With the Moth Queen between them.

He saw Hask's hands close round the lantern. Round the iron handle. Round *HOME*.

He saw the big man's face.

It broke.

Like ice breaking on a river in spring. Like Maren Ashdown's on the step on Fennel Street. All of it. At once. Every part.

And Lex saw his eyes.

The gold went *out*.

Not dim. Not dimmed. *Out*. Like a lamp being blown out. Like a candle pinched. The lit gold of the Moth Queen's light, the light she'd poured into him from her own hands, in her dark drawing room, on the Spire, all summer—

Gone.

And under it, his own eyes. Brown. Ordinary. Wet.

And from somewhere in the middle of his chest, where the flaw was, where the crack was, where Lex had put his hand on Fennel Street and felt the light leaking out like water through a sieve—

A sound.

Low. Deep. Rattling. Wet.

A cough.

---

Silas Hask went down on his knees.

On the black stone. At the foot of the gate-tower. In front of the boy. With the lantern in both hands against his chest. Coughing. Deep and wet and terrible. The way his father had coughed. And his mother. And his sister.

Grey.

Lex could see it. Coming up. On his lips. On his breath. In the lantern-light.

*He gave it back*, thought Lex. *Like Father. The moment the warm thing came home, he didn't need her light anymore. And it went.*

*And the grey came back*.

Hask was coughing so hard he couldn't breathe. Bent over the lantern. Shaking. And Kit was on his knees in front of him, holding his shoulders, holding him up, crying, saying *I'm sorry, I'm sorry, Silas, I didn't know, I didn't know it would*—

And Silas Hask lifted his head.

Grey on his lips. Brown eyes streaming. Breathing like a broken bellows.

And put two fingers in his mouth.

And whistled back.

---

Two notes.

Cracked. Rattling. Barely a whistle at all. The whistle of a man with grey in his lungs and no breath to spare.

*Stop. Danger. Come*.

The whole street would have laughed.

Kit didn't laugh. Kit put his arms round the big man's neck and held on.

---

And the Moth Queen *turned*.

Away from the lamp. Away from Seren. Away from the bottom of the rubble.

*Up*.

Toward the gate-tower. Toward the bubble. Toward the fault in the wind, which was open now, which was flickering, which nobody was standing in front of anymore except a coughing man on his knees and a boy holding him.

Toward the light that was coming.

And she *flew*.

---

She was fast. She'd always been fast. She'd struck Lex across the dark in the deep so fast he never saw her hand move.

She was faster now.

Torn wings and all. She went up the rubble in a single beat, a grey-gold streak, past the coughing man and the boy, toward the bubble, toward the flicker, toward the woman on the blanket screaming the last scream—

And something was in front of her.

---

Lex never knew how Hask did it.

He'd ask him, after. Much after. When Hask could talk without coughing, which wasn't often. And Hask would shrug his enormous shoulders and say *don't know, smith. Twenty-six years down the pits. You learn to move when the roof goes.*

He was on his knees. Coughing. Grey. With a lantern in his arms and a boy round his neck.

And then he was on his feet. In front of the bubble. In the fault. In the gap in the wind.

With his pick.

---

He didn't swing it.

He didn't need to. He was a hewer. Twenty-six years. A Delvebreaker. Fault-Finder, Master. He'd found the weak place in every seam and every wall and every man he'd ever looked at.

He'd looked at her all summer.

In her dark drawing room. In his father's chair. At the top of the Spire. With her lit eyes. With her lovely voice. With her cupped hands, full of stolen light, leaking between the fingers.

He knew exactly where her fault was.

He'd known since the first sip.

He held the pick out. Point first. Straight. In front of him. At the height of a woman's heart.

And the Moth Queen flew onto it.

---

It went in.

Not deep. Not far. A hewer's pick isn't a spear. It went in a finger's width. Through the grey-gold silk of her gown. Into the place in the middle of her. Where the flaw was. Where the hollow was. Where she'd been empty since before the pool, since before the Nine, since she was twenty-nine and cold all the way through and didn't know why.

*Fault*.

And Lex was there.

---

He'd never run so fast. Not in the deep. Not ever. Up the rubble. Behind her. With the little hammer out. Aurelian's. Small. Plain. Worn smooth.

She was hanging in the air in front of Hask. On the point of his pick. With her wings spread and her mouth open and her lit eyes wide.

And Lex reached past Hask's shoulder.

And tapped the end of the pick.

Once. Lightly. The way you'd tap a bell to hear if it's cracked. The way you'd tap the end of a chisel to start a split in a block of stone, exactly where the grain wants to part.

*Tink*.

---

The sound went down the pick.

Into the point. Into the fault. Into the hollow in the middle of her.

And this time, it came back.

Not from her. From the *hollow*. From the empty place. From nine hundred years of nothing. It rang. Like a bell. Like a struck glass. Like a voice in an empty church. The note going round and round inside her, in the hollow, louder and louder, finding every edge of the emptiness, every crack, every place where the light had gone in and through and out—

And she *opened*.

---

Not broke. Not shattered.

Opened. The way a door opens. The way a hand opens. The way a flower opens at dawn.

From the middle. From the point of the pick. The flaw. Widening. Spreading. Through her. Out. And everything in her—

Poured.

---

All of it.

Every sip. Every drink. Every light. Nine hundred years of them. The pool, in the hall of glass. The lanterns of Highcrest. The warm things. Odile's *thank goodness, I've no idea what I'm doing*. Kell's *I've got you, High Warden*. Hollis Penn's. The Keepers at Greyfall. The shepherds in the Greyspine. The little one, in the deep, screaming. Every light she'd ever taken, every warm thing, every promise left on in a window for someone who wasn't home.

It came out of her like water out of a broken dam.

Gold. Bright. Pouring. Out of her chest. Out of her hands. Out of her eyes. Down the point of the pick. Down the rubble. In a great glittering river of light. Down the black slope of Duskhold's gate. Across the floor of the fallen hall. Into the breach.

Into the little one.

Home.

---

And out in the circle, all round, on the south edge, at the foot of the broken wall of moths, where the Dimmed stood in their rows with their dark lanterns held up—

Lex didn't see it. He'd hear about it. From Elowen. From Roole. From Hollis Penn's wife, who was kneeling in the dust in front of her husband with a lantern in her hands and his name in her mouth and her whole heart in her voice when it happened.

Every dark lantern in the hands of every Dimmed person on the field—

Lit.

All at once. From inside. Like the lanterns in the Hollowmaw when Seren walked past. Not because someone gave them back. Because what had been taken was pouring out of the woman who'd taken it, all at once, down a breach into the earth, and the earth didn't want it, the earth was full, the earth was *home*, and the little one, rising through the door with its whole vast soft heart, took every warm thing that came pouring down into it.

And gave it back.

To whoever it belonged to.

---

Hollis Penn blinked.

On the south edge of the circle. In the front row. In his apron. With flour on his hands.

And looked down at his wife. Kneeling in front of him. With a lantern. Crying.

"*Mags*," he said. "*What's happened? Why are you*—"

He looked at the loaf-shaped lantern in his hands. Lit. Gold. Warm.

"*Why am I holding the shop lantern*?" said Hollis Penn.

---

On the rubble, the Moth Queen came down off the pick.

Slowly. Like a leaf. Like a feather. Like the last of the dust coming down after a door's been slammed.

Onto the black stone. Onto her knees.

And Lex saw her.

---

She was *old*.

Not her face. Her face was the same. Long jaw. High cheekbones. Wide soft mouth. A face from a church window.

But everything else.

Her hair, which had been silver, was white. Not white like Arion's. White like ash. Like dust. Like the dust on the floor of a room nobody's lived in for a thousand years. Falling round her on the black stone.

Her skin, which had been grey-gold and glowing, was *grey*. Just grey. Thin. Dry. Like paper. Like the wings of a moth that's beaten itself against a window all night and fallen on the sill at dawn.

Her wings. Spread on the black stone behind her. Torn. Faded. The painted eyes on them gone pale. Dust coming off them. Not glittering. Just dust.

And her eyes.

*Dark*.

Not lit. Not windows. Not lamps. Brown. Warm brown. The color of honey. Like anyone's.

The way they'd been before the pool.

---

She knelt on the rubble and looked at her hands.

Grey. Thin. Empty. Shaking.

"*Oh*," said Maelis. In a whisper. A dry whisper, like a page turning. "*Oh. It's all gone.*"

She looked up.

At Lex. Standing over her with the little hammer. At Hask, behind him, on his knees again, coughing, with the lantern and the boy. At the bubble of wind beyond them, flickering, holding.

"*Nine hundred years*," said the Moth Queen. "*And I'm empty. Just like before. Just like the first day.*" She laughed. It was barely a sound. "*Cold. Right in the middle.*"

She looked at her hands.

"*I always was*," she said. "*Always. Even before. I told her. Hes. In the hall. I said I've always been cold. I never knew why.*"

She shut her eyes.

"*I thought if I drank enough*," said Maelis of the Crowned. "*I thought if I could just find the right light. The newest one. The one nobody'd ever given to anyone. I thought that one would stay.*"

---

Someone was coming up the rubble.

Slowly. Barefoot. On the broken black stone. Small. With grey-brown hair loose down her back.

With a lamp.

---

Seren Harrow climbed the rubble of Duskhold with a market-stall lamp in her square strong hand. Past Lex. Past Hask and the boy. To where the Moth Queen knelt on the black stone, grey, empty, old.

And knelt down in front of her.

And held it out.

---

Maelis opened her eyes.

And looked at it. At the little clay lamp. Brown. Plain. With a little gold flame. Held out in front of her face in a small square freckled hand.

"*I can't*," she said. "*It'll go through. You know it will. I'm empty. It'll go straight through.*"

"*Take it anyway*," said Seren.

"*Why*?"

Seren Harrow looked at her aunt. Her aunt many times over. Her mother's mother's mother's mother's mother's sister. Kneeling on a pile of rubble at the end of the world. Grey. Old. Empty.

"*Because I'm giving it to you*," she said. "*That's all. That's the only reason. Not so you'll go away. Not so you'll leave us alone. Not so it'll fill you.*" Her voice was very gentle. "*Just because nobody ever did.*"

She took the Moth Queen's grey hands. In hers. And put them round the lamp. One by one. The way you'd close a child's fingers round a coin.

"*It's yours*," said Seren. "*From me. Not left on. Given.*"

---

Maelis held it.

In her cupped grey hands. A little clay lamp. Lit.

And Lex watched the light go into her.

He watched it. He'd remember it for the rest of his life. He watched the little gold flame light her hands from underneath. Her fingers. Her palms. Glowing through them, through the thin grey skin, the way a candle glows through a paper lantern.

And then go in.

Up her wrists. Her arms. Into her chest. Into the middle. Into the hollow. Into the place where everything had always gone straight through.

He waited for it to go out the other side.

It didn't.

---

It *stayed*.

Small. Gold. Warm. In the middle of her. Where the hollow was. Where she'd been cold since she was twenty-nine. Where nine hundred years of stolen light had poured in and through and out like water through a sieve.

It stayed.

Not because the hollow had closed. Not because she'd been mended. Lex could hear the flaw still, with the Flaw-Sense, with the little hammer in his hand. Wide open. Empty.

It stayed because it was *given*.

Because it wasn't a promise. Wasn't waiting. Wasn't someone else's. Wasn't stolen. Wasn't drunk. It had been put in her hands by someone who wanted nothing back.

It was *hers*.

And a thing that's yours doesn't leak.

---

Maelis looked down at herself.

At the little gold glow in the middle of her chest. Small. Warm. Steady. Like a lamp in a window. Like a coal in a hearth.

Her face—

Lex had seen a lot of faces. He'd seen his mother's, in the cavern, when his father said *good evening*. He'd seen Maren Ashdown's, on the step on Fennel Street. He'd seen Silas Hask's, a moment ago, breaking.

He'd never seen a face like this.

It was the face of someone who has been cold their whole life, and has just, for the very first time, felt warm. And doesn't know what it is. And is frightened of it. And wants it more than anything. And knows, all at once, that this is what they were looking for. For nine hundred years. At the edge of the pool. At every window. In every light they ever took.

Not a light to drink.

A light someone *gave*.

"*Oh*," said Maelis. Very softly. Wonderingly. "*Oh. Is this what it*—"

She stopped.

She looked up at Seren. With her brown eyes. Honey-brown. Like anyone's. Wet.

"*Is this what she felt*?" said Maelis. "*Hes. When she poured it back. Into the pool. With her empty hands. Was it like this?*"

Seren looked at her for a long time.

"*I think so*," she said. "*I think it was exactly like this.*"

---

The Moth Queen smiled.

Not lovely. Not sad. Not fond. Not patient. Not hungry.

Just a smile. Small. Tired. Surprised. The smile of a woman of twenty-nine, sitting on the edge of a dry fountain in a garden of glass, with her little sister's head on her shoulder.

"*Hes always was the strange one*," said Maelis.

And came apart.

---

Not into dust. Not the way the Mothwing did. Not grey-gold and glittering, gathering again.

Into *light*.

Gold. Soft. Warm. Like the light of a clay lamp. Like the light in a window at the end of a long lane on a winter night. She came apart into it, gently, from the middle out, from the little warm glow in her chest, the way a dandelion comes apart when you blow on it. All of her. Her white hair. Her grey hands. Her torn wings, the painted eyes going last, one by one, like lamps being put out in a house where everyone's gone to bed.

And the light didn't go anywhere.

It hung there. Over the rubble. For a long moment. A shape. A woman's shape. Kneeling. With her hands cupped.

And then it went down. Slowly. Like the last of the dust. Like snow. Into the black stone. Into the rubble. Into the breach.

Into the little one.

---

The lamp was on the stone.

Where her hands had been. The little clay lamp from the Ashbourne market. Upright. On the black rubble.

Still lit.

---

Nobody said anything.

Lex stood with the little hammer in his hand. His mother knelt on the black stone in front of the lamp. Hask was on his knees behind them, coughing, grey, with Kit's arms round his neck and the *HOME* lantern in his lap.

And at the top of the rubble, the bubble of wind—

Went out.

All at once. Like a candle pinched.

---

Lex turned.

He was running before he finished turning. Up. The last of the rubble. To the gate-tower. To the block of Gloam-stone. To the folded blanket.

Old Mag was kneeling on it. With something in her arms. Wrapped in her shawl.

Maren Ashdown was on her knees beside her. With her hands over her mouth.

And Wren—

Wren was lying back against the gate-tower. With her red hair everywhere and her face grey and wet and her eyes shut. Breathing. Hard. Fast. Alive.

Her eyes opened.

She saw him.

"*Hammer*," said Wren Ashdown. In a voice like a rope that's been pulled too long. "*You missed it.*"

---

He didn't hear the rest.

He was on his knees. On the blanket. Beside her. With his hands on her face. He didn't remember kneeling. He heard Old Mag saying something. He heard Maren crying. He heard Wren saying *I told you, I told you, I said if you weren't here I'd never forgive you*, and laughing, and crying.

And he heard something else.

From Old Mag's shawl.

A sound. Small. High. Thin. Furious.

Like a kettle.

---

"*Here*," said Old Mag.

And put it in his arms.

---

It was very small.

That was the first thing. He'd held iron bars that weighed more. He'd held hammers. He'd held his father, on the stairs of the Hollowmaw, weighing nothing.

This weighed less than nothing.

And more than everything.

Wrapped in Old Mag's grey shawl. Red. Creased. Wet. Furious. With its fists up by its face and its mouth open, screaming at the whole world for being so cold and so bright and so loud.

With a shock of dark hair. Curly. That nobody was ever going to be able to cut.

---

*[Lex*,] said Ember. In his hearth. In a voice he'd never heard it use. Not dry. Not precise. Not rude.

Small.

*[Lex. Oh, Lex. Look at her.]*

*Her*.

He looked at Wren.

Wren was looking at him. From against the gate-tower. Grey. Wet. Laughing. Crying.

"*A girl*," said Wren. "*Hammer. It's a girl.*" She held out her hand. Shaking. Toward the bundle. Toward him. "*And she's got big hands. Look at her hands.*"

He looked.

Small. Red. Furious. Clenched by her face.

Square.

Too big for the rest of her.

---

He sat down on the blanket. Beside his wife. With his daughter in his arms. And couldn't stop shaking.

And the baby stopped screaming.

All at once. Like a bell stopping. She opened her eyes. Dark. Unfocused. Newborn. And looked up. At nothing. At the sky. At the dark over Duskhold, where the stars should have been.

And weren't.

Because the little one was there.

Rising. Out of the breach. Up. Over the ruins. Over the circle. Filling it. Soft and dark and starred and enormous. Spreading out across the hundred miles of its own name like night spreading across the sky.

And at the very edge of it, where it touched the rubble, where it touched the gate-tower, where it touched the blanket—

It *leaned*.

Toward the baby. Gently. The way the clay lamp leaned toward the black door. The way a child leans toward another child, to look.

*[...oh...]*

Lex heard it. Everyone on the rubble heard it. Even Old Mag.

*[...oh...a new one...]*

*[...a NEW one...]*

*[...she came through...]*

---

And the baby, three minutes old, looked up at the soft dark leaning over her. With her dark unfocused newborn eyes.

And stopped crying.

And stared.

And the little one stared back.

And for one moment, on the rubble of Duskhold, on the last night of autumn, with three hundred and fourteen miles of given lanterns burning round the rim of a word written in the ground—

The two newest things in the world looked at each other.

---

From the bottom of the rubble, Lex heard his mother start to sing.

Flat.

On the high note.

And far below, at the very bottom of the breach, still standing in the door with their mother's mother's lamp in his hand, waiting for the last of the little one to go up past him—

He heard his brother start to laugh.
