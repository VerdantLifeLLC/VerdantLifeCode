# Mother

The room closed behind them.

Lex didn't see it happen. He was running. Across the grey street of the drowned city, toward the small gold light and the woman sitting in it with her shoes off. Antho beside him. Running. The way they'd run as boys, down the track to the stream at Harrow, racing, elbowing, to see who'd get to the water first.

And then they were in the light.

And the dark folded shut round them. Soft. Silent. Like a hand closing. Like a blanket drawn up. The grey street was gone, and the drowned city, and the glittering ceiling of moths. There was only the room. Small. Round. Warm. Lit gold by a clay lamp.

And their mother.

---

She didn't get up.

She couldn't. Her legs had gone. She told them that afterward, laughing, wiping her eyes. *I'd been sitting so long I'd forgotten how to stand. I tried. Nothing happened. My feet just lay there like a pair of old fish.*

She held out her arms instead.

And they went into them. Both of them. At once. On their knees. Lex on one side and Antho on the other, with the clay lamp between them, and their mother's arms round their necks, pulling their heads down onto her shoulders. Small. She was so small. Lex had forgotten. At the Heart he'd been so tired, and she'd been so faint, half there and half not, light coming through her like light through a curtain. He hadn't *felt* her.

He felt her now.

Thin. Bony. Warm. Smelling of nothing, of the deep, of dust and old wool and lamp-oil. Her hair against his cheek, coarse and grey-brown. Her heart going fast under his ear.

Her hands. In his hair. Square and strong and never still.

"*Oh, my boys*," said Seren Vey. Into the top of his head. "*Oh, look at you. Look at you both. You've got so* big.*"*

---

He didn't know how long they stayed like that.

Long enough that his knees hurt. Long enough that Antho started shaking, the way he did when something was too big to hold, and their mother let go of Lex's hair and put both arms round her elder son and held him while he shook. Long enough that Lex heard someone behind him blow their nose. Kestrel. Very loudly. And then say *sorry* in a cracked voice.

When he finally sat back, his face was wet.

So was his mother's. She didn't wipe it. She looked at him. At his face. The way Brannoc had looked at him the morning of the wedding. The way you look at something you didn't think you'd live to see, and are trying to remember properly.

"*You've got your father's height*," she said. "*Poor love. He was always banging his head on the beams.*"

Lex went very still.

His mother saw it. He saw her see it. Saw her face change. A small thing. A flicker. Like a cloud going over the sun.

"*I know that*," said Seren slowly. "*Don't I. That he banged his head. I know that.*" She looked at the lamp. "*I don't know how I know it. I don't remember him doing it. I just know.*"

Nobody said anything.

"*It's like that*," said their mother. Very quietly. "*All the time. Little things. Like stones in a field after the snow's gone. I trip over them.*" She looked up. "*I know he takes salt in his porridge. I know he hums when he's reading. I don't know what he looks like.*"

---

"*Mam*," said Antho.

She looked at him.

"*This is Seraphine*," said Antho. He reached back without looking and found her hand. Seraphine had been standing at the edge of the gold light. Very still. Not coming closer. The way she always stood at the edge of things, until someone asked her in. "*My wife.*"

Seren looked past him.

At Seraphine. Standing in the gold light of the clay lamp with her spectacles crooked and her hair coming down and her hand in Antho's. Very pale. Very straight. Looking back at her husband's mother at the bottom of the world.

"*Wife*," said Seren.

"*Three months*," said Antho. "*In front of your door. You leaned the lamps.*"

His mother stared at him.

Then she looked back at Seraphine. Slowly. Up and down. At the ink on her fingers. At the scars on her wrists, where her sleeves had ridden up. At the notebook sticking out of her coat pocket.

"*You're the one with the glass eyes*," said Seren Vey.

Seraphine blinked. "*I'm—sorry*?"

"*The little one calls you that*." Seren tilted her head. Listening. To the dark. "*The one with the glass eyes. The one who can hear it.*" Her face changed. Opened. "*It's been talking about you for days. It's so excited. It's never had anyone hear it before. Not from up there. Not on purpose.*"

She held out her hand.

Seraphine looked at it. A small square freckled hand. Held out. In the gold light.

She came forward. And knelt. And took it.

And Seren pulled her down, with surprising strength, and kissed her on the forehead. Hard. The way she'd kiss a child.

"*Thank you*," said Seren. Very low. "*For listening to it. Nobody ever does. Not properly.*" She looked at Antho. "*And for him. Thank you for him. I've been worried about him since he was fifteen.*"

Seraphine burst into tears.

---

Kestrel didn't come into the light.

She stood at the edge of it. In the dark. With her black heat pulled in tight and her arms crossed and her amber eyes very bright. Lex looked round and saw her standing there. Alone. Watching.

He knew that look. He'd seen it on the White Shore, two years ago. On a fire-witch with no thread who'd watched him give back a farmer's name to a faded man by the fire. Who'd watched him say *Tam the Ram* and seen the faded man come back.

Her brother. Tamsin. Whom she'd sold Lex's blood to get back.

Kestrel had had a mother too. Once. She'd never said what happened to her.

Seren saw her.

Over Antho's shoulder. Over Seraphine's head. She looked at the tall red-haired woman standing at the edge of the light with her arms crossed, and something in her face went very soft.

"*And who's this*?" said Seren.

"*Kestrel*," said Lex. "*Kestrel Varn. She's*—" He didn't know how to say it. "*She's family. Sort of. She stole my blood once.*"

"*I gave it back*," said Kestrel.

"*She gave it back*." Lex looked at her. "*She came from the Sunreach. Three weeks, on foot. Because Antho wrote.*"

Seren looked at Kestrel for a long moment.

"*Come here, love*," she said. "*Come into the light. It's cold out there.*"

Kestrel didn't move.

"*I'm fine*," she said. Her voice was very tight.

"*I know you are*," said Seren Vey. Gently. "*I'm not asking because you're not fine. I'm asking because there's room.*"

Kestrel Varn stood at the edge of the gold light for a long time.

Then she came in. And sat down. On the warm dark. A little way off. With her knees pulled up.

And Seren reached out without looking and put her hand on Kestrel's red hair. And left it there. And went on talking to her sons.

And Kestrel, who had walked three weeks from the Sunreach on foot with her hair burning, put her face down on her knees and didn't move.

---

Lex told her about Wren.

He hadn't meant to. Not yet. Not here. He'd thought about it on the way down. He'd thought, *I'll tell her when we're home. When we're safe. When there's a window and a kettle and no moths.*

But she asked.

"*And you*?" said Seren. Looking at him. "*My Lex. Who used to hide under the table when it thundered. There's a ring on your hand.*"

He looked down. He didn't wear a ring. He worked in a forge.

But there was a mark. On the third finger of his left hand. A thin pale band of skin, very faint. Where Wren had put a ring on it in the loft at Harrow one night, just to see, a plain iron one she'd found in Brannoc's scrap box, and laughed, and taken it off again, and he'd worn it for three days anyway before it got caught in a vise and he had to take it off.

His mother had seen it. In the gold light. At the bottom of the world.

"*Wren*," said Lex. "*Her name's Wren. Wren Ashdown. She's a tracker. From Thistledown.*" He swallowed. "*She charged me two silver to walk me up a mountain. She said I walked like a bear in a china shop.*"

His mother laughed. Delighted. "*You do*."

"*Everyone says that*."

"*Because it's true*." Seren's eyes were very bright. "*I know her. I think. Don't I? The one with the wind. I heard her. Through the door, at the Heart. And before. At the Hollowmaw. On the night of the Blade. She was the one who wouldn't let go of your hand.*"

"*Yes*," said Lex.

"*Is she here*?"

"*No*." He hesitated.

His mother looked at him. And the hedge-witch came up into her face. The one who had turned breech babies and stopped fevers in twenty-two cottages in twenty-two valleys. Sharp. Knowing.

"*Lex*," said Seren Vey. "*Why isn't she here?*"

---

He told her.

He watched her face while he did it. Watched it go still. And then go soft. And then, very slowly, go lit. From inside. Like a window when someone throws back the shutters.

"*Oh*," said his mother. Softly. "*Oh, Lex. She's carrying a light.*"

He nodded.

He couldn't speak.

She reached out and took his face in both her hands. And held it. And looked at him. For a long, long time.

And then, slowly, he watched the light in her face go out.

Not all of it. Not gone. Covered. Like a hand cupped over a candle-flame. And under it, something else. Something hard and cold and very old. The Harrow thing. The thing that had sat up all night at the end of summer in twenty-two cottages with the shutters closed and a broom.

"*Does she know*?" said Seren. Very quietly.

"*Who*?"

"*Lex*." His mother's hands were still on his face. "*Does the Moth know? Has she seen Wren?*"

He didn't answer.

He didn't need to. His mother saw it in his face. The way she'd always seen everything in his face, since he was four, since he hid under the table when it thundered.

Her hands tightened.

"*Then you go home*," said Seren Vey. "*As soon as you can. As soon as this is done. You go home and you stand in front of her and you don't let anything with lit eyes within a mile of her. Not one. Not a moth. Not a woman. Not anything.*" Her voice was like iron. "*Do you hear me? Do you understand?*"

"*Mam*—"

"*We never let a moth in*," said his mother. "*Never. Not for nine hundred years. Not once.*" Her eyes were fierce. "*And above all, we never let a moth near a light that's carrying.*"

---

*[Lex*.]

He went very still.

Ember. In his hearth. Very quiet. Very strange. A voice he'd never heard it use. Not the dry voice. Not the precise one. Not the one that made terrible jokes about metallurgy.

Small. Shaking.

*[Lex. That's her.]*

*I know*.

*[No*.] *The coal in his hearth was glowing. Brighter than he'd ever felt it.* [*You don't understand. That's* her. *I remember her. I remember* her.*]*

Lex didn't move.

*[I'm a piece of him*,] said Ember. *[From before the seal. Fifteen years ago. Sixteen. Everything he was, up to that night. Everything he knew. Everything he—]* It stopped. *[Lex, I remember the steps. The rain. I remember her laughing at the lanterns. I remember the flour on her cheek. I remember the night Antho was born, holding her hand, all night. I remember walking you up and down the kitchen at three in the morning when you had the fever.]*

*Ember*—

*[She paid him to the Ferryman*,] said Ember. *[All of him. Every memory. But not mine. The Ferryman took her memories. Not his. Not mine.]* The coal flared. *[I've got them. All of them. Up to the seal. I've had them all along.]*

---

His mother was looking at him.

Not at his face. At his chest. Where his hearth was. Where Ember was. Her head on one side. Listening. The way she listened to the dark. The way she'd listened to the little one for a year and a half.

"*Lex*," she said slowly. "*Who's that?*"

He stared at her.

"*In there*." She lifted one hand. And laid it flat on his chest. Over his heart. Over the place where the little glass bottle was, with Wren's breath in it. And under that, deeper, in the hearth at the center of him— "*There's someone in there. Talking. I can hear it.*"

She was a Listener. An untaught one. A hedge-witch. She heard things nobody else could hear. She'd heard Antho through a door for eight years. She'd heard the hunger turn into a little one. She'd heard the dark under the world.

Of course she could hear Ember.

"*It's*—" Lex didn't know how to say it. "*Mam. It's Ember.*"

"*Ember*."

"*It's a*—" He swallowed. "*It's a piece of Father. A little piece. From when he sealed us. He put it in the seal, like a maker's mark. To look after me.*" He looked down at her hand on his chest. "*It's been with me since I was nineteen. It talks. It's very rude.*"

His mother didn't move.

"*Say something*," she said.

"*What*?"

"*Not you*." Her hand pressed flat on his chest. "*Him. The piece. Tell him to say something. I want to hear his voice.*"

---

*[I can't*,] said Ember.

*She can hear you*.

*[I can't*.] *The coal was trembling.* [*Lex. I can't. What if—what if it's wrong. What if it's not the voice. What if she doesn't*—]

*She can hear you*, thought Lex. *Say something.*

The coal was quiet for a long time.

And then, in his hearth, so softly he almost didn't hear it, in a voice he'd heard every day for five years and never, until this moment, really listened to—

Dry. Quiet. A little old-fashioned. With a habit of tapping, two fingers, on any surface. The voice of a man who'd stood on the steps of the Spire in the rain two hundred years into his long life and seen a woman laughing at the lanterns.

*[Hello, Seren*.]

---

His mother's hand went rigid on his chest.

Her face—

He'd never forget it. He'd see it for the rest of his life. In the forge, at night, with the coals banked down. In the loft at Harrow, waking at dawn. In every window with a lamp in it.

It went white. Then red. Then white again. Her eyes went wide. Her mouth opened. Her breath stopped.

And then she *knew*.

Not remembered. She couldn't remember. The Ferryman had all of that. But she knew. The way you know your own name. The way you know which way is up, at the bottom of the world.

"*That's the voice*," whispered Seren Vey.

*[Yes*.]

"*From the Root*." Her hand was shaking on his chest. "*In the dark. Three years. Every night. Before the Heart.*" She swallowed. "*That's the voice I*—"

She stopped.

*[Yes*,] said Ember. Very softly. *[That was him. That's me. A little bit of him. A piece. From long ago. From before.]* The coal was glowing so bright Lex could feel it in his ribs. *[I'm sorry. I'm only a small piece. I don't know what he's like now. I don't know about the last sixteen years. I only know what he was.]*

"*What was he*?" said his mother. In a voice like a child's.

*[In love with you*,] said Ember. *[Every day. From the steps. From the first night. Every single day until the night he sealed your boys and walked out the door with the lantern still lit in the window behind him.]*

A pause.

*[And I suspect*,] said Ember, very gently, *[every day since.]*

---

His mother took her hand off his chest.

Slowly. As if it hurt. And sat back. On the warm dark. With the clay lamp in her lap. And looked at it. For a long time.

Nobody said anything.

Antho had his hand over his mouth. Seraphine was holding his other hand so tight her knuckles were white. Kestrel had lifted her face off her knees and was staring at Lex's chest with her amber eyes enormous.

"*He wrote you a letter*," said Lex.

His mother looked up.

"*Father*," said Lex. "*The real one. Not—not Ember. Him.*" He reached into his coat. Past the glass bottle. And took it out. Thick. Many pages. Sealed with plain grey wax and a thumbprint. "*He's been writing it for a year and a half. Every night after he fills the lamps.*" He held it out. "*He said to ask you first. If you want it. He said if you don't, I'm to burn it, and not bring it back, and not tell him.*"

His mother looked at the letter.

She didn't take it.

"*Is he old*?" she said.

Lex didn't answer.

"*Antho told me*," said Seren. "*Through the door. A month ago. He said, Mam, Father's getting old. He's going grey. He's so happy about it.*" She was looking at the letter. "*And then a week ago, he said—*" She stopped. "*He said it's going faster. He said, Mam, hurry.*"

She looked up at her sons.

"*How old*?" she said.

"*Seventy-five*," said Antho. Very quietly. "*Maybe more. By now.*"

---

She took the letter.

She held it in both hands. In her lap. Beside the lamp. She turned it over. Looked at the seal. At the thumbprint in the wax. Long. Narrow. A long-fingered hand.

She touched the thumbprint. Very lightly. With one finger.

She didn't open it.

"*Not here*," she said. "*Not now. Not with moths up there and the little one so frightened.*" She put the letter inside her dress. Against her chest. Over her heart. "*When I'm home. I want to read it by a window. In the daylight. With the kettle on.*"

She looked at Lex.

"*Tell him I took it*," she said. "*Tell him I didn't burn it. Tell him*—" She stopped. "*No. Don't tell him anything. I'll tell him myself.*"

---

*[...lamp-lady...]*

They all heard it. Even Kestrel. Even Seraphine, who heard the room, not the voice. It was so close now. So close. In the walls of the gold room. In the warm dark round them.

*[...lamp-lady...the moths...the moths are looking...]*

"*I know, lamb*," said Seren. Not looking up. "*I know. Stay folded. Stay very small. She can't find us if you stay small.*"

*[...I'm trying...]*

*[...I'm so big...it's so hard to be small...]*

*[...brother...]*

Antho lifted his head.

*[...brother...you came down...you came all the way down...]*

"*I came*," said Antho.

He said it out loud. Into the room. Into the dark. His voice was steady. Mostly.

*[...nobody ever came down for me...]*

*[...except Mal...and the lamp-lady...and the Warden...]*

*[...and now YOU...]*

---

His brother was quiet for a long moment.

Lex watched him. Sitting on the warm dark at the bottom of the world, in a room made of the thing that had lived in his head for eight years. The thing that had told him to burn. That had told him he'd killed his mother. That had whispered *little brother smells of warm iron* in a cave, with a knife at Lex's throat.

The thing that had said *please don't be angry*.

"*I'm not angry*," said Antho.

The room was very still.

"*I've been trying to work out what I am*," said his brother. Slowly. "*For three months. Since you came back. Since you started talking to me again. I've been lying awake trying to work out if I'm angry. If I should be. If I'm allowed not to be.*" He looked at his hands. "*I'm not. I thought I would be. I'm not.*"

*[...why...]*

"*I don't know*." Antho's mouth twisted. "*Seraphine says it's because I'm tired. Lex says it's because I've already used up all my anger on Father. Odile says it's because I'm getting old.*" He was quiet. "*I think it's because you said sorry. Nobody else ever did. Not the Order. Not the Wardens who hunted me. Not the people in Ashbourne who said I killed my mother.*" He looked at the dark. "*You were the worst thing that ever happened to me. And you're the only one who ever said sorry.*"

The room didn't answer.

"*I don't forgive you*," said Antho Vey. "*Not yet. I don't know how. I don't know if I ever will.*" He swallowed. "*But I came down. And I'm not angry. And I'm not going to let her drink you.*"

He put his hand flat on the warm dark beside him.

"*That'll have to do*," he said. "*For now.*"

And the room *leaned*. Toward him. All of it. Soft and warm and enormous. The way a child leans against you when it's too tired to say anything else.

*[...for now...]* said the little one. Very small. *[...for now is good...]*

---

"*Show them*," said Seren.

Lex looked at her.

"*The middle*," said his mother. To the dark. "*Lamb. Show them where Mal is. They should see.*"

*[...it's in the middle...it's far...]*

"*Not far*," said Seren. "*Not for you. You're everywhere. Fold the room. Bring the middle here.*"

The little one was quiet.

And then the room moved.

---

Lex couldn't have described it. Not then. Not afterward, to Wren, in the loft at Harrow. He tried. He said *it was like a page turning*. He said *it was like the world folding over*. He said *it was like when you're falling asleep and the bed drops out from under you and you jerk awake*.

It was none of those.

The gold room stayed where it was. The lamp. His mother. His brother. Seraphine. Kestrel. All of them. On the warm dark.

And the dark *came to them*. From very far off. From the middle of everything. From the warmest, deepest, quietest place there was. It folded. And folded. And folded again. Like a map. Like a letter. Like a sheet being folded by two people walking toward each other from opposite ends, until the corners meet.

And the corners met.

And in the dark, just beyond the edge of the gold light, there were two more lights.

---

Small. Pale. Silver-gold. Like the light of very old candles. Very still. Very quiet. Lying together, side by side, curled up, the way two people sleep when they've slept side by side for a long time.

Lex knew them.

One was small. Bent. Brown, once. With a straw hat, he thought. Or the shape of one. And bare feet. Lying on its side, with one arm flung out, the hand open, as if it had been holding something and let go.

And the other was tall. Thin. Old. Very old. Curled round something in its hands. Something small. Something that might have been a hammer.

They didn't move. They didn't speak. They lay there in the deepest middle of the dark, two little lights, very still, very peaceful, the way you'd lie by a fire on a winter night when the work's all done and there's nothing left to do but rest.

And from them, very faint, so faint Lex had to hold his breath, a sound.

Singing.

*Little light, little light, burning low*.

In tune.

---

Round his neck, under his shirt, against his chest, the little silver bell with the crooked clapper moved.

He felt it. A tremble. A shiver. Like something waking.

And it rang.

Once. Very softly.

Not flat. Not the way it had rung on the eve of the wedding, in the dark, flat on the high note, his mother's note.

*True*.

A clear small silver *ting*, perfectly in tune. Like a struck glass. Like a bird. Like an old man laughing.

*It rings when he's sad*, Teo had said. Two years ago. Coming back from the Undertow with it in his hands. *It's never stopped. Not in nine hundred years.*

*It stopped*, Antho had said, at the Heart. *Because he's not sad anymore.*

It rang now. Once. In tune.

And Lex knew, the way he knew iron, the way he knew fire, the way he knew the weight of a hammer in his hand, what it meant.

*I'm here*, said the bell. *I'm all right. I'm resting. I'm with my brother.*

*I'm not sad*.

*Ting*.

---

His mother was crying.

So was Antho. So was Seraphine, and Kestrel, and Lex. All of them. Sitting in a small gold room at the bottom of the world, round a clay lamp, with two little sleeping lights at the edge of it, crying. And laughing. Both. At once.

"*Clever old goat*," said Seren Vey. Wiping her eyes. "*Clever old goat. He always did sing it better than me.*"

*[...that's Mal...]* said the little one. Very softly. Very proudly. *[...that's Mal and his brother...they're in the middle...they're warm...]*

*[...I grew round them...]*

*[...like a shell grows round a pearl...]*

Lex put his hand over the bell. Under his shirt. Against his chest.

*Thank you*, he thought. To the little light with the straw hat. *For sitting with it. For staying. For singing.*

The little light didn't answer.

But the bell, under his hand, was warm.

---

And then the room *shook*.

All of it. All at once. Hard. Like a house when a door slams. Like a horse when it's struck. The gold light of the clay lamp swung wildly. The two little lights at the edge of the room flickered. The warm dark *flinched*.

*[...NO...]*

*[...no no no she's FOUND it...she's found the FOLD...]*

*[...when I brought the middle here...when I opened it...she SAW...]*

Seren was on her feet. Lex didn't know how. Her legs that had gone like old fish. She was standing in the middle of the gold room with the lamp held up high in her square strong hand and her face gone white and hard and cold.

"*Lamb*," she said. "*Fold it back. Now. Send the middle back. Fold it all*—"

*[...I CAN'T...she's got her hand in it...she's got her HAND in the fold...]*

---

The wall of the room tore.

Not like cloth. Not like paper. Like *skin*. A long slow rip, from top to bottom, in the warm dark beside them. And through it, cold air. And grey-gold glitter. And light.

Not lamplight. Not firelight.

Two lights. Gold. Warm. Soft. Like windows at the end of a long lane on a winter night.

And a long pale hand. With too many joints. Reaching in through the tear. Slowly. Gently. The way you'd reach into a nest to take an egg.

Reaching for the middle. For the two little lights, curled side by side, singing in tune.

And past them. Through them. Toward the place behind them where the dark was deepest and softest and warmest and *fullest*.

The heart of it.

"*Oh*," said a voice. Through the tear. Low and soft and hoarse and full of longing. "*Oh, there you are. There you are. I've been looking everywhere.*"

The little one *screamed*.

And Lex felt Antho's hand close on his arm like a vise. And felt his brother's whole body go rigid. And heard him make a sound, low and terrible, the sound of a man who has heard that scream through his own mouth twenty-seven times and is not going to hear it again.

And Antho Vey stood up.

And walked into the tear.
