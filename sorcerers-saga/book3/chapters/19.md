# Room

The handprint wouldn't come off.

Seraphine tried. With water from her flask, and a cloth, and then with her own sleeve, kneeling beside Lex in the gold room with her spectacles pushed up and her tongue between her teeth. Scrubbing at his face.

It smeared. It stuck. It glittered.

Grey-gold. Fine as flour. From his left temple, across his cheekbone, down to the hinge of his jaw. Four long fingers and a thumb. The shape of a hand that had struck him open-palmed across the dark at the bottom of the world.

"*It's in the skin*," said Seraphine at last. Sitting back. "*Lex. It's not on your face. It's* in *it. Like a tattoo. Like a burn.*"

"*Does it hurt*?" said his mother.

"*No*." He touched it. It didn't feel like anything. Smooth. Slightly cool. Like the inside of a shell. "*My nose hurts.*"

"*Your nose is broken*," said Kestrel. Without sympathy. She'd set it herself, ten minutes ago, with a quick hard twist of her fingers that had made him see stars. "*That's what noses do when Sovereigns slap them.*"

His mother was looking at the handprint.

She didn't say anything. She reached out, very slowly, and touched it. With one square freckled fingertip. Lightly. The way she'd touched the thumbprint in the wax of his father's letter.

"*She marked you*," said Seren Vey.

"*She hit me*."

"*She marked you*." Her finger stayed on his cheek. "*That's what they do, Lex. My mother said. When they touch you, they leave a mark. So they can find you again.*" Her hand dropped. "*Like a moth leaves dust on a window. So the next one knows there was a light there.*"

---

Nobody slept.

There wasn't really any sleeping, down here. There wasn't any night or day. There was the gold room, and the clay lamp, and the warm dark round them, folded close. And outside it, the cold, and Shade's stars, and the place in the dark where the little one had been bitten.

Lex could feel it.

He wasn't a Listener of the Hollow. He couldn't hear rooms. But he could feel *that*. The way you can feel a cold draft from a broken window two rooms away. A thinness. A place where something had been taken out.

It ached. Even to him.

And the little one wouldn't stop trembling.

---

"*It can't come through the door*," said Seraphine.

She'd been working it out. For an hour. With her notebook on her knees and her pencil going and the clay lamp beside her for light. She'd covered six pages. Lex had watched her cover them. Lines. Circles. Numbers. A drawing that might have been the Hollowmaw. A drawing that was certainly the little one, a great soft shape like a hill, with a tiny door drawn at its feet for scale.

The door was the size of a grain of rice.

"*It's too big*," said Seraphine. "*Not by a little. By—*" She looked at her numbers. "*I don't know how to say how much. It's the size of the deep, Lex. It's folded round the whole of the bottom of the world. When it unfolds, when it comes up properly, it'll be—*" She stopped.

"*How big*?"

"*A country*," said Seraphine. "*A small one. At least.*"

Nobody said anything.

"*If it comes up through the Hollowmaw*," said Seraphine, very carefully, "*through the door, it'll have to fold itself into a shape the size of the door. And then squeeze through. And then unfold on the other side. In the cavern. Under the mountain.*" She looked at Antho. "*There isn't room under the mountain.*"

"*So it can't come through the door*," said Kestrel.

"*It can't come through any door*," said Seraphine. "*Not the Hollowmaw. Not the shafts. The shafts are worse; they're narrower, and they're wounds.*" She looked at the six pages. "*Not anywhere. There isn't a door in the world big enough.*"

---

"*Then we make one*," said Lex.

They all looked at him.

He didn't know where it came from. It came up out of his hands. It usually did. He was sitting on the warm dark with the little hammer in his lap and his broken nose throbbing and the handprint on his face, and he said it before he'd thought it.

"*Not a door*," he said. "*A door's for walking through. It's not going to walk through. It's going to* come up.*"* He looked at Seraphine's drawing. The great soft hill. The grain of rice. "*Like water coming up in a spring. Like iron coming up a mould.*"

"*A mould*," said his mother. Softly.

"*You said*." He looked at her. "*Somewhere that's been made ready. Somewhere with room.*" He turned the little hammer over in his fingers. "*That's a mould. That's exactly a mould. You make the shape first. The empty shape. In the sand. And then you pour.*"

"*The size of a country*?" said Kestrel.

"*I don't know*." He looked at the hammer. "*I've never made a mould bigger than a cartwheel. Brannoc made one once for a church bell. It took him three months and he nearly killed himself.*"

"*So how*—"

"*I don't know*," said Lex Vey. "*I don't know how. I don't know where. I don't know what it's made of or how deep it goes or who's going to help me dig it.*" He looked up. At the dark. At the warm soft trembling folded dark round the little gold room. "*But I know that's what it needs. That's what new things need. A hollow to come up into. A space someone made for them, on purpose. Before they got there.*"

He put his hand flat on the warm dark beside him.

"*Little one*," he said.

---

It went very still.

He felt it go still. All of it. Round them. Listening. The way Antho said it listened. The way a child goes still when someone it doesn't know very well speaks to it directly for the first time.

*[...the smith...]*

He heard it.

Not like Antho heard it. Not in the back of his skull. Not in an old place where something used to live. He heard it the way you hear someone speak in the next room. Faintly. Through the wall. Through the warm dark under his palm.

*[...the smith is talking to me...]*

"*I'm the smith*," said Lex. "*I claimed your first stone. On the green at Ashbourne. Do you remember?*"

*[...you picked it up...you didn't break it...you held it...]*

"*I held it*."

*[...it was warm in your hand...]*

"*So was it*," said Lex.

The little one was quiet.

*[...you made the cups...]*

"*Me and Pim*."

*[...the little quiet one...the one with the room inside...]*

"*Yes*."

*[...the cups were nice...]* A pause. *[...the cups were like being carried...]*

---

"*Listen*," said Lex.

He didn't know how to talk to it. He'd never talked to anything this big. He'd talked to fire. To stone. To the Undertow, once, in the Wellworld of Tide, and nearly drowned. To the Hearth, in the Wellworld of Flame, which had asked him what he burned for, and he'd said *I don't know, I keep asking*.

He talked to it the way he'd talk to Dunny Tobbin. To Ivy Moss. To any frightened child who'd come to the forge door and stood there with their thumb in their mouth.

"*You can't come up through the door*," said Lex. "*You're too big. That's not your fault. You're the right size. The door's the wrong size.*"

*[...]*

"*So I'm going to make you a new one*," said Lex. "*Not a door. A room. Up there. Under the sky. Big enough for all of you. Somewhere the moths can't get into. Somewhere you can come up slowly, and unfold, and see everything, and not be frightened.*"

*[...a room...]*

"*A room*," said Lex. "*Made ready. For you. Before you get there.*"

*[...nobody ever made a room for ME...]*

*[...I'm the room...I'm the one that makes room...for everything else...]*

*[...nobody ever...]*

"*I know*," said Lex. His voice was rough. "*I know. That's why. Somebody should.*"

---

The warm dark round them trembled.

Not with fear. Lex felt the difference. He couldn't have said how. A different trembling. The way a dog trembles when you've come home after a long time away, and it doesn't know what to do with itself, and it just *shakes*.

*[...how long...]*

He didn't know.

He looked at Seraphine. She was looking at her six pages. At her numbers. At the great soft hill and the grain of rice.

"*It's still rising*," she said slowly. "*It's been rising since the Heart. A year and a half. Faster now. Faster every week.*" She bit her pencil. "*It'll come up whether we're ready or not. It can't help it. It's what it is. It goes toward the light.*"

"*How long*?" said Lex.

Seraphine looked at the dark. Listening. With her hands out, palms up.

"*Autumn*," she said at last. Very quietly. "*Late autumn. When the nights get long and the lights come on.*" She swallowed. "*The end of the year.*"

Lex looked at his mother.

His mother looked back.

They both knew what came at the end of the year. When the nights got long and every window in Caldor had a lantern in it.

*Lantern Night*.

---

"*I'll stay with it*," said Kell.

Everyone turned.

She was sitting against the wall of the gold room. Where Seraphine had propped her. With her back to the warm dark and her long legs stretched out and her ruined white robes stiff and dark down one side where the blood had dried. Seraphine had bound the wound. Tight. With strips of her own petticoat. It had stopped bleeding. Mostly.

Kell's face was grey. Greyer than it had been. Her eyes were still half-dim. Faint. Like a lamp turned down low.

But she was sitting very straight.

"*Isaura*," said Seren. "*No.*"

"*Someone has to*." Kell didn't look at her. She was looking at the dark. At the place, out beyond the gold room, where the little one had been bitten. "*Someone has to stay. She knows where it is now. She's tasted it. She'll send more down the shafts. Moths. Her people. She'll keep coming back. Sipping.*" Her thin mouth twisted. "*Someone has to stand in front of it with a stick.*"

"*You don't have a stick*," said Kestrel.

"*I'll find another stick*."

"*You're hurt*," said Seraphine.

"*I've been hurt before*." Kell looked at her. "*I was unwritten, scholar. I was made into a Warden for the thing it used to be. I stood at the Hollowmaw door from the inside for months with no face and no name.*" She looked back at the dark. "*This is just a knife.*"

---

"*Then I'll stay too*," said Seren.

Lex turned round so fast his broken nose screamed.

"*Mam*—"

"*She can't stay alone*." His mother's face was set. "*Not hurt. Not with half her light gone. Not with that thing sending moths down the holes.*" She held the clay lamp tighter. "*And it needs me. Listen to it. It's shaking. It's been shaking since she bit it. I'm the one it listens to. I'm the one who sings. If I go up*—"

"*If you stay*," said Isaura Kell, "*you'll die down here.*"

Seren stopped.

"*Not of the moths*," said Kell. Very calmly. "*Not of her. Of the waiting. Of the walking. Of a year and a half in the dark with your feet hurting and a coat on a hook you can't remember.*" She looked at Seren with her pale half-dim eyes. "*You've been down here thirteen years, Seren Vey. Holding doors. Walking things home. Singing to children who aren't yours.*" Her voice didn't change. "*Your husband is up there. Dying. He's got a season. Maybe less. He fills your lamp every night and says good evening to it.*"

The gold room was very quiet.

"*Go home*," said Isaura Kell. "*Go home to him. Before he's gone. Read his letter by a window. Find out if he takes salt in his porridge.*" Something moved at the corner of her mouth. "*I'll sit with your little one. I'm not good at much. I'm good at standing in front of things.*"

---

His mother didn't say anything for a long time.

Lex watched her. She was sitting on the warm dark with the clay lamp in her lap, and her hand on the lamp, and her face very still. Looking at Kell. And then at the dark. And then at her sons. And then at the lamp.

He knew that face. He'd seen it on the stairs at Harrow, thirteen years ago. He'd seen it on the night she walked into the dark.

A woman deciding which of the things she loved she was going to have to put down.

"*You hate it*," said Seren. To Kell. Very quietly. "*Don't you. Underneath. It ate you. It ate your name.*"

Kell was quiet.

"*Yes*," she said. "*Underneath. Some days.*" She looked at the dark. "*Some days I wake up and I remember being nothing. Being a shape. Being its Warden. Standing at a door with no face, pushing, for months, because it told me to.*" Her bony hands clenched in her lap. "*And I want to burn it. All of it. I want to take a torch and set fire to the whole of the deep and watch it go.*"

Nobody moved.

"*And then it says*—" Kell stopped. Her mouth worked. "*It says,* Warden, are you awake? Warden, can I tell you something? *In that voice. That little voice.*" She swallowed. "*And it tells me something. Some little thing. That it saw a star. That it heard you singing. That it's frightened of the dark, which is ridiculous, because it* is *the dark.*"

She looked at Seren.

"*And I can't*," said Isaura Kell. "*I can't burn it. I've tried. In my head. I can't.*" Her voice cracked. Just a little. The first time Lex had ever heard it crack. "*Gods help me. I walked it all the way up here. I'm not going to stop now.*"

---

"*Someone sat with it before*," said Lex.

Kell looked at him.

"*When it was the other thing*," said Lex. "*At the Heart. When it unfolded. When it was just a small cold shaking dark.*" He looked toward the edge of the gold room. Where the two little lights had been, before the dark folded the middle back. "*Malachi sat with it. Cupped it in his hands and said, I'm not going to cage you. I'm just going to sit with you.*"

Kell said nothing.

"*He sat with it till it got better*," said Lex. "*Till it wasn't hungry anymore. Till it was this.*" He looked at her. "*And now somebody's going to sit with it till it gets home.*"

Isaura Kell, who had been High Warden of the Order of the Lattice, who had signed forty-one warrants for unlatticed sorcery and remembered every name, who had been carried across the bottom of the world on the back of a man she'd branded, looked at Lex Vey for a long time.

"*Yes*," she said at last. Very quietly. "*Someone is.*"

---

*[...Warden...]*

They all heard it. Even Kestrel.

*[...Warden...are you staying...]*

Kell didn't look up. She was looking at her own hands.

"*I'm staying*," she said. "*Till you're home. Till the smith makes your room. Till you come up.*"

*[...why...]*

Kell was quiet for a moment.

"*Because somebody carried me once*," said Isaura Kell. "*When I couldn't walk. When I was half gone. Across the bottom of the world. In the dark.*" Her voice was very steady. "*He didn't have to. I'd hurt him. I'd branded him. I'd made him cap four thousand children.*" She looked at the dark. "*He carried me anyway.*"

She put her bony hand flat on the warm dark beside her.

"*So I'm carrying you*," she said. "*That's all. I'm just passing it on.*"

---

And the dark—

Lex felt it. They all felt it. The whole warm soft trembling fold of it, round the gold room. It *settled*. Round Kell. Close. Closer. The way a cat settles into a lap. The way a child settles into sleep.

And where it touched her side, where the wound was, under Seraphine's petticoat bandage, something happened.

Lex couldn't see it. None of them could. But Seraphine could hear it. She went very still. With her hands out.

"*It's making room*," she whispered. "*Round the wound. In the wound. It's—*" She shook her head. "*It's not healing it. It can't heal. That's not what it does.*" She swallowed. "*It's making room for it to heal. It's holding the space. So the wound has somewhere to close into.*"

Kell put her hand on her side.

And breathed. Deep. All the way down. For the first time since they'd found her on the steps of the grey house.

"*Oh*," said Isaura Kell. Very softly. "*Oh. That's*—"

She didn't finish.

She didn't have to.

---

They went up the next day.

There wasn't a next day. But it felt like one. They'd sat in the gold room, and eaten Seraphine's bread, and Kestrel had slept for an hour with her head on her pack, and Antho had sat with his back against the warm dark with his hand flat on it and his eyes shut, and his mother had sung. Flat on the high note. Over and over. Until the little one stopped trembling.

And then Seren had stood up.

On her own. Her legs worked now. She stood up in the gold room with the clay lamp in her hand and looked at Kell.

"*I'll come back*," she said.

"*I know*."

"*Before Lantern Night*."

"*I know*."

"*Don't let it go to sleep while I'm gone*," said Seren. "*It sleeps too much when it's frightened. Wake it up. Talk to it. Tell it things. It likes stories.*" Her voice cracked. "*It likes the one about the girl who married the bear. And the one about the miller's cat. It doesn't like the one about the wolf. Don't tell it the wolf.*"

"*I won't tell it the wolf*," said Kell.

Seren looked at her for a long moment.

Then she bent down. And put her arms round the gaunt grey woman in the ruined white robes. And held her. Hard. The way she'd held her sons.

Kell didn't move. For a long time. Her arms stayed at her sides. Rigid. The arms of a woman who hadn't been held since she was nine, except once, on the back of a man in the dark.

And then, slowly, awkwardly, the way a door swings that hasn't been opened in years—

She held on.

---

The little one took them up.

It wasn't like walking. It wasn't like the long slow climb Seren had told them about, a year and a half of it, up and up through Shade's dark with her feet hurting. It was like being *carried*. The warm dark folded round them, soft, close, and then folded again, and again, and every fold brought them higher. Like a map being folded. Like a letter. Like a sheet being folded by two people walking toward each other from opposite ends.

Lex felt the moths go by. Overhead. And then beside them. And then below. A great slow glittering field of them, turning, drifting, in the dark between the stars. Hunting. They didn't see. The little one held them folded so close and so small that there was nothing to see.

*[...this is how I carry the lamp-lady...]* it said. Shyly. To him. Through the warm dark. *[...when the moths come...I fold her up small...like a letter...]*

"*It's very good*," said Lex.

*[...is it...]*

"*It's the best carrying I've ever had*," said Lex. "*And I've been carried by dragons.*"

The warm dark rippled. Like a cat's tail flicking.

*[...dragons...]* said the little one. Wonderingly. *[...the lamp-lady says there are dragons...up there...is that true...]*

"*Two*," said Lex. "*A big one and a little one. The little one's the size of a house.*"

*[...]*

*[...I want to see the dragons...]*

---

Ember talked to his mother on the way up.

Lex didn't hear most of it. He didn't try. It wasn't his.

He felt it, though. The coal in his hearth, glowing. Talking. Very quietly. And his mother, walking beside him in the folded dark, with her hand on his arm and her head bent toward his chest. Listening. Her face very still.

Once, she laughed. Softly. Surprised.

Once, she put her hand over her mouth.

Once, near the top, she stopped walking. And stood very still. With her eyes shut. And Lex felt Ember go quiet in his hearth, and his mother's hand tighten on his arm, and something pass between them that he wasn't part of.

When she opened her eyes, they were wet.

"*He told me about the steps*," she said. Very low. Not to Lex. To herself. "*The rain. What I said about the lanterns.*" She swallowed. "*What he said back.*"

*[Only that one*,] said Ember. In his hearth. Very softly. *[I told her only that one. She asked for the rest. I said no.]*

*Why*?

*[Because they're his*,] said Ember. *[Not mine. I'm only a copy. He should be the one to tell her. While he still can.]* A pause. *[I'll keep them. All of them. Until she wants them. Or until he can't tell her himself.]*

Lex didn't say anything.

He put his hand over his mother's, on his arm. And held it there. All the rest of the way up.

---

And then the door.

He saw it before anything else. A line. Thin. Gold. In the dark ahead of them. The crack Antho had left. *On the latch. We're coming back.*

Light. Lamp-light. Lantern-light. Coming through from the world. From a bell-shaped cavern at the bottom of a mountain. From a niche with three lamps in it.

From an old man in a chair.

The little one stopped.

It set them down. Gently. On the dark. In front of the crack. And unfolded from round them, slowly, the way a hand opens.

*[...I can't go any further...]* it said. Very small. *[...the door's too little...]*

"*I know*," said Lex.

*[...you'll make the room...]*

"*I'll make the room*."

*[...promise...]*

Lex put his hand flat on the warm dark. The last of it. Where it touched the edge of the door.

"*I promise*," said Lex Vey. "*I'll make you room. Up there. Under the sky. Before Lantern Night. Big enough for all of you. And I'll stand in it. With my brother, and my mother, and every lantern in Caldor.*" He swallowed. "*And when you come up, you won't come up into her sky. You'll come up into ours.*"

The little one didn't say anything.

But the dark, under his palm, was warm. And it leaned. Once. Against his hand. The way a horse leans its head against your shoulder.

And then it drew back. Down. Into the deep. Into Shade's starlit dark. Going home. To the gold room. To a gaunt grey woman with no stick, sitting very straight, waiting to tell it the one about the miller's cat.

---

His mother was standing in front of the door.

In front of the crack. The thin gold line of light. With the clay lamp in her hand. And her other hand at her chest. Inside her dress. Where the letter was.

She wasn't moving.

Antho came and stood beside her. On one side. Lex on the other. Seraphine and Kestrel behind.

"*Mam*," said Antho. Very gently.

She didn't look at him.

She was looking through the crack. Lex looked too. Through the thin gold line, into the world.

He could see the cavern. A little of it. The black floor. The black wall. The niche. Three small gold flames. And beside the niche, a chair. And in the chair, a shape.

An old man. Small. Wrapped in a blanket. With his white head fallen forward on his chest. Asleep. With one thin hand hanging down over the arm of the chair. And on the floor beneath it, where it had slipped from his fingers, a little copper oil-flask.

*Fill the lamps*, Lex had told him. *Every night. Don't stop.*

*Every night*, his father had said. *I promise*.

His mother made a sound.

Small. Low. Like a breath let out after a very long time held.

"*What if he doesn't know me*?" said Seren Vey. In a voice like a child's. "*What if I walk through and he looks up and*—"

She stopped.

"*What if I don't know him*," she whispered.

Nobody answered.

Nobody could.

And on the other side of the door, in the chair by the niche, the old man stirred. In his sleep. And lifted his white head a little. As if he'd heard something. Very far off. A voice he knew. A step on a long road.

And the clay lamp in the niche beside him—

Leaned.

All the way over. Toward the door. Toward the crack. Toward the woman standing on the other side of it, with its other half in her hand.

The way it had leaned every night for thirteen years.

Only this time, she was close enough to see.
