# The Uncapped Man

Lex heard it over the hammers.

He was in the long hall. At the great hearth. With Seraphine's map of the Blightmarch spread on the anvil in front of him and Brannoc on one side and Grandmother Asha on the other, arguing about sand. Twenty anvils going behind him. Forty students. The whole long hall ringing like a bell-tower at a wedding.

And through it, over it, under it, coming in through the open doors and the open windows and the great brick chimney, from very far off—

Two notes.

High. Sharp. A bird call.

*Stop. Danger. Come*.

---

He was on his feet before he knew it.

The map went off the anvil. Grandmother Asha's tea went over. Brannoc said something that was certainly not a hymn. The hammers stopped. One by one. Down the long hall. Twenty of them. Until the only sound was the great hearth crackling and the wind.

The wind.

It came in through the doors. Through the windows. Down the chimney. Warm. Soft. Wrong. Not from the north, where the summer wind always came from in the Vale. From the south. From Highcrest. Carrying something in it. A smell. Smoke and river-mud and old stone and dust.

Plums.

And under it, very faint, so faint he wasn't sure he heard it at all, a voice. Hers. Not words. Just the shape of her. The way she shifted her weight from foot to foot when she'd been standing still too long. The creak of her bow-string.

Frightened.

"*Wren*," said Lex.

---

Vharuun was on the chapel green.

She'd been sleeping there for a week. Curled round the old cracked Stone like a cat round a hearth, with the village children climbing all over her and Quill sulking on her horns. She lifted her great blue head when he came out of the forge yard at a run. Her golden eye was already on him.

She'd heard it too.

She didn't wait for a saddle. She didn't wait for Brannoc's chair. She lowered her neck and he was on it, gripping the ridge of scale behind her head with both hands, and she went up off the green in a single leap that cracked the chapel windows and sent every child on the green flat on their backs, shrieking with delight.

And turned south.

And *flew*.

---

He'd flown fast before. On Ashka, on the night of the Blade. On Vharuun, coming out of the Rift. He'd flown so fast the wind tore tears out of his eyes and froze them on his face.

He'd never flown like this.

The Vale went by under them like a ribbon pulled through a fist. The river. The fields. Millbridge. The downs. Green and gold and green. The storm-mother's wings beat so hard the air round them cracked like thunder, and lightning ran along the edges of her scales in long blue threads, and she didn't climb and she didn't glide and she didn't rest.

She went straight. South. Toward the glitter on the horizon.

*[Lex*,] said Ember.

*I know*.

*[Lex, she's*—]

*I know*.

*[No. Listen. The wind. It's not just Wren. There's something else in it.]*

Lex listened.

And under Wren's fear, under the plums and the river-mud, something else. Small. Quick. Like a bird's heart. Like a little drum, very far off.

Beating fast.

---

He saw Highcrest from twenty miles off.

The lid of glitter. The dark city under it. The Spire, lit from every window, gold.

And in the Lower Town, near the river, something else.

A *storm*.

Small. Tight. Fierce. A whirl of wind and dust and grey-gold, spinning in one narrow street like a waterspout. He could see it from twenty miles. The glitter round it, the great slow ceiling, was being dragged down into it. Sucked in. Torn apart. Spat out. Round and round.

*Wren*.

Holding a street. Alone. Against a sky.

---

Vharuun didn't slow down.

She came over the walls of Highcrest at a height of fifty feet, with her wings half-folded and lightning crackling off every edge of her, and the glitter over the city *recoiled*. All of it. A great flinch. The way a hand flinches off a hot stove. The moths nearest her burned. Instantly. Bright. Like sparks. And the ones behind them saw the light, saw the lightning, saw a storm-mother made of sky coming down on them like the end of the world, and turned.

Toward her.

Toward the light.

"*Up*!" Lex shouted. "*Vharuun, go up! Take them up! Take them away from the street!*"

She heard him. She went up. Straight up. Out of the glitter. Into the blue above it. Blazing. Crackling. A beacon.

And the moths followed her.

Not all of them. A great many. A river of grey-gold, pouring up out of the ceiling over the Lower Town after the dragon, like smoke up a chimney. Leaving a hole. A gap in the glitter. Over Fennel Street.

And Lex let go.

---

He fell.

Fifty feet. Through the hole. Toward the street. With the wind roaring in his ears and the glitter on every side of him and the Lower Town coming up at him, narrow roofs and narrow streets, tall thin houses leaning together overhead.

He didn't have Wren's wind. He'd never learned to Windstep properly. He always landed in a hedge.

He asked the Stone instead.

*Catch me*.

---

The street caught him.

Not softly. Stone is never soft. But *kindly*. The cobbles of Fennel Street rose up under him as he came down, a hump, a wave, like a pillow being plumped, and he hit it and rolled and the stone gave a little and took the force of the fall the way a blacksmith's arm takes the shock of a hammer-blow, and then let him go.

He came up on one knee. In the middle of Fennel Street. In a ring of buckled cobbles.

In front of a green door. Third from the end.

---

They were on the step.

Four of them. Squashed together. A thin grey-threaded woman with a stick. A gangly freckled boy of twelve. A gap-toothed red-haired boy of nine. Holding two lit lanterns between them. Tin. Dented. With moths lying dead round their feet like leaves.

And in front of them, in the street, with her bow in one hand and nothing in the other and her hair loose and blazing and the wind coming out of her in a whirl that had dragged half the sky down into Fennel Street—

Wren.

She was whistling.

Still. He could hear it. Under the roar. Two notes. Over and over. Hoarse now. Cracking. *Stop. Danger. Come.* Pouring the wind out of herself. Holding the moths off the step. Off her mother. Off her brothers. Off the lanterns.

She saw him.

The whistle stopped.

---

The wind dropped.

All at once. Like a sail when the sheet's cut. The whirl round Fennel Street sagged. Slowed. The glitter overhead started to come back down. Slowly.

And Wren swayed.

He was there. He didn't know how. He was across the buckled cobbles and he had her. Before she fell. In both arms. Against his chest. She was shaking so hard her teeth were going.

"*You came*," said Wren. Into his coat. Hoarse. Her voice nearly gone.

"*You whistled*."

"*From Highcrest*." She was laughing. Or crying. He couldn't tell. "*From Highcrest to Ashbourne. A hundred miles. I didn't know if it'd work.*"

"*It worked*."

"*Good*." She sagged against him. "*Good. Because I've got nothing left. Not a breath. Not a puff. I couldn't blow out a candle.*" Her hand found his. Pulled it. Down. Flat. Onto her stomach. "*Lex. Lex. It's fast. Feel it. I can feel it. It's going so fast.*"

He couldn't feel anything. It was too early. Too small.

He felt it anyway.

---

"*Smith*."

---

Lex looked up.

Over Wren's head. Down Fennel Street.

Elowen Dunmore was on one knee in the gutter. With Oathkeeper in both hands. Bleeding from a cut over her eye. Ilse and Ivo were behind her, in a doorway, white-faced, holding each other. Pim was standing on the step in front of Wren's mother. Small. Silent. With his arms spread.

And in the middle of the street, twenty yards off, with his wings folded and his pick on his shoulder and the glitter coming down round him like snow—

Silas Hask.

He was bleeding too. A long scratch down one cheek. Elowen's work, Lex thought. His patched leather coat was torn. One of his great grey-gold wings had a rent in it.

He was looking at Lex.

"*She whistled*," said Hask. "*And you came.*" He shook his head. Slowly. "*A hundred miles. In an hour.*"

"*Less*."

"*Less*." Hask looked at Wren. In Lex's arms. With his gold eyes. "*She held the street the whole time. Against all of it. Against me. With a baby in her and no arrows left.*" Something moved in his face. "*Never seen anything like it. Not in twenty-six years down the pits.*"

"*Then let her go*."

"*Can't*." Hask shifted the pick on his shoulder. "*She wants her. You know that. She told you herself. On the gate.*" His voice was very quiet. "*And anything of yours.*"

---

Lex put Wren down.

Gently. On the step. Beside her mother. Who took her. Who put her thin arms round her daughter and pulled her close and looked up at Lex over Wren's head with her green eyes, Wren's eyes, very hard.

"*Hammer*," said Maren Ashdown.

"*Ma'am*."

"*Don't try*," said Wren's mother. "*Do.*"

---

He turned round.

And walked down Fennel Street.

Not fast. Not running. Walking. The way he'd walked through the Mothwing in the deep. The way Brannoc walked up to a forge in the morning, knowing what the day was going to be. With his fire coming up. Not gold. Not bright.

Dark.

Black heat. Pouring off him. Rippling the air. The dead moths in the gutter curling as he passed. The cobbles under his boots ticking, the way stone ticks when it's too hot.

Hask watched him come.

He didn't move. He didn't spread his wings. He stood in the middle of the street with his pick on his shoulder and watched Lex come, the way a miner watches a seam he's about to work.

"*You're angry*," said Hask.

"*Yes*."

"*Good*." The big man smiled. Slightly. "*Angry's easier. Angry's got faults all over it.*"

Hask moved first.

Lex didn't see it. He *felt* it. The way you feel a roof start to go before you hear it crack. A shift. A change in the air. And then the pick was coming at his head from the left, fast, so fast, a miner's swing, short and hard and absolutely precise—

Not at his head.

At his *face*.

At the left side. At the cheek. At the handprint.

---

He got his arm up. Just. The pick took him on the forearm instead. The flat of it. Not the point. It didn't cut. It hit like a falling beam, and something in his arm *went*, and he staggered sideways into the wall of the cobbler's house.

And his fire stuttered.

The black heat. It flickered. Went thin. For a moment, a breath, gold came through it. Bright. Like a forge door opening.

And the glitter overhead saw it.

And came down.

---

"*Fault*," said Silas Hask. Mildly. Stepping back. "*There. On your face. Where she touched you.*" He tapped the side of his own cheek with one big scarred finger. "*Every seam's got one. Every wall. Every man.*" He tilted his head. "*She put yours there for me. Didn't she. Like a chalk mark on a pit-prop. Hit here.*"

Lex pushed himself off the wall.

His arm hurt. Badly. Not broken. Cracked, maybe. His fire was coming back. Dark. Slower than before. He could feel the place on his cheek. The handprint. Cool. Smooth. Like the inside of a shell.

*Like a hole in a bucket*, he thought. *Like a crack in a crucible.*

*Where the heat gets out*.

*[He's right*,] said Ember. Very quietly. *[Lex. He's right. I can feel it. When he hit near it. Your fire went toward it. Like water toward a drain.]*

*I know*.

*[She marked you so you'd leak*.]

*I know*.

---

The moths were coming down. Thick. Round him. He burned them. They came anyway. Not at him. Past him. Toward the step. Toward the two lit lanterns in Maren Ashdown's lap. Toward Wren.

Pim was holding them. Lex could feel it. The room. The silence. Round the step. Round the lanterns. The moths parting round it, like water round a stone.

But Pim was tired. Lex could feel that too.

*Not long*, he thought. *He can't hold it long.*

He turned back to Hask.

---

"*Why*?" said Lex.

Hask looked at him.

"*Why her*?" said Lex. "*You said it yourself. She held the street. With a baby in her. With no arrows.*" He wiped his mouth. There was blood on his hand. He didn't know where it came from. "*You said you'd never seen anything like it. And you're going to take her up that hill and give her to the Moth.*"

"*Yes*."

"*Why*?"

Silas Hask was quiet for a long moment.

"*Because your father killed my family*," he said.

---

The street went very still.

Even the moths seemed to slow.

"*She told me*," said Hask. "*Two nights ago. In his room. At the top of the Spire. She was reading his books.*" His gold eyes didn't move off Lex's face. "*The grey ones. Two hundred years of them. She read me one page. Out loud. In his handwriting.*"

He shifted the pick.

"*Vhessa*," he said. "*Twenty-one years ago. At the Great Stone. A Rewrite, he called it. To break every Ceiling in the world. In one stroke.*" His voice didn't change. "*And it tore. And the grey came through. And it went down into the deep seams at Coldwater. Into the coal. Into the air.*"

He breathed in. Deep. All the way down. With no rattle.

"*Into my da*," said Silas Hask. "*And my mam. And my sister. Nell. She was eight. She used to whistle at the window when I came home from the pits.*" He looked at Wren. On the step. "*Like that. Two notes. Exactly like that.*"

Lex couldn't move.

"*He wrote it down*," said Hask. "*Your da. In his little grey book. In his little neat hand.* Vhessa, *he wrote.* Rewrite failed. Breach opened. Estimated losses, eastern march: eleven thousand in the first year. *And then a line. And then the price of bread in Highcrest that autumn.*"

His gold eyes were very bright.

"*Eleven thousand*," he said. "*In a column. Between a line and the price of bread.*"

---

Lex didn't say anything.

There wasn't anything to say.

He'd stood in a chamber at Duskhold, five years ago, and heard his father confess. *I tore it*. He'd stood beside him and his brother while the three of them closed the breach with blood. He'd heard the World Notice. *The Vhessa Breach is closed.*

He'd never once thought about Coldwater.

Not really. Not the people in it. Not the miners with grey on their lips. Not a girl of eight who whistled at a window.

He'd Unbound them. Two summers ago. In the rain. Four thousand of them. He'd stood at their Stone and put his hand on it and felt them go up like mine-damp. And flown away. On a dragon. Pleased with himself.

And never once thought: *these are the people my father's breach was killing*.

---

"*He's dying*," said Lex.

Hask looked at him.

"*My father*," said Lex. "*He's dying. He's eighty-three. He has a season left. Maybe less. He can't walk. He can barely hold a cup.*" His voice was rough. "*He knows. About Vhessa. He's known for twenty-one years. He wrote it down because that's what he does. That's how he carries things. In columns.*" He swallowed. "*He's spent twenty-one years trying to make it right. Five years ago he closed it with his own blood. He sat in a crystal for fifteen years holding the breaches shut from inside the net.*"

"*Doesn't bring Nell back*."

"*No*," said Lex. "*It doesn't.*"

---

"*So which of you do I owe*?" said Silas Hask.

He said it quietly. Not angry. Tired. The way a man asks a question he's been asking himself every night in the dark for a long time.

"*Your da, who killed them*?" he said. "*You, who set me free?*" He looked down at his own glowing hands. "*Her, who gave me breath?*"

Lex looked at him.

At the big man in the middle of Fennel Street with grey-gold wings folded down his back and a miner's pick on his shoulder and lit eyes in his heavy weathered face. Level eighty-eight. In two years. From a Common hewer capped at twenty-four.

Breathing. All the way down. For the first time in twenty-six years.

"*Nobody*," said Lex.

Hask's eyes narrowed.

"*You don't owe anyone*," said Lex Vey. "*Not him. Not me. Not her.*" He took a step forward. Black heat rippling. "*That's what free means, Silas. That's what I took off you at the Stone. Not the Ceiling. The* owing. *Nobody puts their hand on your shoulder and pushes down. Nobody gets to say what you owe them.*"

He took another step.

"*Not even her*," he said. "*Especially not her.*"

---

Hask swung.

Lex was ready this time. He didn't block. He *stepped in*. Inside the swing. The way Garrick had taught him, five years ago, on the Old Founders' Road, with a stick. *Never stand where a man wants you to stand, lad. Stand where he doesn't.*

The pick went past his ear. He felt the wind of it.

And he put his hand flat on Hask's chest.

---

He didn't strike.

He didn't burn.

He *listened*.

The way he'd listened to the lined wolf in the Delve. The way he'd listened to the little one in the gold room. The way the Hearth had taught him, in the Wellworld of Flame. *What do you burn for*?

He listened to Silas Hask.

And found the flaw.

---

It was in the middle. Of course it was. Where it always was.

Where the breath went in. Where the light went in. Where Maelis had put it, the sip, the second sip, the bigger one, out of what she'd brought up from the deep. Out of the little one.

It was going in. Through Hask's chest. Through his lungs. Holding up his wings. Burning in his eyes.

And it was going *out*.

Slowly. Steadily. Through a place in the middle of him. Like water through a sieve. Like heat through a crack in a crucible.

*Leaking*.

Not as fast as Maelis. Not nearly. But leaking. Out of the middle. Out of a hollow place where something had been taken out and nothing had been put back.

Lex knew what it was.

He'd felt it once before. In a cave in the Greyspine. In his brother. In the place where a seal had broken and the Murmur had come through.

*A crack*, thought Lex. *A crack where the dark came in. Where the grief came in. Where a girl of eight who whistled at the window used to be.*

*She didn't fill it. She just poured light into a cracked cup.*

*And now he has to keep coming back to her for more*.

---

He could strike it.

He knew he could. Aurelian's little hammer was in his coat. One tap. *Tink*. On the flaw. On the crack. And it would open. And everything she'd poured into him would pour out. All at once. The light. The breath. The wings.

And the grey would come back.

Into his lungs. Into his chest. Twenty-six years of coal-dust and Gloam. All of it. At once. Like a dam breaking.

He'd die. In the street. In front of everyone. Coughing grey.

*Like Nell*.

Lex took his hand off Hask's chest.

---

Hask stared at him.

They were standing very close. Chest to chest, nearly. In the middle of Fennel Street. With the moths coming down round them and the step behind and the dead in the doorways on either side, swaying.

"*You found it*," said Silas Hask. Very quietly. "*Didn't you. The fault. In me.*"

"*Yes*."

"*Where*?"

"*In the middle*." Lex looked at him. "*Where she poured. It's leaking. It's always going to leak. You'll have to keep going back to her for more.*" He didn't look away. "*Forever. Like she has to keep going back to the Well.*"

Hask didn't say anything.

"*I could open it*," said Lex. "*One tap. And it'd all go. Everything she gave you.*" His voice was very quiet. "*And the grey would come back. All of it. At once.*"

"*No*."

"*Do it*." Hask's gold eyes were very bright. "*You've got the hammer. I've seen it. The little one. Do it, smith. End it. Here. In the street. I've got her in me and I've got your da's breach in me and I've got Nell in me and I can't*—"

He stopped.

"*I can't tell which is which anymore*," said Silas Hask.

---

Lex stepped back.

One step. Out of reach of the pick. Out of reach of the man.

"*I'm not going to do it*," he said.

"*Why not*?"

"*Because you held the pick down*," said Lex. "*Wren saw. When she whistled. Just before the moths came. You lowered it. An inch.*" He looked at Hask. "*You heard Nell.*"

The big man's face did something.

All at once. Overhead. The glitter. Going still. The way it went still when she was coming.

*Watching*.
