# Moths at the Keep

Antho was at the door when they came.

He was always at the door now. At midnight. Every night. Since his mother came home. He'd taken it over from his father, the way you take over a job from an old man whose hands have started to shake. He went down the long passage with the copper oil-flask and filled the three lamps in the niche. The clay one, which wasn't the clay one anymore, which was a new one Seraphine had found in the Ashbourne market, because the real one was on the kitchen windowsill now, in his mother's keeping. The iron one. The *ARDENT* one.

And he put his hand on the black glass. And listened.

*[...brother...]*

*Evening*, he thought. *How is it?*

*[...the Warden told me the one about the miller's cat again...]*

*Again*?

*[...I asked...it's my favorite...the cat wears boots...]*

Antho smiled. In the dark. With his hand on the door. He couldn't help it.

*[...brother...]*

*Mm*.

*[...something's coming...]*

---

He felt it a moment after.

Not through the door. Through the mountain. Through the long passage behind him, and the lanterns, and the cave mouth, and the slope. The Threshold. The place he stood. All of it, at once, the way you feel a draft from a door that's been left open three rooms away.

Something was coming in.

Not up. Not from the deep. *In*. From the world. From outside. Over the Greyspine. Down onto the slope. Toward the cave mouth.

Toward the light.

He took his hand off the glass.

And ran.

---

He was halfway up the passage when the first lantern went dark.

He felt it before he saw it. He always did now. A thousand and thirteen lanterns, and he could feel every one, the way you can feel every tooth in your mouth. One went. Near the top. Near the cave mouth. Just *went*. Like a candle in a draft.

Then another. Then five. Then twenty.

Going dark. One after another. Coming down the passage toward him. Like a tide coming in.

"*SERAPHINE*!"

---

The Keep was waking.

He heard it all round him as he ran. Doors banging. Feet on stone. Students shouting. Odile's voice, low and sharp, giving orders. The Millbridge twins. Cass. Teo's old fish-buckets going over somewhere with a crash.

And under it all, coming down the passage from the cave mouth, a sound.

Like rain on a roof.

Like sand on stone.

Like wings.

---

He reached the kitchen.

It was the first room off the passage. Halfway up. Where the long passage widened and the side-caves opened out. The kitchen and the library and the cold-room and the little stone rooms where people slept.

His mother was in the kitchen doorway.

In her nightdress. Too long in the hem. Barefoot. With her grey-brown hair loose down her back and the clay lamp in her hand. Lit. Small. Gold. Held up.

His father was behind her. In the wheeled chair. With his blanket and his spectacles and his face white.

"*Antho*," said Seren. Very calmly. "*How many?*"

"*I don't know*."

"*Lots*?"

"*Lots*."

She nodded. Once. And her face went the way it had gone in the deep, when the moths came down the shafts. Hard. Cold. Old. The Harrow face.

"*Shut the shutters*," said Seren Vey. "*Light a lamp inside. Get a broom.*"

---

They came down the passage like smoke.

Antho saw them. Standing in the kitchen doorway with his mother behind him and the Lantern in his hand. He saw them come round the last bend of the passage above. A great slow grey-gold river, pouring down between the walls, over the lanterns, filling the passage from floor to roof. Glittering. Drifting. Every lantern they passed going dark.

*Nine hundred*, he counted. Without meaning to. *Eight hundred and ninety. Eight hundred and seventy.*

And in the middle of it, walking, pale shapes. With ragged wings. With dim gold eyes. Long hands reaching for the lanterns as they passed.

Mothwing. Dozens.

And behind them, at the back, filling the passage so that he had to stoop under the roof, with his great grey-gold wings folded tight against his back and a miner's pick in one hand—

---

Antho opened the Lantern.

All of it. Full. Twelve Hush-runes blazing. The deep soft gold of it pouring out down the passage like the light from a door flung open on a winter night. And with it, the hush. The quiet that kept voices out.

The moths hit it.

And stopped. The way they'd stopped on the slope, the first night. Hung there. At the edge of the light. A wall of them. Beating. Drinking.

He felt it. The pull. The Lantern thinning. Like a child sucking the juice from an orange.

"*Odile*!"

She was there. Beside him. With the students. Eight of them. Their eyes going dark. Shade, wrapping round the moths at the edge of the Lantern's light, folding them, holding them.

It didn't hurt them. It never had. It just slowed them down.

"*Master Antho*!" Cass. From the library door. "*The cold-room! They're coming in through the cold-room! Through the bats' way!*"

The bats' way. The crack at the back of the cold-room where the water dripped. Where four hundred bats went in and out every night. Too small for a person.

Not too small for a moth.

---

"*Seraphine*—"

"*I've got it*." She was coming out of the library. In her nightgown. With her spectacles crooked and her hair in a plait and an iron cup in each hand. Hush-cups. Pim's. Two of the last ones he'd filled before he went to Highcrest with Wren. "*I've got it. I can hear where they're coming in.*"

"*Seraphine, that's not what they're*—"

"*I know what they're for*." She was already past him. Running. Awkwardly. Toward the cold-room. "*They make room. Let's see what moths make of a room.*"

---

He couldn't go after her.

He couldn't go anywhere. He was standing in the kitchen doorway with the Lantern full open and his mother behind him and the moths in front of him and the Mothwing pressing in behind the moths, and if he moved, the Lantern's light moved with him, and the hush went with it, and the wall of moths came down on the kitchen like a wave.

So he stood.

He was the Threshold Warden. He stood in doorways. That was what he did.

He held this one.

---

And then Hask came through the moths.

Not round them. Through them. Walking. Stooping under the roof of the passage. His wings folded tight. His pick on his shoulder. The moths parting round him like a curtain round a man stepping onto a stage.

He stopped at the edge of the Lantern's light.

And looked at Antho. With his lit gold eyes.

"*Threshold Warden*," said Corwin Hask.

Antho didn't answer.

"*I've heard of you*," said Hask. "*The mad warlock of the Greyspine. Eight years in a cave. Killed your mother.*" His mouth twisted. "*That's what they said in Coldwater. When I was a boy. That's what everyone said.*"

"*They were wrong*."

"*I know*." Hask looked past him. Into the kitchen. At the small woman standing in the doorway behind Antho, in her too-long nightdress, with the clay lamp held up. "*There she is. Alive. Barefoot.*" He looked back at Antho. "*Everyone was wrong about everything, weren't they. Your da wasn't a merchant. You weren't a murderer. She wasn't dead.*"

"*What do you want*?"

"*You know what I want*," said Corwin Hask.

---

Antho held the door.

He felt the Lantern pulling at him. Thinning. The moths at the edge of it, drinking. The Mothwing behind them, pressing. And Hask, in front, with his pick, not pressing at all. Just standing there. Looking.

*Fault*, thought Antho. *He's looking for the fault. Lex told me. He finds the weak place. In anything.*

*What's mine*?

He knew. He'd always known.

*Alone*. *I'm weakest when I'm alone. When I'm holding something by myself and there's nobody beside me.*

He wasn't alone. Odile was beside him. The students. His mother behind him. His father.

*Seraphine*, he thought. *Seraphine's in the cold-room. With two cups. Alone.*

He felt it go. A flicker. In the Lantern. In the hush. In him.

Hask saw it.

---

"*There*," said Corwin Hask. Mildly. "*That's it.*"

And he didn't strike.

He *stepped back*.

Out of the light. Back into the moths. Into the glitter. Gone. Like a man stepping back into the dark at the edge of a firelit room.

And the moths came forward.

All of them. All at once. Not drifting now. *Pouring*. Down onto the Lantern's light like a river onto a weir. Too many. Too fast. The edge of the gold buckled. Bent. Like a sheet of glass under a weight.

"*Hold it*!" Antho was shouting. "*Odile! Hold the edge! Hold*—"

And from behind him, from the kitchen, his father's voice.

Thin. Old. Cracked.

"*Seren*—"

---

He turned.

He shouldn't have. He knew it as he did it. He was in a doorway. He was the Threshold Warden. You don't turn round in a doorway.

He turned anyway.

There was a window in the kitchen. Small. High. Cut through the rock of the mountain, looking east. With a clay lamp on the sill. His mother had put it there every night since she came home. The Harrow lamp. Lit. Facing out.

The window was full of moths.

Pressed against the glass from outside. Hundreds. Thousands. So thick the glass was gone, and in its place a wall of grey-gold wings. Pressing. Pushing.

And the glass was *bending*.

---

It broke.

Not like glass breaks. It didn't shatter. It *gave*. Softly. Like ice going rotten in spring. Like a window that's had something leaning on it for a very long time. The glass went soft and grey and glittering, and then it wasn't glass, it was dust, and the dust fell inward onto the sill and the floor.

And through the hole where the window had been came pale shapes.

Three of them. Mothwing. Thin. Ragged. With long hands and dim eyes. Squeezing in through the window the way a moth squeezes through a gap in a shutter. Folded. Patient.

Onto the sill. Past the clay lamp. Down into the kitchen.

Toward his mother.

---

His father got there first.

Antho would never know how. His father couldn't walk. He couldn't stand. He'd been in a wheeled chair for two weeks.

He got there first.

Out of the chair. Onto his feet. With his stick. Three steps across the kitchen floor, staggering, his thin legs shaking under him, his white hair flying, his face set. Between the Mothwing and his wife.

And swung the stick.

It wasn't much of a swing. He was eighty-three. He'd never been a fighter. He'd been a Warden-Architect, a scholar, a man of ledgers and council chambers. He swung the stick the way an old man swings a stick at a dog.

It hit the first Mothwing on the side of the head.

And the Mothwing came apart. Into dust. Like the others did. And drifted. And started, slowly, to gather.

And the second one put its long pale hand on Arion Vey's face.

---

"*NO*—"

Antho didn't know who screamed. Him. His mother. Odile.

The Mothwing's hand was on his father's cheek. Very gently. Like a mother. And the dust came off its fingers onto his father's skin. Grey-gold. Glittering. The shape of a hand.

And his father's eyes—

Didn't dim.

Antho saw it. He'd never forget it. He saw the dust on his father's cheek, and saw his father's eyes, grey, clear, behind his spectacles, and saw them stay *exactly the same*.

His father looked at the Mothwing. With its hand on his face. Calmly. With mild interest. The way he'd looked at the Moth Queen in her dark drawing room on the Crown Terrace.

"*Nothing there*," said Arion Vey. "*I'm afraid.*"

The Mothwing went very still.

"*She took it already*," said his father. "*Thirteen years ago. On a grey river. She paid it to the Ferryman.*" He smiled. Faintly. "*Every warm thing I had was her. And she gave it all away. There's nothing left in me for you to drink.*"

He looked past the Mothwing. At his wife. Standing in the middle of the kitchen with the clay lamp in her hand.

"*Except what she's given back*," said Arion. "*This summer. A little at a time.*" His voice was very soft. "*Burnt porridge. You have a nice voice. She fell asleep on my shoulder.*"

He swayed.

"*And you can't have those*," said Arion Vey. "*Those are already mine.*"

His knees went.

---

He fell.

Antho saw him go down. Slowly. The way a tree goes down. His thin old legs folding. His stick clattering. His white head hitting the stone floor of the kitchen with a sound Antho would hear in his sleep for the rest of his life.

And didn't move.

And the third Mothwing, the one who hadn't touched anyone yet, stepped over him.

Toward Seren.

---

Antho moved.

Out of the doorway. Into the kitchen. With the Lantern. With the dark. With everything he had.

And behind him, the moths came through.

He felt them. The doorway, empty. The light gone out of it. The hush gone. The moths pouring into the kitchen behind him like a river through a broken dam. Over Odile. Over the students. Over him.

He didn't care.

He had the dark round the third Mothwing before it could take another step. Shade. Commanding. Not asking. *Taking*. He wrapped it in black and squeezed, and it came apart into dust, and he squeezed the dust, and the dust went *out*, the way a candle goes out when you pinch the wick.

He'd never done that before.

He'd never let himself.

"*So the little one won't come up toward it*," said Seraphine Ilvane. "*Will it. Not toward Seren's lamp.*"

Antho looked down at the lamp.

Small. Brown. Clay. Lit. In his hands.

"*No*," he said slowly. "*Not toward Mam's.*"

He looked up.

At his brother. At his wife. At his father in the chair with the bandage on his head and his grey eyes, open now, watching.

"*Toward mine*," said Antho Vey.
