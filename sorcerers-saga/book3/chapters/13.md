# The Drowned Archive

Lex made the first Hush-cup out of a horseshoe.

It was the only iron to hand. He was at the Lantern Keep, in the kitchen, at dawn, with his brother's letter still in his fist. Quill had brought it to Ashbourne in the night. Three lines. *Pim folded one. From the top. Without going in. Come.* He'd come on Vharuun before the sun was up, with Wren behind him, and found them all in the kitchen, Antho and Seraphine and Pim and the students, with a black pebble on the table and porridge nobody had eaten and Seraphine's notebook open to forty pages of arrows.

Seraphine had explained it to him. Twice. With diagrams.

*It doesn't want dark*, she'd said. *It doesn't want light. It wants room. A space round it. Not touching it. Not pressing it. Just—held, without being held.*

*Like Pim's hands*, Lex had said.

*Like Pim's hands*. Seraphine had looked at him over her spectacles. *But there's only one Pim. And forty-one Delves. And more every week.*

So Lex had gone out to the stable, where Ashka slept, and found a cast horseshoe in the straw, and brought it back, and sat down at the kitchen table, and started to think.

---

*[You're going to make a hand*,] said Ember.

*I'm going to make a cup*.

*[A cup shaped like a pair of hands*.]

*A cup shaped like the space inside a pair of hands*, thought Lex. *That's different.*

He held the horseshoe in his palm. Old iron. Worn thin at the toe where it had struck stone. A good shoe. It had carried a horse a long way.

He didn't put it in a fire. There was no need. He was a Worldforger. He asked it.

And the horseshoe softened in his hand. Like wax. Like warm bread. And he shaped it with his thumbs. Slowly. Not into a cup. Into the space a cup would hold. A curve. A bowl. Shallow. Open at the top. Like two hands cupped together, if the hands were made of iron and there was nothing inside them but air.

Then he turned to Seraphine.

"*The glyph*," he said. "*From Malachi's vellum. The one scratched into the black circle.*"

She was already drawing it. On a scrap of paper. The Wellscript glyph for the seventh. *Hollow*. A circle with a break in it. Like a ring that doesn't quite close.

He couldn't read it. He'd lost that with the Root. But he didn't need to read it. He only needed to cut it.

He cut it into the inside of the iron cup. On the curve. Where the palms would be.

And then, round the rim, very small, very fine, the way he'd cut them into the Keeper's Lantern five years ago in Malachi's valley: Hush-runes. Seven of them. Not twelve. Seven.

He held it up.

It was small. Rough. The size of his two hands cupped. Grey iron, with a broken circle on the inside and seven runes round the edge.

"*Pim*," said Lex.

---

The boy came round the table.

Lex held the cup out. Pim looked at it. Then at Lex. Then he put out his small thin hands. And cupped them round it. Not touching. An inch away. The way he'd cupped them round the hole in the cold-room.

And was silent.

Lex felt it go in.

Not much. A little. The way warmth goes into iron when you hold it. Pim's silence. The room in him. Going into the curve of the cup. Settling there. Like water settling into a bowl.

And the cup went *quiet*.

That was the only word for it. It didn't glow. It didn't hum. It didn't do anything a person could see. It just—went quiet. The way a room goes quiet when someone you love walks into it.

Lex looked at that line for a long time.

*[That's going to be a problem*,] said Ember. Quietly.

*I know*.

---

They made forty more in three days.

Not Lex alone. Everyone. Brannoc came up the mountain with Tilda and a cart full of iron and twenty students from the Forge, grumbling about his knees the whole way, and set up three anvils in the Keep's cave mouth. Hedda came with a box of Hush-rune templates she'd been keeping since the Hollowmaw lanterns. Seraphine cut the broken circle into every cup with a steel stylus, sitting at the kitchen table, tongue between her teeth.

And Pim filled them.

One at a time. In the evenings. Sitting on his rock at the cave mouth with a cup in his lap and his hands round it, silent, for an hour each, while the sun went down over the Greyspine. He didn't complain. He didn't say anything. He just sat. And filled them. And when each one was done he put it in a basket at his feet, carefully, the way a hen puts down an egg, and picked up the next.

By the third evening he was grey with tiredness.

"*He can't keep doing this*," said Antho. Watching him from the cave mouth. Very low.

"*I know*."

"*He's thirteen*. *Or twelve. Or fourteen. He's a child.*"

"*I know*," said Lex. "*I'm working on it.*"

He was. He'd been working on it for three days. Every cup he made, he made a little differently. Trying to find a way to make the room in it without Pim. To make the quiet *himself*. He'd tried Shade, and the Stone in him, and his own breath, and the space between hammer-strokes.

None of it worked.

*[You can't make a room by putting things in*,] said Ember. *[That's what Seraphine says. The Hollow's the space. Not the thing. You keep trying to put something in the cup.]*

*Then how do I make the space*?

*[I don't know*,] said Ember. *[I'm a coal in a hearth. I'm not very good at nothing either.]*

---

The raven came on the fourth morning.

Not Roole's. Not Quill. A bird from the Saltreach, bedraggled, with sea-salt on its feathers and a slip of paper on its leg in a scrawled, ink-blotted hand.

*Qel'Varran*. *The Drowned Archive. It's opened. In the dome. The middle. Under the water.*

*The Coldwater crew passed through Saltmere village yesterday. Six of them. With a big man with a pick.*

*There were moths with them. A lot of moths.*

*— Arno Vell, Captain, Saltreach garrison*

---

"*Qel'Varran*," said Seraphine.

She'd gone very white.

Lex knew why. Five years ago, in the Saltmere Fens, in the half-sunk dome of the Drowned Archive, she'd been carried in on a litter with a gold-lightning burn down her side, half-dead, and watched him burn Inkwraiths with ordinary fire. She'd found the Listeners' Codex there. The book that changed everything. The book she'd copied nine times.

It was the most important library in the world. It was where she'd found out who she was.

And now it was a Delve.

"*I'm coming*," said Seraphine.

Antho opened his mouth.

"*I'm coming*," said his wife. "*I know that dome better than anyone alive. I know every shelf. Every gallery. Every broken window.*" She looked at him. "*And I can hear it now. I can hear the room. I can tell you where the core is before anyone goes down.*"

Antho closed his mouth.

"*And Pim*," said Seraphine. "*Pim has to come. And you know it.*"

---

They went seven.

Lex. Seraphine. Pim, with the basket of Hush-cups on his back, forty of them, wrapped in straw. Tilda, with Brannoc's hammer. And the twins.

Ilse and Ivo Marrow had turned up at the Keep on the third evening. In their cart. With a great deal of noise. They'd been touring the Bitter Hills with *The Knights of the Ford* and heard about the Delves, and the Uncapped, and a big man with a pick, and the moths. And had turned the cart round.

"*You need a distraction*," said Ilse. Standing in the cave mouth with her hands on her hips. "*Everyone always needs a distraction. That's what we're for.*"

"*We're very distracting*," said Ivo.

"*He fell off the stage in Hollin's Rest*," said Ilse. "*Into a pig-trough. The whole village came out to see.*"

"*That was on purpose*."

"*It was not on purpose*."

Lex looked at them. Twenty-six now. Mouse-brown hair. Patched green coats. Holding hands without seeming to notice they were doing it. Two years ago Ivo had made his own wrist look like nothing so his sister would let go, and been dragged down to the bottom of the world by a knight made of two knights. And come back.

"*Yes*," said Lex. "*Come.*"

And Antho.

Antho came. Lex had expected him to stay. To hold the door. To watch for moths at the Keep.

He didn't.

"*Odile has the door*," he said. "*And Father's lamp. And Ivy can hear it coming from three valleys off.*" He looked at Lex. "*And when you set one of those cups at the mouth of that Delve, and it folds, I want to be there. I want to feel it go home. I want to feel it not scream.*"

Lex looked at him.

"*I've felt it scream twenty-three times*," said his brother. Very quietly. "*I'd like to feel it do something else.*"

---

Wren came as far as the fen.

She'd said she would. She'd said it at breakfast at Harrow, standing at the stove with her back to him, in a voice that dared him to argue. *As far as the fen. To the edge. Where the reeds start. I'll stand on dry ground with my bow and my wind and I'll watch the dome, and if anything comes out of it that isn't you, I'll shoot it.*

He didn't argue.

She was nine weeks gone. She'd been sick every morning for a month. Old Mag said it would stop soon. Old Mag said a great many things, most of them about ginger.

She stood at the edge of the Saltmere Fens in the grey morning, in her leathers, with her bow strung and her red braid over one shoulder and her hand resting, without her seeming to notice, on her stomach.

"*Come back*," said Wren.

"*I'll come back*."

"*And don't*—" She stopped. Looked at the dome, far off across the reeds, rising out of the black water like the top of a buried skull. "*Don't let them break it, Lex. Please.*"

---

The Saltmere Fens hadn't changed.

Reeds. Black water. Mist. Miles of it. Flat and grey and silent, stretching away to the edge of the sky, with here and there a stunted alder or a hump of drier ground. The smell of rot and salt and old water. The cry of a bittern somewhere, hollow, like someone blowing across the top of a jug.

And in the middle of it, rising out of the black water, the dome.

Qel'Varran. The Drowned Archive. Grey stone. Huge. Half-sunk. Its great curved roof broken in places, its round windows staring out over the fen like empty eye-sockets. It had been built nine hundred years ago, before the net. By the Listeners. To keep their books safe. And then the sea had come in, and the land had gone down, and the fen had swallowed it up to its shoulders.

They went in the way they'd gone in five years ago. Through a broken window, just above the waterline. Onto the gallery.

And stopped.

---

The dome was *lit*.

Five years ago it had been dark. Pitch dark. A great hollow of black, with the shelves spiralling down into it, and at the very bottom, far below, the faint gleam of still water.

It wasn't dark now.

There was light at the bottom. Faint. Grey-gold. Shifting. Like light through dust. Like the light in a room where the curtains are drawn and the sun is shining outside.

And it was moving.

Lex stood on the gallery and looked down. Down the long spiral of the shelves. Down past the rotted books and the stone galleries and the broken stairs. To the bottom. To the black water. Where, five years ago, there had been nothing.

There was a hole in the water now.

Round. Flat. Perfect. Black. *In* the water. Not under it. *In* it. As if someone had cut a circle out of the surface of the pool at the bottom of the dome and lifted it away. The water stood up round the edge of it like the walls of a well. Twenty feet across.

And around it, over it, above it, filling the bottom of the dome like smoke fills a chimney—

Moths.

---

Thousands.

Lex had seen the cloud at the Keep. Antho had told him. He hadn't understood. Not properly. Not until now.

They hung in the air at the bottom of the dome in a great slow turning column. Grey-gold. Glittering. Drifting. Round and round. Like water going down a drain. Like leaves in a whirlpool. Their wings caught the faint light and threw it back, so the whole bottom of the dome shimmered. It was beautiful. It was the most beautiful thing Lex had ever seen.

It made him want to be sick.

And among them, standing on the narrow stone ledge that ran round the edge of the water at the very bottom of the dome, people.

Not people. Mothwing. Twenty. Thirty. Pale. Thin. Ragged dust-wings folded on their backs. Standing very still. Facing the hole. Waiting.

And beside them, on the ledge, six figures in patched leather and steel plate. With picks.

---

"*They're already down*," said Antho. Very low.

Lex looked.

He was right. The six on the ledge weren't moving. They were standing at the edge of the hole. Looking down into it. Coldwater crew. Lex knew them. Brigid with her hammer. The Tull brothers. Old Absalom with his helmet-lamp. Kit.

Five.

Not six.

*Hask's gone in*, thought Lex. *Hask's already in it.*

"*How long*?" he said.

Seraphine had her eyes shut. Standing on the gallery with one hand on the stone shelf beside her and her head tilted. Listening. To the room.

"*Not long*," she said. "*An hour. Less. He's—*" She frowned. "*He's on the second floor. I think. I can hear the space change. Where he's walking. Like—like a mouse in a wall.*" She opened her eyes. "*He's fast, Lex. He's very fast.*"

"*Can we fold it*?" said Antho. "*From up here? Now?*"

"*With him inside*?"

"*Yes*."

Seraphine looked at him.

"*I don't know*," she said. "*Pim folded the cold-room with no one in it. I don't know what happens if there's someone inside.*" She swallowed. "*At Ashbourne, when Lex claimed the core, it folded up round him and put him out on the green. Gently. But that was the core going home. I don't know what a fold does to a person who's still on the second floor.*"

Lex looked down at the hole. At the moths. At the five figures on the ledge.

"*Then we go down*," he said. "*And get him out. And then we fold it.*"

---

The moths noticed them on the third turn of the stair.

He'd known they would. He'd brought fire. He *was* fire. Every one of them was carrying a light of some kind. Antho had the Lantern. Seraphine had a lamp. Tilda had a lamp. Even Pim had a little tin lantern on his belt, that Odile had given him.

The cloud at the bottom of the dome shifted. All at once. Like a flock of starlings. And began to rise.

Up the spiral of the shelves. Slowly. Glittering. Toward the gallery. Toward the lamps.

And the Mothwing on the ledge turned their pale faces up.

"*Lights out*," said Lex. "*All of them. Now.*"

They put them out. Seraphine's lamp. Tilda's. Pim's little tin one. Antho shuttered the Lantern.

The cloud kept coming.

"*It's you*," said Antho. "*Lex. It's you. You're the light.*"

Lex looked down at himself.

He hadn't noticed. He never noticed anymore. He'd carried the fire in him so long it was like his own heartbeat. But in the dark of the dome, with every lamp out, he could see it. A faint gold. Coming off his skin. Off his hands. Off his hair. The glow of a forge banked down for the night. The glow of a Worldforger.

He couldn't put it out. It was him.

"*Ilse*," he said. "*Ivo.*"

---

The twins had been waiting for this.

Lex saw it in their faces. In the way they grinned at each other in the dark. The quick fierce thieves' grin from the Wickhollow, before Dorran died, before the Rift, before everything.

"*Lights*," said Ilse.

"*Lots of lights*," said Ivo.

And they lit the dome.

---

Not real lights. Illusions. They were the best illusionists in Caldor. They'd made fifty Queens dance in a ballroom. They'd made a dragon of light the size of a house wear a veil at a wedding.

They made lanterns.

Hundreds of them. All at once. Hanging in the air of the dome. Up and down the spiral of the shelves. On the galleries. Over the black water. Gold and red and white and blue. Paper lanterns and iron ones and great brass temple-lamps. Swinging gently on chains that weren't there, from hooks that weren't there. Burning with a light that wasn't there.

It was beautiful.

The moths went mad.

---

The cloud broke apart. Into a thousand streams. Each one making for a lantern. Any lantern. The nearest. The brightest. They poured over the illusions like water over rocks. Brushing them. Settling on them. Beating against them.

Drinking.

And getting *nothing*.

Lex watched it. Watched a moth land on the glass of an illusory brass lamp and press its grey-gold wings against it and *drink*, and find nothing, and drink harder, and find nothing, and flutter off, confused, and go to the next one. And the next. And the next.

The Mothwing on the ledge were moving now. Rising. On their ragged wings. Gliding up into the dome toward the nearest lanterns. Reaching for them with their long pale hands.

Their hands went through.

*Like the Unfinished*, thought Lex. *Like my fire through the lined wolf. There's nothing there to hold.*

"*That won't hold them long*," said Ivo. Sweating. His hands were moving. Shaping. Holding the illusions in the air. "*They'll work it out. They're not stupid. They're just hungry.*"

"*How long*?"

"*Ten minutes*," said Ilse. "*Maybe less.*" She grinned. Fierce. "*Go.*"

---

They went down.

Fast. Down the spiral stair, past the rotted shelves, past the moths swarming the lanterns that weren't there. Lex first. Antho behind him, with the shuttered Lantern. Seraphine. Tilda with the hammer. Pim with the basket of cups on his back.

The five on the ledge saw them coming.

Brigid turned. With her hammer. A big rawboned woman with a face like a hatchet. She saw Lex coming down the last turn of the stair with gold coming off his skin and fire in his hands, and her face went white. And then set.

"*Smith*," she said.

"*Where is he*?"

"*Down*." She didn't lift the hammer. Not yet. "*Second floor. Third, by now. He'll be at the core soon.*" Her eyes went to the basket on Pim's back. "*What's that?*"

"*A way to close it*," said Lex. "*Without breaking it.*"

Brigid looked at him.

"*He told us you'd say that*," she said.

"*And*?"

"*And he said you'd mean it*." The hammer came up. Slowly. "*He also said it's not your call.*"

---

It was Kit who stopped it.

The boy. Seventeen. White-faced. Standing at the back of the five on the ledge with a short pick in his hands that he didn't seem to know what to do with.

"*Brigid*," he said.

"*Not now, Kit*."

"*Brigid*. *Look at them.*"

He wasn't looking at Lex. He was looking up. At the moths. At the Mothwing. At the pale ragged figures gliding through the illusory lanterns with their long hands reaching and going through.

"*Look at them*," said Kit. His voice was shaking. "*That's what he's turning into. Isn't it? That's what she's making him.*"

The ledge went very quiet.

Brigid didn't look round. But her hammer stopped rising.

"*His eyes*," said Kit. "*You've seen. Since Thistledown. Since he went to see her in Highcrest. They've gone gold. Like hers. Like* theirs.*"* He pointed up. At the Mothwing. "*And the cough's gone. Gone completely. Not better. Gone. In a week.*"

"*Kit*," said one of the Tull brothers. Low. Warning.

"*And there's something on his back*," said Kit. "*Under his coat. I've seen it. When he takes it off at night. Something*—"

He stopped.

He was crying.

"*Two humps*," said Kit. "*Like something folded. Under the skin.*"

---

Nobody said anything.

The moths whirled overhead. The illusory lanterns swung. Ivo's voice came down from somewhere far above, strained, *five minutes, Lex, maybe four*.

Brigid looked at Lex.

For a long moment. Her hatchet face very still.

"*Get him out*," she said. Very low. "*Smith. If you can. Get him out of there before he breaks it.*" Her voice cracked. "*He's going to break it and go up and she's going to give him another sip and the wings are going to come through and he won't be Silas anymore.*"

She stepped aside.

---

The hole in the water was twenty feet across. Black. Breathing.

Lex stood at the edge of it.

"*Seraphine*."

"*Third floor*." She had her eyes shut. Her hand on Antho's arm. "*Near the bottom. He's—he's near the core. He's walking toward it. Slowly.*" She opened her eyes. "*He's not fighting anything. There's nothing left to fight. He's just walking.*"

"*Can we fold it with him in it*?"

"*I don't know*."

"*Guess*."

Seraphine looked at the hole.

"*I think*," she said slowly, "*I think the room doesn't want to hurt anyone. I think if it goes home with him in it, it'll put him out. Like it put you out at Ashbourne.*" She swallowed. "*I think. I can't hear well enough to be sure. I've only been listening for four days.*"

Lex looked at Pim.

The boy had the basket off his back. He'd unwrapped one of the cups. He was holding it in his two hands, cupped round it, the way he held everything. Looking up at Lex.

Waiting.

"*Set it*," said Lex.

---

And then, from the hole, a voice.

Not the small one. Not the frightened one. Not *brother*. Not *Mal*.

A woman's.

Coming up out of the black. Out of the round dark in the water at the bottom of the Drowned Archive. Faint. Far. Echoing. As if it had come up a very long way. Up a shaft. Up a hole that went straight down to the bottom of the world.

"*Lex*."

He couldn't move.

"*Lex, love*."

His mother's voice.

---

Not through the door. Not through the black glass at the Hollowmaw, where Antho heard her. Not through the clay lamp. Here. In a drowned library in the middle of a fen. Coming up out of a hole in the water.

*Up a shaft*, thought Lex. *The broken ones. The shattered cores. The wounds that stay open.*

*Straight down*.

*She's at the bottom of one*.

"*Mam*," he said. Out loud. Hoarse. To the hole.

"*Listen to me*," said Seren Vey. Her voice was fading in and out. Like a voice on the wind. Like someone calling from the bottom of a well. "*Listen. I don't have long. It hurts to talk through these. They're wounds. It's like—like shouting through a cut.*"

"*Mam, we're coming*—"

"*No*. *Listen.*" Her voice came stronger. Hard. Cold. The voice Antho had heard through the door. The Harrow voice. "*They're coming down the holes. The moths. All of them. Down every broken one. There's twenty-six now. They're pouring down like smoke down a chimney.*"

A pause. The voice fading. Coming back.

"*And she's coming down*," said his mother. "*Herself. I can feel her. On the way. In the dark. She's coming down to us, Lex. To the little one.*"

A long pause.

"*To me*."

---

And then, from below, from the third floor of the Delve, from somewhere very close to the core—

A sound.

Not a scream. Not yet. A *tightening*. Lex felt it through the Stone in him. Through the floor of the dome. Through the black water. A drawing-in. Like a breath before a blow. Like the moment before a hammer falls.

Antho went white.

"*He's at the core*," said Seraphine. "*He's standing over it. He's*—"

"*Pim*!"

Pim set the cup.

---

He knelt at the edge of the hole. On the narrow stone ledge. In the faint grey-gold light, with the moths whirling overhead and the illusory lanterns swinging and the Mothwing reaching and reaching through nothing. He knelt and set the iron cup down on the black water at the edge of the hole. Gently. As if he were putting a candle on a windowsill.

And cupped his hands round it.

And was silent.

---

The hole folded.

Not slowly. Not like the cold-room. Fast. Like a door closing. Like an eye shutting. Like a hand drawn back from a flame.

Twenty feet. Ten. Five. Gone.

And as it went, it *put out* what was inside it.

Lex felt it happen. Felt the world turn over, gently, like a page. Felt something come up out of the folding dark and land on the ledge beside him, hard, rolling, in a clatter of steel plate and a grunt and the ring of a pick on stone.

Silas Hask.

On his back. On the wet stone ledge at the bottom of the Drowned Archive. With his pick still in his hand. Raised. Over his head. Ready to fall.

On nothing.

The water at the bottom of the dome was just water again. Black. Still. Faintly gleaming. With a small iron cup floating on it, rocking gently, quiet.

And under the cup, on the bottom of the pool, under a foot of black water, Lex could see it. A pebble. Black. Smooth. The size of a hen's egg.

Whole.

Silas Hask got up.

Slowly. On one knee. Then the other. With his pick in one hand and the other flat on the wet stone. Breathing hard. He'd lost his helmet in the fall. His cropped grey-brown hair was wet.

He looked up at Lex.

And Lex saw his eyes.

Gold.

Not like Maelis's. Not lit. Not windows. Fainter. Duller. Like the Mothwing at Greyfall. Two dull gold sparks, deep in his weathered face, like the last coals in a grate.

And he wasn't coughing.

Not at all. His breath came deep and clean and easy, in and out, in the cold wet air of the dome. No rattle. No catch. Nothing.

"*Smith*," said Silas Hask.

His voice was the same. Rough. Calm. Reasonable.

"*You closed it*," he said. "*With me in it.*"

"*It let you out*."

"*I was standing over the core*." Hask looked down at the pick in his hand. At the point. "*I had it. It was right there. At my feet. I had the pick up.*" He looked at Lex. "*Do you know what that was worth? At my level? Five times? Do you know how far that would have taken me?*"

"*Yes*," said Lex.

Hask looked at him.

"*And you closed it anyway*."

"*Yes*."

---

The moths had stopped.

Lex noticed it all at once. The illusory lanterns were gone. Ivo had let them go. The dome was dark again, except for the faint glow off Lex's own skin and the grey-gold glitter of the cloud overhead.

And the cloud wasn't moving.

It hung in the air of the dome. Still. Every moth. Thousands of them. With their wings spread. Their painted eyes. All turned the same way.

Toward Silas Hask.

And the Mothwing, on the galleries and the ledges and in the air, had stopped too. Their pale faces turned. Their dull gold eyes.

Toward him.

The way you'd turn toward someone who's just walked into the room. Someone important. Someone you've been told to watch.

Hask felt it. Lex saw him feel it. Saw the big man's shoulders go tight under his patched coat. Saw something move under the leather, between his shoulder blades. Two humps. Folded. Shifting.

Hask didn't look up at them.

He looked at Lex.

"*I'm not hers*," said Silas Hask. Very quietly. "*Whatever the boy told you. I'm not.*"

"*Your eyes*—"

"*She gave me a sip*." Hask's voice didn't change. "*In Highcrest. In her drawing room. In the dark. Out of her own hands.*" He touched his chest. "*And I breathed. For the first time in twenty-six years. All the way down. No grey.*" He looked at Lex with his dull gold eyes. "*Do you know what that's like, smith? To breathe?*"

Lex didn't answer.

"*I'm not hers*," said Hask again. "*I'm mine. I'm finally mine. Nobody's Ceiling. Nobody's hewer. Nobody's.*" He picked up his helmet off the stone. Put it on. "*She just helped.*"

He turned. And whistled. Two notes.

And the Coldwater crew came to him. Slowly. Brigid. The Tulls. Absalom.

Not Kit.

Kit stood at the edge of the ledge. White. Shaking. And didn't move.

Hask looked at him. For a long moment.

"*Kit*," he said.

"*I'm staying*," said the boy. His voice cracked in half. "*I'm sorry. I'm sorry, Silas. I'm staying.*"

Silas Hask looked at him a moment longer.

Then he nodded. Once. Like a man who's heard the roof creak and decided which way it's going to fall.

And turned. And walked up the spiral stair of the Drowned Archive. With his crew behind him.

And the moths went with him.

All of them. The whole glittering cloud. Rising off the bottom of the dome in a great slow spiral, following him up the stair. Like a cloak. Like a train. Like a shadow made of light.

---

Lex stood on the ledge for a long time after.

With his brother beside him. And Seraphine. And Pim, kneeling at the edge of the water, with his hands still cupped round nothing, and tears running down his face, very quietly, because he was tired, and because it was over, and because it hadn't screamed.

It hadn't screamed. Lex had felt it go. Felt the thread draw back. Softly. Into the dark. Home.

*Like a hand drawn back from a flame*.

But he couldn't stop hearing the other thing.

*She's coming down. Herself. To the little one.*

*To me*.

---

Antho put the Lantern on the stone. And knelt beside Pim. And put his arm round the boy's shoulders.

"*It didn't scream*," said his brother. Very low. "*Lex. It didn't scream. I felt it go home. It was*—" He didn't have the word.

"*I know*."

"*It was like the first one. The cold-room. It was glad.*"

"*I know*." Lex was looking at the water. At the small iron cup, rocking gently on the black. "*Antho.*"

His brother looked up.

"*Twenty-six holes*," said Lex. "*She said. Twenty-six broken. Wide open. Straight down.*" He swallowed. "*And Maelis is going down one of them.*"

"*I know*."

"*To Mam*."

"*I know*."

"*We can't fold those*," said Lex. "*Can we. The broken ones. There's no core to go home. There's just the wound.*" He looked at Seraphine. "*Can we?*"

Seraphine shook her head. Slowly.

"*I don't know*," she said. "*I don't think so. Not with a cup. A cup's a room. You can't put a wound in a room.*" She looked at the water. "*You'd have to—mend it. Stitch it. Somehow.*" Her voice was very small. "*I don't know how.*"

Lex didn't say anything.

He stood on the ledge at the bottom of the Drowned Archive and looked down at the black water. At the pebble lying whole on the bottom of the pool, under a foot of dark, humming.

And thought about a woman with lit eyes, folding her wings, leaning into a hole the way a moth leans into a gap in a shutter. Going down. Down. Down. Patient. Hungry. Toward the bottom of the world.

Toward a woman with a clay lamp, singing flat on the high note.

Toward something enormous and soft and young and frightened, that had been drunk dry once already, a long time ago, by a woman with lit eyes kneeling at the edge of a pool.

*She looked at me like I was a lamp*, it had told his brother. *Like I was a lamp she wanted to put her hands round.*

"*We have to go down*," said Lex.

Nobody answered.

They didn't have to.
