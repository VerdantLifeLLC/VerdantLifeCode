# The Long Walk Up

Seren Vey had been walking for a year and a half, and her feet hurt.

That was the thing nobody ever told you about the bottom of the world. That your feet would hurt. All the old stories, the ones her mother told by the fire in twenty-two different cottages in twenty-two different valleys, the stories about heroes going down into the dark under the world, Listeners and kings and girls with lamps, none of them ever said *and her feet were killing her*.

They should have. It was the truest thing about it.

She stopped. And sat down. On nothing. On the dark. It held her up; it always did, down here. Like sitting on a cushion of air. And she took off her left shoe, which had been her right shoe once, a long time ago, until the left one wore through and she swapped, and rubbed her foot.

"*Again*?" said Isaura Kell.

"*Again*."

"*You stopped an hour ago*."

"*There aren't any hours down here*," said Seren. "*You keep saying that like there are hours. There aren't. There's walking and there's stopping. And I'm stopping.*"

Kell looked at her.

She was standing a little way off. On the dark. Straight-backed. In robes that had been white once, Warden-white, the white of the High Warden of the Order of the Lattice, and were now the color of old candle-wax, torn at the hem and stained at the knees. She was tall and gaunt and grey, with her hair cut short, very short, the way she'd cut it herself with a knife in the first month. Her face was all bones. Her eyes were very pale and very steady.

She'd been the most powerful woman in Caldor once. Tier IV. Level eighty-one. She'd had kings bow to her. She'd sent Wardens to burn hedge-witches.

She'd sent Wardens to burn women exactly like Seren.

"*Fine*," said Isaura Kell. And sat down too. On the dark. A little way off. Very straight. With her hands on her knees.

---

They'd walked a long way.

Seren didn't know how far. There was no way to know. There were no roads in the Hollow. No landmarks. No sun, no moon, no stars—

No. That wasn't true anymore. There were stars now.

That had been the first thing. After the Heart. After her boys reforged the net and the eaten worlds went up in gold and the river ran clean and the deep went from hunger to quiet. She'd looked up, at the bottom of the world, and there had been stars.

Not real stars. She didn't think. Little lights. Very far up. Scattered across the dark like seeds. Soft and silver and faint.

*Shade*, Antho had said. When she'd told him. Through the door, a hundred times since, in the long nights. *That's Shade, Mam. That's what Shade's dark looks like, when it's not being eaten. It's not empty. It's never been empty. It's just dark. Like the sky's dark.*

Her clever boy. Her clever dark boy, who listened.

So they walked under Shade's stars. Up. Always up. Not up like a hill. Up like a dream of rising. She'd tried to explain it to Kell once and given up. You just *knew* which way was up, down here. The way a plant knows. The way a child in the womb knows which way is out.

Toward the light.

Toward the lamp.

---

She had the lamp in her lap now. She always had it. In her lap when she sat, in her hand when she walked. A small brown clay lamp. Round. Plain. With a chip in its spout and a thumbprint in its base. Her own thumbprint. She'd made it at the kitchen table at Harrow, the summer Lex was born, out of clay from the stream.

It wasn't really the lamp. She knew that. The lamp was on the other side of the door. In a niche, in a cavern at the bottom of a mountain, with an iron one beside it and a new one beside that. Her boys had put it there. And a man she didn't remember filled it every night, and said good evening.

This was the *flame*. The other half of it. The light that came through. One flame on both sides of the door. That was how her mother had taught her to make it, a long time ago, in a cottage in the Ironbacks, by the fire. *A Harrow lamp*, Wenna had said. *For the ones who go. So there's always a light on both sides.*

She'd carried it into the dark thirteen years ago. With her hands shaking and her eldest son screaming on the stairs.

She'd never once let it go out.

---

"*It's quiet*," said Kell.

Seren looked up.

The Warden was looking past her. Behind her. Down. The way they'd come.

At the little one.

---

She'd never be able to describe it. She'd tried. To Kell, who didn't need it described, who could see it as well as she could. To herself. To her boys, through the door.

The best she'd managed was: *it's like the sky.*

Not the sky with stars. The sky *between* the stars. The soft deep dark that's not empty, that's full of something you can't see. If you took that, all of it, the whole night sky over the Vale on a clear winter night, and folded it up, and folded it again, and made it soft, and made it *warm*, and made it *young*—

And then put it behind you. On the long road up out of the dark. Following. Close. The way a child follows its mother through a crowd. With its hand in the back of her skirt.

That was the little one.

It went back and down from where they sat for miles. She couldn't see the end of it. A great soft slope of dark, darker than the dark, rising behind them like a hill. Like a sleeping animal. Like a wave that hasn't broken yet. You could only see it because it blotted out Shade's stars. Where it was, there were no stars. Just soft deep black, going up and back and away.

And it was quiet.

That was what Kell meant. It was *quiet*. It was usually humming. Singing. Talking. Asking. It talked all the time. In its small young voice, in Seren's head and in the dark all round them. *Lamp-lady. Lamp-lady, where are we going. Lamp-lady, what's up there. Lamp-lady, is Mal up there. Lamp-lady, will they like me.*

It wasn't talking now.

---

"*Little one*," said Seren. Out loud. Softly. "*Little lamb. What's wrong?*"

Nothing.

Then, very small, from everywhere at once:

*[...it's going to happen again...]*

Seren went still.

*[...I can feel it...far up...someone standing over one of my hands...]*

*[...with something heavy...]*

She was on her feet. She didn't remember standing. The lamp was in her hand and she was turning toward the great soft slope of dark behind her with her free hand out, open, the way you'd hold out your hand to a frightened horse.

"*I'm here*," she said. "*Lamb. I'm here. Hold on to me.*"

*[...lamp-lady...]*

And then the snap.

---

She'd felt it twenty-six times now. She'd never get used to it.

It came from far up. From the world. From one of the holes. The little hands it put up through the ground, the threads it reached toward the light. Somewhere up there, very far, in a place she couldn't see, someone brought something heavy down on the end of one of them.

And it *broke*.

She felt it go. Like a bone snapping. Like a twig. A small sharp crack, far off, that came down the thread like a shock down a rope.

And the little one *flinched*.

All of it. All at once. The whole vast soft slope of it behind them, miles of it, folded night-sky, shuddering. Like a dog shudders when it's struck. Like a child flinches from a raised hand. The dark round them went cold for an instant. The stars overhead flickered.

And it screamed.

---

It wasn't loud. That was the worst thing. It was never loud. It was small. High. Thin. A sound you felt more than heard. In your chest. In your teeth. A child's cry in the night, in another room, when you're too far away to get there in time.

*[...WHY...]*

*[...why why why I didn't DO anything...]*

*[...it HURTS...]*

Seren was holding it.

She didn't know how. She never knew how. She just did. She stood on the dark with the lamp held up in one hand and her other hand flat on the nearest edge of it, on the soft cool dark that was its skin, if it had skin, and *held*. With her whole self. With the Listener in her, the old untaught hedge-witch part of her that had been talking to things nobody else could hear since she was three. She held on. And sang.

*Little light, little light, burning low.*

Flat. On the high note. She always was. She'd never been able to hit it. Her mother used to laugh at her.

*Keep the door for the ones who go.*

And the shudder went on. And on. And slowly, slowly, under her hand, under her voice, it began to ease.

*[...lamp-lady...]*

*If the dark comes calling, don't you mind*—

*[...lamp-lady it hurts...]*

"*I know*," said Seren. Singing it. Into the dark. "*I know, my lamb. I know. I've got you.*"

*A lantern's lit for the ones behind*.

---

When it was over, she sat down again.

Not on purpose. Her legs went. She sat down on the dark with the lamp in her lap and her hands shaking and her face wet.

Kell was there. Crouched beside her. With one hand on her shoulder. Not holding. Just there. Kell was never very good at holding.

"*Twenty-seven*," said Isaura Kell. Quietly.

"*I know*."

"*It's getting faster*."

"*I know*."

The Warden was silent for a moment.

"*They don't know what they're doing*," said Kell. "*Up there. They don't know it's alive.*"

"*I know that too*."

"*I did*," said Kell.

Seren looked at her.

The gaunt grey face was very still. The pale eyes were looking at the dark. At the great soft slope of it, behind them, settling. Trembling a little still. The way a horse trembles after a fright.

"*For forty years*," said Isaura Kell. "*I put children's hands on Stones and pushed down. I sent Wardens to hunt women who talked to fire. I signed warrants for unlatticed sorcery. Forty-one of them. I remember every name.*" She was quiet. "*I didn't think they were alive either. Not really. Not the way I was. They were threads. Numbers. Risks.*"

She looked at her own hands. Long. Thin. Bony.

"*I knew*," she said. "*Somewhere. I always knew. And I did it anyway. Because it was the law, and the law kept things safe, and safe was the only thing I knew how to want.*"

She looked at Seren.

"*Those people up there don't know*," said Kell. "*That's not the same as me. They're better than me. They just don't know.*"

---

Seren didn't say anything for a long time.

She'd had a year and a half of this. Walking up out of the dark with the woman who'd been High Warden of the Order of the Lattice. Who'd hunted her mother, though she didn't know it. Who'd hunted women exactly like her. Who'd been unwritten by the Key, eaten by the hunger, dragged down to the bottom of the world with her name torn off, and had stood at the Heart with no face and no name and been given both back by a smith reading out of a ledger.

*Dorran Vask carried you*. That was what had done it. That was what Lex had said. And something had come back into Isaura Kell's empty face, like a lamp being lit in a window.

And then she'd gone and sealed the Hollowmaw threshold from the inside. *Warden by gift*. Seren had watched her do it. Stand in front of the great black door at the bottom of the world, from the dark side, and put her bony hands on it, and *hold*.

And then she'd turned round. And looked at Seren. And said, *I'm not going back. Not yet. You'll want company.*

Seren had laughed. She couldn't help it. *You*? she'd said. *Company? You sent Wardens to burn people like me.*

*Yes*, Kell had said. *I know. That's why.*

They'd been walking ever since.

---

"*You're not that woman anymore*," said Seren.

"*I am*," said Kell. "*I'm exactly that woman. I just know what she did now.*" She stood up. Straight-backed. Brushing off her ruined robes, which didn't need brushing, which nothing could clean. "*That's not redemption. That's arithmetic.*"

Seren looked up at her.

"*You know*," she said, "*for a woman who keeps saying she's not good company, you're very good company.*"

Isaura Kell looked down at her.

Something moved at the corner of her thin mouth. Very slightly. It might have been a smile. It was hard to tell. Kell didn't smile often, and when she did it came out sideways, like a door that hadn't been opened in years and had forgotten how to swing.

"*Put your shoe on*," said Isaura Kell. "*We should keep walking.*"

---

They kept walking.

Up. Under Shade's stars. With the little one behind them, following, its great soft dark rising and falling like breath.

Seren talked to it. She always talked to it, when they walked. It liked that. It liked her voice. It had liked it, it told her, since the very beginning. Since a woman with a lamp came into the dark thirteen years ago and sat with her back against a door and sang to a boy on the other side, flat on the high note, every night, for eight years.

*[...I heard you...]* it said. *[...all that time...I didn't know what you were...I was the other thing then...]*

*I know, lamb*.

*[...I was so hungry...I wanted to eat you...and then I wanted to listen...]*

*I know*.

*[...you sang flat...]*

"*Everyone says that*," said Seren. Out loud. Indignant. "*Everyone. My mother. My boys. Kell.*"

"*You do sing flat*," said Kell.

"*It's not flat*. *It's a choice.*"

*[...it's flat...]* said the little one. And something came with it. Something she'd never felt from it before, in all the months of walking. A ripple. Through the whole great soft dark of it. Small and bright and quick. Like a breeze across a still pond. Like a cat's tail flicking.

Seren stopped walking.

"*Did you just*—" She turned round. Looked up at the great soft slope of dark behind her. "*Did you just* laugh *at me?*"

*[...]*

*[...maybe...]*

Seren Vey stood on the dark at the bottom of the world and laughed. Out loud. Until her sides ached. Until Kell, beside her, made a small dry sound that might, if you were being generous, have been a laugh too.

---

It asked about Mal again that night.

It asked every night. When they stopped. When Seren lay down on the dark with the lamp beside her and her shoe off and her head on her arm. When it was quiet, and the stars were very far, and the little one curled round them in the dark like a cat round a hearth.

*[...lamp-lady...]*

"*Mm*."

*[...where's Mal...]*

She'd told it. A hundred times. She told it again.

"*He's gone, lamb*," she said. Softly. "*He went down. Into the deep. Into the very middle of you. With his brother. The one who held you. The old one, with the hammer.*" She was quiet. "*They went down together. Singing. And they stayed.*"

*[...but where...]*

"*In you*," said Seren. "*In the middle. Where it's warmest. That's where they are.*"

The little one was quiet for a long time.

*[...I can feel them...]* it said at last. Very small. *[...in the middle...two little lights...very still...]*

*[...they don't talk...]*

"*No*," said Seren. "*They don't talk anymore. They're resting.*"

*[...why...]*

She didn't know how to answer that. She'd never known. Her mother had died in a cottage in the Vale when Seren was nineteen, and she'd sat by the bed and held her hand, and at the end Wenna Harrow had opened her eyes and said *light the lamp for me*, and closed them. And Seren had sat there all night with the lamp lit and asked *why*. And nobody had ever answered.

"*Because they were very old*," she said at last. "*And very tired. And they'd done what they came to do.*" She swallowed. "*Mal sat with you. So you wouldn't be alone. That's what he came to do. And when you weren't alone anymore, when you had me, and Kell, and my boys through the door, he could rest.*"

The little one was quiet.

*[...he sang...]* it said. *[...the whole time...he sang the same song you do...]*

"*Did he*?"

*[...he sang it in tune...]*

Seren laughed. Into her arm. Her eyes stinging.

"*Well*," she said. "*He would. Clever old goat.*"

---

She dreamed of the man again.

She always did. She'd dreamed of him for thirteen years. Since the first winter. Since she'd stood on the shingle at the edge of a grey river and the Ferryman had said *the memory you would least wish to lose* and she'd paid.

Not of him. Of the hole where he'd been.

That was the dream. Always. Her life. The whole of it. Every day she remembered. And in every one of them, a gap. A shape. Where someone should have been standing.

She dreamed of the night Antho was born. In the cottage at Harrow. With her mother dead a year and the midwife from Ashbourne and the snow coming down outside. And someone holding her hand. All night. She could feel it. A hand in hers. Long fingers. Cool. Steady. She could feel the hand.

She couldn't see who it belonged to.

She dreamed of Lex at two. Sick with a fever. Screaming. And someone walking him up and down the kitchen at three in the morning, up and down, up and down, singing something low that wasn't the Lantern Song, that was something else, something old, in a voice—

She couldn't hear the voice.

She dreamed of a coat on a hook by the door. Grey. Long. A man's coat. She'd seen it every day for eight years. She'd brushed it. She'd mended its elbows. She could feel the wool under her fingers.

She didn't know whose it was.

---

She woke with her face wet.

Kell was sitting up. A little way off. Straight-backed. Watching the dark. She always watched, when Seren slept. She said she didn't need to sleep much. Seren didn't believe her.

"*The coat again*?" said Kell.

"*The coat*."

"*You were talking*."

"*What did I say*?"

"*You said, why are your hands so cold*." Kell didn't look round. "*And then you said, don't, I'll do it, you'll burn the porridge.*"

Seren laughed. Wetly.

"*I don't know him*," she said. "*Kell. I don't know him at all. I'm going home to a man I've been married to for thirty years and I don't know what he looks like. I don't know what he sounds like when he laughs. I don't know if he snores. I don't know if he takes salt in his porridge.*"

"*You know his voice*."

"*I know a voice*." Seren sat up. "*Through the Root. In the dark. Three years of nights, before the Heart. A voice that talked to me. And I talked back.*" She looked at the lamp. "*I fell in love with it. Isn't that stupid? I fell in love with a voice in the dark. Not knowing who it was. And then the boys told me, at the Heart. That's him. That's Father. That's your husband.*"

She was quiet.

"*And now the Root's gone*," she said. "*My boy gave it away. And the voice is gone with it. And I'm walking home to a man who was that voice. And I don't know if I'll know him.*"

Kell didn't say anything for a long time.

"*He fills the lamp*," she said at last. "*Every night. You said.*"

"*Every night*."

"*And says good evening*."

"*Every night*."

"*For a year and a half*," said Isaura Kell. "*To a woman who doesn't know who he is.*" She looked at Seren with her pale steady eyes. "*I knew him for forty years. Arion Vey. Warden-Architect. The most infuriating man in the Spire. Always right. Never once raised his voice. Never once told anyone anything.*" Something moved at the corner of her mouth. "*I hated him. Very much. For a long time.*"

"*And*?"

"*And I don't think he's ever said good evening to anything in his life*," said Kell. "*Not before you.*"

---

The moths came on the next walk.

Seren felt them first. Before Kell. Before the little one. Before anything.

A prickle. On the back of her neck. On her arms. Like the feeling in the air before a storm.

Like the feeling her mother used to get, at the end of summer, in the evenings, in twenty-two different cottages. When she'd stop in the middle of whatever she was doing, and go still, and lift her head, and sniff the air like a dog. And then go and close the shutters. All of them. And light the lamp inside. And get the broom.

"*Kell*," said Seren.

"*I feel it*."

They stopped walking.

Above them. Far up. In the dark between Shade's stars. Something was coming down.

---

She couldn't see them at first. Only the stars. Going out. One by one. Far up. As something passed in front of them.

And then the glitter.

Faint. Grey-gold. Coming down out of the dark from very high up. Not falling. *Drifting*. The way snow drifts when there's no wind. The way dust drifts in a sunbeam. Thousands of tiny points of glitter, coming down in long slow spirals.

From the holes.

She knew it at once. She'd felt them open. Every one. Twenty-seven of them. Twenty-seven wounds in the little one, twenty-seven threads snapped off, twenty-seven open shafts running from the world down into the deep. Like chimneys. Like wells. Like holes in a roof.

And down them, now, like smoke down a chimney when the wind turns, the moths.

---

The little one saw them.

Seren felt it see them. The whole vast soft slope of it, behind them, going rigid. Every inch of it. Like a hare when the hawk's shadow crosses the field.

*[...no...]*

*[...no no no no...]*

*[...THEM...]*

"*Lamb*—"

*[...THEM...it's THEM...they're coming...they're coming BACK...]*

And it moved.

Not toward them. Away. Down. Back. The great soft dark of it pulling in on itself. Folding. Drawing back down the long slope the way they'd come. Away from the glitter. Away from the moths.

Away from her.

"*No*!" Seren was running. Back. Down. After it. With the lamp held up. "*No, lamb, don't—don't go back down, you've come so far—*"

*[...they DRANK me...]*

*[...they knelt down and they DRANK me...]*

*[...she looked at me like a LAMP...]*

---

The first moths reached them.

Seren didn't stop running. She heard Kell shout something behind her. She didn't turn. She ran down the dark after the little one with her lamp held high and the moths came down round her like snow.

They went for the lamp.

Of course they did. It was the only light down here. The only real light, in all the soft deep dark at the bottom of the world. A Harrow lamp. A light left on.

They came down on it in a cloud. Grey-gold. Glittering. Wings beating. Drinking—

And couldn't.

Seren felt it. Felt them press against the flame, the little flame in its clay cup, and *pull*, and get—nothing. Not nothing. Something. But not what they wanted. They pressed and pressed and the flame stayed the same. Small. Gold. Steady.

And they fell back. Confused. Fluttering.

*Of course*, she thought. Running. *Of course. Mam's lamp. A Harrow lamp.*

*It's not kept. It's given. It's a light for someone else.*

*They can't drink what's already been given away*.

---

The Mothwing came after the moths.

She saw them in the glitter. Pale shapes. Gliding down out of the dark on ragged wings. Long hands reaching. Lit eyes, dull gold, like coals. Five. Ten. Twenty.

They didn't go for the lamp.

They'd seen it. They'd seen the moths fail. They were older than moths, and cleverer, and hungrier. They glided down past the lamp. Past Seren. Down the long dark slope.

Toward the little one.

---

Kell met them.

Seren would never know how she got there. One moment she was behind, shouting. The next she was in front. Between the Mothwing and the great soft retreating dark. Standing on nothing. Straight-backed. In her ruined white robes.

With a staff.

Seren had never seen her with a staff. She didn't know where it came from. A long plain stick of dark wood, like a shepherd's crook without the crook. Maybe Kell had carried it all along. Maybe she'd made it out of the dark. Maybe it was just the shape of what she'd been, for forty years, a Warden of the Order, standing in front of things with a staff in her hands.

The first Mothwing reached her.

And Isaura Kell, sixty-something, gaunt, grey, with no class and no level and no thread in the net at all, swung the staff.

---

She wasn't trying to kill them. Seren saw that. She *couldn't* kill them, probably. They came apart into dust when you struck them, the way they did, and then the dust drifted and began to gather again.

She was trying to *stop* them.

She stood in front of the little one and swung the staff and every Mothwing that came at her, she knocked aside. Not hard. Precisely. A Warden's training. Forty years of it. The way they taught cadets in the Spire to hold a door. Not with strength. With position. With knowing exactly where to stand.

They broke round her like water round a stone.

And behind her, the little one stopped.

---

Seren felt it stop. Felt it turn. The whole vast soft dark of it. Pausing in its flight. Looking back. At the gaunt grey woman in white, standing in the dark between it and the things that had drunk it, with a stick.

*[...the Warden...]*

*[...the Warden is standing in front of me...]*

"*Yes*," said Seren. Breathless. Running up beside Kell with her lamp. "*Yes, lamb. She is.*"

*[...why...]*

Kell didn't look round. She swung the staff. A Mothwing came apart into dust. She shifted her feet. Waited. Swung again.

"*Because you're mine*," said Isaura Kell. Through her teeth. "*Gods help me. I walked you all the way up here. A year and a half. I'm not letting them have you now.*"

---

And then the glitter stopped.

All at once. The moths. The Mothwing. Every one. Hanging in the dark. Not moving. Wings still. Lit eyes turned. Up.

Toward something coming down.

Seren looked up.

She saw a star go out. Far up. Directly overhead. And then another. And another. A line of them. Going out one by one. Coming down. Toward her.

Something large.

Something folded. Close. Wings tight against its back. Leaning into the dark the way a moth leans into a gap in a shutter. Patient. Slow. Coming down the longest, deepest shaft in the world.

And at the front of it, two lights.

Gold. Warm. Soft.

Like windows.

---

She came down onto the dark in front of them.

Lightly. On her toes. Like a dancer. Her long grey-gold gown settling round her. Her silver hair falling to her knees. Her wings unfolding, slowly, enormously, behind her, filling the dark, patterned with rings and whorls and eyes.

She didn't look at Kell.

She didn't look at Seren. Not at first.

She looked past them both. Up. At the great soft slope of dark behind them. Rising. Folded night. Miles of it. Young and soft and full and *frightened*.

And her face—

Seren had seen a lot of faces. She'd been a hedge-witch for thirty years. She'd sat by sickbeds. She'd delivered babies. She'd watched a man see his wife come back from the dead, once, in a cottage in the Ironbacks, when the fever broke.

She'd never seen a face like this.

It was *longing*. Pure. Total. The way a person looks at the sea after thirty years inland. The way a starving man looks at bread. The way a lover looks at someone they lost a long time ago and never thought they'd see again.

Maelis of the Crowned stood at the bottom of the world and looked at the Well she'd drunk dry nine hundred years before.

And her lit eyes filled with tears.

"*Oh*," she whispered. "*Oh, look at you. Look at you. You've grown back.*"

---

Behind Seren, the little one made a sound.

Not a word. Not a scream. A sound like nothing she'd ever heard. Low. Long. Shaking. The sound a thing makes when the worst thing it has ever known walks back into the room.

And it went *still*.

Not frozen. Not fleeing. *Still*. The way a rabbit goes still when it knows the fox has seen it. When there's nowhere left to run, and running would only make it worse.

Seren felt it. Felt the whole vast soft dark of it go still behind her. Felt it make itself small. As small as something the size of a mountain can make itself. Pressing down. Pressing in. Trying not to be seen.

She lifted the lamp.

She didn't decide to. Her hand did it on its own. The way her mother's hand had done it, in twenty-two cottages, at the end of every summer. Lifted the lamp. Up. High. Between the thing behind her and the thing in front of her.

A light. Given. Not kept.

The Moth Queen's eyes moved.

Down. From the little one. To the lamp. To the small clay lamp in Seren's square strong hand. And then, slowly, to Seren's face.

And stopped.

---

She looked at Seren for a long time.

At her face. Small and freckled and fierce. At her hair, brown, going grey, tied back with nothing, because the blue cloth had worn through years ago. At her hands. At her hand on the lamp.

And something moved in the Moth Queen's beautiful face. Something that wasn't longing. Something older. Something that had been waiting nine hundred years in a grey country with no sun.

Recognition.

"*Oh*," said Maelis. Very softly. "*Oh, there you are.*"

Seren didn't move.

"*I'd know those hands anywhere*," said the Moth Queen. "*Square. Strong. Too big for the rest of you.*" She tilted her head. "*Holding up a lamp. Of course. Of course you are. She always did that too.*"

She took a step forward.

Kell's staff came up. Between them. Straight. Steady.

Maelis didn't look at it. She didn't look at Kell. She looked at Seren. Over the staff. With her lit eyes warm and soft and gold and very, very hungry.

"*Sister's girl*," said Maelis of the Crowned.

She smiled.

"*You have something of mine*."
