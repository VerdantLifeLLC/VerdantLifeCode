# The Door Opens Down

Kestrel Varn walked into the Lantern Keep on a Tuesday, with her hair on fire.

Not badly. Just at the ends. She'd been walking for three weeks, she said, from the Sunreach, north across the Scald and up through the Vale and over the foothills, and somewhere around Thistledown she'd stopped bothering to keep it down. It burned gold. Quietly. All the time. Like a lamp left on in a window. It had singed her collar black.

She stood in the cave mouth in the late afternoon sun with a pack on her back and a new scar on her chin and a Sunreach blade at her hip, curved, in a red leather sheath. Brown from the southern sun. Thinner. Harder. With something in her amber eyes that hadn't been there a year ago.

She looked at Antho. Then past him. Down the long passage. At the lanterns. At the two hundred and six dark ones, still dark, with dust on their glass.

"*I got your raven*," said Kestrel.

"*Which one*?"

"*All four*." She shrugged off her pack. "*The first one said moths. The second one said a woman with lit eyes. The third one said she's my great-great-something aunt. The fourth one said we're going down.*" She looked at him. "*I stopped reading after that and started walking.*"

"*It's dangerous*," said Antho.

Kestrel looked at him with her amber eyes for a long moment.

Then she laughed. The old laugh. The one from the Temple of Ysmir, two years ago, on the White Shore, when she was a fire-witch with no thread who'd do anything to get her brother's name back.

"*Antho*," said Kestrel Varn. "*Love. It's always dangerous. That's why you write to me.*"

---

They held the council in the library.

All of them. Around the long table, with Malachi's box at one end and Seraphine's map on the wall, black pins everywhere, forty-one, fifty-three now, and a new cluster of red ones. Twenty-seven red pins. The broken ones. The shafts.

Lex. Wren. Seraphine. Kestrel. Odile. Pim, under the table, as always. Ivy Moss on her stool. The twins, who'd come back from Qel'Varran with them and hadn't left. Tilda, with Brannoc's hammer across her knees. Kit, the Coldwater boy, who'd walked out of the Drowned Archive behind them and kept walking, all the way to the Keep, and sat now at the very end of the table, very white, not speaking, with his hands clenched in his lap.

And at the head of the table, in the big chair, wrapped in a blanket, with his grey book open in front of him and his stick against the arm of the chair—

Their father.

---

He'd come back from Highcrest that morning.

On Vharuun's neck. In Brannoc's chair. He'd heard about Qel'Varran from a Keeper's raven. *Seren's voice. Up a shaft. She's coming down*. And he'd got up out of his chair by the window in the Spire, and called for the dragon, and come.

Lex had carried him up the stairs from the cave mouth.

All two hundred and eleven. Without stopping. With his father's arms round his neck and his father's head on his shoulder. He weighed nothing. Nothing at all. Like carrying a bundle of sticks. Like carrying a bird.

Antho had watched them come up. And counted the steps. And had to turn away.

He was seventy-five now. Or near it. The grey book said so. His hair was white and thin as thistledown. His hands shook all the time. He had to hold his cup with both of them. His eyes, behind Seraphine's spare spectacles, were still the same grey. Still clear. Still steady.

Still listening.

---

"*We have to go down*," said Lex.

He said it to the whole table. He'd said it at Qel'Varran, standing on the ledge at the bottom of the Drowned Archive. He'd said it on the flight back. He'd said it at dinner, and at breakfast, and to Wren in the dark in the guest room, over and over, until she'd put her hand over his mouth and said *I know, Hammer. I know. I heard you the first nine times.*

Nobody argued.

That was the strange thing. Antho had expected an argument. There was always an argument. That was how they thought, round this table. Somebody said a thing, and somebody else said *but*, and they went round and round until the thing was worn smooth and true.

Nobody said *but*.

"*She's going down to Mam*," said Lex. "*Mam said so. Up the shaft. She's coming down to us. To the little one. To me.*" He looked round the table. "*She's going down there to finish what she started nine hundred years ago. And Mam's standing between them with a lamp.*"

"*And Kell*," said Seraphine. Quietly.

"*And Kell*." Lex nodded. "*Two women. One with a lamp and one with no thread. Against her.*" He put his hands flat on the table. "*We have to go down.*"

"*How*?" said Kestrel.

---

That was the argument.

Not *whether*. *How*.

"*The shafts*," said Tilda. "*The broken ones. That's how she's going. Straight down.*"

"*They're wounds*," said Seraphine. "*Open ones. You'd be climbing down the inside of a cut. It would hurt it. Every step.*" She shook her head. "*And they go straight to the little one. Into it. Not to your mother. We don't know where she is.*"

"*The Ferryman*," said Ilse. "*He took you all the way to the Heart last time.*"

"*He won't*," said Antho.

Everyone looked at him.

"*He won't take anyone down*," said Antho. "*Not now. He said so. He said he wouldn't carry the Crowned. And he's angry. I could feel it. Angry about the shafts. About his river. About what she's doing.*" He looked at his hands. "*He's not going to row anyone into the deep while she's down there. Not for any price.*"

"*Then how*?" said Kestrel again.

Antho didn't answer.

He didn't need to. He felt his brother look at him. Felt his father look at him. Felt every eye at the table turn, slowly, to him.

And then, one by one, past him.

To the floor. To the stone. Down. Through two hundred feet of mountain. To the bottom of the long passage. To a bell-shaped cavern, and a niche, and three lamps.

And a door.

---

"*It's Kell's*," said Antho.

He hadn't meant to say anything. He'd been sitting very still, the whole council, with his hands folded round the Lantern. He'd been thinking about it since Qel'Varran. Since the moment Lex said *we have to go down*. He'd known it then.

"*The door*," he said. "*At the Heart. Lex gave it to her. You said it was yours now. And she took it. And sealed it from the inside.*" He looked up. "*She's the Warden of it. By gift. It's hers. Not mine.*"

"*You're the Threshold Warden*," said Seraphine.

"*I'm the one who stands between*." Antho looked down at the Lantern. "*That's what the net said. Not the one who opens. Not the one who closes. The one who stands in the doorway and decides.*" He was quiet. "*I don't know if I can open someone else's door.*"

"*Have you tried*?" said Kestrel.

Antho looked at her.

"*No*," he said.

"*Well*," said Kestrel Varn. "*There's your problem.*"

---

Wren didn't say anything during the council.

Antho noticed. He noticed everything about Wren now. He couldn't help it. He was going to be an uncle. He'd been going to be an uncle for three weeks and it had changed something in him he didn't have a name for. He watched her the way he'd watched Pim, the first winter. The way he watched the bats.

She sat beside Lex. Very straight. With her bow on the table in front of her and her hand on the bow. Not on her stomach. On the bow. As if she'd decided to put it there.

She didn't argue. She didn't say *I'm coming*. She didn't say *I'm not staying*.

She'd known since Qel'Varran. Antho could see it. She'd known standing at the edge of the fen on dry ground, with her bow and her wind, watching the dome. That this time she couldn't come.

He saw Lex look at her. Over and over. Through the whole council. And look away.

---

He found them on the slope that night.

He hadn't meant to. He'd gone out to check on Ashka, who'd been restless since the moths, and found her curled in the snow at the bottom of the slope with her great blue head on her forepaws. And beyond her, sitting on the rock where Pim always sat, two figures.

He stopped. In the shadow of the dragon's wing. And didn't go any closer.

"*I hate this*," said Wren.

Her voice carried. The night was very still.

"*I know*."

"*I hate it*. *I hate it more than anything. More than the Spire. More than the month on the roof.*" She was quiet for a moment. "*At least on the roof I chose it. I could have come after you. I didn't, because Arion needed guarding and I promised. But I could have.*"

"*I know*."

"*This isn't a choice*." Her voice cracked. "*This is just—a fact. Like weather. Like a Ceiling. Somebody else decided it and I have to live with it.*" She laughed. It didn't sound like a laugh. "*It's the size of a pea, Lex. A pea. And it's put a Ceiling on me.*"

Lex didn't say anything.

Antho saw him reach out. In the dark. And take her hand. The left one. With the black iron ring on it.

"*It's not a Ceiling*," said Lex. "*Wren. It's not.*"

"*Then what is it*?"

"*It's a door*," said his brother. "*You're standing in it. For someone else. So they can come through.*" He was quiet. "*That's what Antho does. That's what Kell does. That's what Mam did for thirteen years.*"

Wren didn't say anything.

"*You're holding the top*," said Lex. "*That's not staying home. That's the hardest job there is. Someone has to be here when we come up. Someone has to hold the door open from this side. Someone has to watch for moths.*" His voice dropped. "*Someone has to be the light we're coming home to.*"

There was a long silence.

"*That's a terrible speech*," said Wren. Her voice was wet. "*That's the worst speech I've ever heard. Did Ember write that?*"

"*No*."

"*It's terrible*." She was crying. Antho could hear it. "*I hate it. I hate you. Come here.*"

Antho went back up the slope very quietly. And didn't look back.

---

In the morning, at the cave mouth, she gave Lex a bottle.

Antho was there. Everyone was. Kestrel with her Sunreach blade and her hair burning. Seraphine with her notebook and a pack of food and three lamps. Odile and Pim and the students, crowded in the cave mouth to see them off. Ivy Moss. Kit.

Wren held it out.

It was small. Glass. Clear. The size of a thumb. With a cork in it, sealed with wax.

It was empty.

Lex looked at it. Then at her.

"*It's not empty*," said Wren. "*It's a breath.*"

He stared at her.

"*From the Wellworld of Gale*," she said. "*From the eye of the storm. The First Breath. I carried it in my lungs for three days, to the Hollowmaw, to forge the Lockward. Remember?*" She looked at the bottle. "*I kept a little. At the end. Just a little. I breathed it into this. I don't know why. I thought—*" She stopped. "*I thought someday someone might need air. Somewhere there isn't any.*"

She put it in his hand. And closed his fingers round it.

"*There's no wind in the deep*," said Wren Ashdown. "*Seren told Antho. Nothing moves. Nothing breathes.*" She looked up at him. "*If you need air down there, Hammer. If you need wind. If you need* me.*"*

She let go of his hand.

"*Open it*," she said. "*And breathe.*"

---

Their father came down to the door.

Lex carried him. Down the long passage. Past the lanterns, lit and dark. Past nine hundred and seven gold ones and two hundred and six with dust on their glass. Into the bell-shaped cavern at the bottom of the mountain.

He set him down in a chair Odile had brought. By the niche. By the three lamps.

The clay one. The iron one. The *ARDENT* one. Burning. Small and gold and steady.

Arion looked at them for a long time.

Then he reached into his coat. Slowly. With his shaking hand. And took out a letter.

Folded. Thick. Many pages. Sealed with plain grey wax and a thumbprint.

"*For your mother*," he said.

Lex looked at it.

"*If she wants it*," said Arion. "*Only if. Don't give it to her if she doesn't want it. Don't give it to her if she's frightened, or hurt, or—*" He stopped. "*Ask her first. Ask her if she wants a letter from a man she doesn't remember. And if she says no*—" His voice went very quiet. "*Burn it. Please. Don't bring it back. I don't want to know.*"

"*What's in it*?" said Antho.

His father looked at him.

"*Everything*," said Arion Vey. "*The steps. The rain. The lanterns. What she said about the story. What I said back.*" He swallowed. "*I've been writing it for a year and a half. Since the Heart. Every night after I filled the lamps.*" He looked at the clay one. "*In case I don't get to tell her myself.*"

---

Antho stood in front of the door.

Black glass. Tall. Smooth. Sealed. A door to nowhere. A door to the dark under the world. He'd stood in front of it a thousand times. He'd stood behind it, once, with his back to the hunger. He'd put his hand on it every night for three years and said *evening, Mam* to a lamp.

He'd never opened it.

He'd never tried.

He put his hand on it now.

Cold. Smooth. On the other side, the deep. Shade's dark. Starlit. Quiet. And very far down, so far he could barely feel it, something vast and soft and young, gone very still. Like a hare in a field. Like a child hiding under a bed.

*[...brother...]*

So faint. A whisper from the bottom of a well.

*[...brother she's here...the moth...she's HERE...]*

*I know*, thought Antho. *I'm coming.*

He pushed.

---

Nothing happened.

The glass was cold and smooth and sealed and didn't move. He pushed harder. With his hand. With the Lantern. With Shade, Commanding, reaching into the dark of the door and asking it to open.

Nothing.

He reached for the thing the net had given him. *Threshold Warden*. The one who stands between. He reached for it the way you reach for a word you know you know. And found it. A sense. A weight. A place in him that was a doorway, that he could stand in, that he could decide from.

He stood in it. And decided.

*Open*.

The door didn't move.

---

"*It's not yours*," said Seraphine. Quietly. Behind him. "*Antho. It's hers. You said.*"

"*I know*."

"*You can't decide for a door that isn't yours*."

"*I know*." He was breathing hard. His hand was flat on the glass. His forehead was nearly touching it. "*I know. I know.*"

"*Then don't*," said Kestrel.

He turned his head. She was standing beside Lex. With her arms crossed and her hair burning and her amber eyes on him.

"*Don't decide*," said Kestrel Varn. "*Ask.*"

---

He looked at her for a long moment.

*Ask*.

He thought about the Undertow, in the Wellworld of Tide, which hated stillness and had to be asked nicely. He thought about Lex, in the cellar at Harrow, asking a stone in the wall to move. He thought about the Hearth. *You're not making it do anything, lad. You're asking it nicely to be something else for a while.*

He thought about every door he'd ever stood in. Every one. Not one of them had been his. The cave in the Greyspine had been the mountain's. The Hollowmaw had been the world's. The doorway at the bottom of the Heart had been the hunger's, and Lex's, and he'd stood in it and decided because there was nobody else to.

He'd never once knocked.

He lifted his hand off the glass.

And knocked.

---

Three times. With his knuckles. On the black glass. Softly. The way you knock on a door at night in a house where someone might be sleeping.

The sound went into the door. And through it. And down.

Into the deep. Into the dark. Into Shade's starlit nothing. Down and down and down. Antho felt it go. Like a stone dropped down a well. Like a voice calling into a cave.

*Kell*, he thought. Into the knock. Into the door. *Isaura Kell. Warden of this door. It's Anthoren Vey. Seren's son.*

*May we come in*?

---

For a long time, nothing.

The door was cold. The cavern was silent. Behind him, he could hear his brother breathing. Seraphine. Kestrel. His father, in the chair by the niche, very still.

And then, very far off—

He felt it.

A hand. On the other side of the door. Not near. Very far down. Very faint. A long thin bony hand, laid flat on the black glass from the dark side, the way he'd laid his own on it a thousand times.

Weak. Shaking.

*Hurt*.

And a voice. Dry. Thin. Precise. A voice that had given orders for twenty years. Coming up through the door from the bottom of the world, so faint he had to hold his breath to hear it.

*[...Vey...]*

*[...about time...]*

*[...come in, then...]*

*[...hurry...]*

---

The door opened.

Not like a door. Not swinging. Not sliding. The black glass simply—*stopped being there*. From the middle outward. Like ice melting. Like breath clearing off a window. A circle of not-glass, opening in the center of the door, and widening, and widening, until there was an arch. Tall. Round-topped. The shape of the door. Full of dark.

Not black. Not the round flat black of a Delve.

*Dark*. Deep. Soft. Endless. Going down. With faint silver points scattered through it, very far off, like stars.

And cold air coming out of it. Cold and still and smelling of nothing at all.

Behind Antho, someone made a sound. Small. Like a breath let out after a long time held.

His father.

---

Arion was on his feet.

Antho didn't know how. He couldn't stand without his stick. He couldn't do stairs. Lex had carried him down two hundred and eleven of them that morning like a bundle of sticks.

He was standing. By the niche. With one hand on the stone and the other reaching out. Toward the open door. Toward the dark. His thin old face white. His grey eyes enormous behind Seraphine's spectacles.

"*Seren*," said Arion Vey.

The dark didn't answer.

He took a step. Toward it. And another. Shaking. Slowly. An old man walking toward a door.

Lex caught him.

Gently. Both arms round him. From behind. And held him. And didn't let him go.

"*Father*," said Lex. "*No.*"

"*She's there*—"

"*I know*." Lex's voice was rough. "*I know she is. I'll bring her. I promise. I'll bring her home.*" He held on. "*You can't come. You'd never make it back up. You know you wouldn't.*"

Arion stood in his son's arms. Shaking. With his hand out. Reaching for the dark.

And then, slowly, very slowly, the hand came down.

---

Lex put him back in the chair.

And knelt in front of him. And took his father's shaking hands in his own big scarred ones. And held them.

"*Fill the lamps*," said Lex. "*Tonight. Like always. And say good evening.*"

His father looked at him.

"*She'll hear it*," said Lex. "*Wherever she is. She'll hear you. That's how she's been finding her way all this time. That's the light she's walking toward.*" He swallowed. "*Don't stop. Not for one night. Whatever happens.*"

Arion Vey looked down at his son for a long, long time.

"*Every night*," he said. "*I promise.*"

---

They stepped through.

Antho first. Because it was a door, and he was the Threshold Warden, and someone had to stand in it. Then Seraphine, with her notebook in her coat and three lamps on her belt, unlit. Then Kestrel, with her hair burning.

Then Lex.

He stopped in the arch. With one foot on the black floor of the cavern and one in the dark. And turned round.

Wren was standing at the mouth of the cavern. Where the long passage came down. With her bow on her back and her hand on the stone wall and Pim beside her, holding her other hand, very tight.

She didn't say anything.

Neither did Lex.

He looked at her. For a long moment. Across the bell-shaped cavern. Past the three lamps and his father in the chair and Odile and the students and the twins and Tilda and Kit. Just at her.

And then he put his hand flat on his chest. Over his heart. Where the little glass bottle was. In his inside pocket. With a breath in it.

Wren put her hand flat on her stomach.

And held it there.

---

Lex turned.

And stepped into the dark.

And behind him, the arch began to close. From the edges inward. Like ice forming. Like breath fogging a window. The black glass coming back. Slowly. Until there was only a circle in the middle. Then a hole. Then a crack.

Antho, standing in the dark on the other side, put his hand on it.

*Not all the way*, he thought. To the door. To Kell, far below. *Leave it on the latch. We're coming back.*

The crack stayed.

A thin line of gold. Lantern-light. Lamp-light. Coming through from the world. From the cavern. From three small flames in a niche, and a thousand lanterns on the walls of a long passage, and an old man in a chair.

It was the only light in the dark.

They turned their backs on it. And looked down.

---

Down.

Into Shade's dark. Starlit. Soft. Endless. Going down forever. Antho had been here before. At the bottom of the world. On the way to the Heart. He knew this dark. He knew it the way he knew his own breath.

He'd never seen it look like this.

It *glittered*.

Far below. Very far. Like a field of frost seen from a hilltop. Like the surface of a lake at night under a full moon. A faint grey-gold shimmer. Spread out. Across the whole of the dark beneath them. As far as eyes could see.

Moving.

Slowly. Like smoke. Like a tide. Turning. Drifting. Settling. Rising.

"*Oh*," said Seraphine. Very quietly. "*Oh, no.*"

"*How many*?" said Kestrel.

Nobody answered.

Nobody could count that many.

---

And then, from somewhere in the glitter, so far below that it might have been the bottom of the world, a sound.

Not a moth. Not a voice.

A *crack*. Sharp. Bright. Like wood splitting. Like a staff breaking.

And after it, a cry. Thin. Dry. Human.

And then nothing.

Lex was already moving.

Antho caught his arm. "*Lex*—"

"*That was Kell*."

"*I know*."

"*That was Kell's staff*." His brother's face was white in the faint gold light from the crack in the door behind them. His fire was coming up. All the way up. Gold. Bright. Lighting the dark round them like a torch. "*Antho, that was*—"

And below them, far below, in the great slow glitter of the deep, every single moth turned.

Toward the light.

Toward *him*.

And began, slowly, enormously, like a tide coming in, to rise.
