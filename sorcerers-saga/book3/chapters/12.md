# Hush

The Delve opened in the cold-room.

Of course it did. Antho would think about that afterward, often, with a kind of tired fondness. Of all the places in Caldor. Of all the village greens and inn yards and tavern cellars and dry wells. It opened at the bottom of the Lantern Keep, in the cold-room, among the bats.

He was there when it happened. That was the other thing.

It was the night after his father went back to Highcrest. He couldn't sleep. He'd lain in bed beside Seraphine for three hours looking at the ceiling, and then got up, and put on his coat, and taken a book down to the cold-room the way he did on bad nights. To read to the bats.

The cold-room was a cave off the lower passage. Long and low and dark, with water dripping somewhere at the back and a smell of stone and bat and Teo's old fish-buckets, which nobody had ever quite managed to get rid of. There were about four hundred bats. Small brown ones, mostly. Hanging from the roof in great soft clusters, like bunches of dried fruit.

Antho sat on his usual crate, by the lantern he kept there, and opened the book.

*The History of the Reign of King Aldric the Second*, by a Warden-Historian of the Spire, in eleven volumes. He was on volume four. It was the dullest book ever written. The bats loved it.

"*In the third year of his reign*," read Antho Vey, "*the King turned his attention to the vexed matter of the river tolls at Saltreach*—"

And the floor of the cold-room opened.

---

It made no sound.

That was what he'd tell Lex. No crack. No rumble. No breaking stone. The floor of the cold-room was there, rough grey rock, wet, with a puddle where the drip landed. And then, between one word and the next, ten feet from his crate, it wasn't.

A circle. Round. Flat. Black. Perfect.

Breathing.

The bats went mad.

Four hundred of them. All at once. Dropping off the roof in a great brown roaring cloud, wheeling, shrieking, a storm of wings that put out his lantern and knocked the book out of his hands and filled the cold-room with a sound like tearing silk. They poured past him. Out. Up the passage. Toward the cave mouth. Gone.

And then silence.

And darkness. Except for the round black on the floor, which wasn't light, which was the opposite of light, but somehow you could see it anyway. The way you can see a hole in the dark.

And humming.

*Little light, little light*.

Antho sat on his crate in the dark with his empty hands in his lap.

"*Well*," he said. To no one. "*That's the end of volume four.*"

---

*[...brother...]*

He went still.

It was loud. Louder than it had ever been. Not through the door. Not through a pebble. *Here*. In the room. Coming up out of the round dark on the floor of the cold-room like breath out of a mouth.

*[...brother...brother you're here...]*

*Yes*, thought Antho. *I'm here.*

*[...it hurts...the holes...they keep breaking...]*

He knew. He'd felt every one. Since Thistledown. Twelve more. Twelve more times something had snapped, somewhere in Caldor, and the thing under the world had flinched, and screamed. He'd learned to feel them coming. A kind of tightening, in the back of his skull. And then the snap. And then the scream. And he'd learned to hold on through it. To grip the edge of the table, or the bed, or Seraphine's hand. To not let it come out of his mouth.

He hadn't always managed.

*[...why...why do they break them...]*

"*Because they don't know*," said Antho. Out loud. In the dark. "*They don't know you're there. They think you're stones.*"

*[...I'm not stones...]*

"*I know*."

*[...I just wanted to see...I just wanted to come UP...]*

"*I know*," said Antho Vey.

He got up off the crate. In the dark. And walked across the cold-room floor. Slowly. Until he was standing at the very edge of the round black. With his boots an inch from it.

And knelt down.

---

He didn't go in.

He'd thought about it. Kneeling there. With the warm breath of it on his face and the humming in his bones. He'd thought, *I could go down. Like Lex did, at Ashbourne. Find the core. Claim it. Close it softly.*

He didn't.

He'd made a promise. To himself. To Seraphine. To his brother on a bench in the Ashbourne Forge. *I'm trying very hard to be the kind of man who doesn't carry things alone*.

So he knelt at the edge of the hole in his cold-room floor, in the dark, and put his hand flat on the stone beside it. And waited.

And after a while, he heard footsteps.

---

Seraphine came first. In her nightgown. With her spectacles on and a lamp in her hand and her hair in a long dark plait down her back. She came down the passage at a run, the way she always ran, awkwardly, like someone who'd learned to run late in life from a book.

She stopped in the doorway. And looked at the hole. And at him.

"*The bats came through the library*," she said. "*All of them. They knocked over the ink.*"

"*Sorry*."

"*It's on the map*." She came in. Slowly. Holding the lamp up. "*Right on Malachi's map. There's a bat-shaped splash over the whole of the Saltreach.*" She looked at the round black on the floor. "*Oh.*"

"*Yes*."

"*In the cold-room*."

"*Yes*."

She was quiet for a moment.

"*Teo will be so annoyed he missed this*," said Seraphine.

---

Then Odile. Then Ivy Moss, on her crutch, very fast, with her pale hair everywhere and her eyes enormous. Then the Millbridge twins and Cass and the rest, crowding in the doorway in their nightclothes.

And Pim.

Pim didn't crowd. He came past the others. Small. Barefoot. In a nightshirt too big for him. And walked straight across the cold-room to where Antho was kneeling. And knelt down beside him. At the edge of the hole.

And looked into it.

"*It's frightened*," said Ivy Moss. From the doorway. In a small high voice. "*It's very frightened. It doesn't want to be broken.*"

"*Nobody's going to break it*," said Antho.

"*It doesn't know that*."

"*No*," said Antho. "*It doesn't.*" He looked down at the black. "*Nobody's ever told it.*"

---

They worked through the night.

It was Seraphine's idea, mostly. Or rather, it was everyone's idea, in pieces, and Seraphine put the pieces together. That was what she did. That was what she'd always done. She sat on Antho's crate with her lamp and her notebook on her knees and listened to everyone talking at once and wrote it all down. And then read it back. And then said, *wait*, and crossed things out, and drew arrows, and said *wait* again.

"*The claim closes it*," she said. "*We know that. Lex claimed the Ashbourne core and the Delve folded up.*"

"*Lex had to go all the way down to do it*," said Odile. "*Three floors. Through the Unfinished. And the guardian.*"

"*And so would we*," said Seraphine. "*Every time. Forty-one Delves. More every week. And the Uncapped get to most of them first.*" She tapped her pencil on the notebook. "*We can't go down every hole in Caldor. There aren't enough of us. There isn't enough time.*"

"*So what*?" said one of the twins.

Seraphine looked at the hole.

"*So we don't go down*," she said slowly. "*We make it go down. From up here. Before anyone goes in.*"

---

"*How*?" said Antho.

"*I don't know yet*." She was writing. Fast. "*But think. What's a Delve? What is it, really?*"

"*A hole*," said Cass.

"*A hole*." Seraphine nodded. "*A way. A thread of the little one, reaching up toward the light. Like a—like a root growing toward water. Or a plant growing toward the sun.*" She looked up. "*It's reaching. That's all a Delve is. It's the little one putting its hand up out of the ground.*"

Nobody said anything.

"*And when the core's claimed*," said Seraphine, "*the hand draws back. Softly. Like a hand drawn back from a flame.*" She was quoting the Ferryman. Antho had told her. Every word. "*And when the core's broken, the hand gets cut off. And the wound stays open.*"

She looked down at her notebook. At the arrows.

"*So what if we just*—" She stopped. Bit her pencil. "*What if we just made it feel safe? Enough to draw its hand back on its own? Before anyone cuts it?*"

---

They tried everything.

Antho opened the Lantern. Full. All twelve Hush-runes blazing gold. The quiet that kept voices out. He held it over the round black and let the hush pour down into it.

It went still. For a moment. The humming stopped.

And then started again. Louder. Frightened. *[...brother...brother it's DARK...why did you make it dark...]*

He closed the Lantern.

Odile and the twins tried Shade. Wrapping the hole in darkness, gently, the way you'd wrap a frightened bird in a cloth.

It didn't like that either. *[...dark...it's always dark...I don't want dark I want to SEE...]*

Ivy Moss tried singing to it. *The Lantern Song*. In her thin small voice. Sitting at the edge of the hole on the cold-room floor with her crutch beside her.

It liked that. It hummed along. Flat. On the high note.

But it didn't draw back. It reached further. Toward her voice. The round black on the floor grew. A foot. Two. Until its edge was touching Ivy's knee, and she scrambled back with a squeak, and it stopped.

"*It wants to come up more*," said Ivy Moss. Breathlessly. "*When I sing. It wants to come up and see who's singing.*"

---

It was nearly dawn when Pim did it.

They'd stopped. All of them. Tired. Sitting round the edge of the cold-room in their nightclothes with blankets that Odile had fetched, and cold tea that nobody was drinking, and the round black humming in the middle of the floor.

Antho was sitting with his back against the wall and Seraphine's head on his shoulder. She'd fallen asleep in the middle of a sentence. Her notebook was open on her knees. The last thing she'd written, in a trailing line that went off the edge of the page, was *what does a frightened thing want, if not dark and not light and not*—

Pim got up.

He'd been sitting at the edge of the hole all night. Not saying anything. Watching it. The way he watched everything. The way he'd watched the moths. The way he'd watched the Ferryman.

He got up. And walked round the edge of the hole. Slowly. All the way round. Once. Looking down at it.

And then he knelt down. On the far side. Facing Antho.

And held out his hands.

Over the black. Not touching it. Cupped. The way he'd cupped them round the Fenmoor lantern, when the wick wouldn't catch. The way he'd cupped them round Odile's dusty hand in the snow.

Round it.

Making a space.

---

Antho felt it before he saw it.

The silence. Pim's silence. The deep kind. The room in him where nothing was. He'd felt it at the moths, and on the night of the Blade, and at the Highcrest singing. He felt it now. Coming out of the boy's small thin hands. Not into the hole. *Around* it. Like the walls of a cup.

And the hole—

*Listened*.

The humming stopped. Not the way it had stopped under the Lantern. Not frightened. *Curious*. The way a child stops crying when someone does something unexpected.

*[...oh...]*

Antho sat up very straight. Seraphine woke. Slid off his shoulder. Blinked.

*[...oh...this is...]*

*[...this is like ME...]*

---

Pim didn't move.

He knelt on the cold-room floor with his hands cupped round the round black, an inch above it, and was silent. And the silence went round the hole the way a hand goes round a candle-flame in a draft. Not touching. Not pressing. Not putting it out.

Just there. Round it. A room around a room.

*[...it's quiet...]* said the voice. Wondering. *[...it's quiet but it's not dark...it's not cold...it's...]*

It didn't have the word.

*[...it's like being held without being held...]*

And the round black on the floor of the cold-room, very slowly, very softly, began to get smaller.

---

Nobody breathed.

Ten feet across. Nine. Eight. The edge drawing in. Smoothly. Not like a wound closing. Like a flower closing at dusk. Like an eye shutting, slowly, in a warm room, when you're very tired and someone has put a blanket over you.

Six feet. Four. Pim's hands drew in with it. Staying round it. Cupped. An inch above.

Two feet. A foot.

The size of a dinner plate. A saucer. A coin.

And then nothing. Rough grey stone. Wet. The puddle where the drip landed.

And on the stone, where the hole had been, lying in the puddle, a pebble.

Black. Smooth. The size of a hen's egg. Humming. Very softly. Flat on the high note.

Not claimed. Nobody had gone down. Nobody had touched it.

It had just—*come up*. On its own. Like a child coming out from under a bed when it's sure the shouting has stopped.

---

Pim picked it up.

He held it in his cupped hands for a moment. Close to his face. Looking at it. With his grey-violet eyes.

Then he turned round on his knees. And held it out to Antho.

Antho took it.

Warm. Smooth. Humming.

*[...brother...]* said the voice. In the old place in the back of his skull. Very small. Very sleepy. *[...brother that was nice...can he do that again...]*

Antho looked at Pim.

Pim looked back. And for the first time Antho could remember, in three years at the Keep, the boy's thin solemn face did something it never did.

It smiled. Small. Shy. Proud.

"*Room*," said Pim. "*It wanted room. Not dark. Not light.*" He looked down at his own empty hands. "*Just room.*"

Pim stared at his panel. At the last line.

Then he turned very red. And got up. And went out of the cold-room at a run. And they heard him all the way up the passage, his bare feet slapping on the stone, and then a door bang somewhere, and then nothing.

"*He's gone to tell Ashka*," said Odile. Smiling. "*He always tells Ashka first.*"

---

*[...brother...]*

The pebble was warm in Antho's hand. Humming. Sleepy.

*[...brother...can I show you something...]*

He went still.

Seraphine felt it. She was beside him again, on the floor, with her notebook. She looked at his face. And put her hand on his arm.

*Show me what*? thought Antho.

*[...before...]* said the voice. *[...a long time before...the first time...when they came...]*

*[...I want you to know...I want SOMEONE to know...]*

*[...nobody ever asked...]*

Antho looked down at the pebble in his hand.

"*Antho*?" said Seraphine.

"*It wants to show me something*," he said. Very quietly. "*Something old.*"

Her hand tightened on his arm.

"*I'm here*," said Seraphine Ilvane. "*I'm not going anywhere. Go on.*"

He closed his eyes.

---

A hall.

Glass. All of it. Walls and roof and floor. Tall. Vaulted. Like the inside of a great frozen waterfall, or a cathedral made of ice. Light came in through the walls from everywhere at once, gold and soft, the light of a late summer evening. It made the whole hall glow.

And in the middle of the floor, a pool.

Small. Round. No bigger than the round black on the cold-room floor. Ten feet across. Its edge was a ring of plain grey stone, worn smooth, much older than the glass hall built over it. And inside the ring, water.

No. Not water.

*Dark*. Still. Deep. So deep that looking into it was like looking up at the night sky. Not empty. *Full*. Full the way a held breath is full. Full the way the moment before a word is full, when everything that might be said hasn't been said yet.

Antho had never seen anything so beautiful in his life.

*That's me*, said the voice. Very small. Very far off. *That was me. Before.*

---

They came in through the great doors at the end of the hall.

Nine of them.

Tall. Beautiful. In robes of silk and cloth-of-gold, with crowns on their heads. A crown of gold leaves on a tall broad man with a golden beard, who walked first. A crown of glass on a woman with a face like a statue. A crown of black iron on a dark heavy man who didn't smile. A crown of roses on a woman Antho couldn't quite see; she kept turning her face away.

And a crown of silver wire, fine as frost, shaped like folded wings, on a tall pale woman with silver hair to her knees and eyes the color of candlelight.

Not lit. Not yet. Just a color. Warm brown-gold. Like honey. Like the eyes of anyone.

*Maelis*.

Antho knew her at once. Even without the lit eyes. Even without the wings. Something in the way she walked. In the way she held her head. Slightly tilted. Toward the light coming in through the glass walls. Toward the glow.

Four others. He didn't know them. Crowns of bronze and bone and copper and something that might have been coral. They walked behind.

And they knelt. All nine. Round the edge of the pool. On the old grey stone.

And cupped their hands.

And drank.

---

He felt it.

Through the voice. Through the pebble. Through the old place in his skull where the hunger used to live. He felt the pool feel it.

The first sip.

It didn't hurt. That was the terrible thing. It didn't hurt at all. It felt like being *tasted*. Like a hand on your face. Like someone leaning close to look at you. Like being seen. The pool had never been seen before. It was a room. A door. A place things came *through*. Nobody had ever stopped at it. Nobody had ever knelt down and looked.

They knelt. They looked. They cupped their hands and lifted it up to their mouths.

And the pool was *glad*.

*I didn't know*, said the voice. Very small. *I didn't know what they were doing. I thought*—

*I thought they liked me*.

---

They came back the next day. And the next. And the next.

Every evening. In the gold light. The nine of them. Kneeling round the pool. Drinking. And then their households. Lines of them. Courtiers and servants and soldiers. Kneeling at the edges while the Crowned held out their own cupped hands, full of dark, and let them sip.

And the pool went down.

Antho felt it go. Days. Weeks. Seasons. Like water going down in a well in a dry summer. Like breath going out of a body. The dark in the stone ring getting lower. And lower. Until you could see the stone sides of it, going down. Grey. Dry.

And the Crowned *changed*.

He saw it. Through the pool's eyes. Watching them come back, every evening, in the gold light. Their faces didn't age. Their hands didn't shake. Their eyes—

Their eyes began to *glow*.

Maelis first. Of course Maelis first. She drank more than any of them except the Gilded King. She drank slowly. Savoring. With her eyes closed. And when she opened them, one evening in the late autumn, they were lit. Soft. Gold. Warm. Like two windows at the end of a long lane.

And she looked down at the pool. With her lit eyes.

And smiled.

And the pool, which by then was very low, and very cold, and very frightened, and didn't understand why, looked up at her.

*She looked at me like I was a lamp*, said the voice. *Like I was a lamp she wanted to put her hands round*.

---

And then the tenth.

Antho almost missed her. She was standing in the doorway. At the end of the hall. Behind everyone. In the shadow of the great glass doors. Not kneeling. Not drinking.

Watching.

She was young. Younger than the others. Seventeen. Eighteen. Small. Thin. With brown hair tied back and a plain grey dress, not silk, not cloth-of-gold. No crown.

No. There was a crown. In her hands. She was holding it. Not wearing it. Holding it in front of her, the way you'd hold a bowl. A small crown. Plain silver. With seven points.

And her hands—

Square. Strong. Never still. Too big for the rest of her.

Antho's breath stopped.

---

She came forward.

On the last evening. When the pool was nearly gone. When the dark in the stone ring was no deeper than a puddle after rain, and the stone sides were dry and cracked, and the whole glass hall had gone cold, and the Crowned were kneeling round the edge of it, scraping, with their lit eyes and their cupped hands.

She came forward through them. Small. In her grey dress. With the crown in her hands.

The Gilded King looked up. And said something. Antho couldn't hear it. Something sharp. A command.

The girl didn't stop.

She came to the edge of the pool. And looked down into it. At the little puddle of dark at the bottom of the dry stone ring.

And she knelt down.

Not to drink.

She set the crown down beside her on the stone. Carefully. As if it didn't matter. As if it were a cup she'd finished with.

And she cupped her hands.

And she *poured*.

---

There was nothing in her hands.

Antho could see that. They were empty. Cupped. Square and strong and empty.

But she poured. She tipped them. Over the dry stone ring. Over the little puddle of dark at the bottom. Slowly. Carefully. The way you'd pour water into a dry well, a cup at a time, knowing it won't be enough, doing it anyway.

And something went out of her. Into the pool.

Not much. Not water. Something smaller. Something warmer. Antho didn't know what it was. He'd never know. But the pool knew. The pool felt it go in, a cupful of something, a drop in a dry well, and for one moment, in the cold glass hall, at the very end, it was *less empty*.

A cupful less.

*She gave me back*, said the voice. Very small. Very far off. Wondering. *Everyone took. She gave me back. Just a little. Just what she had.*

*Nobody else ever did*.

---

Maelis turned her head.

She was kneeling beside the girl. Her sister. Antho could see it now. The same long jaw. The same high cheekbones. Not the same eyes. The girl's eyes were brown. Ordinary. Unlit.

Maelis looked at her sister with her lit gold eyes.

And her face changed.

Not anger. Not surprise.

*Hunger*.

She looked at the girl's empty cupped hands. At the place where something had gone out of them into the pool. And her lit eyes widened. And she leaned closer. Toward the girl. Toward her hands. The way a moth leans toward a lamp.

And the girl looked back at her.

And said something.

Antho heard it. Clearly. Across nine hundred years. In a young voice, steady, low, not frightened at all.

"*No*," said Hesper Harrow. "*Not me. Not ever.*"

And she got up. And left the crown lying on the stone. And walked out of the hall of glass. And didn't look back.

---

Antho opened his eyes.

He was on the cold-room floor. On his back. He didn't remember lying down. Seraphine was bending over him, with her spectacles crooked and her face white. Odile was holding his hand. Ivy Moss was sitting at his feet with her crutch across her knees and tears running down her face.

The pebble was on his chest. Over his heart. Humming.

"*Antho*," said Seraphine. "*Antho. You stopped breathing. For a moment. You stopped*—"

"*I saw her*," said Antho.

His voice was a croak.

"*Who*?"

"*The tenth*." He looked up at his wife. "*At the pool. The one who didn't drink. She had a crown in her hands. She put it down. She gave something back. She said no.*" He swallowed. "*She had our hands, Seraphine. Lex's. Mam's. Square. Too big for the rest of her.*"

Seraphine stared at him.

"*Harrow*," she said. Very softly.

"*Hesper*," said Antho. "*Her name was Hesper.*"

---

They didn't sleep.

They sat in the kitchen as the sun came up, with porridge nobody ate and Odile's terrible tea, and Antho told them. All of it. The glass hall. The pool. The nine. The gold light. The lit eyes. The tenth girl in the doorway, with the crown.

Seraphine wrote it all down.

Every word. In her small neat hand. Filling page after page. Not looking up. Her pencil moving and moving.

When he got to the end, she put the pencil down.

And sat very still. With her hands flat on the notebook.

"*Seraphine*?"

She didn't answer.

She was staring at the pebble. On the kitchen table. Between the porridge and the teapot. Black. Smooth. The size of a hen's egg.

"*Antho*," she said.

Her voice was very strange. Very quiet. Very far away.

"*Antho*. *Say something.*"

"*What*?"

"*Anything*. *Say anything. Say a word.*"

He looked at her. Confused. "*Pebble*," he said.

Seraphine shut her eyes.

"*There*," she said. "*There. There.*" She put her hand flat on the table. Next to the pebble. Not touching it. "*The space. Before you said it. And after. The—*" She shook her head. "*It's like reading. I told you. It's like the space between letters.*"

She opened her eyes.

And they were wet.

"*It's not the song*," said Seraphine Ilvane. "*I've been trying to hear the song. That's why I couldn't. I've been trying to hear the—the* thing.*"* She looked at the pebble. "*It's not a thing. It's the room the thing goes in. It's the quiet before the word.*"

She was shaking.

"*Antho*," said his wife. "*I can hear it.*"

---

He'd been with her at the eternal fire in the Pale Wastes. For nineteen days. Watching her sit with Malachi's Codex on her knees, copying, every word, every lesson, while the rest of them learned to Listen. While Lex heard the fire and Wren heard the wind and Antho heard the dark. While the old man laughed and the goats wandered and every single one of them, one by one, heard *something*.

She'd heard nothing. Nineteen days. Not a whisper. Not a flicker.

*Some people don't*, Malachi had said. Gently. *It's not a failing. Someone has to write it down.*

She'd written it down. All of it. For five years. Every word anyone had ever said about the Wells. Every lesson. Every story. She'd copied the Codex nine times. She'd taught it. She'd translated it. She'd explained it to the Queen.

She'd never heard a single one of them.

He watched her face now. Across the kitchen table. In the dawn light. With the pebble between them.

And watched a panel come up over her head. Silver and gold. Faint. And something else. Something that wasn't silver or gold or violet or any color he'd ever seen on a panel. A kind of *clear*. Like the color of a window. Like the color of the air in an empty room.
