# The Uncapped

By the end of spring there were forty-one of them.

Seraphine kept the count. She had a map of Caldor pinned to the wall of the library at the Lantern Keep, a big one, hand-drawn, from the Saltreach to the Blightmarch and the Greyspine to the Scald. Every time a raven came in, she put a pin in it. Black pins. Forty-one.

*DELVE — UNCLASSIFIED*.

On village greens. In farmyards. At the bottom of a dry well in Millbridge. In the cellar of a tavern in the Saltreach, which the landlord had boarded over and was now charging a copper a look. In the middle of the Old Founders' Road, between the Lithic Oracle and the Thornwood, where a merchant's cart had driven straight into one at dusk and come out the next morning, horse and all, a mile away, with the driver white-haired and unable to say where he'd been.

Round. Flat. Black. Perfect. Breathing warm air that smelled of the Hollowmaw.

And humming.

"*They're not random*," said Seraphine. Standing in front of the map with her arms crossed and a pin in her teeth. "*Look.*"

Lex looked.

At first it was just pins. Black pins, scattered across Caldor like seeds thrown on a field. Then she took a piece of string and pinned one end of it to the Greyspine. To the Hollowmaw. And swung it in a slow circle round the map.

And the pins were on the circle. Most of them. Or near it. Or on another circle, further out. Or another, further still. Rings. Spreading out from the Hollowmaw across the whole of Caldor like the rings on a pond when a stone drops into it.

"*It's coming up under the Keep*," said Seraphine. "*Under the door. And the closer it gets, the more it*—" She stopped. Looked for the word.

"*Sheds*," said Lex.

She looked at him.

"*Like a snake*," said Lex. "*Or a dog in spring. When it's getting bigger. Bits come off.*" He looked at the pins. "*It's growing. And it's coming up. And bits of it are coming up ahead of it. Like—*"

"*Like the first drops*," said Seraphine softly. "*Before rain.*"

---

The net had started paying for them.

That was the trouble. Lex understood that quite quickly. That was the whole trouble, in one.

He'd claimed the Ashbourne core. He'd held a warm black pebble in his palm and felt it hum and the net had given him thirty-eight thousand experience and a level and said *it's all right, it's still here, it's just somewhere else now*.

And it had offered him five times that. If he broke it.

He hadn't. He hadn't even thought about it, really. It would have been like breaking a bird's egg.

But other people had.

---

The first was a company out of the Saltreach. Six of them. Sailors, mostly. Kindled [Deckhand] and [Netmender] and [Rigger], all capped at twenty-four, all Unbound at the great Stone on the Saltreach quay the summer after the Heart. They'd heard about the Delve in the tavern cellar. They'd paid the landlord a silver each. They'd gone down.

They'd come up three days later with a black pebble. And put it on the bar. And one of them, a big woman named Hesk with a rigger's arms and a scar through her eyebrow, had looked at the panel that came up over it, and laughed, and brought a belaying pin down on it as hard as she could.

It shattered.

Lex had heard about it from three different people, and they'd all told it the same way. It shattered like glass, they said. Like a black glass bottle. Into a thousand pieces. And the pieces went up in smoke. Black smoke. Very fine. That hung in the air of the tavern for a moment and then went down. Through the floor. Into the ground. Like water draining away.

And the six of them went up three levels each.

*Three*. In a breath. Standing at a bar in the Saltreach.

The story was all over Caldor in a week.

---

They called themselves the Uncapped.

Not a guild. Not a company. Nothing so organized. Just a word that had started in the taverns and caught, the way words do. *Uncapped*. Everyone who'd been Unbound and wanted to see how high they could go. Everyone who'd lain awake for years at twenty-four, the way Brannoc had, thinking *just one more*.

There were thousands of them.

Lex saw them on the roads. Bands of five and six and ten, with swords and bows and packs and the bright hard look Dunny Tobbin had had, coming down off the Greyspine. Heading for the nearest black pin on Seraphine's map. Fighting each other for them, sometimes. Racing. Selling the locations in tavern back rooms. *A Delve in Fenmoor, nobody's been in it, three floors, I'll tell you where for a crown.*

And shattering the cores.

Every one they could reach.

---

*[It's not their fault*,] said Ember.

Lex was in the forge yard at Ashbourne. At dawn. Standing beside the warm spring that had come up out of the cinders. It was bigger now. The size of a cart-wheel. Steaming. With a little channel running away from it down the street that Brannoc had lined with stones.

*I know it's not their fault*.

*[They don't know what it is. Nobody's told them. The net certainly isn't telling them. The net's offering them five times the reward.]*

*I know*.

*[Because the net doesn't know either*,] said Ember. Quietly. *[Since you reforged it. It's nobody's. It just—notices. And pays for what it notices. It noticed a Delve with a core in it. It noticed someone could break the core. It priced it the way it prices every Delve core. It doesn't know the core's alive.]*

*So I tell it*.

*[You can't. You gave away the Root. Nobody can tell it anything now.]* A pause. *[That was the point. That was the whole point of the reforging. Nobody at the center.]*

Lex looked at the spring. Steaming in the cold. New. Clean. Bubbling up out of nothing, out of the cinders of the forge yard where he'd learned his trade, because something under the ground was rising and making room.

*Then I tell them*, he thought. *The Uncapped. Every one. I go and stand at the Delves and tell them.*

*[There are thousands of them*.]

*I've got time*.

*[Lex*.]

*What*?

*[You've got a wife who's carrying a light*,] said Ember. *[And a mother walking up out of the deep with something the size of a mountain. And a woman with lit eyes sitting in a house on the Crown Terrace telling the whole council of Caldor that the something is the Unmaker reborn and offering to put it back in a cage.]* A pause. *[I don't think you've got time.]*

---

Wren came with him to Thistledown.

He'd known she would. He'd lain awake half the night in the loft at Harrow working out how to ask her not to, and the other half working out how to say it so she wouldn't throw a boot at him. He'd had eleven versions by dawn.

He didn't use any of them.

He came down the ladder and she was at the table, in her leathers, with her bow strung and her quiver full and two bowls of porridge in front of her, one of which she was eating and one of which she pushed across the table at him without looking up.

"*I'm coming*," she said.

"*I know*."

"*I'm not staying home*."

"*I know*."

"*I'm staying at the top*," said Wren. "*That's different.*" She looked up. "*I'm not going down. Not into a Delve. Not with—*" She glanced down. At herself. Quickly. As if it embarrassed her. "*Not now. Old Mag says no. Seraphine says no. My own stomach says absolutely not.*" She stabbed her porridge. "*But I'm coming to Thistledown. And I'm standing at the top of the hole with my bow and my wind, and if anything comes up out of it that isn't you, I'm going to put an arrow in it.*"

Lex looked at her.

"*Say it*," said Wren. "*Go on. Say it's dangerous.*"

"*It's dangerous*."

"*Everything's dangerous*." She took a mouthful of porridge. "*Being married to you is dangerous. You set the bed on fire in your sleep last week.*"

"*That was once*."

"*It was a very nice bed*," said Wren. And then, quieter, not looking at him: "*I'll stay at the top, Lex. I promise. I'm not stupid. I'm not going to do anything that—*" She stopped. "*But I'm not staying home. I'm not going to sit at Harrow with my hands in my lap waiting for you to come back. I did that. On the roof of the Spire. For a month.*"

She looked up at him. And her green eyes were very bright.

"*Never again*," she said. "*Remember?*"

He remembered.

He sat down and ate his porridge.

---

Thistledown was a trading post on the lower slopes of the Greyspine. Twenty houses and an inn and a market cross, at the place where the mountain road came down out of the high passes and met the road from the Vale. Wren had grown up there. In a grey-plank house with a horseshoe over the door, the wrong way up, so the luck wouldn't run out. Before her mother got sick and they moved to Highcrest for the physic.

Lex had met her there. Five years ago. At the inn. She'd been sitting on the edge of a water-trough in the yard, whittling, and he'd asked her for a guide up the mountain, and she'd looked him up and down and said *you walk like a bear in a china shop* and charged him two silver.

The Delve had opened in the inn yard.

Right where the water-trough had been.

---

It was bigger than the Ashbourne one.

Twice as big. Twenty feet across. Round and flat and black and perfect. The water-trough was gone. The cobbles round it were cracked, pushed up, as if something underneath had *bulged*. Warm air breathed out of it in a long slow sigh that Lex could feel on his face from across the yard.

And it was humming. Louder than the one in Ashbourne. Low and deep. He could feel it in the soles of his feet.

*Little light, little light*.

There were people round it.

Of course there were. A ring of them. Thirty. Forty. Standing at the edge of the black in the inn yard, with weapons, in groups. Uncapped. He could tell them by the look. The bright hard hungry look. And by the way they stood. Apart. In their bands. Eyeing each other.

And at the very edge of the dark, standing with one boot actually *on* the lip of it, where the cracked cobbles met the black, a man.

---

He was big.

Not as big as Brannoc. Bigger than Lex. Broad and heavy-shouldered and deep in the chest, in a coat of oiled leather patched with steel plates, scratched and dented and scorched. He had a pick over his shoulder. Not a war-pick. A miner's pick. Iron-headed, ash-hafted, the haft worn black and shiny with years of hands. His hair was cropped close. Grey-brown. His face was square and heavy and weathered. Lined round the eyes from squinting in the dark.

And he coughed.

That was the first thing Lex noticed about him. Before anything else. He coughed. A deep wet rattling cough that bent him over. That went on for a long time. That Lex had heard before. In a narrow stone house on Fennel Street in Highcrest, five years ago. Every winter of Wren's childhood.

Grey-lung.

The man straightened up. Wiped his mouth on the back of his hand. Spat, carefully, to one side, away from the hole.

And saw Lex.

And grinned.

---

"*Well*," he said. His voice was deep and rough, with a burr in it Lex didn't know. East. Far east. "*Well, well. Look who's come up the mountain.*"

The yard went quiet.

Lex felt it. Every head in the ring turning. Toward him. Toward the big man with the pick. Back and forth. The way a crowd turns at a fair when two bulls see each other across a field.

"*You know me*," said Lex.

"*Everyone knows you*." The man's grin widened. "*You're the smith. The Worldforger. The big lad with the gold in his eyes who went down to the bottom of the world and took the lid off.*" He spread his arms. The pick swung. "*Course I know you. There's a song about you. Three songs. One of them's filthy.*"

Somebody in the ring laughed. Then stopped.

"*I meant*," said Lex, "*you know me. Not the songs. Me.*"

The big man looked at him for a long moment. And the grin went somewhere else. Not gone. Just quieter.

"*Coldwater*," he said. "*Two summers back. The Stone in the square. You came through on a dragon with your brother and the scholar woman and stood at our Stone in the rain and put your hand on it.*" He was quiet. "*And four thousand of us went up. All at once. Like a mine-damp going off.*"

Lex remembered.

Not him. Not the man. He'd Unbound two hundred and sixty Stones by then. More. He couldn't remember the faces. But he remembered Coldwater. A grey town in a grey valley in the far east of Vhessa, at the edge of the old Blightmarch, built round the mouths of mines. The rain. The black slag-heaps. The cough. Everyone in the square coughing.

Grey-lung. From the Gloam in the mines. From the breach in Vhessa.

His father's breach.

---

"*Silas Hask*," said the big man. And held out his hand.

Lex took it.

It was like taking hold of a lump of granite. Hard. Rough. Callused in places Lex had never seen calluses. Scarred across the knuckles. A miner's hand. A hand that had swung a pick in the dark every day of its life.

"*I was a [Hewer]*," said Silas Hask. Still holding on. "*Common. Capped at twenty-four. Twenty-six years down the pits at Coldwater. My da before me. His da before him.*" His grip tightened. Not hard. Just enough. "*You know what a hewer does, smith?*"

"*He cuts the coal*."

"*He cuts the coal*." Hask nodded. "*At the face. In the dark. On his side, mostly, in a seam two foot high, with the roof creaking over him. Twelve hours a day. Till his back goes. Or his lungs. Or the roof.*" He let go of Lex's hand. "*My da's lungs went. My mam's. My little sister's. All three. In one winter. The year the grey came up out of the deep seams.*"

He coughed. Short. Into his fist.

"*Mine are going*," said Silas Hask. Matter-of-fact. "*Slower. I'm big. I've got more to go.*" He shrugged his heavy shoulders. "*Physic in Highcrest says three years. Maybe five.*"

He looked at Lex.

"*And then you came*," he said, "*and put your hand on the Stone.*"

---

He opened his status.

Right there. In the inn yard. In front of everyone. The way people did now, sometimes, with no Wardens watching. The way a man might pull up his shirt in a tavern to show you the scar.

The ring of people murmured.

Lex read it. *Level 71*. Two summers. From twenty-four to seventy-one. He'd gone up nearly fifty levels in two years. More than Lex had in five.

*Fault-Finder*. He looked at that one twice.

"*You find the weak place*," said Lex slowly. "*In stone. In anything.*"

"*Every seam's got a fault*," said Hask. "*Every wall. Every roof. Every man.*" He tapped the pick on his shoulder. "*Find the fault, put the point in it, and the whole thing comes down. Twenty-six years in the dark, smith. You learn to feel where things are going to break.*"

He grinned.

"*Bit like what you do*," he said. "*With your little hammer. I heard. At the bottom of the world. You found the flaw in the old man's folding. And struck it. And the whole thing came apart.*"

He nodded at the black hole in the inn yard.

"*Same thing*," said Silas Hask. "*Just bigger.*"

---

"*Don't break the core*," said Lex.

The yard went silent.

Hask looked at him. His grin didn't move. But his eyes did. Something went still in them. Watchful. The way a miner's eyes go still when he hears the roof creak.

"*Say that again*," he said.

"*Don't break it*." Lex didn't raise his voice. He turned. Slowly. So that he was speaking to all of them. The whole ring. Thirty, forty Uncapped, with their weapons and their bright hungry faces. "*The cores. In the Delves. The black stones at the bottom. Don't shatter them. Claim them. Take them whole. The net gives you less, I know. It's still a lot. It's still a level, maybe more.*"

"*And why*," said Hask, "*would we do that?*"

Lex looked at him.

"*Because they're alive*," he said.

---

Someone laughed.

A young man at the back of the ring. Thin. Sharp-faced. With a sword on his hip and a Saltreach accent. He laughed out loud and then looked round to see if anyone else was laughing.

Nobody was.

"*They're pieces of something*," said Lex. "*Something big. Something that's coming up under all of us. A Well. Like Flame, or Stone. The seventh one. The one that was starved.*" He looked round at their faces. "*The Delves are bits of it. Coming up ahead of it. Like—like bits of bark off a tree that's growing. And the cores are—*"

He didn't know how to say it.

*The voice it borrowed*. *It's all right. It's still here. It's just somewhere else now*.

"*When you break them*," said Lex, "*it hurts. The thing they came from. It feels it. My brother feels it.*" He looked at Hask. "*It's frightened. It's very young. It doesn't understand what's happening to it.*"

Silas Hask looked at him for a long, long time.

Then he looked down at the black hole at his feet.

"*Alive*," he said.

"*Yes*."

"*A Well*."

"*Yes*."

Hask nodded slowly. Thoughtfully. He scratched his cropped grey-brown head.

"*So's coal*," he said.

---

Lex blinked.

"*Coal's alive*," said Silas Hask. "*Or it was. Trees. Ferns. Great big swamps full of them, a million years ago. My grandda used to say you could see the leaves in it, if you split it right. Little ferns. Pressed flat. In the black.*" He tapped the pick-handle against his palm. "*I've broken a lot of coal, smith. Twenty-six years. Every day. Never once asked it how it felt.*"

"*That's not the same*—"

"*Isn't it*?" Hask's voice didn't change. Calm. Rough. Reasonable. "*You stood in the Hall of Lanterns and told the Queen's council that the thing coming up is a starved Well and it's sorry and it needs a home.*" He smiled. "*Oh, yes. I heard. Everyone heard. It's all over Highcrest.*" His smile went thin. "*And there's a lady in a house on the Crown Terrace who says it's the Unmaker. Who says it ate worlds. Who says it'll eat ours.*"

The ring was very quiet.

"*I don't know who's right*," said Silas Hask. "*I'm a hewer. I don't know about Wells. I know about the dark. I know about seams. And I know that every time I go down one of these holes and come up with a black stone and put my pick through it, I go up three levels.*" He looked at Lex. "*And the cough gets a bit better.*"

---

Lex didn't say anything.

He looked at the big man. At his heavy weathered face. At his scarred miner's hands. At the faint grey tinge round his lips that Lex had seen on Wren's mother, every winter.

"*Better*," he said.

"*Every level*." Hask coughed again. Short. Into his fist. And looked at it. "*VIT goes up. Health goes up. Every level, a bit more.*" He wiped his hand on his coat. "*Physic said three years. Maybe five.*" He looked at the black hole. "*I've been going down these since the first one opened. Six of them. Six cores. Eighteen levels.*" He shrugged. "*He says ten, now. Maybe more. If I keep going.*"

The inn yard was very quiet.

"*You took off my Ceiling, smith*," said Silas Hask. Quietly. Not angry. Not cruel. Just certain. The way a man is certain about the weight of a pick he's carried for twenty-six years. "*You stood at our Stone in the rain and gave four thousand people their lives back. My life back. You did that. I'll never forget it.*" He looked Lex in the eye. "*Don't you dare put a new one on.*"

---

They went in together.

Lex hadn't wanted that. Neither had Hask. But there was one Delve and forty Uncapped standing round it, and nobody was going to let anybody else go first, and in the end it was Wren who settled it. Standing at the edge of the inn yard with her bow strung and her wind curling round her boots, saying *oh, for the Lady's sake, just all go in together and stop posturing, you look like a lot of cockerels*.

So they went in together.

Lex. And Hask, with his pick. And Hask's crew, five of them, hewers from Coldwater, every one Unbound at the same Stone in the same rain. A woman named Brigid with a hammer. Two brothers named Tull. An old man named Absalom, nearly sixty, with one eye and a lamp on his helmet. A boy named Kit who couldn't have been more than seventeen.

And Wren stayed at the top.

She stood at the edge of the black in the inn yard where the water-trough used to be. In her leathers. With her bow. And watched him step off the edge.

"*Don't die*," she said.

"*I won't*."

"*And don't let him break it*," said Wren. Very low. Just for him.

---

It was a might-have-been again. The first floor.

Lex had half expected it. A long corridor. Black glass floor. Doors.

But not the same doors. These were mine-doors. Heavy timber, iron-banded, set into rock, with chalk marks on them. And behind each one, the sound of picks. Ringing. In the dark. And men coughing.

The Coldwater crew went very quiet.

"*That's the Number Four*," said the boy, Kit. Staring at a door. Very white. "*That's my da's seam. That's—I can hear him. That's his cough.*"

"*Don't touch it*," said Lex.

"*He's dead*." Kit's voice cracked. "*He's been dead three years. And I can hear him.*"

"*It's a might-have-been*," said Lex. "*A door you didn't open. A life that didn't happen.*" He looked at the boy. "*The one where the grey never came up. Where he's still down there, cutting coal.*"

Kit stared at the door. With his hand half raised.

And Silas Hask put his big scarred hand on the boy's shoulder. And turned him round. Gently. Away from the door.

"*He'd still be coughing, lad*," said Hask. "*Grey or no grey. Coal-damp gets everyone in the end. You know that.*" He pushed him, gently, down the corridor. "*Walk on.*"

Kit walked on.

Lex looked at Hask.

Hask didn't look back.

---

The Unfinished came on the second floor.

Lines. Charcoal-black lines on grey air. Lex had told them. All of them. On the way in. *Don't hit them. It goes through. Put your hand on them. Listen. Give them something to be.*

Hask had listened politely. And nodded. And said *right you are*.

And then the first one came out of the wall at him, a long sketch of something with too many legs, like a centipede drawn by a child, and he looked at it for exactly one breath with his miner's eyes narrowed—

And put the point of his pick into it. Precisely. At one spot. Behind the third pair of legs, where two lines crossed and didn't quite meet.

And it came apart.

Not into iron. Not into stone. Not into anything. Into *nothing*. The lines just—unravelled. Like a knitted sock when you pull the right thread. Fell apart into loose black squiggles on the grey air. And faded. And were gone.

"*Fault*," said Silas Hask. Mildly. "*Every pattern's got one. Even the ones that aren't finished.*" He looked at Lex. "*Specially the ones that aren't finished. They're all fault, near enough.*"

They did it his way, mostly.

Lex couldn't stop them. He tried. He went ahead and caught the Unfinished before Hask's crew could reach them, and put his hand on them, and listened, and finished them. A small stone dog. An iron hare. A thing that wanted to be a bird made of wind, which he couldn't do without Wren, and had to let go. They sat down on the black glass floor behind him. Done. Patient.

But there were too many. And the Coldwater crew were fast. And Hask was faster.

Fault. Point. Pull. Gone.

Fault. Point. Pull. Gone.

He was very good. Lex had to admit it. He'd seen a lot of fighters. Garrick. Elowen. Teodra Hale. Kestrel, who fought like a bonfire. Antho, who fought like a held breath.

He'd never seen anyone fight like Silas Hask. Patiently. Without anger. Without hurry. Like a man at a coal-face, twelve hours into a shift, working a seam he'd worked a thousand times. Finding the place. Putting the point in. Moving on.

*[He's not cruel*,] said Ember. Quietly. Watching. *[Lex. He's not cruel. He doesn't enjoy it.]*

*I know*.

*[He's just—working*.]

*I know*, thought Lex. *That's what frightens me.*

---

The guardian was at the bottom of the third floor.

A mine.

A real one. Or the shape of one. A long low gallery cut into black rock, with pit-props of grey timber holding up the roof, and a narrow-gauge rail running down the middle into the dark. And at the end of it, at the coal-face, a man. Made of lines. Kneeling. Swinging a pick. Over and over. Into black that wasn't there.

And coughing.

The Coldwater crew stopped dead.

And Silas Hask, at the front, with his pick on his shoulder, stood very still in the mouth of the gallery and looked at the lined man kneeling at the face.

He knew who it was. Lex could see it. Everyone could see it.

"*Da*," said Silas Hask.

---

It wasn't his father. Lex knew that. It was a pattern. A sketch. A might-have-been, with the shape of Hask's father on it, because the Delve had reached into Hask's head and found the deepest door there and opened it.

The voice it borrowed.

The lined man at the coal-face stopped swinging. And put down his pick. And turned round. Slowly. On his knees. In the low gallery. And looked at his son.

And from inside the lines came a cough. Deep and wet and rattling. And under it, small and frightened and young, so faint Lex barely heard it—

*...sorry...*

*...didn't know...*

*...only borrowed...it's the only shape I found...*

Silas Hask stood in the mouth of the gallery. And didn't move.

And then he did.

---

He walked down the gallery. Between the pit-props. Along the rail. Not fast. Not slow. The way a man walks down a seam at the start of a shift. His boots crunching on the black floor.

He stopped in front of the lined man.

And looked down at him. For a long time.

"*You're not him*," said Silas Hask.

The lined man looked up at him. With its loop-eyes. Coughing.

"*He's dead*," said Hask. "*He died in my arms. In the front room. In the winter. With the grey coming up out of his mouth like smoke.*" His voice didn't change. Calm. Rough. "*You're not him. You're a picture of him. Something drew him on the dark to get at me.*"

He lifted the pick.

"*Hask*," said Lex. "*Don't*—"

"*I know what you're going to say, smith*," said Silas Hask. Without turning round. "*Put my hand on it. Listen to it. Give it something to be.*" He looked at the lined man. "*I don't want to give it anything. I want it to stop wearing my da.*"

He found the fault.

Lex saw him find it. Saw the miner's eyes narrow and go still, the way they did, reading the lines. One place. Over the heart. Where two lines crossed and didn't meet.

He put the point in.

The lined man came apart. Gently. Like smoke. Like charcoal blown off a page. And the cough went with it.

And the gallery, and the pit-props, and the rail, and the coal-face.

And there was a round room. Small. Bare. Black glass.

And on the floor, in the middle, a pebble.

---

*CLAIM (core remains whole)*.

*SHATTER (core destroyed; reward ×5)*.

Lex was moving before the panel finished. Across the room. Toward the pebble. He could hear it. Humming. Faint. Frightened. *Little light, little light*.

He was fast. He was a Worldforger, Tier IV, Level 91, with AGI eighty-six.

Silas Hask had been standing two feet from it.

He didn't hurry. He didn't need to. He looked down at the pebble at his feet. Black. Smooth. The size of a hen's egg.

"*Hask*!"

"*Sorry, smith*," said Silas Hask.

And brought the pick down.

---

It shattered.

Like glass. Like a black glass bottle. Into a thousand pieces. That went up in smoke. Fine. Black. Hanging in the air.

And then down. Through the floor. Into the ground.

And Lex, standing three feet away with his hand out, felt it go.

Not the core. Not the smoke. Something else. Something on the other end of the smoke. A long way down. Something enormous and soft and young, rising slowly through the dark.

He felt it *flinch*.

The whole of it. All at once. The way a hand flinches off a hot stove. A great soft shudder, running through the dark under the world, from Thistledown to the Hollowmaw to the bottom of the deep. And a sound. Not a sound. A *feeling*. Coming up through the floor of the Delve, through the soles of his boots, through the Stone in him, through his chest—

A scream.
