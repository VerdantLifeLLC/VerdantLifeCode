# The Moth Queen Drinks

The tear was a doorway.

Antho knew it the moment he stepped into it. Before his foot touched the edge. Before the cold air from the other side hit his face. He knew it the way he knew the black glass at the bottom of the Hollowmaw. The way he'd known the doorway at the bottom of the Heart, two years ago, with the hunger at his back.

A gap. Between one place and another. Between the warm gold room with his mother in it, and the cold glittering dark outside. Between the middle of the little one, the softest deepest warmest place in all the deep, and a long pale hand reaching in.

A threshold.

And he was the Threshold Warden.

He stood in it.

The hand stopped.

An inch from his chest. Long. Pale. Grey-gold. Fingers spread. Too many joints in them. Reaching past him. Through him. Toward the two little sleeping lights behind him, and the soft deep dark behind *them*.

It stopped as if it had hit glass.

And on the other side of the tear, in the cold, in the glitter, the two gold lights that were her eyes went very wide.

"*Oh*," said Maelis.

She didn't pull her hand back. She left it there. An inch from his chest. Pressed flat against nothing. The way a moth presses against a window.

"*Oh, look at that*," she said softly. "*A little Warden. In a little door.*"

---

He could see her now.

All of her. Through the tear. Standing on the dark outside, on nothing, in the cold, with the moths swirling round her in a great slow glittering column, like smoke round a chimney. Tall. Taller than he remembered from the Hall of Lanterns. Her silver hair loose. Her long grey-gold gown moving in a wind that wasn't there. And her wings—

Open.

He'd only seen them folded. In the Hall. On the West Way. A cloak. A train. A fold of silk down her back.

They weren't folded now.

They filled the dark behind her. Enormous. Grey and gold. Patterned with rings and whorls and eyes. Dozens of eyes. Painted eyes, dark, gold-rimmed, on every part of every wing, all of them turned toward him. Toward the tear. Toward the light behind him.

And they were *shedding*. With every slow beat. A fine glittering dust, falling off them like snow off a branch. Drifting down into the dark.

And behind her, on the dark, her Mothwing. Thirty. Forty. Pale and ragged and dull-eyed. Standing very still. Waiting.

"*Let me in*," said Maelis.

"*No*."

---

She smiled.

It was the loveliest smile he'd ever seen. Sad and fond and patient. The smile she'd given the little girl on the West Way. The smile she'd given the whole council of Caldor from the floor of the Hall of Lanterns.

"*You sound just like her*," she said. "*Did you know? Your mother. She said no to me too. Just now. With her lamp.*" Her lit eyes moved past him. Into the room. To where Seren was standing, with the lamp held high. "*And her mother before her. And hers. All the way back.*" She sighed. "*Nine hundred years of Harrow women saying no to me through windows.*"

"*Then you should be used to it*."

She laughed.

"*Oh, I like you*," said Maelis. "*I like you very much, little Warden. You're so* angry.*"* Her head tilted. "*You've been angry your whole life, haven't you? At your father. At the Order. At the thing in your head.*" Her eyes moved. Over his shoulder. To the soft deep dark behind him. "*At* that.*"*

He didn't answer.

"*It's in you*," said Maelis softly. "*Still. I can smell it. Eight years of it. The voice. The hunger. It's in the walls of you, the way smoke gets into the walls of a house and never comes out.*" Her hand, pressed against nothing an inch from his chest, spread its long fingers. "*And you're standing in front of it. Protecting it. The thing that broke you.*"

"*Yes*."

"*Why*?"

---

He thought about it.

He actually thought about it. Standing in the tear. With the cold on his face and the warm at his back. With her hand an inch from his heart and her lit eyes on him and thirty Mothwing waiting in the dark.

He'd been asked that before. By himself. Every night for three months. Lying awake. *Why*? *Why am I doing this? Why do I care? It broke me. It took eight years of my life. It made me hold a knife to Lex's throat.*

He'd never had an answer.

He had one now.

"*Because it said sorry*," said Antho Vey.

Maelis looked at him.

"*And you never did*," said Antho.

---

The smile went out of her face.

Not all at once. Slowly. Like a lamp being turned down. The lovely sad fond patient smile, going. And under it, something else. Something he'd seen once before, through the little one's eyes, in a hall of glass nine hundred years ago, on the face of a woman kneeling at the edge of a nearly empty pool.

*Hunger*.

"*Sorry*," said Maelis. Very quietly. "*Sorry.*" She tilted her head. "*What would I be sorry for, little Warden? For being thirsty? For drinking? For wanting to live?*"

She leaned closer. Her face an inch from the edge of the tear. From the edge of his doorway.

"*Would you be sorry*?" she whispered. "*If you were dying? If you'd watched everyone you loved grow old and wither and go into the ground, one by one, and you found a spring in the cellar that would stop it? Would you kneel down at the edge of it and say no thank you? Would you* not drink?*"*

"*My grandmother's sister didn't*," said Antho.

Maelis went very still.

"*Hesper*," said Antho. "*She stood in the doorway. And watched. And when it was nearly empty, she came forward. And knelt down. And poured something back.*" He looked into her lit eyes. "*I saw it. It showed me. She put her crown down on the stone and cupped her empty hands and poured. And you looked at her the way you're looking at me now.*"

Maelis said nothing.

"*And she said no*," said Antho. "*Not me. Not ever.*"

He held the doorway.

"*Neither will I*," he said.

---

She took her hand away.

Slowly. Drawing it back from the inch of nothing in front of his chest. Folding the long pale fingers in. Letting it fall to her side.

She looked at him for a long time.

"*All right*," said Maelis. Softly. Reasonably. "*All right, little Warden. You hold your door.*"

She smiled again. A different smile. Small. Thin. Not lovely at all.

"*I'll just go round*," said the Moth Queen.

---

He didn't understand.

Not for a moment. Not for the space of a breath. He stood in the tear with his feet set and his hands at his sides and the Lantern hanging from his belt, holding the doorway, and didn't understand.

Then she turned.

Away from him. Away from the tear. Away from the gold room and the two little lights and his mother with her lamp.

And spread her enormous wings.

And knelt.

On the dark. On the nothing. On the soft cold deep under her feet. The dark that wasn't just dark. The dark that was the room. The space. The Well. All of it. Everywhere. Folded round the whole of the deep like a hand round a lamp.

*We're inside it*, Seraphine had said. *We've been inside it all along.*

Maelis knelt on it. And cupped her long pale hands.

And put them down into the dark.

---

"*NO*!"

He didn't know who shouted. Him. Lex. His mother. Seraphine. All of them.

It didn't matter.

The Moth Queen's hands went into the dark like hands going into water. Up to the wrists. Up to the elbows. Into the soft deep folded night-sky that was the little one's body, its skin, if it had skin.

And she lifted them out.

Full.

---

He'd never forget what was in them.

Dark. But not dark. *Full* dark. The dark of the pool in the glass hall. The dark of the moment before a word. Soft and deep and alive and *glowing*, faintly, from inside, the way the night sky glows when you're far from any town. Pouring between her long fingers like water. Like light. Like the first breath of something being born.

And she lifted it to her mouth.

And drank.

---

The little one screamed.

Not the way it screamed when the cores broke. Not small. Not high. Not a child's cry in another room.

It screamed the way a *world* screams.

Antho felt it. Everyone felt it. It came up through the dark and through the room and through the tear he was standing in and through his feet and his legs and his chest and the old place in the back of his skull, and it was so big, so *enormous*, that for a moment there was nothing else. No deep. No moths. No mother. No brother. Just the scream. Filling everything. Filling him.

*[...AGAIN...]*

*[...she's doing it AGAIN...like BEFORE...like the HALL...]*

*[...brother brother brother she's DRINKING me...]*

His knees went.

He didn't fall. He wouldn't let himself fall. He went down on one knee in the doorway with both hands clamped to his head and his eyes shut and his teeth gritted and the scream going through him like a river through a broken dam, and he *held the doorway*. He held it. Nothing came past him. Nothing.

But she wasn't trying to come past him.

She was drinking from the other side.

Antho looked at his mother.

She was sitting very straight. With the lamp in her lap and her hand on the dark beside her. Her face was grey with tiredness and dust. Her eyes were very bright.

He'd seen that face before. In memory. Thirteen years ago. On the stairs at Harrow. In the kitchen, with the hole opening behind the hearth and the dark coming in. Her face, turning toward him, with the lamp in her hand.

Making a decision.

"*You have to*," said Seren Vey. Very gently. To the dark. "*Lamb. You have to come up. You can't stay down here forever. Down here there's nothing. No sun. No rain. No new things.*" She swallowed. "*You're the door new things come in by. You can't be a door at the bottom of the world. A door has to open onto somewhere.*"

*[...but SHE'LL be there...]*

"*I know*."

*[...she'll DRINK me...]*

"*No*," said Seren.

She looked up. At her sons. At Lex, with the handprint on his face and the little hammer in his lap. At Antho, with his Clarity creeping back up and his hands still shaking.

"*No*," said their mother. "*She won't. Because when you come up, lamb, you're not going to come up alone. And you're not going to come up into her sky.*"

She looked at Lex.

"*You're going to come up somewhere that's been made ready for you*," said Seren Vey. "*Somewhere with room.*"

Lex looked back at her.

And Antho watched his brother understand. Watched it come up into his face the way it always did. Out of his hands. Out of the part of him that had stood at an anvil every day since he was eleven.

*Every new thing needs a hollow to be made in*.

"*A mould*," said Lex. Very slowly. "*You want me to make it a mould.*"

His mother didn't answer.

She didn't need to.

Out in the dark, beyond the gold room, the little one went very still.

Listening.
