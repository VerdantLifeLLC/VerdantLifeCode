@@PART Part Two: The Moth and the Flame
# The Dimming

Highcrest went dark in a single night.

Lex heard about it from a raven at dawn. Roole's. Grey-black, with the Keepers' ring on its leg. It came down out of a grey sky onto the slope of the Hollowmaw and stood in the snow with its feathers fluffed and its beak open, panting, as if it had flown the whole hundred miles without stopping.

The note on its leg was four words.

*Highcrest is lost. Come.*

---

He went on Vharuun. With Antho, and Kestrel, and the dragon's great blue wings beating so hard the air sang. Ashka stayed at the Keep. Wren stayed with her. And their mother, and their father, and the students, and Pim with a basket of Hush-cups, filling them.

Wren didn't argue.

She'd stood in the cave mouth at dawn with the raven's note in her hand and read it, and gone white, and then gone very still. And looked at him.

"*Mam's in Highcrest*," she said. "*And Tam. And Robbie.*"

"*I know*."

"*Fennel Street. The narrow house with the green door. Third from the end.*" Her voice was very steady. "*If you see them. If you can. If they're*—" She stopped.

"*I'll find them*," said Lex.

"*Don't promise*."

"*I'm not promising*." He took her face in his hands. "*I'm telling you. I'll find them.*"

She looked at him for a long moment. At the handprint on his cheek. At his crooked nose.

"*Go*," said Wren Ashdown. "*Go on. Before I change my mind and come with you and Old Mag kills me.*"

---

They saw it from forty miles off.

Lex had seen Highcrest from the air a hundred times. On dragons. In all weathers. At dawn, when the golden stone of the walls caught the first light and the whole city glowed like a lamp. At night, when every window had a lantern in it and the city on its hill looked like a heap of embers in a grate.

He'd never seen it like this.

It was noon. Clear. Summer. A blue sky. A high bright sun.

And over Highcrest, a cloud.

Not a rain-cloud. Not smoke. It sat on the city like a lid. Low. Grey-gold. Glittering. Turning slowly, the way a whirlpool turns, round and round, over the walls and the towers and the roofs, so thick the sun couldn't get through it. Under it, the city was in shadow. Grey. Dim. A golden city with the gold drained out of it.

And in the middle of the cloud, at the top of the hill, rising out of the glitter like a finger pointing at the sky—

The Spire.

Lit.

Not with lanterns. Not with fire. Gold. Soft. Warm. Glowing. From every window. From the top floor to the bottom. Like a lamp. Like a beacon. Like two eyes at the end of a long lane on a winter night, multiplied by a thousand.

The only light in Highcrest.

---

"*She's in the Spire*," said Antho. Behind him. In the wind. Very quiet.

"*I know*."

"*Father's rooms*." His brother's voice was very flat. "*The top floor. Where the light's brightest.*"

Lex didn't answer.

He was looking down. At the downs west of the city. At the long white road that ran out of the West Gate toward the Blightmarch.

There were people on it.

Hundreds. Thousands. A long dark stream of them. Coming out of the West Gate. Spreading across the downs. Walking. Running. Carts. Horses. Children carried on shoulders. Old people carried on backs.

Running away.

And behind them, coming out of the gate after them, slowly, drifting, low over the road like smoke over water—

The glitter.

---

Vharuun came down on the road like a thunderbolt.

Lex didn't ask her to. She just did. Out of the sky. Folding her wings. Dropping. A hundred feet in a breath. And landing, in a crash of blue scale and a gust of wind that knocked half the people on the road flat and blew the leading edge of the glitter back fifty yards, between the running crowd and the moths.

And spread her wings.

Across the road. From hedge to hedge. A wall of blue. Crackling with lightning at the edges.

And roared.

---

The glitter stopped.

Not because of the roar. Not because of the wings. Lex saw it stop. Saw it hang there, over the road, fifty yards off. A great slow turning curtain of grey-gold, from the ground to the sky.

And saw why.

Vharuun was glowing.

Faintly. Blue-white. All along the edges of her wings, where the lightning crackled. Light. Bright. Living. The light of a storm. The light of the sky.

The moths had seen it.

And they were *coming*.

"*Kestrel*!"

She was already off the dragon's neck. Already on the road. With her hair gone dark and the air round her rippling. Black heat. Lex was beside her. Dark. Hot.

And Antho behind them, with the Lantern shuttered and the dark wrapped round him and his face like stone.

---

It was not like the deep.

In the deep there had been nothing to protect. Just the three of them, and Seraphine, and the dark. Here there were people. Thousands. Behind them on the road. Screaming. Falling. Crawling. Children. Old women. A man pushing a handcart with a grandmother in it.

And the moths came for the light. And the people *had* light.

Lanterns. Candles. Fire in a pot. A girl with a little tin lamp clutched against her chest. Every one of them carrying something. Because it was Highcrest. Because every house in Highcrest had a lantern in the window. Because when you run from your house in the dark, you take the light with you.

The moths came over Vharuun's wings like water over a weir.

And went for the lamps.

---

Lex burned them.

He walked into them with black heat pouring off him, and they curled and crumpled and fell, the way they had in the deep. Hundreds. Thousands. Kestrel beside him, doing the same, with her Sunreach blade out and her teeth bared. A wedge. A plough.

It wasn't enough.

There were too many. And they weren't coming at him. They were going *past*. Round. Over. To the lamps. To the people. And every time one of them settled on a lantern, the lantern dimmed. And every time one of them settled on a person—

He saw it happen.

A woman. Thirty, maybe. In an apron. Running with a baby in her arms. A moth came down out of the glitter and landed on her cheek. Just for a moment. Lightly. Like a kiss.

And flew off.

And the woman stopped running.

Stood still. In the middle of the road. With the baby in her arms. And turned. Slowly. And looked back. Toward the city. Toward the Spire. Toward the gold light at the top of the hill.

Her eyes were dim.

And she started to walk. Back. Toward it. With the baby still in her arms.

---

Antho caught her.

He came out of the dark beside her like a shadow coming off a wall, and put his arm round her, and turned her. Gently. Back toward the downs. She didn't fight. She didn't pull. She just kept leaning. Toward the city. The way Odile had leaned in the snow.

"*Lex*!" said his brother. "*I can't hold them all! They're turning back! All of them!*"

Lex looked.

All down the road. Up and down the long stream of people coming out of the West Gate. Here and there. One. Then another. Then ten. Stopping. Standing still. Turning round.

And walking back. Slowly. Into the glitter. Toward the gold light at the top of the hill.

Not running. Not screaming. Not afraid.

*Peaceful*.

---

"*Shut your lamps*!" Lex was shouting. He didn't remember starting. "*Shut your lamps! Put them out! All of them! Now!*"

Some of them heard him. Some of them did it. Blew out their candles. Dropped their lanterns in the ditch. Covered their faces.

Not enough.

He saw the girl with the little tin lamp. Eight. Nine. Clutching it against her chest. In the middle of the road. Frozen. Staring at the glitter coming down on her. Her lamp held tight. As if it was the only thing she had.

It probably was.

He ran.

He got there first. Just. He put himself between the girl and the moths, dark and hot, and they hit the air round him and curled and fell like burnt paper. And he crouched down in front of her. In the road. With the moths dying round them both in a ring.

"*Give me the lamp*," said Lex.

She stared at him.

"*Give it to me*. *I'll keep it safe. I promise.*"

"*It's for my da*," said the girl. In a whisper. "*He's on the wall. He's a soldier. He didn't come home last night. I lit it for him.*"

Lex looked at the little tin lamp.

At the little flame in it. Small and gold and steady.

A light left on. For someone who hadn't come home.

"*I know*," he said. Very gently. "*I know you did. That's why they want it.*"

---

He took her and the lamp both. Picked her up, with the lamp held in her arms against her chest, and wrapped them both in black heat, as close as he could without burning her, and ran.

Back down the road. Away from the gate. Toward Vharuun. Toward the wall of blue scale and lightning.

And over his shoulder, high up, coming out of the glitter over the West Gate—

A shape.

Big. Heavy. Broad-shouldered. With something on its back. Spread. Grey-gold. Ragged. *Wings*.

Not the small dry furled wings of the Mothwing. Big ones. Strong ones. Beating. Carrying a man the size of a bear through the air over the road as easily as a hawk carries a mouse.

A miner's pick in one hand.

---

Silas Hask came down on the road in front of Vharuun.

Lightly. On his toes. Like a moth on a window. His great grey-gold wings folding behind him. His patched leather coat torn open down the back to let them through.

He looked at the dragon.

Then at Lex. With the girl in his arms.

His eyes were gold.

Not dull now. Not the dim coal-spark gold of the Mothwing. Brighter. Warmer. Lit. Like two candles behind a curtain.

"*Smith*," said Silas Hask.

---

Lex put the girl down.

Behind him. Behind Vharuun's foreleg. With her lamp. And said *stay there*, and *don't move*, and *close your eyes*, and turned round.

Hask hadn't moved.

He was standing in the road. With his pick on his shoulder. And his wings folded. And the glitter coming down round him like snow. Settling on his shoulders. On his hair. Not touching him. Moving round him. The way the moths had moved round Maelis in the deep. Like a cloak. Like a court.

He wasn't coughing.

He was breathing. Deep and slow and easy. All the way down.

"*She made you her Champion*," said Lex.

"*She asked*." Hask's voice was the same. Rough. Calm. Reasonable. "*Last night. When she came back. Lit up like a lighthouse.*" He looked down at his own hands. Big. Scarred. Faintly glowing at the knuckles. "*She said she needed someone who could stand in front of her. Someone strong. Someone who'd been capped his whole life and knew what it was worth to be free.*"

"*And you said yes*."

"*I said what's the pay*." Hask's mouth twitched. "*She gave me a sip. Another one. Bigger. Out of what she brought up from the deep.*" He breathed in. Out. "*And I said yes.*"

---

"*That was the little one*," said Lex.

Hask looked at him.

"*What she brought up*," said Lex. "*What she gave you. That's a piece of it. She knelt down in the deep and put her hands in it and drank. And it screamed. My brother heard it. Everyone at the bottom of the world heard it.*" He didn't look away from Hask's gold eyes. "*You're breathing with it. That's what's in your lungs. That's what's holding up your wings.*"

Hask didn't say anything.

"*You felt it*," said Lex. "*At Thistledown. When you broke the core. You said it was like a seam settling. A groan in the rock.*" He took a step forward. "*You know what it is. You've known since the first one.*"

"*I know*," said Silas Hask.

---

The road was very quiet.

Behind Lex, people were still running. Still falling. Still turning back. Kestrel was burning moths. Antho was holding a woman with a baby who kept trying to walk toward the city. Vharuun was standing with her wings spread and her lightning crackling and her great golden eye fixed on the winged man in the road.

And Hask stood with his pick on his shoulder and looked at Lex.

"*I know what it is*," he said again. Quietly. "*I'm not stupid, smith. I'm a hewer. I know what's alive and what's rock. I've known since Thistledown.*" He looked down at his glowing hands. "*It's alive. It hurts it. Every time.*"

"*Then why*—"

"*Because I can breathe*."

He said it simply. Without anger. Without pleading. The way a man says the weight of a pick he's carried for twenty-six years.

"*Because my da died coughing grey in my arms*," said Silas Hask. "*And my mam. And my sister. And I was going to be next. And now I'm not.*" He looked up. "*Because I was a Common hewer capped at twenty-four for twenty-six years, and now I'm level eighty-six and I can fly.*"

He shifted the pick on his shoulder.

"*Because you gave me that*," he said. "*You. At the Stone in Coldwater. In the rain. You took off my Ceiling and you said, go. As high as you can.*" His gold eyes didn't waver. "*So I'm going.*"

---

"*Not like this*," said Lex.

"*How, then*?" Hask's voice didn't rise. "*Tell me how, smith. Tell me how a coughing hewer from Coldwater gets to eighty-six without breaking anything. Without hurting anything.*" He spread his free hand. "*You went down to the bottom of the world and reforged the net and broke a Ceiling for every soul in Aldermere. Who'd you hurt doing that?*"

Lex didn't answer.

"*The nobles*," said Hask. "*The Order. The ones who sold Sanctions. Every one of them lost everything the day you did that. Their land. Their seats. Their sons' futures.*" He shrugged. "*You hurt them. You didn't care. Because it was right.*"

"*That's not the same*—"

"*It's always the same*," said Silas Hask. "*Somebody's always getting hurt so somebody else can rise. That's what rising* is.*"*

He looked past Lex. At the road. At the people. At the woman with the baby in Antho's arms, still leaning toward the city.

"*She's not killing them*," he said. "*You know that? She's not. She could. She's not.*" He looked back at Lex. "*She's taking the light. That's all. The warm bit. And they don't mind. They walk back to her and stand outside her house and they're* happy.*"* He paused. "*Happier than they've been in years, some of them. No fear. No grief. No worrying about the rent. Just—quiet.*"

"*That's not happy*."

"*No*," Hask agreed. "*It's not. But it's not hurting either.*"

---

"*Silas*."

The voice came from behind Hask. From the gate. Soft. Low. A little hoarse.

Lex looked up.

And didn't look away fast enough.

---

She was standing on the West Gate.

On top of it. On the battlements. In the shadow of the glitter. Tall. Grey-gold. With her silver hair loose to her knees and her wings folded down her back like a cloak. Glowing. All through. Not just her eyes. Her skin. Her hair. Her hands. The way she'd glowed in the deep, after she drank.

She was smiling.

Lex had looked into her eyes once. In the Hall of Lanterns. When she leaned down and said *Harrow was my sister's*. He'd felt it then. The pull. The *there, someone's home*.

He felt it now. A hundred times worse.

It was like standing at the bottom of a hill on a winter night, in the cold, in the dark, with nothing in your belly and nowhere to go, and looking up, and seeing at the top of the hill a house. With every window lit. And the door open. And someone standing in it. Waiting.

For you.

His foot moved.

---

*[LEX*.]

Ember. Loud. Louder than he'd ever heard it. A clang in his hearth like a hammer on an anvil.

*[LEX. LOOK AT HER HANDS.]*

He looked at her hands.

Long. Pale. Glowing. Hanging at her sides. And at the ends of the fingers, very faint, so faint he'd never have seen it if he hadn't been looking for it, if he hadn't stood in front of her in the deep with Aurelian's little hammer and heard the *tink* go straight through—

Flickering.

The glow. At the fingertips. Going dim. Coming back. Going dim.

Leaking.

He breathed out. And his foot stopped. And he looked at her hands, at the leak, at the flaw, and not at her eyes, and the warm lit house at the top of the hill went away.

---

"*Smith*," said Maelis. From the gate. Fondly. "*You found your way home. And look. You brought your mother.*"

"*She's not here*."

"*No*. *But she's home.*" The Moth Queen tilted her head. "*I felt her come through the door. The Warden's door. I felt the little one let her go.*" She smiled. "*I felt your father say good evening.*"

Lex said nothing.

"*Wasn't that lovely*," said Maelis softly. "*After all those years. She came through the door, and he said good evening, and she said you have a nice voice.*" Her lit eyes were very warm. "*My moths told me. They were there. In the passage. Under the lanterns. They're always there.*"

Lex's hands were burning. Black heat. Pouring off him.

"*I've taken the city*," said Maelis. Conversationally. "*Did you notice? It wasn't hard. Everyone in it had a light in the window. For someone. For the Lady. For the lost.*" She shook her head. "*Every single window. Do you know how* easy *that is?*"

"*Give them back*."

"*I will*." The Moth Queen smiled. "*Of course I will. I don't want them. Not really. They're very small lights. They don't last.*" She looked at her own flickering fingers. "*Nothing lasts.*"

She looked back at him.

"*Bring me the blood*," said Maelis of the Crowned. "*All of it. Every drop of Harrow in the world. Your mother. Your brother. You.*" A pause. "*And anything of yours.*"

Lex went cold.

"*And when the little one comes up*," said Maelis, "*under the sky, at the end of the year, when every window in your country has a lantern in it for Lantern Night*—" She smiled. "*Bring me that too.*"

"*And then*?"

"*And then I'll give them all back*," said the Moth Queen. "*Every light in Highcrest. Every Keeper at Greyfall. Every shepherd in the Greyspine. Every one.*" Her lit eyes were very soft. "*And I'll go. Back through the Veil. With my brothers and sisters. And you'll never see me again.*"

"*Liar*," said Lex.

She laughed.

"*I've never lied to you*," said Maelis. "*Not once. Ask your little coal. It's been checking.*"

*[She hasn't*,] said Ember. Very quietly. *[That's the worst part. She hasn't.]*

"*I'll go back through the Veil*," said Maelis. "*Exactly as I said. With my brothers and sisters.*" Her smile widened. "*All four of them. Awake. Through a door your mother's blood will open. Into the grey country. Where we'll drink what we've brought, and be full, and never come back.*"

She tilted her head.

"*Not this year*," she said. "*Not next year.*" A pause. "*Perhaps not for a hundred years. Perhaps not for nine hundred.*"

She spread her long glowing hands.

"*You'll be long dead, smith*," said Maelis. Gently. "*What do you care?*"

---

And then she was gone.

Not flying. Not walking. Gone. Like a light going out. One moment on the gate, glowing, smiling, with her wings folded. The next, nothing. Just the battlements, grey, in the shadow of the glitter.

And Hask.

He'd turned. Away from Lex. Toward the gate. Toward where she'd been. With his wings half open. His gold eyes on the empty battlements.

Then he looked back at Lex.

For a long moment.

"*She doesn't lie*," said Silas Hask. Quietly. "*I'll give her that. She's the only one who never has.*"

He spread his wings.

"*Think about it, smith*," he said. "*A hundred years. Two hundred. Nine hundred. You'll be dead. Your brother. Your wife. Your kid.*" He looked at Lex. "*What do you care?*"

And he went up. Into the glitter. Into the shadow over Highcrest. Toward the gold light at the top of the hill.

---

They got the Queen out at dusk.

Not through the West Gate. Through the old sally port on the north side, under the castle, a tunnel that Lysandra's great-great-grandfather had cut through the rock in the wars with the Saltreach and that nobody had used for a hundred years. Elowen knew about it. Elowen knew about everything.

They came out of it into a ditch at the bottom of the north wall. Thirty of them. The Queen in a plain cloak with the hood up and her silver circlet in her pocket. Elowen with Oathkeeper drawn and her armor black with dust. Roole, grey-faced, with his arm in a sling. Fenn, carrying a girl of six who wouldn't stop crying. Duke Harlowe's daughter, the young one, who'd refused to leave her father until Elowen picked her up and carried her. A dozen Keepers. A dozen guards.

Lex was waiting in the ditch with Vharuun.

Lysandra came up out of the tunnel and stood in the ditch in the dusk with her hood up and looked at him.

She didn't say anything.

She didn't need to.

He'd seen faces like that. At Fort Caldris, after Elric. At Duskhold. At the Rift. The face of someone who's lost a thing they'd sworn to keep. Who is standing in a ditch, alive, because someone else stood in front of something for them.

"*How many*?" said Lex.

The Queen of Caldor looked at him with her sharp dark eyes. Red-rimmed. Dry.

"*Most of them*," she said.

---

She told him on Vharuun's neck. In the dark. Flying north. With the glitter over Highcrest falling behind them and the stars coming out.

The moths had come at dusk. The night before. The night of the World Notice. Out of the west. Over the walls. So many they put out the sunset.

*They came for the lanterns*, said Lysandra. *That's all, at first. Every window in the city. They went from lantern to lantern and the lanterns went dim and we thought, well. Lanterns. We can live without lanterns.*

*And then she came out of the house on the Crown Terrace*.

*She didn't break the door. She didn't fight the Keepers on it. She walked out. Glowing. And the four Keepers on the door looked at her. And she touched them. On the cheek. One by one. Like a mother.*

*And they turned round and followed her*.

She walked through the city, said the Queen. Slowly. All night. Down the Crown Terrace. Through the Upper Town. Through the market. Through the Lower Town. Touching people. Everyone she passed. A hand on a cheek. A hand on a shoulder.

*And they followed her*.

*By midnight there were a thousand behind her. By dawn, ten thousand*.

*She walked them all up to the Spire. And went in. And they stood outside. In the square. In the streets. On the steps. Looking up at it. Swaying a little. Like grass.*

*And she lit it*.

"*Lit it*?"

"*From inside*." Lysandra's voice was very quiet. "*Every window. Gold. Like her eyes.*" She was quiet for a moment. "*She's in Arion's rooms. At the top. I saw the light come on. In his window. Where I used to sit on the floor when I was nine.*"

---

"*Hollis Penn*," said Elowen.

She was sitting behind the Queen. With Oathkeeper across her knees and her face like stone.

"*The baker*," she said. "*From the Lower Town. The one with the loaf-shaped lantern. The one who made the bread for the wedding.*"

Lex remembered. Hollis Penn. Unwritten by Elric's Key, two years ago, in the square at Highcrest. Hollow-born. Restored at the Highcrest singing. Woken holding a loaf. *Nobody's getting married at the Lantern Keep without a proper loaf*.

"*I saw him*," said Elowen. "*On the steps of the Spire. At dawn. When we went for the sally port.*" She looked down at her sword. "*He was standing in the front row. With his apron on. And flour on his hands. Swaying.*"

She was quiet.

"*He was holding his lantern*," said Elowen Dunmore. "*The loaf-shaped one. From his window. Dark. With dust all over it.*" Her voice cracked. "*He was holding it up. Toward her window. Like he was offering it to her.*"

---

They came down at the Ashbourne Forge at midnight.

Brannoc was waiting in the forge yard. With Hedda. And Tilda. And forty students with torches. They'd seen the dragon coming from ten miles off.

The Queen of Caldor slid down off Vharuun's neck into the forge yard at Ashbourne. In her plain cloak. With her hood up. With dust on her face and her circlet in her pocket and thirty people behind her who were all that was left of her court.

And Brannoc Holt, sixty-three, bald, soot-bearded, enormous, who had capped himself at twenty-four for nineteen years and hummed hymns off-key and cried at weddings, looked at her.

And went down on one knee. In the cinders. In front of the warm spring that had come up out of the forge yard at the end of winter.

"*Majesty*," he said. "*You're welcome here. Long as you like.*"

Lysandra looked down at him.

And then she did something Lex had never seen a queen do. She knelt too. In the cinders. In front of him. And took his enormous scarred hands in hers.

"*Thank you*," said the Queen of Caldor. "*Thank you, Master Holt.*"

And put her head down on his hands. And cried.

---

Lex stood in the forge yard and looked south.

Toward Highcrest. A hundred miles off. Beyond the edge of seeing.

He couldn't see it. He knew he couldn't. It was too far. There were hills in the way. The Vale. The downs.

But he looked anyway.

And thought he saw it. Very faint. Very far. At the very edge of the dark, where the sky met the hills.

A light.

Small. Gold. Warm.

Not a lantern. Not a fire. Not a star.

A window. At the top of a tower. In a dark city full of people standing in the streets, swaying like grass, holding up their dark lanterns toward it. In a room on the top floor of the Spire, with long windows looking north, and shelves of grey books, and a narrow bed, and a tall bookcase that swung open onto a little round room full of portraits.

His father's room.

*Leave a light on*, the Harrow women never said. *Never. Not once. Not in nine hundred years.*

And someone, in his father's room, at the top of the tallest tower in Caldor, had left one on.

For him.

---

*[Lex*,] said Ember. Very quietly.

*I see it*.

*[She's reading them*.] *The coal in his hearth was very still.* [*His books. The grey ones. On the shelves. Two hundred years of them.*]

*I know*.

*[Everything he ever wrote down*.] *A long pause.* [*Every Kindling. Every Ceiling. Every name.*] *Another pause.* [*The day each of you was born. The hour. Where.*]

Lex didn't say anything.

*[She's learning us*,] said Ember. *[Lex. She's sitting in his chair by his window reading his books, and she's learning everything about us he ever knew.]*

Lex stood in the forge yard at Ashbourne with the Queen of Caldor weeping on Brannoc's hands behind him, and the warm spring steaming in the cinders at his feet, and looked south at a light he couldn't see.

And for the first time since he was eleven years old, standing in the ashes of a cottage at Harrow with his mother gone and his brother gone and the whole world on fire—

He was afraid of the dark.

Not the dark.

The light *in* it.
