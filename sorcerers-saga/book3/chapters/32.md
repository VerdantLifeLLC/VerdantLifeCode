# The Way Up

The little one couldn't find the way.

That was the thing nobody had thought of. Not Seraphine, with her notebooks. Not Lex, with his three hundred and fourteen miles. Not Antho, lying every night on the black floor of the fallen hall with his hand on the scar.

It was *everywhere*. Folded round the whole of the deep like a hand round a lamp. It could feel the breach under Duskhold, pressing up against the bottom of it like a child pressing its face to a window. It could feel the Hollowmaw door, three hundred miles north, where Kell sat with it and told it about the miller's cat.

It could feel the circle. The word. The hundred miles of room. Above it. Waiting.

It couldn't *get* there.

*[...I don't know how...]* it said. On the fifty-second night. When Lex came down off the rubble and the whole camp went to sleep under the stars, exhausted, and Antho lay down by the scar with the clay lamp. *[...I'm too big...I'm everywhere...if I go toward the circle I have to leave the door...if I leave the door I leave the Warden...]*

*[...and if I go all at once I'll*—]*

It stopped.

*[...I'll pull things...]* it said. Very small. *[...like before...when I was the other thing...if I move too fast I'll pull everything in after me...]*

Antho lay very still.

*[...I need someone to show me...]* said the little one. *[...the way...slowly...a little at a time...]*

*[...brother...]*

*[...will you come down and show me...]*

---

He told them at breakfast.

In the tent by the ruins. With porridge, which Seraphine had burned, because she'd learned it from his mother. With his father in the chair by the tent-flap, wrapped in blankets, with the grey book on his knee and his mother's hand on his shoulder. With Lex asleep across three stools, snoring. With Wren beside Lex, eating two bowls of porridge at once because the baby, she said, wanted porridge.

"*I'm going down*," said Antho.

Nobody said anything.

"*It needs someone to lead it*," he said. "*From the Hollowmaw door to here. Through the deep. Under the world. Slowly.*" He looked at the clay lamp in his lap. "*It's following the light. My light. Mam's lamp. Where I go, it goes.*" He looked up. "*It's three hundred miles, under. It'll take a month. More. I have to start now. Or it won't be here by Lantern Night.*"

Seraphine had put down her spoon.

"*Alone*?" she said.

Antho looked at her.

"*No*," he said. "*Not alone. I don't do alone anymore.*" He swallowed. "*I was going to ask you.*"

---

They went down the Hollowmaw door four days later.

Seraphine. Pim. And him.

Lex wanted to come. He'd stood at the cave mouth of the Lantern Keep with his face grey and his hands in fists and said *I'm coming*, and Antho had said *no*, and they'd had the argument they always had, the one about who carries what.

And then Wren had said, very quietly, from behind Lex, "*Hammer. He's right.*"

And Lex had turned round.

"*You've got to hold the top*," said Wren. With her hand on her stomach. Six and a half months gone. "*The circle. The camp. Your father.*" Her voice didn't shake. "*Someone has to be here when they come up. You said so. On the slope. The night before the deep. You said someone has to be the light we're coming home to.*"

She looked at Antho.

"*This time it's you*," she said to Lex. "*This time you're the window.*"

---

So it was the three of them.

Down the long passage. Past the lanterns. Six hundred and seven lit, five hundred and six dark. Into the bell-shaped cavern. To the niche. To the three lamps. Burning.

To the door. On the latch. A thin gold crack down the middle of the black glass.

Antho knocked.

*Kell*, he thought. *It's me again.*

*[...Vey...]*

Faint. Far. Dry. Tired.

*[...you're early...]*

*We're coming down. To take it home*.

The Warden was quiet for a long moment.

*[...about time...]* said Isaura Kell. *[...I've told it the one about the miller's cat four hundred times. It knows the cat's name. It knows the cat's* boots *by name.]*

---

The deep was quiet.

Antho noticed it the moment they stepped through. Different. From the last time. From the moths and the glitter and the tide of grey-gold rising out of the dark toward Lex's light.

There were no moths.

None. Not one. Not a glitter. Not a flicker. Just Shade's dark. Soft. Starlit. Endless. The way it had been after the Heart. Before.

"*They've gone up*," said Seraphine. Very quietly. With her hand out. Listening. "*All of them. Up the shafts. Back to her.*" She frowned. "*She's not sending them down anymore. She doesn't need to.*"

"*Why*?" said Pim.

Seraphine looked at him.

"*Because she knows where it's going*," she said. "*She'll be waiting at the top.*"

---

Kell was in the gold room.

The little one had kept it. The fold. The warm place. Round a clay lamp, the copy, the one from the Ashbourne market that Seraphine had given Seren on the way up, that Seren had left with Kell. Lit. On the warm dark. With Kell sitting beside it.

Very straight.

She'd healed. Mostly. The wound in her side, where the Mothwing's knife had gone in, was a scar now. Pink. Puckered. Under her ruined robes. The little one had held the room round it for months, the way it held the room round everything, and the wound had closed into it.

She was thinner. Greyer. Her short hair had grown out a little, and was white. All of it. Her eyes were clear. Not dim. Not half-dim. Pale and steady and very, very tired.

She looked up as they came in.

"*Vey*," said Isaura Kell. "*Scholar.*" Her pale eyes went to Pim. And stopped. And something moved in her thin face. "*And who's this?*"

"*Pim*," said Antho. "*He's mine. One of my students. He came because he wanted to see it.*"

Kell looked at Pim for a long time.

Pim looked back.

"*The quiet one*," said Kell at last. "*It's told me about you. The boy with the room inside.*" Her mouth moved. Sideways. "*It says you're its favorite. After the lamp-lady. And the cat.*"

Pim went very red.

---

*[...brother...]*

All round them. In the warm dark. Close. So close. Antho had never heard it so close. Not even in the gold room, the first time. It was pressed round them on every side, soft and enormous, the way a crowd presses round a fire on a cold night.

*[...brother you came...and the glass eyes...and the QUIET one...]*

Pim was smiling.

Antho had seen Pim smile three times in three years. He was smiling now. Standing in the gold room at the bottom of the world with the dark pressing round him, soft and warm and huge, and his thin face lit up like a lamp.

"*Hello*," said Pim. Out loud. To the dark.

*[...hello...]* said the little one. *[...you made the cups...]*

"*Me and Master Lex*."

*[...the cups were like being carried...]*

"*I know*," said Pim. "*That's why I made them.*"

---

They went at once.

There was no time. Lantern Night was forty days off. The little one was three hundred miles from where it needed to be, under the world, and it was the size of a country, and it didn't know the way.

So Antho showed it.

He didn't know the way either. Not properly. He'd never been under the world between the Hollowmaw and Vhessa. Nobody had. There was no map.

But he had the lamp.

He held it up. The clay one. His mother's mother's. Lit. In his hand. And he turned until he was facing south and east. The way his father had told him. The way the edges ran. The way the deep sloped, under the world, from the oldest crack to the first wound.

And he walked.

---

It followed.

He felt it. Behind him. Around him. Above and below. The whole vast soft dark of it, folded round the deep, beginning, very slowly, very carefully, to *move*. Not all at once. Not fast. A little at a time. The way a river moves when the ice starts to break. The way a sleeping animal shifts in the night.

Toward the lamp.

Toward him.

*[...this way...]* it said. Wondering. *[...this way...where the light goes...]*

*This way*, thought Antho. *Slowly. Don't pull. Don't hurry. Just follow.*

*[...I'm following...]*

*I know*.

*[...I've never followed before...]*

*[...I've only ever been the place things come through...]*

*[...nobody ever led me anywhere...]*

---

It took thirty-four days.

He'd remember them, afterward, as one long walk. Through the dark. Under Shade's stars. With his mother's lamp held up in front of him and his wife on one side and a boy on the other and a gaunt white-haired woman behind, very straight, walking.

And behind her, following, folded, soft, enormous, the little one.

They slept when they were tired. On the warm dark. In the gold room, which came with them, which the little one carried the way a snail carries its shell. They ate what Seraphine had brought, and when that ran out, what the little one gave them, which was nothing, which was room, which somehow, down there, was enough.

And they talked.

---

Kell talked most.

That surprised him. He'd never heard Isaura Kell say more than ten words together, except at the Heart, when she'd told Seraphine about Dorran. She'd been High Warden of the Order of the Lattice. She'd given orders. She didn't *talk*.

She talked now.

Walking. In the dark. With her hands clasped behind her back. Very straight. She told them about the Spire. About the dormitory, when she was a cadet, at nine, the youngest they'd ever taken. About the older cadets blowing out the candle and whispering *never leave a light on, or the Nine will come*.

"*I believed them*," said Kell. "*Of course I did. I was nine. I lay awake every night for a year, terrified of lanterns.*" Her mouth moved sideways. "*And then I grew up and became High Warden and sent people to burn women who weren't afraid of the dark.*"

She told them about Dorran Vask. The first time she met him. A big frightened boy of nineteen, in the Kindling hall at the Spire, with a brand on his arm that she'd put there herself. *You'll stand at the Stones*, she'd told him. *In the west. On Kindling-day. You'll put your hand on their shoulders. And push down.*

"*He cried*," said Kell. "*When I branded him. He tried not to. He was so big. He cried like a child.*"

She was quiet for a long time.

"*I didn't*," she said. "*I watched him cry and I didn't feel anything at all.*"

---

She told them about the Rift.

Not all of it. Antho knew most of it. Lex had told him. Elric unwriting his own men in the Umbral Rift, at the edge of the world. Kell found forty-one percent gone, faded, thin, half a woman. Dorran picking her up.

"*He said*," said Kell, "*I've got you, High Warden.*" She stopped walking. In the dark. Very straight. With her hands clasped behind her back. "*He said, I've carried heavier.*"

Nobody said anything.

"*He was lying*," said Isaura Kell. "*I was the heaviest thing he ever carried. I could feel it in his back. He was shaking the whole way.*"

She started walking again.

"*Your brother got that back for me*," she said. Not looking at Antho. "*In the drowned city. After she took it. He read it to me. Out of the Ledger. And then he said the rest. The bit that wasn't written down.*" Her voice was very steady. "*I'd like to keep it, this time. I'd like*—"

She stopped again.

"*Vey*," she said.

"*Yes*."

"*When this is done*," said Kell. "*When it's up. Whatever happens.*" She turned round. In the dark. And looked at him. With her pale eyes. "*Take my name out of the Ledger.*"

Antho stared at her.

"*It's still in there*," said Kell. "*Isn't it. Your brother carries it. Ysmir's book. With the Unnamed. Two names left. Mine and Elric's.*"

"*Yours came back. At the Heart.*"

"*It came back. It's still written there.*" She looked at her hands. "*Faded. Pale. The way they go when the person comes back. But it's there. In a book for people who've been forgotten.*" Her mouth twisted. "*I don't want to be in a book for people who've been forgotten.*"

"*Where, then*?"

Isaura Kell was quiet for a long time.

"*Somewhere people read*," she said at last. "*Somewhere ordinary. In a list. With other people. Not the Unnamed. Not the Wardens. Just—people.*" She looked at Seraphine. "*You're writing names. Your father-in-law. In his grey book. The ones from the breach.*"

Seraphine nodded. Slowly.

"*Put me in there*," said Kell. "*At the end. After the Vhessans. When you're done.*" Her voice didn't change. "*Isaura Kell. Of Highcrest. She was High Warden. She signed forty-one warrants and remembered every name.*" A pause. "*She was carried once. By a man she'd branded. And she sat with a frightened thing in the dark until it got home.*"

She looked at Antho.

"*That's all*," she said. "*That's enough.*"

---

"*You're not going to die*," said Seraphine.

Kell looked at her.

"*You're talking like you're going to die*," said Seraphine. Her voice was shaking. "*You're asking us to put you in a book. At the end. After the dead.*" She stopped walking. "*You're not. You're going to come up. With it. On Lantern Night. Into the circle. With all of us.*"

Isaura Kell looked at her for a long time.

"*Scholar*," she said. Very gently. "*I've been High Warden of the Order of the Lattice. I've stood in front of kings. I've sat in council with Aurelian's ghost. I've been unwritten and eaten and made into a door.*" She looked at the dark. At the little one, following. "*I know what's waiting at the top. I know what she is. I know what she'll do.*"

"*Then*—"

"*Then I know someone will have to stand in front of it*," said Kell. "*With a stick. When she comes for it. When it's halfway up and can't go back and can't go forward.*" She looked at Seraphine. "*And I've had a lot of practice.*"

---

Pim talked to the little one.

All the time. In his own way. Which was mostly not talking. Walking beside Antho with one hand held out to the side, palm open, toward the dark. As if he were holding a hand. As if someone were walking beside him, very big, very soft, and he were holding its hand to show it the way.

Sometimes he'd stop. And cup his hands. Round nothing. And be silent.

And the little one would make a sound. Not a word. A ripple. Like a cat purring. Like a breath let out.

"*It likes that*," said Pim. Once. When Antho asked. "*It says it's like being little again. Before the Nine. When it was a pool. When nobody stopped.*" He looked at his hands. "*It says when I make a room round it, it feels the size it's supposed to be.*"

"*What size is that*?"

Pim thought about it for a long time.

"*The size of a person*," he said.

---

On the twentieth day, the bell rang.

They were in the middle of the dark. Halfway. Nowhere. With nothing round them but Shade's stars and the little one following.

And Antho felt something in the warm dark beside him change. Shift. Fold. The way it had folded in the gold room, the first time, when his mother said *show them where Mal is*.

The middle came.

Close. Very close. Right beside them, in the dark, to the left of the path, where nothing had been. Two little lights. Pale. Silver-gold. Like very old candles. Curled together. Side by side. Asleep.

One small. With the shape of a straw hat.

One tall. With something in its hands.

The little one had brought them. It hadn't been asked. It just did it. Folded the middle of itself up to the path and laid it there, in the dark, beside them. The way a child brings you something it's proud of.

*[...look...]* it said. *[...Mal and his brother...they're coming too...]*

And very faintly, so faint Antho had to stop walking and hold his breath, from the two little lights, singing.

*Little light, little light, burning low*.

In tune.

---

He didn't have the bell. Lex had it. Three hundred miles up. On a cord round his neck. On the black rubble of Duskhold.

But Antho heard it anyway.

Far off. Very faint. Coming down through the world. Through the circle. Through the breach. Through the dark.

*Ting*.

In tune. Clear. Like a struck glass.

And then, after it, so faint he almost didn't hear it at all—

*Ting*.

Flat.

Slightly off. A little crooked. Like a bell with a bent clapper.

The way it had rung for nine hundred years.

---

Kell had stopped walking.

She was standing very still. In the dark. With her hands at her sides. Looking at the two little lights. At the small one. With the straw hat.

"*That's the Listener*," she said. Very quietly. "*Isn't it. The old one. The one who sat with it.*"

"*Malachi*," said Antho.

"*Malachi*." Kell didn't move. "*I met him. At the Heart. Before. When I was its Warden. When I had no face.*" Her voice was very strange. "*He looked at me. With those eyes. The shifting ones. And he said*—"

She stopped.

"*What did he say*?" said Seraphine.

Isaura Kell looked at the little light with the straw hat for a long time.

"*He said, oh, my dear*," she said. "*You look so tired. Would you like to sit down?*"

She was crying. Antho had never seen her cry. Not at the Heart. Not in the drowned city. Not when Lex read her name from the Ledger.

She was crying now. Standing very straight in the dark with her hands at her sides and the tears running down her thin grey face.

"*Nobody had asked me that*," said Isaura Kell. "*Not in fifty years. Would you like to sit down.*"

She sat down.

On the warm dark. Beside the two little lights. Very slowly. Like an old woman lowering herself onto a bench in a churchyard.

And sat there. For a long time. With her hands in her lap. Beside Malachi. Not saying anything.

The little one held the room round all three of them. Very gently. The way you'd hold a cup you'd been told was precious.

---

Antho and Seraphine slept apart from the others that night.

A little way off. In the dark. Not far. Just far enough. On the warm floor of the little one, with the clay lamp between them and their hands touching across it.

"*She's going to die*," said Seraphine. Very quietly. "*Isn't she.*"

Antho didn't answer.

"*She knows it*," said Seraphine. "*She's been knowing it since we found her. Since the gold room. Since she said, I'll stay with it.*" Her voice cracked. "*She's been getting ready. All this time. Telling us things. Telling us what to write.*"

"*I know*."

"*Can we stop it*?"

He looked at her. Across the lamp. At her face, in the gold light. Her spectacles off. Her eyes very wet.

"*I don't know*," he said honestly. "*I don't know what's going to happen. Nobody does. Not Lex. Not Mam. Not Kell.*" He swallowed. "*I know she's going to stand in front of it. When it's halfway up. With a stick. Because that's who she is now.*"

"*That's who she chose to be*."

"*That's who she chose to be*," Antho agreed. "*And you can't stop someone being who they chose. You can only stand next to them.*"

---

Seraphine was quiet for a long time.

"*Antho*," she said.

"*Mm*."

"*Four hundred and twelve*," said his wife.

He looked at her.

"*Reasons*," said Seraphine. "*The list. From the wedding. I only read you the first one.*" She took something out of her coat. The little leather notebook. The size of her palm. Battered now. Its corners soft. "*I've been adding to it.*"

"*How many*?"

"*Five hundred and three*."

He laughed. He couldn't help it. In the dark. At the bottom of the world.

"*Read me one*," he said.

She opened the notebook. In the light of the clay lamp. And turned the pages. Past four hundred and twelve. Past the wedding. Past the moths, and the Delve in the cold-room, and the deep, and the tear, and the Keep.

To the last one.

"*Five hundred and three*," read Seraphine Ilvane. "*He held the door. With the little one screaming and his Clarity at thirty-eight and the old thing in his head telling him to squeeze. And it said, stay. And he listened.*"

She closed the notebook.

"*That's the best one*," she said. "*I think. So far.*"

---

He didn't say anything.

He reached across the lamp. And took the notebook out of her hand. And opened it. To a blank page. At the back. And took the pencil from behind her ear.

And wrote. In the gold light. In his own hand, which wasn't as neat as hers, which was cramped and slanting from eight years of writing in a cave by the light of a single candle.

*Number one*, he wrote. *She asked how long. Not what's wrong. How long. She'd counted.*

He gave it back to her.

She read it. And put her hand over her mouth.

"*That's mine*," said Antho Vey. "*My list. Starting now.*" He lay back on the warm dark. "*I'm behind. I'll be behind for the rest of my life.*"

"*You're very good at counting*," said Seraphine. Wetly.

"*I'm very good at counting*," he agreed.

---

On the thirty-fourth day, the dark changed.

Antho felt it first. Through the lamp. Through his feet. The warm dark under him, that had been flat and endless for a month, began to *slope*. Up. Gently. Like the floor of a valley rising toward a pass.

And the stars overhead—

Fewer. Closer. Shade's stars, that had been so far, were coming down. Or he was going up. The dark between them getting thinner. Less deep.

"*We're close*," said Seraphine. With her hand out. "*Antho. We're close. I can hear it. Above. A room. A big one.*" Her face was lit. "*The circle. I can hear the circle.*"

And then he saw it.

Far ahead. Up. At the top of the long slope of dark. A line. Thin. Gold.

Not a crack in a door. Not lamp-light.

*Warm* light. Coming *down*. Through something. Like light through a gap in a floor. Like sunlight through the cracks in a barn.

The scar.

The bottom of the breach. Under Duskhold. Where his father had knelt twenty-one years ago and pulled. Where the three of them had knelt five years ago and mended.

Where his brother had stood on the black rubble fifty-two days ago and said *come home*.

---

The little one stopped.

All of it. All at once. The whole vast soft enormous fold of it, behind them, round them, under them. Stopped. At the bottom of the slope. Under the scar. Under the circle.

And went very still.

*[...is that it...]*

Very small. Very quiet. Very frightened.

*[...is that the room...]*

"*That's it*," said Antho.

*[...it's so close...]*

*[...I can feel it...the circle...my name...a hundred miles of it...]*

*[...it's like...]*

It didn't have the word. It never had the word.

*[...brother...]*

*[...brother, what if they don't like me...]*

---

Antho sat down.

On the warm dark. At the bottom of the slope. Under the thin gold line of the scar. With the clay lamp in his lap.

Seraphine sat beside him. Pim on his other side. Kell a little way off, very straight, with her hands on her knees.

"*They made you a room*," said Antho. To the dark. To the little one. "*All of them. Thousands. With spades and hammers and their bare hands. For fifty-one days. They dug it and baked it and washed it and tapped on the walls so it would know someone was coming.*" He looked up at the gold line. "*They don't even know you. Most of them. They've never heard you. They just heard my brother say* something's coming home *and they came.*"

The little one was quiet.

"*They made a room for you*," said Antho, "*before you got there. That's what you do for someone you're going to love. Before you've met them.*" He swallowed. "*That's what my mother did for me. And my brother. And Wren's doing it now. For hers.*"

*[...]*

"*You don't have to worry about whether they'll like you*," said Antho Vey. "*They already do. They made you a room.*"

---

And from far above, through the scar, through the breach, through three hundred miles of a word written in the ground—

A sound.

Hammering.

One anvil. Slow. Steady. In the dark.

Like a heart.

Like a knock on a door.

*I'm here. I'm home. I've been waiting*.

---

*[...that's the smith...]* said the little one. Wonderingly.

*[...he's knocking...]*

*[...he's knocking for ME...]*

And Antho, sitting at the bottom of the world under the first wound with his mother's lamp in his lap and his wife's hand in his, laughed. Out loud. He couldn't help it.

"*He never stops*," he said. "*He never stops hammering. Not since he was eleven.*"

*[...why...]*

Antho looked up at the thin gold line. At the light coming down through the scar. At the place where, in six days, on Lantern Night, he'd stand with his hands on a door his father tore, and ask.

"*Because that's how he says I love you*," said Antho Vey.

The little one was quiet for a long time.

And then, all round them, soft and enormous and young, it did the thing Antho had felt once before, in the deep, when his mother said *it's a choice*.

It laughed.

---

And far above, on the black rubble of Duskhold, at the head of a trench three hundred and fourteen miles long, a man with a grey-gold handprint on his face stopped hammering.

And put down the hammer.

And put his hand flat on the anvil of black Gloam-stone. And felt it. Through the stone. Through the rubble. Through the scar. Through the bottom of the breach.

Something enormous. Soft. Young.

*Laughing*.

And Lex Vey sat down very suddenly on the rubble beside his anvil. In the dark. Alone. With his face in his hands.

And on the horizon, all round him, in every direction, in a ring a hundred and fifty miles across, outside the circle, glittering grey-gold under the stars like frost on a window—

The moths were waiting.
