# The Council of Lanterns

They held it in the long hall of the Ashbourne Forge, because it was the only room in Caldor big enough.

Twenty hearths. Twenty anvils. A great brick chimney at each end. Lex had swept it himself that morning, with Tilda and the students, and pushed the anvils back against the walls, and carried in every bench and stool and barrel in the Forge, and set them round the great hearth at the north end in a ring.

It wasn't enough.

They came all day. From every road. On horses and dragons and carts and on foot. By noon the forge yard was full. By mid-afternoon the street outside was full. By dusk there were people sitting on the woodpile and the roof of the lean-to and the rim of the warm spring, and Quill the raven had been driven off his chimney by a Sunreach hawk and was sulking on the weathervane of the chapel, shrieking abuse.

And when the sun went down, Lex lit the great hearth.

And everyone came in.

---

He looked round at them from the anvil block where he was sitting. And couldn't breathe for a moment.

The Queen of Caldor. In a borrowed brown dress from Mother Orla's niece, too short at the wrists. With her silver circlet on again. Sitting on a barrel at the front of the ring with her back very straight.

Dame Elowen Dunmore behind her. Standing. Oathkeeper point-down on the floor.

Commander Roole. Arm in a sling. Hatchet face grey and set. Fenn beside him.

Queen Ishara of the Sunreach. She'd come in at noon on a golden horse with forty riders and Zahir on her shoulder and three old women in red behind her who she said were Ashari, fire-women of the deep desert, who remembered things about fire that nobody in Caldor ever knew. They sat behind her now on a bench. Very old. Very still. Their faces brown and creased as walnuts. Watching the great hearth with black eyes that didn't blink.

Teo Brask. Seventeen now. Taller. Still dripping. Who'd come in off the coast road at three in the afternoon on foot, soaking wet in a clear blue sky, and said *the sea told me to come* and then *sorry about the floor*.

Benno, the standard-bearer of the Ashen Banners. Eighteen. With two hundred swords behind him in the street and Teodra Hale's greatsword on his back and his young face set like stone.

The twins. Kestrel. Tilda. Hedda. Brannoc. Kit, the Coldwater boy, at the very back, very white.

His father. In the wheeled chair. Wrapped in a blanket. With his grey book on his knee.

His mother. Beside his father. On a stool. With the clay lamp in her lap.

Seraphine, with her notebook. Antho, with the Lantern. Pim, under a bench, because there wasn't a table. Ivy Moss, on a stool by the hearth, with her crutch across her knees.

And Wren.

Beside him. On the anvil block. With her hand on his knee.

---

"*Well*," said Brannoc. Into the silence. "*Somebody'd better start. Or we'll be here till Lantern Night.*"

A few people laughed.

Lex didn't. He looked at the faces round the ring. All of them. Looking at him. Waiting.

He'd never been good at this. Antho was good at this. Arion was good at this. The Queen was very good at this. He was a smith. He thought with his hands. When he had to talk to a room he always said the wrong thing first, and then the right thing, and then something embarrassing.

*[Just say it*,] said Ember. *[Like you'd say it to Brannoc. Over the anvil.]*

He stood up.

"*There's three things*," said Lex.

---

He held up one finger.

"*The little one*," he said. "*The Well. It's coming up. Lantern Night. The end of the year. We can't stop it. Nobody can. It's what it is. It goes toward the light.*" He looked round. "*It's the size of a country. It can't come through any door. So we have to make it a place to come up into. A room. Big enough. Somewhere she can't get at it.*"

He held up a second finger.

"*Her*," he said. "*Maelis. The Moth Queen. She's taken Highcrest. She's dimmed half of it. She's got thousands of moths and hundreds of her own people and Corwin Hask with wings, and she's Tier V, and she's sitting in my father's room at the top of the Spire reading two hundred years of his books.*" His voice didn't shake. He was proud of that. "*She wants our blood. Mam's. Antho's. Mine. To open the Veil and let her brothers and sisters through. And she wants the little one, when it comes up. All of it. To drink.*"

He held up a third finger.

"*And them*," said Lex. "*The Dimmed. Everyone she's touched. The Keepers at Greyfall. Half of Highcrest. Every shepherd and woodcutter in the Greyspine who had a light in the window.*" He looked at Elowen. "*Hollis Penn. Standing on the Spire steps in his apron holding up his lantern.*"

The long hall was very quiet.

"*Three things*," said Lex Vey. "*Make it a room. Stop her. Bring them back.*" He put his hand down. "*I don't know how to do any of them. That's why you're all here.*"

He sat down.

---

Seraphine stood up.

She had a map. Of course she did. A big one. Hand-drawn. The whole of Caldor and the Sunreach and the Saltreach and the Greyspine and the Blightmarch, from the edge of the sea to the edge of the Veil. She and Antho had been up all night making it. She pinned it to the great brick chimney-breast over the hearth with four horseshoe nails that Tilda handed her one by one.

It had pins in it. Black ones and red ones. Fifty-three black. Twenty-nine red.

And one gold one.

At the far eastern edge of the map. Past the Blightmarch. Past Duskhold. In a place that was drawn in grey, with nothing in it. No villages. No roads. No rivers. Just grey, and the word *VHESSA* in Seraphine's small neat hand, and a ring drawn round it in red.

---

"*It needs room*," said Seraphine. "*Lex is right. A lot of room. More than you'd think.*"

She tapped the map.

"*It's the size of a country. A small one. I've measured it. As well as I can. By listening.*" She looked round. "*When it comes up, it'll need somewhere it can unfold. Somewhere with nothing in the way. No villages. No farms. No forests. No rivers it would drown, no towns it would swallow. Nothing alive that it would*—" She stopped. "*That it would make room* in. *By accident.*"

"*There's nowhere in Caldor like that*," said Duke Harlowe's daughter. From the back. Very young. Very frightened. "*Is there? Everywhere's got something in it.*"

"*There's one place*," said Seraphine.

She put her finger on the gold pin.

"*The Blightmarch*," she said. "*Eastern Vhessa. Past Duskhold. Where the Gloam was.*"

---

The hall went very still.

Everyone knew the Blightmarch. Everyone in Caldor had grown up on stories of it. The eastern march, eaten. Twenty years of Gloam. Grey grass. Silent birds. Villages gone quiet. Duskhold, the black citadel, grown out of Gloam-stone in the middle of it like a tumor. Morvane's fortress. The Quiet, standing in rows.

The Gloam was gone now. Five years gone. Since the brothers closed the breach. But nothing had come back. Not really. Not in the deep east. The land was dead. Grey. Empty. A wasteland a hundred miles across where nothing grew and nobody went, and the wind blew grey dust off the hills, and the ruins of Duskhold stood in the middle of it, black and cracked and falling, like the bones of something enormous that had died there.

"*There's nothing there*," said Seraphine quietly. "*Nothing alive. Nothing that would be hurt. It's been dead for twenty years.*" She looked at the map. "*It's the only place in the world big enough and empty enough.*"

"*And it's got a door*," said Antho.

Everyone looked at him.

He was sitting very still. With the Lantern in his lap. Looking at the map. At the gold pin.

"*The Vhessa breach*," he said. "*Where the Gloam came through. The first breach. The oldest wound in the net.*" He looked at his father. "*It goes all the way down. To the deep. We closed it, five years ago. The three of us. With blood.*" His voice was very quiet. "*But closed isn't gone. It's still there. Like a scar. Like a door that's been shut.*"

"*A door can be opened*," said Kestrel.

"*A door can be opened*," Antho agreed. "*If someone asks it right.*"

---

"*I tore it*," said Arion.

The hall turned.

He was sitting in his wheeled chair beside the hearth. With his blanket and his book and his white head. Looking at the map. At the grey place. At the red ring round it.

His face was very calm.

"*Twenty years ago*," said Arion Vey. "*Twenty-one. At the Great Stone in Vhessa. With all my Authority and a Rewrite that I'd spent fifty years designing. I tried to break every Ceiling in the world at once. In one stroke.*" He looked at his hands. "*It didn't work. It tore. And the Gloam came through the tear, and ate the east, and took Morvane, and took twenty years of everyone's lives.*"

Nobody said anything.

"*I know that breach*," said Arion. "*Better than anyone alive. I made it. I know its edges. Every one. I know where it's thin and where it's thick and where it was stitched shut five years ago by my sons with their own blood.*" He looked at Antho. "*If you're going to open it again, you'll need someone who knows where the edges are.*"

"*Father*—" said Lex.

"*I'm eighty-three*," said Arion. "*I think. The book says so.*" He tapped the grey book on his knee. "*I can't walk. I can barely hold a cup. I have a season left. Perhaps less.*" He looked round the hall. At all of them. "*I can still remember. That's all I've got left. I'd like to use it for something.*"

His mother put her hand on his arm.

Arion looked down at it. Her square strong freckled hand on his thin old arm.

"*I tore it trying to free everyone at once*," he said. More quietly. "*It would be nice to help open it for something that needs to come home.*"

---

"*So that's the room*," said Lysandra. "*The Blightmarch. Through the old breach.*" She looked at Lex. "*How do you make a room the size of a country, smith?*"

"*I don't know yet*."

"*That's not very reassuring*."

"*I know*." He looked at the map. "*It's a mould. That's all I know. You make the empty shape first. In the ground. And then you pour.*" He rubbed his face. The handprint glittered under his fingers. "*I've made moulds. I've never made one bigger than a cartwheel. Brannoc made one for a church bell once.*"

"*Nearly killed me*," said Brannoc. From the bench. "*Three months. Lost a toe.*"

"*The Blightmarch is a hundred miles across*."

"*So it'll take longer*," said Brannoc Holt. Unperturbed. "*And you'll lose more toes.*" He scratched his grey beard. "*But it's just a mould, lad. A big one. Same as any other. You find the shape. You pack the sand. You make the empty bit just so, and you let it set.*" He shrugged his enormous shoulders. "*I'll help. So will every smith in this hall. So will every student. You don't make a bell mould alone.*"

He looked round. At the students. At Tilda with his hammer. At the forty young faces round the walls of the long hall, lit gold by the great hearth.

"*We're the Ashbourne Forge*," said Brannoc. "*It's what we do.*"

---

"*Fire*," said a voice.

Old. Cracked. Dry as sand. In an accent Lex didn't know.

Everyone turned.

One of the three old women behind Queen Ishara had stood up. The smallest. The oldest. She was tiny. Bent nearly double. In a robe of faded red. With her face as brown and creased as a walnut and a pair of black eyes as bright as a crow's.

She was looking at Lex.

"*You need fire*," she said. "*To set a mould. You need to bake the sand. Hard. Hot. So it holds the shape.*" Her black eyes didn't blink. "*Very hot. A long time. Over a hundred miles.*"

"*Yes*," said Lex. Slowly. "*Yes. I do.*"

"*We have fire*," said the old woman. "*The Sunwell. The grieving one. Under the glass.*" She smiled. Toothless. Fierce. "*It burned its own people once. To kill a hungry thing.*" Her black eyes glittered. "*It would very much like to help make a home for one.*"

Queen Ishara, sitting in front of her, didn't turn round. But Lex saw her mouth twitch.

"*This is Grandmother Asha*," said Ishara. "*She is three hundred years old. Or she says she is. She was a fire-woman before the Treaty of Scald, when the Order put a Ceiling on the Sunreach.*" She looked at Lex. "*She has been waiting two hundred years for someone to ask her for fire.*"

"*Two hundred and eleven*," said Grandmother Asha. "*But who's counting.*"

Antho laughed. Out loud. He couldn't help it.

---

"*And water*," said Teo.

He was standing up. Dripping. Red to the ears. With everyone looking at him.

"*For the quench*," he said. "*When the mould's set. When the—when the thing comes up.*" He swallowed. "*The Undertow says it'll help. The sea. It says it remembers the seventh. From before. When it was little. It says the seventh used to make room for the tide to come in.*" He went redder. "*It says it misses it.*"

"*The sea misses it*," said Lex.

"*The sea's very sentimental*," said Teo. "*You wouldn't think so. But it is.*"

---

"*That's the room*," said the Queen.

She was standing now. At the front of the ring. In her too-short borrowed dress, with her circlet on. Looking at the map.

"*The Blightmarch. A mould. Ashbourne's smiths and Sunreach fire and the Undertow's quench. The old breach for a door.*" She turned. "*Lantern Night.*"

"*Lantern Night*," said Lex.

"*That's the first thing*." Lysandra looked round the hall. "*Now the second. Her.*"

Nobody said anything.

"*She's Tier V*," said Roole. Grimly. From his bench. "*She took Highcrest in one night. She dimmed four Keepers at her door with a touch each. She's got Hask, and he's eighty-six, and he flies.*" He shook his head. "*I've got eleven hundred Keepers, Majesty. Six hundred are in the Greyspine and the west trying to keep shepherds from walking toward the moths. Three hundred were in Highcrest.*" His jaw tightened. "*Were.*"

"*Swords don't work*," said Elowen. "*On the Mothwing. They come apart into dust and pile up again. You need wind. Or fire. Or*—" She looked at Lex. "*Whatever he does. The black thing.*"

"*Black heat*," said Lex.

"*Can you teach it*?"

He looked at her.

He'd never thought of that. He'd never thought of it as a thing you could *teach*. It was just a thing he'd done. In the deep. Because Kestrel asked him *what's fire that isn't light* and Brannoc's voice came up out of his hands.

"*Yes*," said Kestrel. Before he could answer. "*He can. I learned it in one go. Anyone who can talk to fire can learn it. You just have to ask it to hide.*"

"*How many people in Caldor can talk to fire*?"

There was a pause.

"*Forty*," said Tilda Crane. From the wall. Very quietly. With Brannoc's hammer in her hands. "*In this hall. Forty of us. Students. We all hear it. Master Lex taught us.*" She swallowed. "*And there's other forges now. Fifteen. Every one with a Flame-Listener.*"

"*And the Ashari*," said Grandmother Asha. "*There are two hundred of us. Old women. In the deep desert. We've been doing black heat since before your Founders were born.*" She sniffed. "*We just call it* sand.*"*

---

"*It's not enough*," said Antho.

Everyone looked at him.

He was sitting very still. With the Lantern in his lap. And his clear grey eyes on the map.

"*Black heat burns them*," he said. "*If they come close. If they come* looking. *But they won't come close to black heat. Not after the first time. They'll learn. She'll teach them.*" He looked up. "*She'll come for the light. That's what she is. She goes toward the light.*"

"*So we put the lights out*," said Roole.

"*On Lantern Night*?"

The hall went very still.

---

Antho stood up.

"*That's why she said Lantern Night*," said his brother. Very quietly. "*Isn't it. That's why the end of the year.*" He looked round. "*Not because that's when the little one comes up. Because that's when every window in Caldor has a lantern in it.*"

Nobody spoke.

"*Every year*," said Antho, "*on Lantern Night, everyone lights a lamp. For the lost. For someone who didn't come home. Every house. Every window. Every grave.*" His voice was very flat. "*It's the night the whole world leaves a light on.*"

He looked at his mother.

"*It's her feast*," said Antho Vey. "*Isn't it, Mam. It's the night she's been waiting nine hundred years for.*"

His mother didn't answer.

She didn't need to.

---

The long hall was silent for a long time.

The great hearth crackled. Outside, in the street, someone's horse stamped. On the chapel weathervane, Quill shrieked something about Sunreach hawks that Lex was fairly sure wasn't repeatable.

And then Ivy Moss said, from her stool by the hearth, very small: "*Not all of them.*"

---

Everyone turned.

She went red. All the way up into her pale hair. She gripped her crutch. But she didn't stop.

"*Not all the lights*," said Ivy Moss. "*She can't drink all of them. Some of them, she can't.*" She looked at Seren. "*The lamp-lady's. The clay one. The moths couldn't drink it. In the deep. And Master Antho's six. In the drowned city. They couldn't drink those either.*"

"*Because they were given*," said Seraphine slowly. "*Not kept. Not waiting. The Harrow lamp's for someone else. And Antho's six were for a promise that was already kept.*"

"*Yes*." Ivy Moss nodded. Hard. "*She drinks the waiting ones. The lights left on for someone who's not home. The promises.*" She swallowed. "*That's what a moth wants. A light that's* waiting.*"* She looked at the map. "*Lantern Night's all waiting. Every lantern's waiting for someone. That's why it's a feast.*"

"*So*—" said Lysandra.

"*So change them*," said Ivy Moss.

---

Nobody understood. Not at first. Lex didn't. He saw Seraphine's face change. Saw her pencil stop. Saw her look at Ivy with her eyes going very wide.

And then he saw his mother's face.

Seren Vey was looking at the small pale girl on the stool by the hearth. With the clay lamp in her lap. And her face was doing the thing it had done in the gold room at the bottom of the world, when Lex said *a mould*.

"*Oh*," said Seren. Softly. "*Oh, clever girl.*"

"*What*?" said Wren.

"*Don't leave the lights on*," said Seren Vey. "*Give them away.*"

---

She stood up.

With the lamp. In the middle of the long hall of the Ashbourne Forge. Small. Fierce. Freckled. Holding it up. So everyone could see it. Little. Brown. Clay. With a chip in the spout.

"*My mother made this*," she said. "*For a man she didn't trust. Who was going to take her daughter away from her. Who was a Warden of the Order that hunted women like her.*" She looked at Arion. "*She'd never left a light on in forty years. And she made this, and lit it, and put it in the window. For him.*"

She turned the lamp. So the light fell across the ring of faces.

"*Not waiting for him*," said Seren. "*He was already there. Already coming up the path. She didn't light it hoping he'd come. She lit it because he* had.*"* She looked round. "*That's not a promise. That's a gift. You can't drink a gift. It's already gone. It's already somebody else's.*"

"*On Lantern Night*," said Seraphine. Her pencil was flying. "*Everyone lights a lantern. Waiting. For the lost. For the dead. For someone who isn't home.*"

"*And this year*," said Seren Vey, "*they don't wait.*"

---

"*They bring them*," said Lex.

He was on his feet. He didn't remember standing. The idea was coming up out of his hands the way they always did. Hot. Fast. Like iron coming up out of a fire.

"*To the Blightmarch*," he said. "*All of them. Every lantern. Every person who can carry one. They bring them to the room. To the mould. To the place where the little one's coming up.*" He looked at the map. "*And they don't leave them on for someone. They* give *them. To it. To the thing that's coming home.*"

"*A light given*," said Antho. Slowly. "*To the seventh. To the room.*"

"*Every lantern in Caldor*," said Lex. "*Set down. Round the edge of the mould. A ring of them. Ten thousand. A hundred thousand. Not waiting. Given. So when it comes up, it comes up into light that's already its own.*" He looked at his mother. "*Light she can't drink.*"

"*And she'll come for it anyway*," said Kestrel. "*Because she can't help it. Because it's the brightest thing in the world.*"

"*And she'll find black heat round every one*," said Grandmother Asha. Her toothless grin was very wide. "*Sand. Two hundred old women and forty young smiths. Waiting in the dark round the edge of the light.*" She cackled. "*Oh, I like that. I like that very much.*"

---

The hall erupted.

Not in shouting. In *talking*. All at once. Everyone. Brannoc and Hedda arguing about sand. Teo and Seraphine with their heads together over the map. Roole and Elowen and Benno at the back, talking about where to put two hundred swords and eleven hundred Keepers on a dead plain a hundred miles across. Ishara saying something sharp and fast in Sunreach to her three old women, who were all cackling now. The twins already talking about illusions. *Lanterns, Ilse. Lanterns made of nothing. Ten thousand. To make her think there's light where there's sand.*

Lex stood in the middle of it and couldn't stop smiling.

*[That was Ivy*,] said Ember. Very quietly. In his hearth. *[That whole thing. The fourteen-year-old with the crutch. She did that.]*

*I know*.

*[Your mother finished it. But Ivy started it.]*

*I know*, thought Lex. *I know.* He looked at the small pale girl on her stool by the hearth, red to the ears, gripping her crutch, with half the long hall now turning to look at her. *Kindled beside an open way. Class unrecognized.*

*[What do you think she is*?] said Ember.

*I don't know*, thought Lex. *Something new*.

---

The third thing came last.

Late. When the fire was down and half the hall was asleep on the benches and the other half was still arguing about sand. When Lex was sitting on the anvil block with Wren's head on his shoulder and his father asleep in the wheeled chair and his mother asleep beside him with the lamp in her lap.

The Dimmed.

Nobody knew.

"*They don't wake up*," said Fenn. Quietly. Sitting on the floor by the hearth, with his narrow sandy face in the firelight. "*The ones from Greyfall. Twenty-eight of them. We've had them in the infirmary at the Keepers' house in Millbridge for two months. They eat if you put food in their mouths. They walk if you lead them.*" He looked at his hands. "*They don't wake up.*"

"*Odile woke up*," said Antho. "*At the Keep. On the first night.*"

"*Pim*," said Lex. "*Pim cupped his hands round hers. Made room.*"

"*Pim can't do ten thousand*."

"*No*."

"*And Kell*," said Seraphine. "*In the deep. You read her name. From the Ledger. And what Dorran said.*" She looked at Lex. "*She came back a little. Not all the way. But some.*"

"*Because I knew what was taken*," said Lex slowly. "*I knew what Maelis took. The warm thing. The only one she had. And I gave it back. Out loud.*" He looked at the fire. "*I don't know what she took from Hollis Penn. Or the girl with the baby. Or the Keepers at Greyfall.*"

"*Someone does*," said a voice.

---

Wren lifted her head off his shoulder.

She'd been awake. He'd thought she was asleep. She sat up. Slowly. With her hand on her stomach and her fox-red braid over her shoulder and her green eyes very bright in the firelight.

"*Someone knows*," she said. "*For every one of them. What the warm thing was.*" She looked at Fenn. "*Someone who loves them.*"

The hearth crackled.

"*Odile*," said Wren. "*What did Maelis take from her?*"

"*The first time she came up the mountain*," said Antho. "*When she knocked on the cave door. And I said, thank goodness, I've no idea what I'm doing.*" His voice was very quiet. "*She told me after. It was the warmest thing she had.*"

"*And who knew that*?"

Antho looked at her.

"*Me*," he said.

"*You*." Wren nodded. "*And Kell. What Maelis took from Kell. Dorran carrying her. Who knew that?*"

"*Seraphine*," said Lex. "*Kell told her. At the Heart.*"

"*Someone who loved her enough to listen*," said Wren.

She stood up.

---

"*I'm going to Highcrest*," said Wren Ashdown.

Lex was on his feet.

"*No*—"

"*Yes*." She didn't look at him. She was looking at the map. At Highcrest. At the dark city on the hill with the gold light at the top. "*My mother's there. Fennel Street. The narrow house with the green door. Third from the end. With Tam and Robbie.*"

"*You don't know that she's*—"

"*I know*," said Wren. Her voice was very steady. "*I know. I felt it. In the wind. Three nights ago. When Highcrest went dark. I felt something go out.*" She swallowed. "*Something I've known my whole life. Something that used to cough in the next room.*"

The hall had gone quiet. Everyone who was awake was looking at her.

"*I know what the warm thing is*," said Wren. "*For Mam. I'm the only one who does. Tam's too little. Robbie's too little. Da's dead.*" She looked at Lex. Finally. "*I'm the only one who knows what to give back.*"

"*Wren*—"

"*I'm not asking*," said Wren Ashdown. Very gently. "*Hammer. I'm not asking. I'm telling you. Before I go. Not after. Not from the road.*" She put her hand on his face. On the handprint. "*Like Antho did. Like you taught him.*"

He couldn't say anything.

"*I'll take Pim*," said Wren. "*Nothing can dim Pim. And Elowen, because she knows the sally port. And Ilse and Ivo, because they can make me look like nothing.*" Her thumb moved on his cheek. "*I'll go in. I'll find her. I'll find out if I'm right.*"

"*And if you're not*?"

"*Then I'll come home*," said Wren. "*And we'll think of something else.*"

She looked at him. With her green eyes. For a long time.

"*And if I am*," she said, "*then we'll know how to bring them all back.*"

---

He let her go.

He'd think about that later. Many times. In the forge. In the loft at Harrow. Lying awake. That he let her go. Into the dark city with the gold light at the top. With his child in her. With the Moth Queen sitting in his father's chair by the window, reading.

He'd think: *I should have stopped her*.

And then he'd think: *I couldn't have*.

And then he'd think, the way Brannoc had taught him to think about iron that wouldn't take the shape you wanted: *I didn't want to*.

Because she was right.

Because she always was.

Because she was Wren Ashdown, who'd been hungry once, and hated waste more than anything in the world, and wasn't going to waste her mother.

---

She left at dawn.

On the wind. With Pim on her back, small and solemn with his arms round her neck. With Elowen on a grey horse below, and the twins in their cart, and Oathkeeper drawn.

Lex stood in the forge yard and watched her go.

Over the roofs of Ashbourne. Over the chapel elms, where Quill was still sulking. South. Toward the downs. Toward the dark city on the hill.

She didn't look back.

But at the edge of the village, where the road went over the river, she lifted one hand. Without turning round.

And the wind that went with her, that always went with her, that had come up out of her in a Wellworld of Gale and never left—

Came back.

Just for a moment. Round him. In the forge yard. Warm. Soft. Smelling of her hair.

Like a hand on his face.

Like *stay*.

And then it was gone. South. With her.

And Lex stood beside the warm spring in the cinders of the yard where he'd learned his trade, and put his hand on his face, on the handprint, where her thumb had been.

And went inside.

And started to think about sand.
