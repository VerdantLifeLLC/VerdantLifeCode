# The Seventh Well

The last of it went up at dawn.

Lex felt it go. Sitting on the folded blanket at the foot of the gate-tower with his daughter asleep on his chest, inside his shirt, against his skin, where Old Mag had put her. *Warm*, Old Mag had said. *That's what they want. Warm and a heartbeat. You've got plenty of both.*

He felt it through the stone. Through the rubble. Through the Stone in him, the Well he Commanded, which had been listening to the ground all night.

The breach emptied.

The last soft fold of the little one, the very back of it, the part that had been at the bottom of the deep pressed against the underside of the world for six days and thirty-four days and a year and a half and nine hundred years—went up. Past his brother. Through the door. Out. Into the circle.

And under it, at the bottom, standing in the door with a clay lamp, alone—

*[...brother...]*

*[...brother, that's the last of me...]*

*[...you can come up now...]*

---

He heard his brother come up.

Not with his ears. Through the Bloodbond. Felt him climb. Out of the deep. Up the walls of the breach. Slowly. With the lamp held up. The way you'd come up out of a cellar in the morning after a long night.

And felt him stop. At the top. At the edge of the opening. On the black floor of the fallen hall.

And look round.

*[Lex*,] said Antho. Through the Bloodbond. Very quietly. *[Lex. Come and see.]*

---

He went down the rubble.

Slowly. With Lark on his chest. With Wren asleep against the gate-tower, finally, with her mother beside her and Old Mag beside her mother, both of them asleep too, sitting up, heads together. With the sun coming up.

He went down through the gap where Duskhold's gate had been.

And stopped.

---

The hall was gone.

Not fallen. *Gone*. The broken walls. The black floor. The rubble. Morvane's citadel, grown out of Gloam-stone over fifteen years like a tumor out of the grey, falling to pieces for five.

Gone.

In its place, grass.

Short. Soft. Green. The green of a field in the first week of spring. Covering the ground where the hall had been, and the floor, and the breach. Going away down a long gentle slope. Wet with dew. With small white flowers in it. Daisies. Snowdrops. Things Lex didn't know the names of.

At the bottom of the slope, where the breach had been, where his father had knelt twenty-one years ago and torn the world, where three of the blood had knelt five years ago and mended it, where his brother had stood six days in a door—

A pool.

---

Small. Round. No bigger than a cartwheel. Ten feet across. With an edge of plain grey stone, worn smooth, much older than anything round it.

And inside the ring, dark.

Not water. Not empty. *Dark*. Still. Deep. So deep that looking into it was like looking up at the night sky. Not empty. *Full*. Full the way a held breath is full. Full the way the moment before a word is full.

Antho was kneeling beside it.

On the grass. In the dawn. With the clay lamp beside him on the grey stone rim. Lit. Looking down into the pool.

He didn't look up as Lex came.

"*It's the same*," said Antho. Very quietly. "*Lex. It's the same pool. From the hall of glass. From before the Nine.*" His voice was shaking. "*It showed me. In the gold room. That's what it looked like. Before they drank.*"

Lex knelt down beside him.

And looked.

---

It was the most beautiful thing he'd ever seen.

He'd thought the little one rising was. He'd stood on the rubble six days ago and told his brother *it's the most beautiful thing I've ever seen*, and meant it.

This was more.

Small. Still. Dark. Full. In a ring of old grey stone in a field of new grass on the last morning of autumn.

And at the bottom of it, very far down, so far he could barely see them—

Two little lights.

Curled together. Side by side. Silver-gold. Like very old candles.

---

"*Is that all of it*?" said Lex.

He didn't understand. The little one was the size of a country. He'd felt it. Rising through the breach all night. Spreading out across the circle. A hundred miles of soft dark sky coming up out of the ground.

"*No*," said Antho. "*Look.*"

He lifted his head. And looked out. Past the pool. Past the field. Past the last of the black rubble, at the top of the slope, where the gate-tower still stood with Wren asleep at its foot.

Lex looked.

---

The Blightmarch was gone.

He'd stood on the rubble fifty-two days ago and seen it. A hundred miles of grey. Dust. Ash. Stumps. Roofless towns. Hills with nothing on them. Valleys with nothing in them. Rivers that had stopped.

He looked now.

*Green*.

Not all of it. Not yet. Not like the Vale in summer. Like the Vale in the first week of spring. A faint green. A mist of it. Over the hills. In the hollows. Along the old dry riverbeds, which weren't dry. Which had *water* in them. He could hear it. A chuckle over a shallow. A gurgle round a stone.

The stumps of the burned woods had shoots on them. Green. Thin. Reaching.

And over everything, hanging in the morning air, soft, faint, enormous, stretching from one edge of the circle to the other, a hundred miles across—

A haze.

Not mist. Not cloud. *Dark*. Very faint. A soft dusk in the dawn. Like the shadow of something very large that's lying down. Like the dark between the stars, in the daytime, if you knew where to look.

It was *settling*.

He could see it. Very slowly. Coming down. Over the hills. Into the valleys. Into the ground. Into the trench round the rim, three hundred and fourteen miles of baked grey earth that had been dug and washed and knocked on like a door.

Into its name.

---

"*It's lying down*," said Antho. "*In the circle. In the word. All of it.*" He looked at the pool. "*And the middle's here. The heart. Where Mal is. Where the breach was.*" He swallowed. "*It's making itself small. The way it did for Mam, in the deep. Folding up. So it fits.*"

"*Will it stay small*?"

His brother looked at him.

"*It's not small*," said Antho. "*It's everywhere in the circle. In the grass. In the water. In the ground.*" He put his hand flat on the new green grass beside the pool. "*Here. Feel.*"

Lex put his hand down.

And felt it.

Under the grass. Under the soil. Under everything. Soft. Warm. Vast. *Resting*. The way a person rests at the end of a very long walk, when they've finally got home, and sat down by the fire, and taken off their boots.

And everywhere it rested, things were coming up.

Grass. Shoots. Springs. Room. Making room. For everything new.

*[...smith...]*

Very soft. Very sleepy. From everywhere. From the grass.

*[...is this the room...]*

*Yes*, thought Lex.

*[...it's so big...it's bigger than I thought...]*

*[...it's MINE...]*

*It's yours*, thought Lex Vey. *All of it. We made it for you.*

---

The World Notice came at sunrise.

Lex felt it before it came. Everyone did. Everyone in the world. The pressure. Behind the eyes. The net reaching out, through every thread, to every person, all at once.

Not silver. Not gold. Not violet. Not grey-gold.

*Clear*.

The color of a window. The color of the air in an empty room. The color he'd seen once, on a panel over Seraphine's head in a kitchen at the Lantern Keep.

He read it twice.

And beside him, on the grass, his brother made a sound. Small. Low. Like a breath let out after a very long time held.

"*They wrote it down*," said Antho. "*The net. It's in the net now. Seven.*" He was laughing. Crying. "*Lex. Seven circles. Six round and one in the middle. It's not empty anymore.*"

Lex looked at his own panel.

He didn't mean to. It came up on its own. The way they did, sometimes, now, since the reforging. When the net noticed something and wanted you to see.

*[I didn't write that one*,] said Ember. Quickly. *[The second one. I want that on record.]*

*You absolutely wrote that one*.

*[I made a suggestion*,] said Ember. *[The net took it. The net's very sentimental this morning. I think it's the baby.]*

Lex laughed.

Lark stirred against his chest. Inside his shirt. And made a small cross sound. And went back to sleep.

---

Seraphine came down the slope at a run.

Awkwardly. With her notebook in one hand and her spectacles in the other. Barefoot. In the grass. With her face lit up like a lamp.

"*It's got a name*," she said. "*The Attunement. In my panel. It says Hollow. It says Hollow, Speaking.*" She stopped in front of them. Gasping. "*It's not unrecognized anymore. It's in the net. It's* real.*"*

Antho stood up.

And picked her up. Off the grass. Notebook and all. And swung her round. Once. In the dawn. Beside the pool. The way Lex had swung her at the anvil on the night she ran across the plain with a word.

"*Put me down*!" She was shrieking. Laughing. "*Antho! You'll drop the notebook in the Well!*"

He put her down.

And kissed her.

In front of everyone. In front of the pool and the grass and the dawn and a Worldforger with a baby in his shirt.

"*Five hundred and four*," said Seraphine Ilvane. Against his mouth. "*Reasons. That one. Right now. Number five hundred and four.*"

---

And then Ivy Moss.

She came down the slope on her crutch. Slowly. Very slowly. With her pale hair everywhere. She'd been at the rim all night. At the trench. With the families. Telling the dust what was coming.

She stopped at the edge of the pool.

And looked down into it.

And a panel came up over her head. Clear. Like a window.

---

She read it.

And then she looked up. At Lex. At Antho. At Seraphine. With her small pale face very still.

"*Keeper*," she said. In a whisper. "*It says keeper.*"

"*It does*," said Antho.

"*Of it*?" She looked down at the pool. "*Of the room?*"

"*Somebody has to*," said Antho Vey. Gently. "*Somebody has to sit with it. And tell it things. And listen. And let people come and look, and make sure nobody ever kneels down and drinks.*" He looked at her. "*Somebody who's always been on the edge of things. Watching.*"

Ivy Moss looked at the pool for a long time.

"*I've never done anything*," she said. "*I told Master Lex. On the green. I've never done anything. I've just watched.*"

"*I know*," said Antho. "*That's why.*"

---

They found Kell on the rubble.

Lex had known. He'd seen her, in the night. Lying on the black stone halfway up the slope. Very still. Small. Grey. With empty hands. He'd seen her and run past, because Wren was screaming, because the Moth Queen was on the pick.

He'd known.

His mother found her first. In the dawn. When she came down from the gate-tower, where she'd been sitting with Wren and the baby. She came down the rubble barefoot, and stopped, and knelt.

And didn't move.

---

Lex went to her. With Lark. And Antho. And Seraphine.

Isaura Kell was lying on her back on the black stone. On the last of the rubble, where the grass hadn't reached yet. With her white hair. With her ruined robes. With her bony hands at her sides, open, palm up.

Her eyes were shut.

Her face—

It was the first time Lex had ever seen her face *still*. Not set. Not hard. Not holding anything. Just still. The way a face is when there's nothing left to hold. When the door's shut and the work's done and everyone's home.

Beside her, on the stone, a stick.

Long. Plain. Dark. Like a shepherd's crook without the crook.

---

"*She held on*," said his mother. Very quietly. Kneeling. With her hand on Kell's hand. "*All the way up. Out of the breach. Up the rubble. She held on to the wings.*" Her voice cracked. "*Till she fell.*"

Nobody said anything.

"*She said she would*," said Antho. "*In the deep. She said, someone has to stand in front of it. With a stick. When it can't run.*" He knelt. On Kell's other side. "*She didn't say she'd hold on to the wings.*"

"*The little one said that*," said Lex.

His brother looked at him.

"*In the night*," said Lex. "*When it carried me up. It said, she said someone had to stand in front of me with a stick. She didn't say she'd hold on to her wings.*"

Antho looked down at Kell's face.

At her still face. At her white hair.

---

And from the grass, from the ground, from everywhere, soft, faint, very young—

*[...the Warden...]*

They all heard it. Even Lex. Even his mother.

*[...the Warden's asleep...]*

Antho didn't answer for a long time.

"*Yes*," he said at last. Very gently. "*The Warden's asleep.*"

*[...she told me the one about the miller's cat...four hundred and eleven times...]*

*[...she held on...]*

*[...she held on to HER...so she couldn't get me...]*

*[...brother...]*

"*Yes*."

*[...will she wake up...]*

Antho put his hand on Kell's still face.

"*No*," he said. "*No. She won't.*"

The ground was quiet.

*[...like Mal...]*

"*Like Mal*."

*[...]*

*[...can she come in the middle...with Mal...where it's warm...]*

---

Lex looked at his brother.

His brother looked at him.

And his mother, kneeling on the black stone with her hand on Kell's, looked up at both of them. And then down. At the dead woman. At the stick beside her. At the grass, coming up through the rubble, an inch from Kell's open palm.

"*She'd like that*," said Seren Harrow. "*I think. She said once. In the deep. That nobody had asked her to sit down in fifty years. Not until Mal.*"

She took her hand off Kell's.

"*Ask her*," she said to the ground. "*Little one. Ask her if she'd like to sit down.*"

---

The grass came up.

Not fast. Not like the springs and the shoots. Slowly. Gently. Through the black rubble. Round Kell's hands. Round her feet. Round her shoulders. Green. Soft. With small white flowers in it.

And the ground under her—

*Made room*.

Lex felt it. Through the Stone. Through the rubble. A space. A hollow. Opening under Isaura Kell, very gently, the way a bed gives when you lie down on it at the end of a long day. Soft. Warm. Deep.

And she went down into it.

Slowly. With her hands at her sides and her eyes shut and her face still. The grass closing over her. Like a blanket drawn up. Like the earth folding round her the way the little one had folded round his mother in the deep, to hide her from the moths.

And then there was only grass.

And a stick, lying on it. Long. Plain. Dark.

And a scattering of small white flowers, in the shape of a woman lying down.

---

*[...she's in the middle now...]* said the little one. Very softly. *[...with Mal...and his brother...]*

*[...three little lights...]*

*[...she's sitting down...]*

---

Antho picked up the stick.

He held it for a long time. In both hands. Crosswise. Like a bar across a door.

Then he walked down the slope. Through the new grass. To the pool. And laid it on the grey stone rim. Very carefully. Beside the clay lamp.

"*For whoever keeps the room*," he said. Not to anyone. "*So they know someone stood in front of it once.*"

And Ivy Moss, standing at the edge of the pool on her crutch, looked down at the stick.

And didn't say anything.

But she didn't take her eyes off it either.

---

Their father woke at noon.

In the tent. On the cot. Under three blankets. Where they'd carried him in the night, after the breach, after his hand went slack and slid out of Antho's and fell on the black floor.

Lex was there. With Lark. Wren was there, on the other cot, propped up, grey and exhausted and refusing to sleep any more because she wanted to look at the baby. His mother was there. On the stool. Where she'd been all night. With her hand on his father's chest.

Seraphine was there. With her eyes shut. Listening.

"*It's making room*," she said. Very quietly. Before he woke. With her hand out. Palm up. Over the old man on the cot. "*In him. Lex. Antho. It's making room.*"

"*It can't heal*," said Antho. "*You said. It doesn't heal.*"

"*It doesn't*," said Seraphine. "*It can't. That's not what it does.*" She opened her eyes. They were wet. "*But it's making room. In his time. In the years. The flood. The two hundred years coming all at once.*" She looked at Arion. "*It's making room for them. So they don't have to come all at once. So they can come*—"

She stopped.

"*Slowly*," said Seren.

"*Slowly*." Seraphine nodded. "*Like an ordinary man. A year a year. Like everyone else.*" Her voice cracked. "*It's not giving him time back. It can't. He's old. He's the oldest man I've ever seen. He'll stay old.*"

"*But*," said Lex.

"*But he'll stay*," said Seraphine Ilvane. "*For a while. Not a season. Longer.*" She shook her head. "*I don't know how long. A year. Two. Ordinary years. It's made room for them.*"

---

And his father opened his eyes.

Grey. Clear. Steady. In a face like a walnut left in a drawer for a hundred years.

He looked at the tent roof.

Then at his wife. On the stool. With her hand on his chest.

"*Seren*," he said.

His voice was a thread. A whisper. A breath with a word in it. But it was *his*.

"*I'm here*," said Seren.

"*I let go*."

"*I know*."

"*I couldn't hold any more*." His eyes were wet. "*I held the edges. I held his hand. And then I couldn't.*"

"*I know*," said his wife. "*It's all right. He held the rest. Your son. He held the door all night. It's through. All of it. It's home.*"

Arion closed his eyes.

"*It's home*," he whispered.

---

"*Father*," said Lex.

His father opened his eyes again. And turned his head on the pillow. Very slowly.

And saw what Lex was holding.

---

Lex had never seen his father's face do that.

Not when he said *you look old*, on the slope of the Hollowmaw, and his father smiled the wide startled smile. Not when Seren knelt in front of his chair in the cavern and said *you have a nice voice*. Not when Ember said *hello, old man*.

It did something Lex didn't have a word for.

All the furrows. All the bones. All the hundred years in a drawer. All of it, at once, going soft. Going open. Going *young*. Not young like the Moth Queen's light. Young like a boy who's just been handed something he never thought he'd be allowed to hold.

"*Oh*," said Arion Vey.

"*She's called Lark*," said Wren. From the other cot. Hoarse. "*Lark Vey. Born on Lantern Night. At the top of the rubble. While you were asleep.*"

His father didn't say anything.

He lifted his hand. Off the blanket. Thin. Bone. Brown-spotted. Shaking.

And Lex leaned down. And held his daughter close. Close enough.

And his father touched her.

One finger. On her cheek. Very lightly. The way you'd touch a soap bubble. The way you'd touch a flame to see if it's real.

Lark opened her eyes.

Dark. Unfocused. Newborn. And looked up. At an old man. Older than anyone.

And reached out. With one small square hand. Too big for the rest of her. And closed it round his finger.

And held on.

---

"*She's got big hands*," whispered Arion Vey.

"*She's going to be trouble*," said Seren. Very softly. Beside him. "*Just like her grandfather.*"

His father looked at her.

And for a moment, just a moment, Lex saw something go across his face. A flicker. A stone turning over in a field.

"*You said that*," said Arion. "*Didn't you. When Lex was born. You said, he's going to be trouble. Just like his father.*"

"*Page twelve*," said Seren Vey.

"*You remembered*."

"*No*," said his mother. "*I read it.*" She put her hand on his. On Lark's. On the three of them. "*I'm making new ones. Remember?*"

---

The naming happened at dusk.

At the pool. In the middle of the circle. With everyone.

Everyone who was left. Which was nearly everyone. Lex had been afraid. All night. All day. Afraid of the counting that would have to come, after. The names for the grey book.

There weren't many.

Eleven Ashari, at the east rim, where the moths came thickest, who'd held black heat round their lanterns until there was nothing left in them. Six Keepers. Four Banners. A shepherd from the Greyspine who'd walked into the moths after his Dimmed son and relit him and been dimmed himself, and his son had relit him back.

Fenn.

Keeper-Lieutenant Fenn. Narrow. Sandy. Quiet. Who'd straightened every leaning Stone in the west with his own hands. Who'd stood at the south edge all night with the families, between them and the Mothwing who came with knives, holding a Keeper's staff crosswise. Like a bar across a door.

Roole carried him in. And laid him on the grass by the pool. And stood there with his hatchet face set like stone and his arm still in its sling and didn't say anything at all.

Twenty-three.

Lex would write them all. In the grey book. After the Vhessans. Before Kell. Every one.

---

They came down the slope at dusk. From the rubble and the tents and the rim. Thousands. Into the field of new grass. Round the pool. In a great ring. Quiet. The way people are quiet in a chapel. In a churchyard. At a window, on a winter night, waiting.

The lanterns were still burning.

All of them. Round the rim. Three hundred and fourteen miles. Given. And the little one hadn't taken them. It hadn't needed to. They'd been given to it, and it was home, and they were *its* now, and it had left them exactly where they were.

Lit.

A ring of small gold lights at the edge of a hundred miles of soft new green.

---

"*It needs a name*," said Antho.

He was standing at the pool. With the clay lamp. With Seraphine beside him and the stick on the rim and Ivy Moss on her crutch at the edge of the grey stone.

"*It's been asking*," he said. To everyone. To the ring of thousands round the field. "*For months. Every night. What am I? What's my name?*" He looked at the pool. "*The net calls it Hollow. That's what it is. Like calling my brother smith. It wants its own.*"

The field was very quiet.

"*Someone who made room for it*," said Lex. From the other side of the pool. With Lark asleep in his shirt. "*That's what we said. In the dark. Someone who made room should name it.*"

"*Everyone made room*," said Seraphine. "*Three hundred and fourteen miles of people. With spades.*"

"*Someone first*," said Antho.

And everyone looked at Pim.

---

He was standing at the very front of the ring. Small. In his blue coat with the brass buttons buttoned wrong. With his hands in his pockets and his mop of black hair and his grey-violet eyes.

He'd been there all day. At the pool. Sitting on the grey stone rim with his legs dangling over the dark. Not saying anything. Looking down.

He went red.

All the way up. Into his hair.

He didn't hide. He didn't run to tell Ashka. He stood at the front of the ring with thousands of people looking at him and his face like fire.

And then he walked forward. To the pool. And knelt down. On the grey stone rim.

And cupped his hands.

Round nothing. Over the dark. An inch above it. The way he'd cupped them round the hole in the cold-room floor. Round the Fenmoor lantern. Round Odile's dusty hand in the snow.

Making a space.

And was silent.

---

The pool *leaned*.

Toward him. All of it. The dark in the stone ring. Rising. Very slightly. Toward his hands. The way the clay lamp leaned toward the black door. The way the little one had leaned toward Lark on the rubble.

*[...the quiet one...]*

*[...the one with the room inside...]*

Everyone heard it. Every person in the field. Soft. Young. From the grass. From the ground. From everywhere.

*[...he made the cups...]*

*[...he made me feel the size I'm supposed to be...]*

*[...the size of a person...]*

Pim didn't move.

*[...quiet one...]*

*[...what's my name...]*

---

Pim looked down into the pool.

At the dark. At the full. At the moment before a word.

At the bottom of it, very far down, three little lights. Curled together. One small, with the shape of a straw hat. One tall, with something in its hands. And one thin and very straight, newer than the others, with white hair.

Sitting down.

He looked at them for a long time.

And then he said it. Not loudly. Pim never said anything loudly. Very quietly. Into his cupped hands. Into the space he'd made. The way you'd whisper a secret to someone very small.

"*Mal*," said Pim.

---

The field was silent.

"*It always asked for him*," said Pim. To his hands. To the pool. "*First thing. Before brother. Before lamp-lady. It said Mal. Mal? Mal? Like a child calling in the dark.*" He swallowed. "*Because that's the name it heard most. From someone who loved it. Who sat with it. Who sang in tune.*"

He looked down at the three little lights.

"*It doesn't know names are for one person*," said Pim. "*It just knows that's the name that meant somebody stayed.*"

He took his hands away.

"*So that's its name*," he said.

---

And on the cord round Lex's neck, against his chest, under his shirt, beside his sleeping daughter—

The little silver bell with the crooked clapper—

Rang.

Once.

In tune.

The pool didn't do anything.

It didn't flare. It didn't rise. It didn't sing.

It just lay there, in its ring of old grey stone, in the middle of a hundred miles of new green, under the first stars of winter. Small. Still. Dark. Full.

Like a pool that someone, at last, after nine hundred years, had stopped at.

And knelt beside.

And not drunk from.

And *named*.

---

*[...Mal...]* it said. Very softly. Wonderingly. To itself. To everyone. To the grass and the water and the stars.

*[...I'm Mal...]*

*[...that's me...that's what I am...]*

*[...I'm the one somebody stayed for...]*

And Lex, standing at the edge of the pool with his daughter on his chest and his brother across the water and his mother behind him with her hand on an old man's shoulder in a chair someone had carried down the slope—

Laughed.

And couldn't stop.

And neither could anyone else.
