# The Deep

They came up out of the dark like a tide.

Lex had seen the sea come in once. On the Saltreach coast, the summer after Duskhold, with Wren. A spring tide, at night, under a full moon. They'd stood on the sand and watched it come. Not a wave. Not a rush. Just the water, rising. Spreading. Silver. Silent. Filling the flats and the channels and the hollows, coming closer and closer without ever seeming to move, until suddenly it was round your ankles and you hadn't seen it come.

The moths came like that.

From below. From everywhere. Rising out of the deep in a great slow sheet of grey-gold glitter, spreading as it came, filling the dark between Shade's stars. Not hurrying. Not swarming. Just *coming*. Up. Toward the light.

Toward him.

"*Lex*," said Antho. Very calmly. "*You need to put it out.*"

"*I can't*."

"*You need to*."

"*I can't*." He looked down at his hands. Gold. Glowing. Bright as a forge-mouth in the dark. It was coming off him in waves. Off his skin. His hair. His breath. He'd let it come up when he heard Kell's staff break, and now it was up, all the way, the way it went when he was afraid, and he couldn't make it go back down. "*It's not a lamp, Antho. I can't shutter it. It's me.*"

The glitter was closer. A hundred yards. Less. He could see them now. Single moths. Wings beating. Painted eyes. Thousands upon thousands, a field of them, a sky of them, rising.

"*Then make it something else*," said Kestrel.

---

He turned his head.

She was standing beside him. On the dark. With her hair burning gold and her Sunreach blade drawn and her amber eyes fixed on the rising glitter. Calm. Utterly calm. The way she'd been calm in the Glass Desert, on the fire-bridge, with the Sunwell roaring under her.

"*Make it what*?" said Lex.

"*I don't know*." Kestrel didn't look at him. "*You're the smith. What's fire that isn't light?*"

He stared at her.

*What's fire that isn't light*?

And something came up out of his hands. The way most of his thoughts did. Out of the part of him that had stood at an anvil every day since he was eleven.

*Black heat*.

---

Brannoc had taught him. The first winter. When he was eleven and couldn't stop shaking and the old smith had given him the bellows to work because he said the work would help.

*Look at it, lad*, Brannoc had said. Holding a bar of iron in the tongs. Fresh out of the fire. *What color is it?*

*Black*, Lex had said.

*It's not black*. Brannoc had held it closer. Not close enough to burn. Close enough to feel. *Put your hand near it. Go on. Not on. Near.*

Lex had put his hand near it.

And snatched it back. With a yelp. Because it was *hot*. Hotter than anything. Hot enough to blister at a foot away. And it was black. Dull grey-black. Not glowing at all. Not red. Not orange. Not anything.

*Black heat*, Brannoc had said. *Iron gets hot long before it glows. Hotter than your oven at home. Hot enough to take your skin off. And it doesn't look like anything at all.* He'd put the bar back in the fire. *That's the one that gets apprentices. Not the red. Not the white. The black. You see red, you know to be careful. You see black, you think it's cool. You pick it up.*

He'd looked at Lex with his soot-black beard and his kind fierce eyes.

*Most dangerous heat there is*, Brannoc Holt had said. *The kind with no light in it.*

---

"*Black heat*," said Lex.

Kestrel looked at him.

"*Fire that's hot*," said Lex. "*Without glowing. Iron does it. Before it goes red.*" He looked down at his hands. Gold. Bright. "*The moths drink light. Not heat. They can't drink heat.*"

"*Can you do it*?"

"*I don't know*."

The glitter was fifty yards away. Forty. He could hear it now. A sound like rain on a roof. Like sand blowing over stone. Thousands of wings.

He looked at the fire in his hands. And asked it.

*Not brighter*, he thought. *Hotter. Hotter and darker. The way iron goes. Before it shows.*

*Hide*.

---

It didn't want to.

He felt it not want to. The fire in him. The Flame he Commanded. It was a bright thing. A loud thing. It liked to be seen. It had been burning since before there were people to burn for, and it had always, always shown itself. That was what fire *was*.

*I know*, thought Lex. *I know. Just for a while. Just down here. Please.*

*It's dark down here. There's things in the dark that eat light.*

*Hide with me*.

And slowly, grudgingly, the way a dog lies down when you ask it to and it doesn't want to—

The gold went out of his hands.

Not out. *Down*. Into them. Under the skin. The glow faded off his arms, his chest, his hair, his breath. Dimmer. Dimmer. Until he was standing in the dark at the bottom of the world in the faint gold light from the crack in the door behind them, and he wasn't glowing at all.

He was *hot*.

He felt it. Pouring off him. Invisible. Like the heat off a stone wall at the end of a summer day. Like the air above a forge chimney, shimmering. The dark round him rippled with it. Antho, beside him, took a step back.

"*Gods*," said his brother. "*Lex. What*—"

"*Black heat*," said Lex.

---

The moths reached them.

And didn't stop.

They'd been coming for the light. The light was gone. But they were close now, and the tide doesn't stop when the moon goes behind a cloud. They came on. In a great slow glittering sheet. Over and round and past.

And the ones that came near Lex—

*Burned*.

He didn't see it, at first. There was nothing to see. No flame. No flash. A moth would drift close to him, a foot away, two feet, wings beating, painted eyes turning, looking for the light that had been there a moment ago—

And it would go *still*. In the air. For a breath.

And then crumple. Like a leaf held too near a stove. Curl. Blacken at the edges. And fall.

And another. And another.

They didn't know what was killing them. They couldn't see it. They came on, looking for light, and found heat instead. Black heat. Invisible. The most dangerous kind there is.

The kind with no light in it.

---

"*Oh, that's clever*," said Kestrel. Softly. "*That's very clever.*"

She was watching him. With her amber eyes narrowed. The way she watched the Ashari fire-women in the Sunreach, Lex thought. The way she watched anything she wanted to learn.

And then she did it too.

He saw her do it. Saw the gold go out of her hair. All at once. Not out. Down. Into her. The fire that had burned at the ends of her hair for three weeks, that had singed her collar black, that had been burning quietly ever since the Heart like a lamp left on in a window—

Gone dark.

And the air round her began to ripple.

"*Huh*," said Kestrel Varn. Looking at her own hands. "*That's harder than it looks.*"

"*You did it in one try*."

"*I've been watching fire-women for a year*." She flexed her fingers. A moth drifted near them, and curled, and fell. "*They do something like it. With the sand. Make it hot without making it bright. So you don't see the trap till you step in it.*" She grinned. Fierce. In the dark. "*I never could get the trick of it. I kept making it too pretty.*"

---

They went down.

Through the moths. Through the great slow glittering tide of them. Lex at the front, dark and hot, and Kestrel beside him, dark and hot. A wedge. A plough. The moths parting in front of them and curling and falling, and closing again behind.

Antho came after. With the Lantern shuttered. With Shade wrapped round him so close he was hardly there at all, a darker shape in the dark, the way he'd learned to walk in the Greyspine in the old days when the Wardens were hunting him.

And Seraphine.

She had no fire. No Shade. No wind. She walked between the brothers with her three lamps on her belt, unlit, and her notebook in her coat, and her eyes shut.

Listening.

"*Down*," she said. "*And left. A little. There's a—the room changes. Over there. It's thicker.*"

"*Thicker*?"

"*Like a fold in a blanket*." She didn't open her eyes. "*Like when you shake out a sheet and there's a crease where it was folded. It's—it's folded over there. Something's folded.*"

---

The moths thinned.

Not all at once. Slowly. The tide going out. Lex felt it before he saw it. Fewer wings. Fewer painted eyes. More dark between them. More of Shade's stars, faint and silver, showing through.

And then they were through. Below it. In clear dark. With the glitter overhead now, a great slow-turning ceiling of grey-gold, like a sky full of snow that hasn't started falling.

And below them, *something*.

---

Streets.

Lex knew them. He'd seen them before. Two years ago. On the way to the Heart. From the Ferryman's boat. A city. Drowned. Grey. Enormous. Stretching away under the dark in every direction. Towers and domes and bridges and long straight streets. Pre-Lattice. Older than anything in Caldor. Older than the Founders.

The Drowned Country.

Two years ago it had been full.

Full of the dead. Grey. Drifting. Thousands of them. Hundreds of thousands. Walking the streets with their faces slack and their hands empty. Everyone the hunger had ever pulled down. Everyone who'd ever drowned in it.

It was empty now.

The streets were empty. The squares. The bridges. The great flights of steps. Not a soul. Not a grey shape. Not a drifting hand.

They'd gone up. At the Heart. When the net was reforged and the Tithe ended and the eaten worlds rose in gold. Lex had seen it. Every grey dead thing in the deep, rising. Going up. Gold. Into the light.

Gone home.

---

They walked down a street.

It was strange. Lex had never walked here. He'd only looked down at it from the boat. It was wide. Paved with grey stone. With tall grey houses on either side, their doors open, their windows dark. There were things in the street. A cart, overturned. A child's shoe. A basket. Grey. Everything grey. Like a drawing in charcoal. Like the Unfinished.

And at the end of the street, in a little square, by a fountain that had no water in it, six lanterns.

On the ground. In a circle. Iron. Small. Each with a single rune on its glass.

*ARDENT*.

They were burning.

Small. Gold. Steady. In the empty grey square at the bottom of the world. The only light in the whole drowned city.

Antho stopped.

---

He stood in the mouth of the square and looked at them. For a long time.

Lex knew. He'd been there. On the way to the Heart. When the Ferryman took them down the river, and they passed the Drowned Country, and Antho had asked the old man to stop. Just for a moment. And got out of the boat. And walked up a grey street to a grey square, with six lanterns in his arms.

*I made a promise*, he'd said. *Two years ago. To a little girl. I said I'd come back with a light.*

A little grey girl. Drowned. Drifting in a grey street. Who had held his sleeve.

He'd set the six lanterns down in a circle in the square where he'd found her, and lit them, and she'd come. Out of the grey. And stood in the middle of them. And held his sleeve again.

And at the Heart, when the eaten worlds rose, she'd gone up. Gold. Holding his sleeve.

He'd never come back to see if the lanterns were still burning.

They were.

---

Antho walked into the square.

He didn't say anything. He walked to the circle of lanterns and knelt down in the middle of it, on the grey stone, among the six small gold flames.

And put his hand flat on the stone. Where she'd stood.

Lex didn't follow him. Neither did Kestrel. Neither did Seraphine. They stood at the edge of the square and waited. The way you wait at the edge of a grave.

After a while Antho got up. And came back.

His face was wet. He didn't wipe it.

"*They stayed lit*," he said. Very quietly. "*Two years. Nobody filled them. Nobody's been here.*"

"*They're Ardent*," said Lex. "*They don't go out.*"

"*I know*." Antho looked back at the circle. "*I know. I just*—" He stopped. "*I thought maybe nobody would ever see them. Down here. In the dark. I thought they'd just burn and burn and nobody would ever know.*"

He looked at Lex.

"*Moths didn't take them*," he said.

Lex looked at the lanterns. Six small gold flames. Untouched. In the middle of the drowned city. With the great glittering ceiling of moths overhead, turning, so close, so many.

"*No*," he said slowly. "*They didn't.*"

"*Why*?"

Seraphine answered.

"*Because they weren't for anyone*," she said. Quietly. "*Not anymore. She went up. They're not waiting for someone to come home.*" She looked at the circle. "*They're just—light. Left on. For no one. For the dark itself.*"

She was quiet.

"*You can't drink a promise that's already kept*," said Seraphine Ilvane.

---

They found Kell in the next street.

Seraphine heard her first. Not her voice. Her *quiet*. A place in the room where something was missing. "*There*," she said. "*There. In the doorway. Someone's—someone's gone thin there.*"

She was sitting in a doorway. The doorway of a tall grey house, three steps up from the street, with its door hanging open on one hinge. Sitting on the top step. With her back against the door-frame. And her long legs stretched out in front of her. And her hands in her lap.

There was a stick across her knees. Long. Plain. Dark wood.

Broken. In two. Snapped clean through in the middle, as if someone had brought it down across their knee.

Her white robes were grey with dust. Grey-gold. All down the front. All over her hands. Her face.

And she was looking up.

Past them. Past the grey roofs. Up. At the glittering ceiling of moths overhead. With her pale eyes.

Dim.

---

"*Kell*," said Lex.

She didn't look at him.

He went up the three steps. And crouched in front of her. Close. With his black heat pulled in tight, as tight as he could make it, so it wouldn't burn her.

Her eyes were the eyes of the Keepers at Greyfall. The eyes of Odile, on the night of the moths. Open. Dim. Like a window in a house where someone has turned the lamp down very low. Something behind them. Far back. Not coming forward.

She was leaning. Very slightly. Up. Toward the glitter.

"*Isaura*," said Lex.

Nothing.

Antho was beside him. With the Lantern. He opened the shutter. Just a crack. And the gold light fell across Kell's grey dusty face.

She flinched. A little. As if the light hurt.

And her lips moved.

"*...took it...*" said Isaura Kell. In a voice like dry paper. Like a page turning. "*...she took it...*"

"*Took what*?"

"*...the warm thing...the only one...*" The pale dim eyes didn't move off the glitter. "*...I only had the one...*"

---

Lex looked at her.

At the broken staff across her knees. At the dust on her face. At her thin bony hands, lying in her lap, palm up, empty. The way Pim's hands had lain on the floor of the cold-room after he folded the Delve. The way a pair of hands lies when it's let go of something and there's nothing left to hold.

*The only warm thing*.

He knew what it was.

He'd been there. At the Heart. Standing in the white forge on the white mountain at the bottom of the world, with the Ledger of What Was open in his hands. A book that kept the names of the Unnamed. And a faceless woman in front of him. A woman with no name and no face, who'd been High Warden of the Order of the Lattice once, and had been unwritten, and eaten, and made into the hunger's Warden.

He'd read her name out of the Ledger. *Isaura Kell*. And nothing had happened.

And then Ilse had spoken. Ilse, whom Kell had saved, at the bottom of the Rift, by letting herself be taken below in Ilse's place.

And then Lex had said the only other thing he knew about her. The last thing. The thing that had happened at the Rift, when Dorran Vask was dying, holding up a wall of the world.

*Dorran Vask carried you*.

And her face had come back. Like a lamp being lit in a window.

---

"*She took Dorran*," said Lex. Very quietly.

Antho looked at him.

"*The memory*," said Lex. "*At the Rift. When she was forty-one percent unwritten. When she couldn't walk. When Dorran picked her up and carried her across the bottom of the Umbral Rift on his back.*" He swallowed. "*She told Seraphine about it once. At the Heart. After. She said it was the only time in her life anyone ever carried her.*"

Seraphine had her hand over her mouth.

"*She said*," said Lex, "*nobody had touched her kindly since she was nine. Not once. Not in fifty years. And then a man she'd branded with a Kindling-Warden's mark picked her up in the dark and carried her and said, I've got you, High Warden. I've got you.*"

He looked at the dim pale eyes. Leaning. Up. Toward the glitter.

"*That's what Maelis took*," he said. "*That's the warm thing. The only one she had.*"

---

He took the Ledger out of his coat.

He'd carried it everywhere since the Heart. The *Ledger of What Was*. Legendary. Bound in grey leather, gone soft. Ysmir's book. The book that kept the names of the Unnamed, so that someone would remember them when no one else could. There were only two names left in it that hadn't been restored. Two. Out of thousands.

*Isaura Kell*.

*Elric Thorne*.

No. One. Kell's had been restored at the Heart. Her face had come back. Her name. He'd watched it happen.

He opened the book anyway. To her page.

It was still there. In Ysmir's clear old hand. Faded now. Pale. The way a name fades in the Ledger when the person comes back to themselves, and the book doesn't need to hold it anymore.

*Isaura Kell*. *Of Highcrest. High Warden of the Order of the Lattice. Sixty-one. She signed forty-one warrants for unlatticed sorcery and remembered every name.*

And under it, in a different hand. Lex's own. Big and clumsy and careful. Written in at the Heart, in charcoal, kneeling on a white floor.

*Dorran Vask carried her*.

---

He read it out loud.

All of it. Ysmir's line, and his own. Slowly. Clearly. Kneeling on the top step of a grey house in a drowned city at the bottom of the world, with a dim-eyed woman in front of him and a sky of moths overhead.

"*Isaura Kell*," read Lex Vey. "*Of Highcrest. High Warden of the Order of the Lattice. She signed forty-one warrants for unlatticed sorcery and remembered every name.*"

The pale eyes didn't move.

"*Dorran Vask carried her*," said Lex.

Nothing.

He looked at the page.

And then, without deciding to, without knowing he was going to, he said the rest. The bit that wasn't written down. The bit he'd heard from Seraphine, in the white forge, after. The bit that Kell had told her, sitting on the floor, shaking, with her face just back.

"*Through the dark*," said Lex. "*At the bottom of the Rift. On his back. Like a sack of grain. And she was so light he hardly felt her. And he said, I've got you, High Warden.*" His voice cracked. "*I've got you. Don't you worry. I've carried heavier.*"

---

Isaura Kell blinked.

Slowly. Once. Like someone waking from a long sleep. Like a lamp being turned up, a very little, in a dark window.

Her eyes moved. Off the glitter. Down. To Lex's face.

Still dim. But not *as* dim. Something behind them. Coming forward. A little. A very little. The way a person comes forward in a dark room when they hear their name.

"*...heavier*," she whispered.

"*He'd carried heavier*." Lex didn't look away from her. "*That's what he said.*"

Her thin mouth moved. Very slightly. Sideways. Like a door that hadn't been opened in years.

"*Liar*," said Isaura Kell. Faintly. "*He said that. But he was lying. I was very heavy.*" Her eyes were wet. "*I was the heaviest thing he ever carried. I could feel it. In his back. He was shaking.*"

"*He carried you anyway*."

"*He carried me anyway*."

And something came back into her face. Not all of it. Not the way it had at the Heart, all at once, like a lamp. Just a little. Like a single coal catching in a cold grate.

Enough.

---

They gave her water. And bread, from Seraphine's pack. And sat with her on the steps of the grey house while she ate. Slowly. Mechanically. Like someone who'd forgotten how.

"*She came*," said Kell. When she could. Her voice was thin. Dry. Still faint. But hers. "*Down the longest shaft. Like a moth down a chimney. Folded.*" She looked at her broken staff. "*Seren held up the lamp. I held up this.*"

"*And*?"

"*And she laughed*." Kell's thin mouth twisted. "*She has a lovely laugh. She looked at me over my stick and said, oh, Isaura. Isaura Kell. High Warden. I've heard of you. You burned women like my sister's girl.*" She was quiet. "*And then she took my stick. In one hand. And broke it. Over her knee. Like kindling.*"

Nobody said anything.

"*And then she touched me*," said Kell. Very quietly. "*Here.*" She touched her own cheek. Where the dust was thickest. "*Very gently. Like a mother. And I felt it go.*"

"*And Mam*?" said Antho. "*Where's Mam?*"

Kell looked at him. With her half-dim eyes.

"*Gone*," she said.

Antho went white.

"*Not like that*." Kell shook her head. Slowly. "*Not taken. Gone. Inside.*" She looked up. Past them. Past the grey roofs. At the dark. "*The little one took her. When the moth turned toward her. It moved. Faster than I've ever seen it move. It folded round her. Like a hand closing. Like a—*" She stopped. Looked for the word.

"*Like a room*," said Seraphine.

Kell looked at her.

"*Like a room*," she agreed. "*It made a room. In itself. And put her in it. And shut the door.*" Her voice was very quiet. "*And then it went away. Down. Into itself. And the moth's been hunting for the door ever since.*"

---

"*Where*?" said Lex.

"*I don't know*." Kell closed her eyes. "*Everywhere. Nowhere. It's big, Vey. It's so big. It's the size of a country. It's the size of the dark. And it's folded itself up like a—like a letter. Like a map. And she's somewhere in one of the folds.*" She opened her eyes. "*The moth can't find her. That's the only reason she's still—*"

She stopped.

Because Seraphine had stood up.

---

She was standing in the middle of the grey street. With her eyes shut. And her hands out. Palms up. Like someone feeling for rain.

"*Seraphine*?" said Antho.

"*Shh*."

She stood there for a long time. In the dark. In the drowned city. Under the glittering ceiling of moths. Very still.

"*Oh*," she said.

Her voice was very small.

"*Oh*. *Oh, it's—Antho. Lex. It's—*"

She opened her eyes.

And looked round. At the grey street. The grey houses. The empty squares and the bridges and the great flights of steps, going away in every direction. The whole of the drowned city, lying silent at the bottom of the world.

At the dark *over* it. Under it. Around it. Between Shade's faint silver stars.

"*It's here*," said Seraphine Ilvane. "*It's not somewhere else. It's not far away. It's* here.*"*

She turned in a slow circle. With her hands out.

"*We've been walking in it*," she said. "*The whole time. Since we came through the door. The dark. All of it. That's not Shade.*" Her eyes were enormous behind her spectacles. "*I mean—it is Shade. But it's not* only *Shade. The room. The space. The thing that makes the dark have room in it.*"

She stopped turning.

"*We're inside it*," said Seraphine. "*We've been inside it all along. It's all round us. It's folded round the whole of the deep like a—like a hand round a lamp.*"

She looked at Lex.

"*And it's listening*," she said. "*Right now. It's been listening to us since we came through the door. Since Antho knocked.*"

---

The street was very quiet.

Lex looked up. At the dark. At Shade's stars, faint and silver. At the great slow glittering ceiling of the moths, turning overhead, so close, so many, looking for a light that wasn't there.

At the space *between*.

He couldn't feel it. He wasn't a Listener of the Hollow. He was a smith. He felt fire, and stone, and a little of water and wind. He couldn't feel rooms.

But Antho could.

His brother had gone very still. Standing in the middle of the grey street with the Lantern in his hand and his clear grey eyes wide. Listening. To the dark. To the old place in the back of his skull.

"*Little one*," said Antho Vey. Out loud. Very softly. Into the dark. "*Little one. Is that you?*"

Nothing.

"*It's me*," said Antho. "*It's your brother. I came down. I knocked. The Warden let me in.*" His voice shook. "*I've brought my brother. Lex. The one who claimed your first stone. The one who made the cups.*"

Nothing.

"*Where's the lamp-lady*?" said Antho. "*Where did you put her? I want to see her. She's my mother.*"

---

The dark didn't answer.

But something changed.

Lex felt it. Not with his skin. Not with his fire. Not with the Stone in him. With something else. Something older. Something that had sat on a woodpile at Harrow at ten years old, beside a brother who heard voices, and not asked, and not pushed. Just been there.

The dark *leaned*.

Toward them. All of it. Gently. The whole great soft fold of it, round the drowned city, round the street, round the four of them standing in it and the gaunt woman on the steps. Leaning in. The way a child leans toward you when you crouch down to its height. The way a horse leans its head against your shoulder.

And in the dark, in the street, ten feet in front of them, a light.

Small. Gold. Low down. At the height of a woman's hand.

A lamp.

---

It hung there. In the dark. On nothing. A small brown clay lamp, round and plain, with a chip in its spout. Burning. Small and gold and steady. Not attached to anything. Not held by anything.

And then, slowly, the dark round it *opened*.

Not like a door. Like a hand. Like fingers uncurling. Like a fold in a blanket being smoothed out. The dark drew back from round the lamp in a long soft curve, and there was a space there. A room. Small. Round. Warm. Lit gold by the little flame.

And in the room, sitting on nothing, with her shoes off and her knees pulled up and the lamp held in both hands in her lap, a woman.

Small. Fierce. Freckled. With brown hair going grey, tied back with nothing. In a dress that had been blue once, a long time ago.

She looked up.

At the four of them, standing in the grey street of the drowned city. At Kell, on the steps. At Seraphine with her hands out. At Kestrel with her dark hot fire. At her two sons.

Her face did something Lex had never seen a face do. All of it at once. Every part. Crumpling and opening and lighting up, like a window when someone throws back the shutters on a winter morning.

"*Oh*," said Seren Vey. "*Oh, my boys. You came down.*"

And behind her, around her, above her, so close Lex could feel it in his teeth, the dark said one word. Small. Frightened. Young. In his brother's old voice.

*[...brother...]*

And far above them, in the great glittering ceiling of the moths, every single moth went still.

And turned.

Toward the lamp.
