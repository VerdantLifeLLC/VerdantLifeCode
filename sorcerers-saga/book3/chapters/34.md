# The Opening

The sun went down on Lantern Night at the bottom of the world, and Antho Vey couldn't see it.

He felt it.

He was sitting on the warm dark under the breach. Where he'd sat for six days. With his back against the little one and the clay lamp in his lap and his face turned up toward the thin gold line of the scar overhead. The underside of the world. The first wound. Mended once, five years ago, by three of the blood.

And through it, all day, light.

Not much. A seam of it. Gold. Warm. Coming down through the scar like sunlight through a crack in a barn roof. And with it, sound. Faint. Far. Muffled through rubble and rock.

Voices. Thousands of them. Hammers. Carts. Someone singing.

*For the little one. From.*

*For the little one. From.*

All day. He'd heard it all day.

And then, a little after what must have been the middle of the afternoon, the gold seam above him had begun to change color. Gold to amber. Amber to red. Red to a deep soft violet, like the sky an hour after sunset. Like the inside of a closed eye.

And then it went dark.

And then, very slowly, it went gold again.

Different gold. Not sun. *Lamplight*. Three hundred and fourteen miles of it. Coming down through the scar from a ring of lanterns round the rim of a word written in the ground.

---

*[...brother...]*

All round him. Close. The whole vast soft weight of it. Pressed against the underside of the world. Against the scar. The way you'd press your face against a window on a winter night, looking out at the lights.

*[...brother, it's dark up there now...]*

*[...but it's not dark...it's LIGHT...it's all light...]*

*[...is it time...]*

Antho put his hand on the warm dark beside him.

*Nearly*, he thought. *Nearly. Just a little longer.*

*[...I'm frightened...]*

*I know*.

*[...the smith said he was frightened too...]*

*He was*, thought Antho. *He told me. Through the Bloodbond. Last night. Sitting on the rubble with Wren.* He looked up at the gold line. *Everyone's frightened. That's all right. You can be frightened and still come up.*

*[...can you...]*

*I did*, thought Antho Vey. *For thirty years. I'm still doing it.*

---

Seraphine was beside him.

With her hand out. Palm up. Eyes shut. Listening. Not to the little one. To the room. To the space above them. To the circle.

"*They're all there*," she said. Very quietly. "*All round. I can hear them. Where the room changes. Where people are standing.*" Her face was very still. "*Thousands. At the rim. With the lanterns.*"

"*And outside*?"

Seraphine was quiet for a moment.

"*Outside it's—*" She stopped. "*Outside there's nothing. No room. The glitter takes up all the room. It's like*—" She shook her head. "*It's like listening to a wall.*"

"*Is it moving*?"

"*Not yet*."

---

Pim was asleep.

Curled up on the warm dark on Antho's other side. In his blue coat. With his knees pulled up and his head on his arms and a hundred iron cups in two baskets beside him, wrapped in straw. He'd filled every one. Over fifty-one days. On Brannoc's cart, on the circle, on the rubble. A hundred rooms you could carry.

He'd brought them down. Antho didn't know why. Pim hadn't said. Pim never said.

Kell was standing.

A little way off. Very straight. In her ruined white robes, gone grey, gone the color of old candle-wax. With her white hair cut short again. With a stick in her hands.

Pim had found it for her. On the twenty-sixth day. Antho didn't know where. There was nothing down here. No trees. No wood. No stone. Just dark and stars and the little one.

But Pim had walked off into the dark one day, alone, for an hour. And come back with a stick. Long. Plain. Dark. Like a shepherd's crook without the crook. And held it out to Kell without a word.

She'd looked at it for a long time.

*Where did you get that*? she'd said.

Pim had looked at the dark. At the little one. And then back at her.

*It made it*, he'd said. *I asked.*

---

"*Vey*," said Kell.

He looked up.

She was looking at the scar. At the gold line overhead. Her thin face lit by it. Very calm.

"*When it goes*," she said. "*When you open it. It'll go up. All of it. Slowly. Like water coming up a well.*" She shifted her grip on the stick. "*And there'll be a time. In the middle. When it's half through. When the front of it's up there in the circle and the back of it's still down here.*"

"*I know*."

"*It can't go back then*," said Kell. "*Not without tearing. And it can't hurry. Not without pulling.*" She looked at him. "*It'll be stuck. In the door. Like a man with one foot over the threshold.*"

Antho didn't say anything.

"*That's when she'll come*," said Isaura Kell. "*Not before. Not after. Then.*" She looked back at the scar. "*When it can't run.*"

---

*[Antho*.]

Not the little one.

*Lex*.

Through the Bloodbond. Faint. Far. Through the rubble and the rock and the scar. His brother's voice. Not words, exactly. The shape of words. The way you hear someone talk through a wall.

*[It's time. Father's here. At the scar. On the floor. Mam's with him.]*

*How is he*?

A pause.

*[Holding on*,] said Lex. *[He's got his hand on the edges. He says he can feel you. Underneath. He says you're in exactly the right place.]*

*And the glitter*?

A longer pause.

*[It's moving*,] said Lex.

---

Antho stood up.

He woke Pim. He didn't have to. The boy was already awake. Already sitting up. With his grey-violet eyes very wide, looking at the scar.

Seraphine stood up. With her notebook in her coat. With her hand still out.

Kell didn't move. She was already standing.

And all round them, the little one went very still.

---

Antho went to the scar.

It wasn't far. A few steps. Up the last of the long slope. To where the warm dark met the underside of the world. To where the gold line was. Overhead. Close now. So close he could reach up and touch it.

He reached up.

And put his hand flat on it.

Warm. Rough. Like the underside of a stone that's been lying in the sun all day. And through it, on the other side, so close, so very close, separated by nothing but a mended wound—

A hand.

Thin. Bone. Brown-spotted. Shaking. Laid flat on the scar from above. On the black floor of the fallen hall. Exactly where Antho's was, from below. Palm to palm. Through the wound.

His father's.

---

*[Antho*.]

Not through the Bloodbond. Not through the little one. Through the scar. Through the hand. Faint. A thread. A breath with words in it.

*[There you are*.]

"*Father*," said Antho. Out loud. To the stone.

*[You're right under where I knelt*,] said his father's voice. *[Exactly. Not an inch off. I can feel it.]* A pause. A breath. *[You'll be very good at this. I told you.]*

"*I don't know how*."

*[Yes, you do*.]

"*I've never opened anything. I've only ever held things shut.*"

*[Then do it the same way*,] said Arion Vey. *[But the other direction.]*

---

Antho closed his eyes.

He stood at the bottom of the world with his hand on the underside of a scar and his father's hand on top of it and the little one pressed against his back, enormous and soft and trembling.

And he thought about doors.

The cave in the Greyspine. Where he'd lived for eight years. With a crack in it he didn't know was there, and his mother holding it from the other side.

The black glass at the bottom of the Hollowmaw. Where he'd said *evening, Mam* every night for three years.

The doorway at the bottom of the Heart. Where he'd stood with his back to the hunger and shut it out with his own body.

The tear in the gold room. Where he'd stood while the little one screamed and his Clarity went to thirty-eight and something old in his skull told him to squeeze.

The kitchen doorway at the Keep. Where he'd turned round. And shouldn't have.

Kell's door. Where he'd knocked.

*Every door I've ever stood in*, he thought, *I've been holding something back. Keeping something out. Keeping something in.*

*I've never once stood in a door to let something through*.

---

*Ask*, his father had said. On the black floor at Duskhold. In the dark. With both their hands on the scar. *Ask it to open. Ask the little one to come up. Ask the net to let it through.*

*Ask everyone*.

*I tore it alone. You open it with everyone*.

---

He asked.

---

Not the scar. Not first.

The little one.

*Are you ready*? thought Antho.

*[...no...]*

*Will you come anyway*?

A long silence. The whole vast weight of it, pressed against his back. Trembling.

*[...yes...]*

*[...if you're there...if you stay in the door...if you don't go...]*

*I'll stay in the door*, thought Antho Vey. *I'll stay in it the whole time. Until you're through. Until the last of you is up. I won't move.*

*[...promise...]*

*I promise*.

---

Then his father.

*Show me the edges*.

And his father did. Through the scar. Through their two hands, palm to palm. He felt it. His father's hand moving. Very slowly. On the stone above. Tracing. The line. Where it was thick. Where it was thin. Where the old stitches were. Where the gap was, under where he'd knelt, where the edges had never quite met.

*[There*,] said his father. *[That's where it'll go first. It wants to. It's been wanting to for twenty-one years.]*

*Thank you*.

*[Don't pull*,] said Arion Vey. Very faintly. *[Whatever happens. However frightened you are. However slow it is. Don't pull.]*

*I won't*.

---

Then the net.

He didn't know how to ask the net. Nobody did. Nobody held it anymore. Lex had given the Root away. It was nobody's. It just *noticed*.

So he let it notice.

He stood in the doorway. Under the scar. With his hand up and his father's hand on the other side and the little one at his back. And he didn't hide. He didn't use the dark to make himself small. He didn't fold his thread into Shade the way he'd learned in the Greyspine, hiding from Wardens.

He stood there. And let the net see him.

*Threshold Warden*, it had called him. At the Heart. *One who stands in the doorway between dark and dark, and decides.*

*I'm deciding*, thought Antho. *I'm deciding to open it.*

*Will you let it through*?

The net didn't answer. It never answered. It just noticed.

But he felt it notice.

---

And then everyone.

He reached for his brother. Through the Bloodbond. Up. Through the rock. Through the rubble.

*Lex*.

*[I'm here*.]

*I need everyone*.

*[What*?]

*I need them to ask*, thought Antho. *Everyone. Round the circle. At the lanterns. I need them to ask it to come up. Out loud. All at once.*

A silence. Long. Through the stone.

*[How*?]

*I don't know*, thought Antho. *You're the one who makes speeches.*

And through the Bloodbond, far off, he felt his brother laugh.

---

He didn't hear what Lex said. He'd never know. He'd ask, afterward, and Lex would go red and say *I don't remember, something stupid, something about porridge*, and Wren would say *it was beautiful, actually, you idiot*, and Lex would go redder.

He heard what came after.

---

It started at the rubble.

Right above him. Through the scar. A voice. Then two. Then twenty. He couldn't hear the words. Just the sound. Like a crowd at a fair, when someone says *quiet, quiet, listen*, and the noise drops, and then—

*One word*.

He couldn't make it out. Muffled. Through rock. A single word, said by hundreds of voices at once.

And then it *spread*.

Out from the rubble. Along the circle. He felt it go. Through the ground. Through the trench. Through the walls of baked grey earth that his brother had walked three hundred and fourteen miles with his hand on. East and west and round. The word. Passing from lantern to lantern. From person to person. The way a song goes round a room.

Faster. And faster. All the way round.

Until the whole circle was saying it.

Three hundred and fourteen miles of people, kneeling at the rim of a word written in the ground, with their hands on lanterns they'd given away, saying one word. All at once. Over and over.

And this time, he heard it.

Not with his ears. Through the scar. Through his father's hand. Through the little one, pressed against his back, going very, very still.

*Come*.

---

*[...oh...]*

The little one.

*[...oh...they're saying...]*

*[...brother, they're all saying...]*

*[...they're saying COME...]*

---

The scar opened.

---

Not like a tear. Not like a wound. Not like rock breaking.

Like a hand opening.

From the middle. From the place under where his father had knelt. Where the edges had never quite met. Antho felt it go. Under his palm. The stone going soft. Going warm. Going *away*. Not breaking. Not crumbling. Just—becoming room. The way the black glass of the Hollowmaw door had stopped being there when Kell said *come in*.

A gap. Between his hand and his father's.

He felt his father's hand slip through it.

Down. Into the dark. Thin. Bone. Shaking. Reaching.

And Antho caught it.

---

They held hands through the opening breach.

Father and son. One above, one below. One on a black floor in a fallen hall, on a litter, a hundred years in a drawer. One at the bottom of the world with a clay lamp and something the size of a country at his back.

Holding hands. Through the door.

And the door kept opening.

---

It went slowly. Like his father said. Like he'd asked. Not pulled. Not torn. Asked. The gap widening. From the middle. Along the line of the old scar. A foot. A yard. Ten yards. Following the edges. Following the place where it had always been thin.

And light came down.

Gold. Warm. Lamplight. Three hundred and fourteen miles of it. Pouring down through the opening breach into the deep like water into a well. Into the dark. Onto him. Onto Seraphine, with her hand out and her face turned up and her mouth open. Onto Pim, standing, small, with a basket of cups in each hand. Onto Kell, very straight, with her stick.

Onto the little one.

---

*[...LIGHT...]*

Not frightened.

*[...it's LIGHT...it's all the light...it's all for ME...]*

And it began to rise.

---

Antho felt it go past him.

Not through him. Past. Around. The way water goes round a stone in a stream. The way a crowd goes round a man standing in a doorway. The whole vast soft dark of it, folded round the deep, beginning to move. Up. Toward the light. Toward the opening. Toward the circle.

Slowly.

So slowly. Like dough rising. Like the tide coming in. Like the first green coming up through a field in spring, when you can't tell if it's grass or wishful thinking.

He held the door.

He stood in the opening with his father's hand in his and the light pouring down on him and the little one rising round him on every side. Soft. Warm. Enormous. Brushing past him. The way a cat brushes your legs when it goes past you into a room.

*[...brother...]*

*I'm here*.

*[...you're in the door...]*

*I'm in the door*.

*[...don't move...]*

*I won't move*.

Seraphine was crying.

He could hear it. Beside him. Below the opening. With her hand out and the light on her face and the little one rising past her, round her, brushing her hair, her sleeves, her outstretched palm.

"*I can hear it*," she was saying. Over and over. "*Antho. I can hear it go up. I can hear the room filling. The circle. I can hear it filling.*" She was laughing. "*It's like—it's like a jug being filled. At a pump. You can hear the note change. As it fills. Higher. And higher.*"

He could hear it too.

Not the note. The *voices*. Above. Through the opening. Getting louder. Not saying *come* anymore. Saying other things. Shouting. Crying. Laughing.

*There. Look. There. Look at it. Look*.

---

*[Antho*.]

Lex. Through the Bloodbond. Close now. So close.

*[Antho. It's coming up. I can see it.]*

*What does it look like*?

His brother didn't answer for a long time.

*[Like the sky*,] said Lex at last. *[Like night. Coming up out of the ground. Soft. Dark. With stars in it. Shade's stars. Filling the circle. Slowly. From the breach. Spreading out.]* A pause. *[Like ink in water. Like the first dark of evening. Like*—]

He stopped.

*[It's beautiful*,] said Lex Vey. *[Antho. It's the most beautiful thing I've ever seen.]*

---

And then Seraphine stopped laughing.

All at once. Like a door shutting. She went rigid beside him. With her hand out. Palm up. Her face turned up toward the opening. Toward the light.

"*Antho*," she said.

He didn't look at her. He couldn't. He was holding the door. He was holding his father's hand.

"*Antho, the wall*." Her voice was very small. "*The glitter. Outside the circle. I can hear it. I couldn't before. I could only hear the wall. It took up all the room.*" She swallowed. "*It's not a wall anymore.*"

"*What is it*?"

"*It's moving*," said Seraphine. "*All of it. All the way round. From every side. At once.*" Her hand was shaking. "*It's coming in.*"

---

Above, through the opening, the voices changed.

Not *look*. Not *there*.

*Lanterns*. *Lanterns. Hold the lanterns. Black heat. Black heat. Ashari. Ashari to the rim*.

*They're coming*.

---

He felt it hit.

Not the little one. The *circle*. The rim. Three hundred and fourteen miles of lanterns, given, set down on the edge of a word written in the ground.

The moths hit it.

All at once. From every side. Like a wave. Like the tide coming in over the flats, so fast, so silent, round your ankles before you've seen it come. The great grey-gold wall that had sat on the horizon for thirty days. Coming in. Drifting. Pouring. Over the grey. Over the Dimmed in their rows. Over the families at the south edge with their second lanterns.

To the rim.

To the light.

---

And he felt them *fall*.

He'd never forget it. He'd feel it for the rest of his life. Through the ground. Through the walls of the trench. Through the opening breach. Through his father's hand.

Thousands of them. Tens of thousands. Hitting the ring of lanterns. Pressing against them. Drinking.

And getting nothing.

*Given*. Every one. *For the little one. From*. Set down. Not waiting. Not left on for someone who wasn't home. Given away. To something that was coming. That was *here*.

They couldn't drink it. Any of it. Not one lantern in three hundred and fourteen miles.

They pressed. And drank. And got nothing. And pressed harder. And got nothing.

And fell.

Like snow. Like leaves. Onto the rim. Into the trench. Grey-gold. Glittering. Heaps of them. Drifts. Walls.

And behind them, more. Pouring in. Pressing. Falling. Pressing.

And in the dark round every lantern, invisible, rippling, the Ashari and the smiths. Black heat. Burning every one that came too close.

---

"*It's holding*," said Seraphine. Breathless. "*Antho. The rim. It's holding. They can't get through. They can't drink the lanterns.*" Her voice cracked. "*Ivy was right. Ivy was right.*"

He held the door.

The little one was still rising. Past him. Round him. Slowly. Up. Through the opening. Into the circle. He could feel it. Half of it now. More. The front of it up there in the light, spreading, filling the word written in the ground, going out toward the rim in every direction like the first dark of evening.

And the back of it still down here. Round him. Round Seraphine and Pim and Kell. In the deep.

Half through.

*It can't go back then*, Kell had said. *Not without tearing. And it can't hurry. Not without pulling.*

*It'll be stuck. In the door*.

*That's when she'll come*.

---

He looked at Kell.

She was standing very straight. Under the opening. In the light pouring down. With her stick in both hands. Not looking at him.

Looking up.

At the opening. At the light. At the little one rising through it, soft and dark and enormous and starred, filling the hole in the world.

At something else. In the light. Coming *down*.

---

He saw it.

At the very top of the opening. Where the breach met the fallen hall. Where his father lay on the black floor with his hand through the gap.

A shape.

Not falling. Not flying. *Leaning*. Into the opening. Into the light. The way a moth leans into a gap in a shutter. Folded. Tight. Patient.

Tall. Thin. Grey-gold. Glowing. With silver hair to her knees and something on her back, folded close, like a cloak, patterned with rings and whorls and eyes.

And two lights at the front of her. Gold. Warm. Like windows.

She came down through the opening breach like a moth coming down a chimney. Into the light. Past the rising dark. Down. Toward the back of the little one. Toward the part still in the deep. Still round him. Still half through.

Toward the middle.

Toward the warmest, deepest, softest place. Where two little lights lay curled together, singing in tune.

With her hands cupped.

---

*[...brother...]*

All round him. The little one. Rising. Half through. Stuck. Unable to go back. Unable to hurry.

Feeling her come.

*[...brother...]*

*[...brother, it's HER...]*

*[...she's coming DOWN...she's coming down the door while I'm IN it...]*

*[...I can't MOVE...]*

*[...I can't move I can't move I can't*—]

---

Antho held the door.

He held it with his feet set on the warm dark and his hand in his father's through the opening and his other hand round the clay lamp, his mother's mother's, lit, given, *his*.

He didn't move.

He'd promised.

*I'll stay in the door. The whole time. Until you're through. I won't move*.

And the Moth Queen came down through the light toward the heart of the thing he was holding the door for. And he couldn't stop her. Because he couldn't move. Because if he moved, the door would close, and the little one would tear.

He'd never been so frightened in his life.

---

And then Kell moved.

Past him. Under the opening. Into the light. In her ruined robes. With her white hair. Very straight.

With a stick.

She went up.

Antho didn't know how. There was nothing to climb. There was only the light pouring down and the dark rising up. She went up anyway. Into the opening. Along the rising dark. The way you'd walk up a hill. The little one carried her. Folded round her feet. Lifting her. The way it had carried them all up from the gold room, folded like a letter.

Up. Toward the Moth Queen. Coming down.

She stopped. Halfway. In the middle of the opening. In the middle of the light. In the place where the front of the little one was in the circle and the back of it was in the deep.

In the door.

And turned round. And faced up. Toward the shape coming down.

And lifted the stick.

---

"*Isaura*," said Maelis.

She'd stopped too. Just above. Leaning. Glowing. With her hands cupped and her lit eyes on the gaunt white-haired woman in the light below her. Blocking the way.

"*Isaura Kell*," she said. Softly. Fondly. "*High Warden. I broke your stick.*"

"*I got another one*."

"*I see*." The Moth Queen tilted her head. "*Where from?*"

"*It made it*," said Isaura Kell. "*For me. Because a boy asked.*"

Maelis looked at the stick. In Kell's bony hands. Long. Plain. Dark.

Something moved in her face.

"*It made you a stick*," she said. Slowly. "*To hold against me.*"

"*Yes*."

"*The thing I drank*," said Maelis. "*Made you a stick. To stop me drinking it again.*"

"*Yes*," said Kell. "*It's very young. It doesn't know any better.*" Her pale eyes didn't waver. "*Neither do I, apparently.*"

---

Below them, at the bottom of the opening, Antho stood in the door with his father's hand in his and the lamp in the other and couldn't breathe.

*[...the Warden...]* said the little one. Very small. *[...the Warden's in the door...]*

*[...she's standing in the door in front of me...]*

*[...like she said...like she ALWAYS said...]*

"*Yes*," whispered Antho. Out loud. To no one. To the dark. "*Yes, she is.*"

---

And Maelis smiled.

Not lovely. Not sad. Not fond. Not patient.

*Hungry*.

"*Oh, Isaura*," she said. "*You stupid, stubborn, beautiful woman. You've learned nothing. You still think the person standing in the door decides what comes through.*"

She spread her wings.

All of them. Enormous. Filling the opening. Grey-gold. Glowing. Every painted eye on every wing turned down. Toward Kell. Toward the light. Toward the soft dark rising.

"*I don't need to come through*," said the Moth Queen.

And behind her, above her, out of the circle, out of the light, out of the great grey-gold wall that had broken itself against three hundred and fourteen miles of given lanterns—

All of them. Every one that was left. Not to the rim. Not to the lanterns. Down. Through the breach. Past Maelis. Past Kell. Like smoke down a chimney when the wind turns. A river of grey-gold glitter, pouring down into the deep.

Toward the back of the little one. Still in the dark. Still round Antho. Still half through.

Toward the middle.

Toward the two little lights.

And from above, through the opening, through the light, through his father's hand in his, very faint, Antho heard his father say one word.

Not to him.

To someone above. On the black floor of the fallen hall. Beside the litter.

*Seren*.

And then he felt his father's hand go slack.

And slip out of his.
