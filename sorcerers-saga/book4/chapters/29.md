# The Knock

He knocked at noon.

Seren had been waiting since dawn. Since before dawn. Since the footsteps stopped at the bottom of the hill, in the night, and didn't start again, and she lay beside her husband in the dark with her hand on his chest and listened to the silence where they'd been.

She'd got up at first light.

She'd lit the fire. She'd made the porridge. She'd fed Arion three spoonfuls, which was all he'd take, and wiped his mouth, and propped him on his pillows, and combed his hair, and put his spectacles on the blanket by his hand, folded, face down.

She'd filled the kettle.

She'd put out two cups. The chipped blue one with the crack down the handle. And another. The good one. White. Brannoc had made it at the Forge, in the winter, the first thing he'd ever made that wasn't iron, and it was lopsided and too thick and she loved it.

She'd taken the tin down off the shelf. Hollis Penn's tin. The good kind.

And then she'd sat down on the oak ramp outside the blue door. In her bare feet. In the sun. With her hands in her lap.

And waited.

---

She heard him come up through the birch wood.

Not footsteps. Not at first. Heat. A warmth, rolling up the track ahead of him, through the trees, the way the heat from a forge rolls across a yard when someone opens the door. She felt it on her face.

Then the light.

Gold. Through the birches. Not sunlight; it was noon, the sun was overhead, the birch wood was green and dappled and ordinary. *Another* light. Lower. Warmer. Coming up the track between the white trunks like a lantern carried by someone very tall.

And where it passed, the birches went gold.

She watched it. From the ramp. One tree. Then the next. The white bark going gold. The green leaves going gold. The whole tree, trunk and branch and leaf, shining. Still. A gold birch. And then the one beside it.

A gold road. Coming up through the wood. Toward her.

---

Then the footsteps.

*Thud*. *Thud*.

Not hurrying.

Brannoc was in the clearing. She'd told him to go home. She'd told him at dawn, and again at breakfast, and again an hour ago. *Go home. Go to Hedda. Go to the Forge. There's nothing you can do.*

He'd said, *I'm not leaving you*.

He was standing by the stream now. Enormous. Bald. With his arms folded across his chest and his gold fingertips catching the light and his face like a block of grey stone. Between the track and the ramp. Between the light and her.

"*Brannoc*," she said.

"*I know*," he said. Not turning round. "*I'm not going to fight him. I'm not daft. I've heard what happens.*" His jaw worked. "*I'm just going to stand here.*"

"*Somebody has to stand somewhere*," said Seren.

"*Aye*," said Brannoc Holt. "*Something like that.*"

---

He came out of the birch wood into the clearing.

---

Ada Fell had said it. Elowen had said it. Wren had said it, white-faced, coming back from a village in the western shires with a baby on her chest.

None of them had said it right.

He was the biggest thing Seren had ever seen.

Taller than the cottage door. Taller than the lintel by a head and more. Broader than Brannoc. Broader than two of Brannoc. A beard of gold to the middle of his chest. Gold hair to his shoulders. Skin like old ivory. A robe of white and gold that swept the grass behind him and turned it gold as it passed.

A crown of gold leaves. Crooked.

He glowed.

Not like a lamp. Like metal in a crucible. A deep heavy warm light coming up out of him, out of his skin and his beard and his hands, lighting the clearing, lighting the gold birch and the gold woodpile and the gold cart. Lighting Brannoc's face. Lighting hers.

And his eyes.

She didn't look at them. She couldn't. Nobody could. She looked at his beard. At his mouth.

Which was smiling.

---

He stopped at the edge of the clearing.

Not because Brannoc was there. He didn't look at Brannoc. Not yet. He stopped the way a man stops at the top of a hill after a long walk and looks at the view.

He looked at the clearing.

At the stream. At the stepping stones. At the two cottages, one on either side of the water. At the gold birch. At the other cottage, across the stream, with its soaped windows and its east window and a small iron lantern on the sill, gold, kept, beside a candle in a holder with no glass.

At the blue door.

At her.

"*Oh*," said Oszric of the Crowned. Very softly. "*Oh, it's a blue door.*"

---

She didn't stand up.

She'd thought about it. All morning. Whether to stand. Whether to go to the door and put her hand flat on the wood. The way her mother stood. The way every Harrow woman stood, at the end of summer, at the door of every cottage.

She stayed sitting.

On the ramp. In her bare feet. With her hands in her lap.

"*Good afternoon*," said Seren Harrow.

---

The King looked at her for a long moment.

Then he laughed.

Not loud. Not the great bell of a laugh she'd heard through the rose. Softer. Surprised. Delighted. The laugh of a man who's been told something he didn't expect and likes very much.

"*Good afternoon*," he said. "*Hesper's girl.*"

"*Seren*," said Seren. "*Seren Vey.*"

"*Seren Vey*," said the Gilded King. Trying it. "*You married. Hes never married. None of her girls did. Did they?*"

"*No*."

"*But you did*."

"*I did*," said Seren. "*Twice. To the same man. I don't remember the first time.*"

---

He came across the clearing.

*Thud*. *Thud*. Not hurrying. The grass going gold under his feet in a widening path. Brannoc didn't move. He stood by the stream with his arms folded.

The King walked past him.

Not round him. Past. Close. Close enough that Seren saw the old smith flinch from the heat, saw the sweat start on his bald head. The King didn't touch him. He didn't look at him.

Then he stopped.

And turned his head.

And looked down at Brannoc Holt.

At his bald head. At his soot-stained beard. At his arms, folded. At his hands.

At the gold on the tips of his fingers.

"*Oh*," said Oszric. "*Orrin's. At the village. You put your hands over a child.*"

Brannoc didn't answer.

"*I heard*," said the King. "*He told me. He tells me everything.*" He looked at the gold fingertips. "*They'll never burn now. Did you know? You could put your hands in a forge.*"

"*I know*," said Brannoc. "*I do. Every day.*"

The King looked at him for a long moment.

"*That's beautiful*," he said. Very quietly. "*You've found a use for it.*"

And walked on.

---

He stopped at the foot of the ramp.

Seren didn't move.

She sat on the oak planks in her bare feet and looked up at him. Up. A long way up. At the gold beard. At the crooked crown. At the light coming off him like heat off a forge.

He looked down at her.

"*May I come in*?" said the Gilded King.

---

"*No*," said Seren.

---

He smiled.

"*Hes said that*," he said. "*To everything. When she was small. May I have your doll, Hes? No. May I sit with you, Hes? No. Will you come and see the pool, Hes?*" His voice was very gentle. "*No. Not me. Not ever.*"

"*She was right*."

"*She was right*," agreed the King. "*She was always right. It was the worst thing about her.*" He looked at the blue door behind her. "*Like your husband. Mae said he was always right. She said it was very inconvenient.*"

"*He's asleep*."

"*He's not asleep*," said Oszric of the Crowned. "*He's lying on three pillows under a boarded window with his spectacles face down on the blanket and his eyes open, listening to every word.*" He tilted his great head. "*I can hear his heart. From here. Like a little clock. Slowing.*"

Seren's hands tightened in her lap.

"*He can't come out*," she said.

"*I know*," said the Gilded King. "*That's why I came to him.*"

---

He sat down.

On the grass. At the foot of the ramp. Like a mountain settling. With his great gold hands on his knees and his white and gold robe spreading out round him on the grass, turning it gold.

Level with her. Almost. His head was still higher than hers. But he'd sat down to be level.

Seren didn't know what to do with that.

"*Is that tea*?" said the King.

She looked at him.

"*I can smell it*," he said. "*Through the door. On the table. The good kind. In a tin.*" His mouth twitched in his beard. "*You bought it for me. Didn't you. You knew I was coming.*"

"*You said*," said Seren. "*Through the rose. You said you'd come to my door. For tea.*"

"*And you bought tea*."

"*I'm a Harrow*," said Seren. "*We're very practical.*"

---

He laughed again.

The real one, this time. Deep. Warm. Like a bell. It rolled across the clearing and the birches shook with it and a gold leaf fell off a gold birch and didn't flutter, *dropped*, straight down, like a coin.

"*Oh*," he said. When he'd finished. Wiping his eyes. His great gold hand coming away wet. "*Oh, I'd forgotten. I'd forgotten what it was like. Talking to one of you.*" He looked at her. "*Hes used to make me laugh like that. At dinner. When she was twelve. Before.*"

"*Before she said no*."

"*Before she said no*," said the King. "*To the pool. To me. To all of us.*" He was quiet for a moment. "*She never spoke to me again. Not one word. Not on the shingle. Not ever.*" He looked down at his hands. "*Nine hundred years and I can still hear her laughing at dinner. And I've never heard her say my name since she was sixteen.*"

---

Seren didn't say anything.

She didn't want to feel sorry for him.

She'd sat on the floor by the hearth listening to Antho tell about a gold woman on a pillow. She'd listened to Ada Fell tell about Roole in the gate. She'd watched Wren come home white-faced from a village where a boy called Pip held a gold grandmother who couldn't hold him back.

She didn't want to.

She did anyway. A little. Sitting on the ramp in the sun. Looking at the biggest man she'd ever seen, sitting in the grass with tears on his face, talking about his little sister at dinner.

She did anyway.

---

"*Where's the child*?" said the Gilded King.

Seren didn't answer.

He didn't seem to expect her to. He sat in the grass and looked across the stream. At the other cottage. At the soaped windows. At the gold lantern on the sill.

"*She's gone*," he said. "*Isn't she. I could smell her from the hill. For two days. Like bread. Like something new.*" He breathed in. Long. Deep. Through his nose. "*And then this morning I couldn't.*"

"*She's gone*," said Seren.

"*With her mother*."

"*With her mother*."

"*The Mistwalker*." The King's mouth twitched. "*She shot an arrow at me. In the western shires. Off the wind. It was a very good shot.*" He looked at Seren. "*Where did they go?*"

"*I don't know*," said Seren.

It was true. She'd made sure of it. She'd made Wren promise not to tell her. *Not me. Not anyone. Not even Arion. If I don't know, I can't say. If I can't say, nobody can make me.*

The King looked at her.

She felt it. Even without looking at his eyes. A heat. A weight. Like the sun on the back of your neck in high summer.

"*You don't*," he said. Slowly. Wonderingly. "*You really don't. You made sure you wouldn't.*"

"*I'm a Harrow*," said Seren.

---

He looked at her for a long time.

Then he turned his head. North. Toward the hill behind the clearing. Toward the top of the birch wood. Where a woman with a baby on her chest had stopped, two days ago, at the edge of the trees, and turned round, and held up one gold hand so it shone.

He breathed in.

Long. Slow. Deep.

"*There*," said the Gilded King. Very softly. "*There it is. Faint. Two days old. Going north. Toward the mountains.*"

Seren went cold.

"*Bread*," said Oszric. "*And wind. And something new.*" He smiled. "*She can't hide that, your Mistwalker. Not from me. Not with a child like that on her chest. It's like trying to hide a fire in a hayloft.*"

---

He stood up.

Slowly. Like a mountain rising. The grass round him gold in a wide circle where he'd sat. He stood at the foot of the ramp and looked down at her.

"*I'll go and find her*," he said. "*The child. I'll walk. I'm good at walking. I've walked two hundred and forty miles since midsummer.*"

"*She'll keep moving*," said Seren.

"*I know*."

"*She'll never stop*. *She'll never stay. She'll go from valley to valley and cottage to cottage and you'll never catch her.*" Her voice was shaking. She didn't let it stop her. "*Twenty generations of Harrow women ran from you, Oszric. Twenty generations. And you never found one.*"

"*I never looked*," said the Gilded King.

Seren stopped.

"*I was asleep*," he said. Gently. "*For nine hundred years. In silk. In a hall. On the other side of the edge.*" He tilted his head. "*Your women ran from nothing, Seren Vey. They ran from a dream. They ran from a song.*" He smiled. "*I'm awake now. And I'm very, very good at finding things.*"

---

"*And your husband*," said the King.

He looked past her. At the blue door. At the dim room beyond it. At the bed under the boarded window, and the old man on three pillows with his eyes open.

"*Arion Vey*," said Oszric. Raising his voice. Not much. Just enough. "*Warden-Architect. Mae's clever one.*"

Silence from the cottage.

Then, very faint, a thread of a voice—

"*Good afternoon*."

---

The King smiled.

"*Good afternoon*," he said. "*I'm sorry to have come at such a bad time.*"

"*It's always a bad time*," said Arion. From the bed. Through the open door. Faint. Dry. "*At my age. You'll find that.*"

"*I won't*," said the Gilded King. "*That's rather the point.*"

A pause. From the cottage. A breath. In. Catch. Out.

"*No*," said Arion Vey. "*I suppose you won't.*"

---

"*I'll come back*," said Oszric. "*For tea. When I've found the child.*" He looked at the blue door. "*I'd like to talk to you. Properly. Sitting down. Mae said you were the cleverest man she'd ever met. I'd like to see for myself.*"

"*I'll be here*," said Arion.

"*Will you*?"

A long pause.

"*I'll try*," said Arion Vey.

The King laughed. Softly. In his beard.

"*Don't die before I get back*," he said. "*I mean it. I'd be very disappointed.*" He looked at Seren. "*And when I come back, if you're very tired, if your breath's catching and not starting again, if your wife's lying beside you in the dark counting*—"

"*No*," said Seren.

"*I'll offer*," said Oszric. Gently. "*That's all. I'll offer. Like I offered everyone. And you can say no.*" He looked at her. "*You'll say no.*"

"*Yes*."

"*Hes would have*," said the Gilded King. "*Hes always did.*"

---

He turned round.

And walked away.

North. Not back down the track. North. Across the clearing. Past the stream. Past the other cottage with its gold lantern on the sill. Up the hill. Into the birch wood. Toward the top, where a woman with a baby had stopped two days ago and turned round.

The birches went gold as he passed.

One by one. Up the hill. A gold road. Going north.

Seren watched until she couldn't see him anymore.

Then she watched the gold. Going up through the trees. Getting smaller. Further. Into the mountains.

---

Brannoc came and stood at the foot of the ramp.

He looked at the gold circle in the grass where the King had sat. At the gold path going up the hill. At her.

"*You didn't let him in*," he said.

"*No*."

"*You didn't stand up*."

"*No*."

"*You sat on your step in your bare feet and said good afternoon*," said Brannoc Holt. "*To that.*" His voice was very rough. "*Your mam would have thrown a pan.*"

"*I didn't have one*," said Seren. "*I gave it to Antho. To take to the Knot.*"

Brannoc looked at her for a long moment.

Then he laughed.

It came out of him like a landslide. Sudden. Huge. Shaking. He stood at the foot of the ramp in the sun with his gold fingertips and his bald head and laughed until he had to sit down on the grass.

And Seren, on the ramp, after a moment, laughed too.

---

The tea went cold on the table.

She didn't throw it out. She left it. Two cups. The blue one and the white one. Full. Going cold. And the tin beside them. Open.

"*He'll be back*," said Arion. That night. From his pillows.

"*I know*."

"*He'll find her*."

"*He won't*," said Seren. "*She's Wren. She's a tracker. She knows how to hide from things that hunt.*" She sat down on the edge of the bed. "*She'll keep moving.*"

"*And when she can't*?"

Seren didn't answer.

---

The summer went.

Long. Hot. Then shorter. Then cooler. The birch wood was gold on the hill where he'd walked through it. A gold path up through the trees. Shining. Still. It didn't go brown in the autumn like the rest of the wood. It didn't drop its leaves.

It would never drop its leaves.

---

Ravens came.

Not from Wren. Never from Wren. She'd said she wouldn't. She'd kept her word.

From Elowen. From Seraphine. From Keepers on the roads in the north. From farmers in the Greyspine valleys, who'd seen a gold light walking through the mountains at night.

He was following her.

Slowly. Patiently. Up one valley and down the next. Through the high passes. Along the rivers. Stopping in every village. Sitting on the edge of every well. Listening to everyone who came.

Keeping the ones who asked.

And then moving on. North. East. West. Following a smell like bread on a wind nobody else could feel.

He never caught her.

---

"*She's leading him*," said Seraphine.

She'd come down from the Keep. Again. On Ashka. With her satchel of books and ink on her fingers and her spectacles. She was sitting at the table in the blue-door cottage with a map spread out in front of her. Arion's map. The one from behind the bookcase. The world from the inside.

She'd put pins in it.

Little ones. With red heads. One for every village the ravens had named.

"*Look*," she said.

Seren looked.

The pins went north from the Vale. Into the Greyspine. And then they went round. In a great slow loop. Up one side of the mountains and down the other. And round again. Wider. Up into the far north, past the Teeth, into the high country where nobody lived. And round.

"*She's leading him in circles*," said Seraphine. "*Away from the villages. Away from people. Into the high country, where there's nobody for him to stop and visit.*" She touched a pin. "*She's making him walk.*"

"*Why*?"

Seraphine looked at her.

"*To keep him away*," she said. "*From here. From Harrow. From Arion.*" She looked down at the map. "*She's buying time, Seren. For the boys. Till they come back through the door.*"

---

Seren looked at the pins for a long time.

At the slow wide loops in the high country. At the valleys where nobody lived. At a girl from Thistledown with a baby on her chest and a gold hand, walking. Never stopping. Never staying. Drawing a gold king behind her round and round the mountains like a kite on a string.

"*She's not a Harrow*," said Seren. Very quietly.

"*No*," said Seraphine.

"*She's doing it anyway*," said Seren Harrow. "*Better than any of us ever did.*"

---

He told her one a day.

Still. Every morning. Fewer words now. Further apart. His hand slack in hers by the end. Sleeping in the middle.

She didn't mind. She waited. She'd sit on the edge of the bed holding his hand until he woke, and then he'd open his eyes and look at her and say, *where was I*.

And she'd tell him.

The first time Antho walked.

Lex's first word. Which was *hot*. Which was the forge. Which was Brannoc, laughing so hard he dropped a horseshoe on his foot.

A night at the cottage when the roof leaked and they slept under the table with the boys between them and the rain coming down and she sang the old verses, the forbidden ones, very softly, because it was the only song that was long enough.

*You sang them*? said Seren. *To the boys? Mam made me promise never*—

*You sang them to me*, said Arion. *The boys were asleep. You thought I was too.* He smiled. Very faint. *I wasn't. I was lying under the table listening to you sing a song about nine kings and queens who drank a Well dry and went into the grey. And I thought, I'm married to a woman who knows songs nobody else in the world knows.*

---

He stopped getting up.

At the end of summer. Not that he'd been getting up much. An hour in the chair, some days. Ilsa's orders. Seren and Brannoc lifting him between them, slowly, carefully, from the bed to the chair, and wheeling him to the west window, and taking the board off one pane so he could see the birch wood.

He stopped wanting to.

*It's all right*, he said. When she asked. *I can see it from here. I can imagine it.* He looked at the boards over the window. At the two lines of charcoal in her square hand. *I've got a very good imagination.*

---

The leaves came down.

Not on the gold path. On the rest of the wood. Brown and yellow and red, turning, drifting down onto the stream, onto the stepping stones, onto the oak ramp. She swept them every morning. They came back every night.

The days got shorter.

Seraphine came again. With her map. With more pins.

"*He's turning*," she said.

---

Seren looked at the map.

The pins had been going round. In the high country. In wide slow loops. For two months.

The last three weren't.

The last three went south.

Straight. Not looping. Not wandering. Straight down out of the high country. Through the passes. Toward the Greyspine. Toward the Vale.

"*He's stopped following her*," said Seraphine. Very quietly.

"*He's lost her*?"

"*I don't think so*," said Seraphine. "*I think he knows exactly where she is. I think he's always known.*" She touched the last pin. "*I think he's just stopped.*"

"*Why*?"

Seraphine didn't answer.

She didn't need to. Seren looked at the straight line of pins coming down out of the mountains toward the Vale, and she knew.

---

"*Because Harrow women never go back*," said Arion.

They both turned.

He was lying on his pillows with his eyes half open. Listening. The way he always listened. Without moving.

"*He's worked it out*," he said. Faintly. "*She'll never stop. She'll keep moving. Twenty generations. It's in the child. He could follow her for a hundred years.*" A breath. In. Catch. Out. "*So he's stopped following.*"

"*And come here*," said Seren.

"*And come here*," said her husband. "*Because he knows something about Harrow women.*" A faint smile. "*They never go back.*"

He looked at her.

"*But their men do*," said Arion Vey.

---

Seren didn't understand.

Not for a moment. She sat on the edge of the bed and looked at him and didn't understand.

And then she did.

*Lex*.

*Coming back through the door at the edge of the world. Thinking it's the end of summer. Coming home. To Harrow. To find Wren.*

*And Wren comes back to find him*.

*Wren's not a Harrow*.

*Wren will come back*.

"*He's going to wait*," said Seren. "*Here. For them.*"

"*For tea*," said Arion. "*He did say.*"

---

Seraphine sent the dragons west a week later.

Vharuun and Ashka. Both of them. From the high pasture above the birch wood, where they'd been sleeping all summer, curled round boulders like cats round warm stones.

She stood in front of them in the clearing with her map under her arm and her spectacles on her nose and a letter in each hand. She tied one to each dragon. To a horn. With grey thread.

*Ten to one*, said the letters. In her small neat hand. *Lantern Night is in seven days. He's at Harrow. Fly.*

"*Greyfall*," she told the dragons. "*The door. Wait for them. In the snow. As long as it takes.*"

Vharuun looked at her.

One molten eye. Ancient. Patient.

A picture. Clear. Dropped into her head like a stone into a well.

A sheep.

"*Yes*," said Seraphine Ilvane. "*Fine. Four. Roole's quartermaster can bill me.*"

---

She told Seren afterward. Laughing. Crying. Both. Sitting on the ramp in the cold.

"*Roole's quartermaster*," she said. "*Roole's gold. In a gate. With Fenn's staff.*" She wiped her face. "*There isn't a quartermaster. There isn't anybody. They all went east.*" She looked at the dragons, going. Two shapes against the grey autumn sky. West. "*I promised a dragon four sheep from a man who can't move.*"

"*He'd have paid*," said Seren.

Seraphine looked at her.

"*Roole*," said Seren. "*He'd have paid. He paid for six already. Lex told me. He wrote to complain about it.*" She looked west. "*He'd have complained. And then he'd have paid.*"

---

The King came back on the fourth day before Lantern Night.

Seren heard him in the night.

She was lying beside Arion with her hand on his chest. In the dark. Counting.

In. Catch.

Catch.

Out.

It took longer now. The catch. Every breath. She'd lie there with her hand on his chest and wait. And wait. And feel her own heart stop with his breath. And then it would go out. And then in.

And she'd breathe again.

---

*Thud*.

From the top of the hill. From the birch wood. From the gold path.

*Thud*. *Thud*.

Coming down.

Not hurrying.

---

She didn't get up.

She lay beside her husband in the dark with her hand on his chest and listened to the Gilded King come down through the birch wood.

*Thud*. *Thud*.

Into the clearing.

Across it.

To the foot of the ramp.

And stop.

---

She waited for the knock.

It didn't come.

She lay in the dark and listened. To the stream. To the wind in the birches. To her husband's breath. In. Catch. Catch. Out.

No knock.

She got up.

---

She went to the door. Barefoot. In her nightdress. Across the cold flags.

She didn't open it.

She put her hand flat on the wood. The blue wood. The way her mother had stood at the doors of twenty-two cottages. The way Hesper must have stood. The way every Harrow woman had stood for nine hundred years at the end of every summer.

Listening.

He was there. On the other side. She could feel the heat through the wood. Like a forge. Like the sun.

He wasn't knocking.

He was sitting down.

---

She heard it. The creak of the oak ramp. The settling. A mountain coming to rest. On the step. On the other side of the blue door.

And then nothing.

Just the heat. Through the wood. And the light, gold, coming in round the edges of the door, under it, through the cracks, so that the dark room behind her was lit faintly gold.

Waiting.

---

"*Seren*," said a voice behind her.

Faint. A thread. From the bed.

"*He's sitting on the step*," said Seren. Not turning round. Her hand on the door. "*He's not knocking. He's just sitting there.*"

"*Waiting*."

"*Waiting*."

A breath. In. Catch. Out.

"*For the boys*," said Arion.

"*For the boys*. *And Wren. And the baby.*" Her voice was very steady. "*He knows they'll come here. All of them. He knows they'll come home.*"

"*They will*," said her husband.

"*I know*."

"*And I'll be here*," said Arion Vey. "*I told him I'd try.*"

---

She stood at the door a long time.

With her hand flat on the blue wood. With the heat coming through it. With the gold light coming in round the edges.

And then she felt something on her bare feet.

Cold.

She looked down.

There was a gap under the door. There always had been. Brannoc had built it in a hurry, in the snow, in the first two weeks of winter. It let in a draught.

It was letting in something else now.

White. Small. Drifting in under the blue door on the cold night air. Landing on the flags. On her bare toes.

Melting.

---

*Snow*.

The first snow.

She stood in the dark with her hand on the door and the King sitting on the step on the other side of it and the snow drifting in under it onto her feet.

And somewhere far to the west—two hundred and forty miles west, at the edge of the world, at a tall dark door in the grey—

She didn't know it.

She couldn't have.

But she thought, for no reason at all, standing at the door in the dark with snow on her feet—

*They're coming*.

*They're coming now*.

---

She went back to bed.

She lay down beside her husband on top of the blankets with her cold feet and her hand on his chest.

In. Catch. Catch. Catch.

Out.

"*Lantern Night*," said Arion. Very faint. In the dark. "*Four days.*"

"*I know*."

"*Have we got lanterns*?"

"*Lex made one*," said Seren. "*Before he went. For the window. He gave it to Brannoc to keep.*" She swallowed. "*He said, for Lantern Night. In case I'm not back. He said, put it in the west window. Facing the road. So I can see it.*"

"*What does it say*?"

"*I don't know*," said Seren. "*I haven't looked. It's wrapped in a cloth.*"

Arion didn't say anything for a long time.

"*Look*," he said at last. "*On Lantern Night. Not before.*"

"*Why*?"

"*Because I'd like to see your face*," said Arion Vey. "*When you read it.*"

---

Outside, on the step, the King sat in the snow and waited.

Inside, an old man and a hedge-witch lay in the dark and listened to each other breathe.

And the snow came down on the clearing at Harrow. On the stream. On the stepping stones. On the gold birch and the gold woodpile and the gold path going up the hill.

It settled on the gold and didn't melt.

It settled on everything else and did.
