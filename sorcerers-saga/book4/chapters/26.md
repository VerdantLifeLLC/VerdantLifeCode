# The Thorn Garden

The rose opened, and the thorns opened behind it.

Antho watched it happen. Standing at the foot of the black wall in the grey-gold dim with his brother beside him and Pim in Hask's arms. The bud at the height of Lex's chest unfolding, petal by petal, dark red, velvet, the only colour for a hundred miles. And as it opened, the stems round it moved.

Creaking. Unhooking. Drawing back. One from another. Slowly. The way fingers unclench.

A gap. A hand wide. Then an arm. Then a man's width.

A path.

Going in. Into the dark of the thorns. Between black stems as thick as a wrist, hooked with thorns as long as a finger, arching overhead so close they made a tunnel.

And at the end of it, far in, a long way in, grey light.

---

"*She knows we're here*," said Lex.

"*She's been waiting*," said Antho.

He didn't know how he knew. He felt it. The way he felt doors. The thorns were a wall, and a wall is a door that hasn't decided yet. And this one had decided. For them.

*Rosie will betray you*, Ysenne had said. In the stone room. With her glass hands in her lap. *She'll open her thorns. She'll cry. She'll mean it.*

He looked at the path going in.

*And then Oz will ask her where you are. And she'll tell him.*

"*Antho*," said Lex.

"*I know*."

"*It's a trap*," said Hask. From behind them. With Pim against his chest.

"*Everything's been a trap*," said Antho. "*The rose was a trap. The glass palace was a trap. The door at the edge of the world was a trap.*" He looked at the path. "*We walked into all of them.*"

"*And came out*," said Lex.

"*And came out*," agreed Antho.

He went in.

---

The thorns closed behind them.

He heard it. A creak. A rustle. A long dry sigh, like a tired woman sitting down. He looked back and the gap was gone. The path ended a yard behind Hask's heels in a wall of black hooked stems, tighter than before. Tight enough that you couldn't have put your hand through.

Nobody said anything.

They walked.

---

It was dark in the thorns.

Not black. Not the dark of the cellar at Harrow. *Dim*. The grey-gold light of the Court came down through the tangle overhead in thin pale threads, like light through the roof of a barn. It lay on the dust of the path in spots and stripes.

The path wound.

Left. Right. Round. Back on itself. In among the black stems. Antho couldn't see more than a few yards ahead. He couldn't see the tower anymore. He couldn't see anything but thorns and dust and the thin threads of light.

But he could feel it.

Ahead. In the middle. A door. Shut. With something behind it.

And the something was saying *no*.

---

He felt it the way you feel cold air coming down a chimney. A pressure. Not like the pressure of the Court, full and heavy, pushing on the room in his chest. The opposite. A *refusal*. Coming out from the middle of the thorns in slow steady waves. Patient. Quiet. Like a hand laid flat on a door.

*Not you*.

*Not ever*.

He stopped walking.

"*What*?" said Lex.

"*Can you feel it*?"

His brother stood still on the path. In a thread of grey-gold light. Listening.

"*Yes*," he said. Very quietly. "*Like a draught. Like someone saying hush.*"

"*It's the crown*," said Antho.

"*It's saying no*."

"*It's saying no to her*," said Antho. "*To all of them. To everyone who drank.*" He looked at the dark path winding on ahead. "*Not to us. We never drank.*"

---

*[It's beautiful*,] said Ember. Faintly.

Lex told him. His brother's eyes went a little unfocused, the way they always did when the voice in his head spoke.

"*Ember says it's beautiful*," said Lex. "*It says it's the most beautiful no it's ever heard.*" He was quiet. "*It says it sounds like Mam. At the door. At Harrow. When the dust came.*"

---

She was waiting at the bend.

Antho saw her before he understood what he was seeing. A shape. On the path ahead. In a thread of grey light. Small. Thin. Standing very still among the black stems.

At first he thought she was one of them. A thorn. A stem. Thin and dark and twisted, growing up out of the dust.

Then she moved.

---

She was very thin.

That was the first thing. Thin the way a stick is thin. The way a stem is thin in winter, when all the green has gone out of it. Her arms were sticks. Her neck was a stalk. Her face was a skull with skin stretched over it, grey-green, the colour of a leaf that has been lying in a ditch since autumn.

She wore a robe. Grey-gold silk. Rotten. Hanging off her in rags.

Her hair was long and dark and full of dust.

And on her head, a crown.

Not gold. Not glass. Not iron. *Thorns*. Black. Twisted. Dry. A circle of them, woven round on themselves, with the points turned inward. Toward her brow. Where they'd scratched, Antho saw. Thin dark lines on the grey-green skin, old and new.

Her eyes were green.

Not lit. Not like Maelis's, like windows. Not like Oszric's, like furnaces. Just green. Pale. Wet. The colour of new leaves.

The only green thing about her.

---

She looked at them.

At Lex, at the front. At the handprint on his cheek. At Antho beside him. At Hask behind, enormous, with Pim asleep against his chest.

Her hands came up. Pressed together under her chin. The way a child holds her hands when she's very nervous.

"*Oh*," said Rhosyn. "*Oh. You came.*"

---

Her voice was like a stem bending. Thin. Papery. Green. Antho had heard it once before. Through a rose. In the blue-door cottage. His mother had told him. *Like leaves. Like a girl who hasn't spoken to anyone in a very long time and isn't sure she still knows how.*

It was exactly like that.

"*You came*," she said again. Wonderingly. "*She said you might. Seren. Through the rose. She said if one of hers came*—" She stopped. Her green eyes moved. Over Lex's face. Over Antho's. "*Two of hers. Two.*"

"*Lexander Vey*," said Lex. "*Her son.*"

"*Anthoren*," said Antho. "*Her other one.*"

The Thorn-Crowned looked at them for a long time.

"*You've got her hands*," she said to Lex. Very quietly. "*Square. Like a smith's.*" She looked at Antho. "*And you've got her eyes. No.*" She frowned. Her thin brow creasing under the crown of thorns. "*Not hers. Hes's. You've got Hes's eyes. The way she looked at the pool. Like she was listening to something nobody else could hear.*"

---

Antho didn't say anything.

He couldn't. He stood on the path in the thorns and looked at the thin grey-green woman with her hands pressed together under her chin, and felt for her door.

He didn't mean to. He couldn't help it. He was a Threshold Warden. He'd felt for doors in nine thousand gold people on a white road and found none. He felt for hers.

It was there.

---

Not the last door. Not the one everyone gets. That one was gone. Bricked up. Like the kept. Like the cousins at the edge. Like everyone who drank. Smooth and solid and perfect, so you couldn't see where it had been.

But it hadn't been bricked up from the *outside*.

He felt it. The difference. In the kept, the door had been closed by a hand on the forehead. By a finger. By a pinch of gold on the tongue. From outside.

In Rhosyn, it had been closed from inside.

By drinking. By a gold cup at the edge of a pool on the last evening. By her own hands, cupped, lifting the water to her own mouth.

She'd bricked up her own door.

And she knew it. He could feel her knowing it. Every day. For nine hundred years. Standing in front of the place where her door had been, with her hands pressed together under her chin, looking at the bricks.

---

"*You're the warden*," said Rhosyn. Looking at him. "*Aren't you. The one who feels doors. Orrin said.*"

"*Yes*."

"*Can you feel mine*?"

He didn't answer.

"*It's gone*," said the Thorn-Crowned. Very quietly. "*Isn't it. I know it is. I can feel it too. Where it used to be.*" Her green eyes were very wet. "*I've been standing in front of it for nine hundred years.*"

"*Yes*," said Antho.

She looked at him for a long moment.

"*Thank you*," she said. "*For telling me the truth.*"

---

"*She said you'd betray us*," said Lex.

Antho turned and stared at him.

His brother was standing on the path with the basket of Hush-cups on his back and his hand on the bell. Very still. Looking at the Thorn-Crowned.

Rhosyn didn't flinch.

She stood among her thorns with her hands under her chin and looked at Lex. And something in her thin grey-green face—

It didn't break. It *sagged*. The way a stem sags when you've stopped watering it.

"*Ysy*," she said.

"*Yes*."

"*She'd know*," said Rhosyn. "*She's always right. She looks at things and sees what's there.*" Her voice was very small. "*She saw me in the garden. With Hes. Saying oh, Hes, are you sure.*" A pause. "*And she saw me in the hall that night. Drinking.*"

"*Will you*?" said Lex.

The Thorn-Crowned didn't answer for a long time.

"*I don't know*," she said at last.

---

Nobody said anything.

"*I don't want to*," said Rhosyn. "*I don't. I want to take you to the tower and open my thorns and let you up the stair and watch you come down with it and go home.*" Her hands were shaking under her chin. "*I want that more than I've wanted anything since*—" She stopped.

"*Since what*?"

"*Since I wanted to listen*," said Rhosyn. "*To Hes. In the garden. With my roses.*" She looked down at her feet. Bare. Grey-green. Thin as twigs. "*I wanted that so much. I wanted to be the one who listened. And I did. I listened to every word. I cried.*" A pause. "*And then Oz said, come down to the hall, Rosie, it's only water.*"

"*And you went*."

"*I always go*," said the Thorn-Crowned. "*When he calls. I always have. Since I was four and he lifted me onto a pony.*" She looked up. At Lex. At Antho. "*He hasn't called. Not yet. He's in your world. Walking. I can feel him. Far off. Like the sun on the other side of a wall.*"

"*And when he does*?"

Rhosyn didn't answer.

She didn't need to.

---

"*Then why*," said Antho. "*Why open them? Why let us in?*"

The Thorn-Crowned looked at him.

"*Because of the song*," she said.

---

He waited.

"*Hes's girls*," said Rhosyn. "*They sing a song. About us. Seren sang it to me. Through the rose.*" She half-closed her eyes. "*Gold for the eldest. Glass for the vain. Iron for the one who never laughed.*"

"*Thorns for the gardener who listened*," said Antho. Very quietly.

"*And drank*," said Rhosyn.

"*It doesn't rhyme*."

"*It doesn't rhyme*," she agreed. "*It's meant not to. Seren said. So you'll always remember that's where it went wrong.*" Her green eyes opened. "*Nine hundred years. Every Harrow girl. Singing my name and breaking the song on it.*"

She looked at the two of them. Hesper's line. Twenty generations down.

"*I'd like it to rhyme*," said the Thorn-Crowned. "*Just once. Before the end.*"

---

She took them in.

Through the thorns. Along the winding path. Rhosyn first. Barefoot. Silent. The black stems drawing back from her as she came, the way grass leans away from wind. Then Lex. Then Antho. Then Hask, with Pim.

She didn't look back. She walked with her thin arms at her sides and her rotten robe dragging in the dust and her crown of thorns on her bowed head, and the thorns opened in front of her and closed behind.

Antho watched her back.

He'd thought she'd be frightening. One of the Crowned. One of the Nine. One of the five who'd gone through. Deathless. Starving. He'd met two of them now, or the edges of them. A Moth Queen with lit eyes who'd wanted to drink the world. A Glass Queen in a palace of her own face.

He'd thought this one would be the same.

She wasn't frightening.

She was the saddest thing he'd ever seen.

---

"*There were roses*," said Rhosyn.

She said it without turning round. Walking. Her voice going back to them along the path like a leaf on a stream.

"*On a white wall*," she said. "*In my mother's garden. Red ones. When I was four.*" A pause. "*I used to sit under them. All summer. The bees used to come. Hundreds. I'd sit in the middle of them and they never stung me. Not once.*"

Nobody answered.

"*And yellow ones*," said Rhosyn. "*Up an apple tree. And a dark one, nearly black, in a trough by the kitchen door. It smelled of cloves. And rain.*" Her bare feet in the dust. "*And little pink ones in the cabbage bed. Where Hes used to sit.*"

"*You remember them all*," said Lex.

"*I dreamed them*," said the Thorn-Crowned. "*Nine hundred years. In the silk. Every one. Every rose I ever grew.*" A small sound. "*And then I'd wake up. And they'd be thorns.*"

---

"*Your gardeners*," said Antho.

She stopped.

On the path. In a thread of light. With her back to him. Very still.

"*On the road*," said Antho. "*In the gold. You told my mother. Through the rose. Tam and old Wyn and little Bess.*"

The Thorn-Crowned didn't turn round.

"*Bess carried the watering cans*," she said. Very quietly. "*She was nine. She was too small for them. She used to drag them across the lawn with both hands and slop half the water on her boots.*" A pause. "*I used to laugh at her. Not unkindly. She'd laugh too.*"

"*She's on the road*."

"*She's on the road*," said Rhosyn. "*Near the hall. Near the end. Holding a little gold can. With gold water slopping out of the spout.*" Her shoulders were shaking. "*I walk past her every time I leave the hall. I don't look. I haven't looked in nine hundred years.*"

"*They hum*," said Antho.

She went very still.

"*One note*," said Antho. "*All of them. Nine thousand. You said you'd never listened long enough to hear what it was saying.*" He looked at her back. At her thin shoulders under the rotten silk. "*Pim heard it. On the road. A girl said one word, out loud, and the hum remembered.*"

"*What*—" The Thorn-Crowned's voice cracked. "*What is it saying?*"

Antho looked at Pim. Asleep against Hask's chest. White-faced.

"*Home*," he said.

---

Rhosyn sat down.

On the path. In the dust. Among her thorns. Just sat. As if her legs had gone. With her knees drawn up and her thin arms round them.

Like a girl on the edge of a cabbage bed.

She didn't make a sound.

The thorns did.

All round. Every stem. In every direction. Creaking. Shifting. Moaning. A long low dry sound, like wind through a dead hedge in winter. Like a whole forest of black thorns grieving.

And here and there, among them, on the end of a hooked stem—

Red.

Small. Closed. Buds. Dozens. Hundreds. Coming out all over the black wall of thorns at once, in every direction, as far as Antho could see.

And opening.

---

Nobody moved.

They stood on the path among the thorns while the Thorn-Crowned sat in the dust with her arms round her knees and a thousand roses opened round her. Dark red. Velvet. Damp. As if it had rained.

It had never rained in the Court.

The smell came up round them. Faint. Then not faint. Summer. Rain on a white wall. A garden full of bees.

*Home*.

---

Pim woke up.

Antho felt him wake. In Hask's arms. A stir. A breath. His head lifting off Hask's chest.

He looked round. At the thorns. At the roses. At the thin grey-green woman sitting in the dust with her face hidden in her knees.

He didn't say anything.

He wriggled. Hask let him down. Slowly. One finger at a time. The way he did everything now.

Pim stood on the path. Unsteady. Pale. In his blue coat with the brass buttons. He looked at Rhosyn for a long time.

Then he went and sat down beside her.

---

Not close. Not touching. A foot away. In the dust. Cross-legged. The way he'd sat on the white road in front of Ilda. The way he sat on the rim of the pool beside Ivy.

He didn't say anything.

He got quiet.

---

Antho felt it.

Not much. Pim didn't have much. He'd poured most of himself into a gold girl on the white road and given the rest to a Glass Queen in a stone room. He was nearly empty. Antho could feel how empty. Like a cup with only a little water left in the bottom.

But he gave it.

A little. Just a little. A breath of quiet. A small round room. Spreading out from the boy, soft and still, toward the woman beside him in the dust.

Not into her. There was no crack in her to go into. She was full. She'd bricked herself up nine hundred years ago with her own hands.

*Round* her.

Like a coat. Like a blanket round the shoulders of someone who's been out in the cold too long.

---

Rhosyn lifted her head.

Slowly. Her thin grey-green face wet. Her crown of thorns crooked. Her green eyes very wide.

She looked at Pim.

At the small pale boy sitting in the dust a foot away from her with his eyes shut and his hands open on his knees.

"*Oh*," she said. Very softly. "*Oh. It's quiet.*"

Pim didn't open his eyes.

"*Nobody's ever*—" The Thorn-Crowned stopped. Tried again. "*It's so quiet. I can't hear him. I can always hear him. Oz. Like the sun on the other side of a wall. Always. Nine hundred years.*" Her voice was trembling. "*I can't hear him.*"

"*It's a room*," said Pim. Very quietly. Not opening his eyes. "*Nobody can come in.*"

She stared at him.

"*Not even him*?"

"*Not even him*," said Pim. "*For a little while.*"

---

She cried then.

Properly. Not the silent sitting. Not the thorns moaning. She put her face in her thin hands and cried like a girl. Like a girl of fifteen who has just been told something wonderful and terrible and can't hold it.

Pim sat beside her with his eyes shut.

He didn't put his arm round her. He didn't touch her at all.

He just held the room.

---

Hask came and stood over them.

Enormous. With his pick in his good hand and his bandaged arm against his chest and his face like a slab of coal.

Rhosyn looked up at him. Through her fingers. Through her tears.

She went very still.

"*You*," she said.

Hask didn't answer.

"*You're hers*," said the Thorn-Crowned. Very quietly. "*Mae's. I can smell her on you. Like smoke in a coat.*" Her green eyes moved over him. The scarred leather. The two long scars down the back of his jerkin, where something had once torn through. "*You had her wings.*"

"*Yes*," said Silas Hask.

"*You're the hewer*," said Rhosyn. "*Orrin told us. In the hall. A hewer broke her on his pick.*"

"*Yes*."

"*And then you held it out*," said the Thorn-Crowned. "*Orrin said. At the end. On the rubble. You held your pick out. And she flew onto it.*"

Hask didn't say anything for a long time.

"*She was falling*," he said at last. "*Somebody'd held on to her wings. A Warden. Kell. And then let go. And she was falling.*" His voice was very rough. "*She'd been good to me. For a season. She'd filled my chest with light so I could breathe.*" He looked down at the pick in his hand. "*So I held it out. For her to land on. That's all.*"

"*And then the smith tapped it*."

"*Yes*."

"*And she came apart*," said Rhosyn.

"*She was warm*," said Silas Hask. "*At the end. A Harrow woman gave her a lamp. She was warm and she smiled.*" He looked at the Thorn-Crowned. "*I saw it. I was right there.*"

---

Rhosyn looked up at him for a long time.

Then she unfolded one thin grey-green hand from her knees. And held it up. Toward him.

Hask looked at it.

Then he knelt down. Slowly. Enormous. In the dust. In front of her. And took it. In his good hand. His big scarred miner's hand round her thin twig of a hand.

"*Thank you*," said Rhosyn. "*For holding it out.*"

Hask didn't say anything.

He knelt in the dust holding the Thorn-Crowned's hand. And a rose opened on the stem nearest his shoulder. Dark red. Velvet.

And he started to cough.

---

Not the old cough. Not the grey-lung. Not the short dry rattle he'd had all spring.

Antho heard it and didn't understand it at first.

Then he did.

Hask was *crying*. The way a big man cries who hasn't cried since he was nine. Who hadn't cried at his father's grave or his mother's or his sister's, standing at the window looking up at the dark. Who'd held it in his chest so long it had gone in with the grey and the coal-dust and the Gloam and lived there.

It came out of him like a cough. Hard. Wrenching. Kneeling in the dust of the Court with a Crowned queen's hand in his.

Rhosyn held on.

She didn't let go.

---

It was a long time before anyone moved.

The roses stayed open. All round. A thousand of them. Dark red on the black thorns. The smell of summer thick in the dim air.

Pim held the room till he couldn't. Then it went. Antho felt it go. And Rhosyn felt it too. He saw her flinch. Saw her head turn, very slightly, toward the east. Toward the far wall of the thorns. Toward the world. Toward a sun on the other side of a wall.

"*He's there*," she whispered. "*Again. I can hear him.*"

"*I'm sorry*," said Pim. "*I don't have any more.*"

"*No*," said the Thorn-Crowned. "*Don't be sorry. Don't.*" She looked at the boy. At his white face. "*That was the first time in nine hundred years I couldn't hear him.*" She swallowed. "*I'll remember it. I'll remember it forever. I've got a very good memory. I remember every rose.*"

---

She stood up.

Slowly. Like something growing. She wiped her face with the rag of her sleeve. She straightened her crown of thorns.

"*Come*," she said. "*It's not far now.*"

---

The path came out into a clearing.

Small. Round. Grey dust. With the thorns rising on every side like the walls of a well. Black. Hooked. Covered now with red.

And in the middle of it, the tower.

---

Antho had seen it in his mother's dream. She'd told him. She'd told them all. In the Hall of Lanterns. *Small. Slender. Grown, not built. Grey and cracked and dead.*

She'd been right.

It was no taller than a tall tree. Slender. Twisted. The living trunk of something that had been a great white-barked tree once, coaxed up and up and round on itself, the way you'd coax a vine up a pole. It had been beautiful. Antho could see it had been beautiful. The way the wood curled. The way it rose. Like a girl's hair, plaited and wound up on her head.

It was dead now.

Grey. Cracked. The bark hanging off it in long dry strips. The wood underneath gone silver with age. Nine hundred years of standing in a country with no rain.

A door at the bottom.

A window at the top.

---

He felt it.

The *no*. Here. In the clearing. Not waves now. Not a draught down a chimney. A *wall*. Coming out of the tower from the top, from the window, from the room behind it, down the stair, out of the door, into the clearing.

Patient. Quiet. Absolute.

*Not you. Not ever. Hush.*

Rhosyn stopped at the edge of the clearing.

She didn't go further. She stood among her roses with her hands pressed together under her chin and looked at the tower.

"*This is as far as I can go*," she said.

---

"*I grew it*," she said. "*In the first year. For her.*" She didn't look at them. She looked at the tower. "*The others laughed. Ysy laughed hardest. She said Hes would never come.*"

"*She was right*," said Lex. Quietly.

"*She was right*," agreed the Thorn-Crowned. "*Hes never came.*" A pause. "*But her crown did. Thirty-two years ago. In a long grey hand.*"

She looked at the door.

"*I stood here*," she said. "*Where I'm standing now. When the grey man came down the stair. I'd felt it come into the Court. Like a cold hand. Like a no. I woke up for the first time in four hundred years and ran here.*" Her voice was very small. "*He told me it was hers. He told me she'd had a line. Twenty generations.*"

"*And you grew the thorns*."

"*I grew the thorns*," said Rhosyn. "*Round and round. So Oz would never find the stair.*" She looked at the tower. "*And then I went back to the hall. And climbed into my silk. And slept. For thirty-two years.*" She swallowed. "*More soundly than I'd slept in nine hundred.*"

"*It said no*," said Antho. "*Every night. And you slept.*"

"*Like a hand on a forehead*," said the Thorn-Crowned. "*Like Mae's hand. When Hes had the fever.*"

---

"*I can't go up*," she said.

"*I know*."

"*I tried*. *Once. That night.*" Her green eyes were on the door. "*I put my foot on the bottom stair. And it said no. And I couldn't.*" She looked at her bare grey-green foot in the dust. "*Not because it hurt. Because it was right.*"

Nobody said anything.

"*You can*," said Rhosyn. "*You're hers. You never drank. It won't say no to you.*" She looked at Lex. At Antho. "*Go up. Go and get it.*"

"*And you*?"

"*I'll wait*," said the Thorn-Crowned. "*Here. At the bottom.*" A small strange smile. "*I've been waiting at the bottom of this tower for nine hundred years. I'm very good at it.*"

---

Antho looked at her.

At the thin grey-green woman standing among her roses. With her crown of thorns crooked and her rotten robe hanging off her and her hands pressed together under her chin.

*She'll open her thorns. She'll cry. She'll mean it*.

*And then Oz will ask her where you are. And she'll tell him.*

"*Rhosyn*," he said.

She looked at him.

"*When he asks*," said Antho Vey. "*Your brother. Where we are. What we took.*"

The Thorn-Crowned went very still.

"*You don't have to lie*," said Antho. "*I know you can't. I know you always tell him.*" He swallowed. "*Just—wait. A little. Before you answer. As long as you can.*"

She looked at him for a long time.

"*How long*?" she said.

"*Long enough*," said Antho.

---

Rhosyn didn't answer.

She stood among her roses with her green eyes on his face.

And then she nodded.

Once. Very small. The way a stem nods when a bee lands on it.

"*I'll try*," said the Thorn-Crowned.

Antho closed his eyes.

*I'll try*. His father's words. His mother's. Wren's. The words everyone said, this year, to everyone they loved.

*That's all anyone's got*.

---

"*Pim*," said Lex. "*Hask. Stay with her.*"

Hask looked at him.

"*Stay*," said Lex. "*Here. At the bottom. With Rhosyn.*" He looked at the tower. At the door. At the *no* pouring out of it like cold air. "*It's Harrow blood it won't say no to. Ours. Just ours.*" He looked at his brother. "*You and me.*"

Antho nodded.

He looked at Pim. Sitting in the dust at the edge of the clearing. White-faced. Empty. With his hand on the pocket over his heart, where Ivy's cat was.

"*Make room for her*," said Antho. "*If you get any back. If you can. While we're up there.*"

Pim looked at the Thorn-Crowned.

Then at Antho.

"*Yes*," he said.

---

They crossed the clearing.

The two of them. Side by side. Through the grey dust. Through the *no*.

It didn't push. Antho felt it pour past him. Round him. Through him. Cold. Quiet. Patient. Like water round a stone. Like a hand laid on a door that the door was allowed through.

*Not you*, it said. To the thorns. To the roses. To the thin grey-green woman at the edge of the clearing. To the whole grey-gold country round it, and the hall, and the kept, and the King walking in a world on the other side of the Veil. *Not you. Not ever.*

To the two of them, it didn't say anything.

It just let them come.

---

The door was shut.

Grey wood. Grown, not built. The same silver-dead wood as the tower. With no handle. No latch. No lock. Just a door. Shut.

Antho put his hand on it.

His left. The one he'd cut at the edge of the world. With a thin red line across the palm, healing.

He felt it.

Felt the door feel him. Feel the Harrow blood in his hand. Hesper's blood. Twenty generations down.

Felt it not know him.

And open.

---

It swung inward.

Silently. Slowly. On no hinges. Into the dark of the dead tree.

A stair.

Winding. Up. Through the hollow of the trunk. Grey wood. Narrow. Worn. Spiralling up into a dimness that wasn't quite dark.

And coming down it, out of the dimness, out of the room at the top, out of the thing on the chair—

*No*.

Cold. Quiet. Steady.

Not at them.

*For* them.

---

Antho looked back.

Once. From the doorway. Across the clearing.

At Hask. Standing at the edge of it, enormous, with his pick in his good hand and his bandaged arm against his chest. At Pim. Sitting in the dust beside him, small and white, with his eyes shut, trying.

At Rhosyn.

Standing among her roses. With her hands pressed together under her chin. With her crown of thorns crooked. With a thousand red flowers open round her on the black stems.

Watching them go up.

Her green eyes were wet.

She was smiling.

---

They went up.
