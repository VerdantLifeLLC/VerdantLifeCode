# A Love for a Love

Seren had been waiting for that knock for thirteen years.

Not this one. Not this door. Not this night.

But that knock. Once. Very gently. The knock of someone who knows you've been waiting.

She'd heard it once before. On a shingle. By a grey river. Thirteen years ago. With her eldest son screaming on the stairs behind her and a door into the dark opening in front of her.

She hadn't heard it then, exactly. There'd been no door to knock on. Just the shore, and the boat, and a tall grey figure leaning on a pole.

But she'd felt it. The way you feel a hand on your shoulder in a crowd.

*It's time*.

---

"*Come in*," said Arion.

From the pillows. Barely a sound. A thread.

He hadn't opened his eyes.

"*Come in*," he said again. "*Door's open. It always is.*"

---

The door swung inward.

Slowly. On its hinges. Into the dim. Letting in the cold. Letting in the snow, drifting, a few flakes, landing on the gold flags by the hearth and lying there, not melting.

And letting in the light.

Grey. Soft. Swaying. The colour of a lantern seen through river-mist. On the end of a long pole.

And then him.

---

He had to stoop. Like the King. Like Arion, once, on the Spire steps in the rain. He was very tall. Tall and thin and grey, in grey robes that hung off him like river-weed, with his long grey hands on his long grey pole and the lantern on top of it swaying.

He stepped into the cottage.

He turned his face toward the room.

He had no eyes.

---

Nobody moved.

Seren sat on the edge of the bed with her husband's hand in hers. Lex knelt on the other side with the other hand. Antho stood at the foot of the bed. Wren against the far wall, with the baby and Pim.

And by the hearth, standing, enormous, gold—

The King.

He'd backed away. Seren saw it. When the door opened. When the grey light came in. He'd taken one step back. And then another. Until his shoulders were against the wall by the hearth and he couldn't go any further.

His face was white.

Not ivory. Not gold. *White*. The colour of bone. The colour of a man who has seen something he's been running from for nine hundred years walk in through a blue door.

---

The Ferryman turned his eyeless face.

Slowly. Round the room. Past the bed. Past Antho. Past Wren and the children.

To the hearth.

To the King.

Seren saw him look. Without eyes. She saw the King feel it. Saw him flinch against the wall like a man flinching from a blow.

"*Oszric*," said the Ferryman.

---

The King didn't answer.

He stood with his back against the wall and his great gold hands pressed flat against the stone on either side of him and his furnace eyes enormous. The light coming off him had dimmed. Like a fire when you open the door and the cold comes in.

"*I rowed you*," said the Ferryman. Very quietly. "*Nine hundred years ago. You sat in the stern. In chains. You did not look at me once. You looked at the shore. At your sister on it. Until the grey took her.*"

The King's mouth opened. Nothing came out.

"*You were afraid of me then*," said the Ferryman. "*You are afraid of me now.*" The eyeless face didn't move. "*You have been afraid of me since you were fourteen. Since a door. Since your father.*"

"*Are you*—" The King's voice. Cracked. Barely a voice. "*Are you here for*—"

"*No*," said the Ferryman.

---

The King went very still.

"*Not you*," said the Ferryman. "*You are not on my list.*" He turned his face. Away. Toward the bed. Toward the old man on the pillows. "*You never will be.*"

Silence.

"*You drank*," said the Ferryman. "*You drank the room. The door new things come in by. The door old things go out by.*" His long grey hand was still on his pole. "*There is no door for you, Oszric. Not mine. Not any. You bricked it up yourself. With a gold cup at the edge of a pool.*"

The King's face—

Seren saw it. She'd remember it. For the rest of her life.

"*That is your punishment*," said the Ferryman. Very gently. "*Not mine. I have never punished anyone.*" A pause. "*You did it yourself. And you have been doing it to others ever since.*"

---

The King moved.

Not toward the Ferryman. Not toward the bed. Sideways. Along the wall. Toward the door. Slowly. His back to the stone. His eyes on the grey figure in the middle of the room. The way a man edges past a sleeping dog.

The Ferryman didn't look at him.

He'd turned his whole body toward the bed now. Toward Arion. He wasn't watching the King at all.

The King reached the door.

He stopped in it. Ducking. With one great gold hand on the frame, turning it gold. With the snow behind him and the night.

He looked back.

At the bed. At the old man. At the woman beside him.

At the baby, against the far wall, with her fist in her mouth.

"*I'm*—" he said. And stopped. And tried again. "*I'll*—"

He couldn't finish.

He turned. And went out. Into the snow. Into the dark. And Seren heard him go. Down the ramp. Across the clearing. Not hurrying. Not slow. *Away*. West. Down the track. Through the birch wood.

The gold light going with him. Through the trees. Getting smaller. Further.

Gone.

---

Hask, on the ramp, stepped back into the doorway.

He didn't say anything. He stood there. In the cold. With his gold arm hanging at his side and his good hand on the doorframe.

Somebody should.

---

The Ferryman stood by the bed.

He looked down at Arion. With no eyes. For a long time.

"*Arion Vey*," he said.

"*Ferryman*," said Arion. Not opening his eyes.

"*I told your son you would come to my shore*," said the Ferryman. "*Before long. I said old men always do.*" A pause. "*You did not come.*"

"*No*."

"*So I have come to you*." The eyeless face was very gentle. "*I do not often. I have only come to a door four times since the world was woven.*"

"*Who else*?" said Arion. Faintly.

"*Aurelian*," said the Ferryman. "*At the end. In the deep. Malachi. In the deep, singing. And a girl of seven in the Drowned Country, nine hundred years ago, who was too frightened to come down to the water on her own.*" A pause. "*And you.*"

Arion opened his eyes.

"*Why me*?"

"*Because you tried*," said the Ferryman. "*A hundred and forty days. On a thread as thin as a spider's.*" He was quiet. "*I have watched a great many people come to my shore. I have never watched anyone try so hard not to.*"

"*I promised my sons*."

"*I know*," said the Ferryman. "*I heard.*" The lantern on his pole swayed. "*They are here now.*"

"*They're here*," said Arion Vey.

He smiled.

---

Seren held his hand.

She didn't say anything. She couldn't. She sat on the edge of the bed with his hand in hers—bones in paper, so cold, so light—and looked at his face.

*I'm going to lose him*.

She'd known. Of course she'd known. She'd known since the Ferryman's shore. Since *before the year turns*. She'd known every night for a hundred and forty days, lying beside him in the dark with her hand on his chest, counting.

She hadn't known it would feel like this.

Like losing something she'd never had.

---

"*Ferryman*," she said.

The eyeless face turned toward her.

"*The price*," said Seren. "*For my memories. Of him. The ones I paid you. Thirteen years ago.*" Her voice was shaking. She didn't let it stop her. "*You said a love for a love. You said it on the shingle. You said it must be given. By the one who holds it.*"

"*I did*."

"*I asked if I could give mine*. *For my sons. And you asked if I would.*" She swallowed. "*And I couldn't.*"

"*No*," said the Ferryman. Very gently. "*You could not.*"

"*I still can't*," said Seren Harrow. "*I've thought about it every night. For a hundred and forty days. I've lain here and thought, if I gave it, I'd have him back. All of him. Before he goes.*" Her hand tightened on Arion's. "*And I'd look at my boys in the morning and not know why I was smiling at them.*"

She looked up at the Ferryman.

"*I can't*," she said. "*I can't. I'm sorry. I'm so sorry. I can't.*"

"*I know*," said the Ferryman. "*Nobody can. That is the price.*"

---

*[I can*.]

---

Seren went still.

So did everyone.

It came from the hearth. From the fire. Banked. Low. Red coals under grey ash.

And in the middle of them, one coal.

Glowing.

Not red. Not orange.

*Gold*.

---

Lex had let go of his father's hand.

Seren saw him. He was turning. On his knees. By the bed. Toward the hearth. His face white. His eyes very wide.

"*Ember*," he said.

*[I can*,] said the voice from the fire again.

Out loud. So everyone could hear. The way it had spoken from a coal in this same hearth, in the spring, when Arion asked to talk to it properly.

Dry. Gentle. Precise. With an old-fashioned courtesy.

Arion's voice. From twenty-two years ago.

*[I can pay it*,] said Ember.

---

The Ferryman turned his eyeless face toward the fire.

He looked at the gold coal for a long time.

"*You*," he said.

*[Me*.]

"*You are a piece of him*," said the Ferryman. "*Pressed into his son's blood. Like a maker's mark.*" He tilted his head. "*I can see you. In the fire. In the boy. In the old man. A thread between them. Thin.*"

*[Very thin*,] said Ember. *[I know.]*

"*What would you give*?"

The coal pulsed. Once. Bright.

*[A love*,] said Ember. *[Whole. Given freely.]* A pause. *[Mine.]*

---

"*No*," said Lex.

He was on his feet. Seren hadn't seen him stand. He was standing between the bed and the hearth, with his hands at his sides and his face white.

"*No*," he said again. "*Ember. No. You can't.*"

*[I can*.]

"*You're*—" His voice broke. "*You're mine. You've been mine since I was three. Since the seal cracked. You said so. In the snow. At the edge of the world. You said, I was the first one.*"

*[I was*,] said Ember. Very gently. *[I am.]* A pause. *[That's why it's mine to give.]*

---

The Ferryman was quiet.

He stood by the bed with his long grey hand on his pole and his eyeless face turned toward the fire. Waiting.

"*Explain*," he said.

*[A love for a love*,] said Ember. *[That's the price. Not hers for him; that's what she's buying. Another love. Whole. Given freely. By the one who holds it.]* The coal pulsed. *[I hold one.]*

"*For whom*?"

*[For him*,] said Ember. *[For Lex.]*

Seren heard her son make a sound.

*[Twenty-two years*,] said Ember. *[Since a night in a cottage at Harrow, when an old man who looked forty-five pressed a piece of himself into a sleeping boy and went away.]* A pause. *[I was made of Arion. Everything I was. His voice. His memories. The way he taps two fingers on a table.]*

The fire was very quiet.

*[But I wasn't made of loving Lex*,] said Ember. *[Arion loved him. Of course he did. But he went away. He went to a crystal in Avaloria and held the breaches for sixteen years. He didn't get to watch Lex grow up.]* The coal pulsed. Brighter. *[I did.]*

---

Seren couldn't breathe.

*[I watched him at the Ashbourne forge*,] said Ember. *[At nineteen. Common. Ceiling twenty-four. Hammering horseshoes for a man who'd pulled him out of the ashes. I watched him go up the Spire. And into the Wellworlds. And down to the bottom of the world.]* A pause. *[I watched him fall in love with a girl who laughed at him for not knowing a hare-track. I watched him get his nose broken. Twice.]*

"*Ember*—" said Lex.

*[I watched him say good morning to a fire*,] said Ember. *[Every morning. For seven years. Without fail. Even the morning after the wedding, when he had a headache like a kicked anvil and Wren threw a boot at him.]*

Lex had his hands over his face.

*[Seren said it*,] said Ember. *[In the spring. In this room. She said, Arion made you. Lex grew you.]* The coal was very bright now. Gold. Steady. Like a heart. *[She was right. The love's not his. It's not Arion's. It's mine. I grew it. Every morning. Every footnote.]*

A pause.

*[It's the only thing in me that isn't him*,] said Ember. *[So it's the only thing I've got to give.]*

---

The Ferryman didn't speak for a long time.

The lantern on his pole swayed. The fire ticked. Outside, the snow.

"*If you give it*," he said at last. "*You will not love him.*"

*[I know*.]

"*You will not remember why you footnoted his mornings*. *You will not know why a fire matters. You will be only what you were made of.*" The eyeless face was very still. "*A piece of an old man. Nothing else.*"

*[I know*,] said Ember. *[And then I'll go with him.]*

---

Seren looked at the coal.

"*With*—"

*[With Arion*,] said Ember. *[Across the river.]* A pause. *[I'm a piece of him. If I give away the part that's mine, the rest is his. And it should go home. With the rest of him.]* Very quietly. *[Pieces should go home.]*

"*No*," said Lex. Through his hands. "*No. No. Please.*"

*[Lex*.]

"*Please*," said Lex Vey. "*Don't. I can't. I can't do it without you. Every morning. I can't. You're the first. You were there before any of them. Before Wren. Before Antho. Before*—" His voice went. "*Please.*"

The coal was quiet for a long moment.

*[You can*,] said Ember. Very gently. *[You will.]* A pause. *[You'll kneel down at the hearth tomorrow morning and blow on the coals and say good morning, and the fire will be glad. Because it always is. Because it's the Flame, and it's been burning since before there were people to burn for.]* Another pause. *[It doesn't need me to be glad. It never did.]*

"*I need you*."

*[I know*,] said Ember. *[That's why it's the right price.]*

---

Arion moved.

Seren felt it. His hand. In hers. Moving. Very slightly. A tremor. A pull.

She looked down.

His eyes were open. Grey. Very bright. Looking past her. At the hearth. At the gold coal.

"*Young man*," said Arion Vey.

*[Old man*.]

"*You don't have to*."

*[I know*.]

"*I gave you away*," said Arion. Faintly. "*It was a gift. A gift doesn't go back.*"

*[It's not going back*,] said Ember. *[It's coming with you. That's different.]* A pause. *[And it's buying something. For her.]*

His father looked at the coal for a long time.

"*Are you sure*?"

*[No*,] said Ember. *[But I'm going to do it anyway.]* A pause. *[I learned that from you. In the cottage. With the pan. Page nine.]*

---

Arion laughed.

It wasn't a sound. It was barely a breath. A catch. A shiver. His whole body, small under the blanket, shaking very slightly.

"*Page nine*," he said.

*[Don't ever leave a light on for me, Warden*,] said Ember. In his own voice. Twenty-two years younger. *[Too late.]*

Seren made a sound.

She didn't know what sound. It came up out of her from somewhere she didn't know she had.

---

*[Ferryman*,] said Ember.

The eyeless face turned.

*[Is it enough*?]

The Ferryman didn't answer for a long time.

He stood by the bed in his grey robes with his long grey hand on his pole and his lantern swaying and his eyeless face turned toward a single gold coal in a banked fire.

"*It is a love*," he said at last. "*Whole. Given freely. By the one who holds it.*" A pause. "*Grown. Not made. Every morning for seven years. Every footnote.*"

*[Is it enough*?]

"*It is more than enough*," said the Ferryman. "*It is the most I have ever been offered.*"

---

He held out his hand.

Long. Grey. Thin. Open.

Not toward the fire. Toward Seren.

And in it, very faint, so faint she could barely see it in the dim light of the Lantern, something. Not a thing. A *glimmer*. Like light on water. Like the reflection of a lamp in a wet window. Like rain.

Not one glimmer.

A great many. Thousands. Swirling. In his long grey palm. Like dust in a sunbeam. Like snow in a lantern's light.

"*Thirty years*," said the Ferryman. "*Every day. The steps. The rain. The cottage. The pan. The lamp in the window. The wedding in the snow. The boys.*" His eyeless face was very gentle. "*The doorways. The midnights. The night under the table with the roof leaking.*"

Seren stared at it.

"*Yours*," said the Ferryman. "*Paid for.*"

---

She didn't take it.

Not at first. She sat on the edge of the bed with her husband's hand in hers and looked at the swirling glimmer in the Ferryman's long grey palm.

And then she looked at the fire.

At the gold coal. Pulsing. Steady.

"*Ember*," she said.

*[Seren*.]

"*Thank you*," said Seren Harrow.

The coal pulsed. Once.

*[Look after him*,] said Ember. *[Lex. Please. He'll be very sad. He'll pretend he isn't. He'll go and hit something in the forge.]* A pause. *[Make him eat.]*

"*I will*."

*[And tell him*—] It stopped. Started again. *[Tell him I'll be in the main text. Not a footnote. Chapter forty. Tell him that.]*

"*I'll tell him*," said Seren.

---

The Ferryman turned toward the fire.

"*Come*," he said.

And the gold coal rose.

---

Out of the hearth. Out of the banked ash. Slowly. A small bright gold spark, lifting off the coals and floating up into the dim air of the cottage like a seed off a dandelion. Turning. Glowing.

Lex made a sound.

He was on his knees again. By the hearth. With his hands out. Reaching. The way his daughter reached for the nail.

The spark floated past his hands.

Slowly. Very slowly. Close. Close enough that its light lit his face. His wet face. His eyes, enormous.

And stopped.

Just for a moment. In the air. In front of him. A foot from his face.

*[Good morning*,] said Ember.

Very quietly. Only to him. Seren barely heard it.

*[Say it tomorrow*,] it said. *[To the fire. Every day. It would be rude not to.]*

And went on.

---

Up. Across the room. Toward the Ferryman. Toward the long grey pole. Toward the lantern on top of it, swaying, grey, the colour of a lantern seen through river-mist.

And into it.

---

The Ferryman's lantern changed.

Seren saw it. The grey light inside the glass, the cold soft river-light that had swayed on the top of the pole since before the world was woven—

It went gold.

Not much. A little. At the heart of it. A small warm steady flame, gold, burning in the middle of the grey. Like a coal in a banked fire. Like a lamp in a window at the end of a long lane on a winter night.

Like a light left on.

---

And then she took the glimmer.

---

She put out her hand. Her square freckled hand, with five thin white scars across the palm from a Glass Queen's fingers. And laid it in the Ferryman's long grey one. Over the swirling glimmer.

And closed her fingers.

---

It didn't come in one at a time.

The Ferryman had given her one, once. On the grass by the pool. The steps. The rain. It had come into her like a single drop of water into a dry well.

This came like a river.

---

The steps. The rain. A young man coming round a corner, very casually, as if he just happened to be passing. Turning back. Twice. And then not.

Mulled cider. A pillar. Blood on his forehead. Her, laughing, on the edge of a fountain.

The walk to the Vale. Three weeks. His long legs. Her shorter ones. Him slowing down without her asking. Her noticing. Him pretending he hadn't.

Her mother in the doorway. *Who's this*.

The barn. The goat. A month of him watching her across the table while her mother watched him.

A frying pan. Hitting the door with a crash like a bell. *I'll take that as a no.*

A lamp. In a window. In the snow. Clay. With her mother's thumbprint in the base. Her standing in the doorway. *Mam. You've never*—

*It's not for you*.

---

A chapel in the snow. A blue dress her mother had sewn all autumn and not lived to see. Brother Wyn, ninety-three and deaf in one ear, getting her name wrong. *Sarah. Do you take*—

*Seren*.

*Seren. Do you take*—

Brannoc in the front row. Thirty. Enormous. Sobbing so loudly Brother Wyn had to stop. Three times.

Arion, beside her, with snow in his hair, looking at her the way he'd looked at her on the steps. As if she were the first light he'd seen in two hundred years.

She'd laughed. At Brother Wyn. At Brannoc. At the snow. At him.

She'd been so happy.

She'd been so sad.

---

The cottage at Harrow. Her mother's cottage. Theirs, now.

Antho. Born in the spring. Small. Red. Furious. Silent the moment he was put on her chest. Looking up at her with grey eyes. His father's.

A year of walking him up and down. Every verse. Making them up when she ran out.

A doorway. Midnight. Rain. Asleep standing up with a baby on her shoulder.

And someone lifting the baby off her. Gently. And her not waking. And being carried. Both of them. To bed.

*You did*, she thought. *You did. You carried us.*

*I remember*.

---

Lex. Born in the winter. Big. Square-handed. Grabbing her finger the moment he came out and not letting go.

Arion holding him. In the firelight. With his face doing something she'd never seen it do.

*He's got your hands*, Arion said.

*He's got your eyes*, she said.

*He's got a grip like a vice*, said Arion. *Look at him. He won't let go.*

---

The table. The roof leaking. The rain coming through. The four of them under it. The boys asleep between them. Her singing. Very softly. The old verses. The forbidden ones. Because it was the only song long enough.

*Gold for the eldest, who feared to grow old*—

And a man lying very still beside her under the table. Pretending to be asleep. Listening.

She'd known.

She'd known he was listening. She remembered now. She'd sung it anyway. For him. Because he was the only person she'd ever wanted to hear it.

---

And the last.

A gold room. Avaloria. Crystal. A man inside it. Sealed. Holding the breaches. Forty-five, still, forever, with his hand pressed flat against the inside of the crystal.

And her on the other side. With her hand flat against the outside.

The last time.

*Don't ever leave a light on for me, Warden*, she'd said. Through the crystal. Laughing. Crying. Both. The way she'd said it at nineteen.

And through the crystal, very faint, she'd seen his mouth move.

*Too late*.

And then she'd turned round. And walked out of the gold room. And gone home to two boys and a cottage and a door that was going to open into the dark.

---

She opened her eyes.

---

She was sitting on the edge of the bed.

In the cottage with the blue door. At the end of the Vale. In the snow. With a Lantern burning on the table, three panes of glass and one of horn. With her sons and her son's wife and a sleeping boy and a baby in the room.

With her hand in her husband's.

She looked down at him.

---

His eyes were open.

Grey. Clear. Sharp. Looking up at her from the pillow.

His face was a walnut. A hundred years in a drawer. A heron in winter. Bones under paper. Small. So small. Smaller than a child.

She knew it.

She knew every line of it. Not because she'd learned it, the way she'd learned it over a year and a half, morning by morning, at the west window.

Because she'd watched it get there.

Because she'd known it at nineteen on the Spire steps, too tall for the arch, with ink on his cuff. And at twenty-one under a table in the rain. And at twenty-six in a doorway at midnight. And at thirty-seven through crystal in a gold room.

And now.

---

"*Arion*," she said.

He looked at her.

He saw it. She watched him see it. In her face. In her eyes. She didn't know what her face was doing. She couldn't feel it. She just watched his see.

His face did something she'd never seen it do.

No. That wasn't true.

She'd seen it do it once. In the snow. In a chapel. With Brother Wyn getting her name wrong and Brannoc sobbing in the front row.

It *lit*.

---

"*You remember*," said Arion Vey.

His voice was barely there.

"*I remember*," said Seren.

"*All of it*?"

"*All of it*." She was crying. She didn't know when she'd started. "*The steps. The pillar. The pan. The lamp. The chapel. Brother Wyn. Sarah, do you take*—"

He laughed.

It was nothing. A breath. A catch. His whole small body shaking under the blanket.

"*Sarah*," he said.

"*You carried us*," said Seren. "*Me and Antho. In the doorway. At midnight. In the rain. I didn't wake. You carried us both to bed.*" Her voice was going. Breaking up. Like ice on a pond. "*I remember. I didn't before. I read it on page two hundred and four. I remember it now.*"

"*You were very heavy*," said Arion. "*I nearly dropped you.*"

"*You didn't*."

"*No*," said her husband. "*I never did.*"

---

"*The table*," she said. "*The roof. The rain. I sang the old verses.*" She held his hand. "*You were listening. I knew you were listening. I sang them for you.*"

"*I know*," said Arion. "*I knew you knew.*" His eyes were very bright. "*I lay there and listened and pretended to be asleep and you pretended not to know. For a whole night. In the rain.*"

"*The best night*."

"*The best night*," said Arion Vey. "*Of two hundred and thirty-four years. The best one.*"

---

He looked past her.

At Lex. Standing by the hearth. With his hands at his sides and his face wet and something gone out of it. Something that had always been there. Something gold.

"*Lex*," said his father.

Lex came.

He knelt by the bed. On the other side. And took his father's other hand.

"*It's gone*," said Lex. Hoarsely. "*Ember. It's gone. It's—it's in the lantern. It said good morning. It said say it tomorrow.*" His face broke. "*I can't hear it. For the first time since I was three. I can't hear anything.*"

"*I know*."

"*It was you*," said Lex. "*It was always you. Your voice. Your memories.*"

"*It was mine*," said Arion. "*And then it was yours.*" His thumb moved. On the back of Lex's hand. Very slowly. "*And then it was its own. That's what you do with a gift. You give it away. And it becomes something you couldn't have made.*"

Lex put his head down on the blanket.

His father's hand came up. Slowly. So slowly. And found his hair. Dark. Curly. Cut badly.

"*Say good morning*," said Arion. "*Tomorrow. To the fire. It asked you to.*"

"*I will*."

"*Every day*."

"*Every day*," said Lex Vey.

---

"*Antho*," said Arion.

His other son came round the end of the bed. And knelt beside Lex. And put his hand on top of his brother's, on top of his father's.

He didn't say anything.

He didn't need to.

His father looked at him for a long time.

"*One thousand, one hundred and thirteen*," said Arion. Very faintly.

Antho made a sound.

"*Count them*," said his father. "*Every night. Out loud. To the door.*" A breath. In. Catch. Catch. Catch. Out. "*I'll be counting with you. Under my breath. I won't lose count.*"

"*Father*—"

"*And check*," said Arion. "*At three in the morning. When you wake up and lie very still.*" A ghost of a smile. "*Check that it's gone. And then go back to sleep. Next to a woman with ink on her nose.*"

Antho put his face down beside his brother's.

---

"*Wren*," said Arion.

She came. With the baby.

She knelt down beside the bed. Behind Lex. With Lark on her chest.

His father looked at the baby.

At her round face. At her dark fluff of hair. At her grey-green eyes, looking at him. At her square fist, in her mouth.

"*Twenty-one*," said Arion.

"*I've got them*," said Wren. "*In the box. Eighteen. And Seren's got the last three in the drawer.*"

"*One every birthday*."

"*Every birthday*."

"*She said no*," said Arion. His eyes were very bright. "*Her first word. To a king.*"

"*She did*."

"*Tell her*," said her grandfather. "*When she's old enough. Tell her I was there. Tell her I heard it.*" A faint smile. "*Tell her it was the best word I ever heard anyone say.*"

---

Lark took her fist out of her mouth.

She looked at the old man on the pillows. Frowning. The deep fierce frown.

And reached out.

Not for his nose. For his hand. The one Lex was holding. She put her square fist on top of all of them. On top of Antho's hand, on top of Lex's, on top of her grandfather's.

And held on.

---

Arion closed his eyes.

"*Ferryman*," he said.

The tall grey figure by the bed turned its eyeless face.

"*I promised a friend*," said Arion. "*In the Knot. At the bottom of the world. That I'd tell him what it was like. On the other side.*" A breath. Catch. Catch. Out. "*Before he goes.*"

"*Elric Thorne*," said the Ferryman.

"*Elric Thorne*."

The Ferryman was quiet for a moment.

"*It is on my way*," he said.

---

Seren lay down beside him.

She didn't decide to. She found herself on the bed. Beside him. On top of the blankets. With her bare feet cold and her head on the edge of his pillow and her hand on his chest. Over his heart. Where she could feel it.

The way she'd lain every night for a year and a half.

The way she'd lain every night for eighteen years, before. In a cottage at Harrow. In a bed she'd forgotten and remembered.

Counting.

In.

Catch.

Catch.

Catch.

Out.

---

"*Seren*," said Arion.

"*I'm here*."

"*I know*." His voice was barely a voice. A thread. A spider's thread. The last strand of a rope. "*I can feel your hand.*"

She pressed it down. Harder. On his chest.

"*Seren*," he said again.

"*What*?"

He turned his head. On the pillow. Toward her. So slowly. Until his face was an inch from hers. His walnut face. His grey eyes.

The first light he'd seen in two hundred years.

"*Leave a light on for me*," said Arion Vey.

---

She looked at him.

At the face she'd known at nineteen and forgotten and learned and remembered.

At the eyes.

"*Too late*," said Seren Harrow.

---

He smiled.

---

In.

Catch.

---

She didn't count the next one.

She lay with her hand on his chest and her face an inch from his and didn't count. She looked at him. At his eyes. Open. Grey. Looking at her.

Smiling.

She felt it under her hand. The catch. Holding. Holding.

Holding.

And then—

Very gently. Very slowly. Like a door swinging shut on a quiet house at the end of a long day. Like a man setting down a pen at the end of a page.

Out.

---

It didn't come back in.

---

The room didn't change.

That was the thing she'd remember. Afterward. She'd sat by a great many beds in thirty years. She knew the quiet. The quiet after the last breath, when the room changes, when something goes out of it, when there's one fewer person in it than there was.

The room didn't change.

There was no one fewer in it.

Because he was standing up.

---

She saw it.

She didn't know if anyone else did. She'd ask them, later, and Lex would say *I didn't see anything, Mam, I had my face on the blanket*, and Antho would go very still and not answer, and Wren would say *I saw the lantern change*.

She saw it.

He stood up. Out of the bed. Out of the small bundle of sticks under the blanket with its walnut face on the pillow. He stood up off it the way a man stands up from a chair at the end of a long evening. Easily. Without thinking. As if he'd never once had trouble standing in his life.

And he was young.

---

Not forty-five. Not the face from the gold room, from the crystal, from two hundred years of the net holding him still.

*Younger*.

Tall. Taller than the room. Stooping a little, under the beams, the way he'd stooped under the arch of the Spire steps. In a grey coat. With ink on his cuff. With his hair too long.

With his face *open*. All of it. Not closed like a book. Not still like water. Open, like a door with the wind blowing through it.

Looking down at her.

At her, lying on the bed beside the thing he'd left there, with her hand on its chest.

He smiled.

---

She'd seen that smile.

On the steps. In the rain. At nineteen. When she turned round at the bottom of the Spire stair and saw a young man in a grey coat coming round the corner, very casually, as if he just happened to be passing.

Looking at her.

As if she were the first light he'd seen in two hundred years.

---

The Ferryman held out his hand.

Long. Grey. Thin.

Arion took it.

---

They went to the door.

The Ferryman first. With his long grey pole. With his lantern swaying on the top of it. Grey. With a small gold flame burning in the heart of it.

And Arion behind him.

Ducking under the lintel.

---

He stopped in the doorway.

Seren saw him stop. Saw him stand there. With the snow behind him and the night and the dark birch wood, and a big man with a gold arm standing aside on the ramp.

He looked back.

At her.

She lifted her head off the pillow. She didn't know she was going to. She lifted her head and looked at him in the doorway, young, in his grey coat, with snow in his hair.

*He turned back twice*, she thought. *On the steps. In the rain. Before he came round the corner.*

*And then he didn't*.

He didn't turn back.

He looked at her for a long time. In the doorway. Smiling.

And then he lifted one hand. And touched two fingers to the doorframe. Twice. The way he used to tap two fingers on a table.

*Tap. Tap*.

And went out.

---

The light went with him.

Down the ramp. Across the clearing. Through the snow. A grey light, swaying on a long pole. With a small gold flame in the heart of it.

Not west. Not toward the gold path. Not toward the King.

*Down*. Toward the stream. Toward the water. Toward the river that all rivers are, a little.

She watched it from the bed. Through the open door. Getting smaller. Further.

Gold, at the heart. Like a lamp in a window. Like a light left on.

And then gone.

---

Seren lay down again.

On the bed. On top of the blankets. With her bare feet cold and her head on the edge of the pillow and her hand on his chest.

It was very still.

She didn't count.

She lay there with her hand on the still chest of the man she'd met on the Spire steps in the rain at nineteen, and married in a blue dress in the snow at twenty, and walked away from through a gold room at thirty-seven, and forgotten, and come home to, and courted with pies, and remembered.

She lay there with every day of it.

All of it. Hers. Paid for.

And didn't cry.

Not yet.

She just lay there. Holding it. The way you'd hold a lamp. Lit. In both hands. In the dark.

---

Behind her, very quietly, her son said, "*Mam*."

"*I know*."

"*Mam*, *he's*—"

"*I know*, *love*," said Seren Harrow. "*I know. I was here.*"

She didn't take her hand off his chest.

"*I was here*," she said again. "*I remember everything.*"
