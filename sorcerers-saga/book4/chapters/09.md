# Iron in the Deep

Silas Hask didn't want to leave the pool.

He didn't say so. He wasn't a man who said things. He stood on the grey rim in the early light with Kell's stick in his fist and his pick on his back and his face like a slab of coal, and looked at Lex, and didn't move.

"*It's two days*," said Lex. "*Three at the most. There and back.*"

Hask didn't answer.

"*Kit's here. And Brigid. And the Tulls. And Absalom.*"

Nothing.

"*And Ivy*," said Lex. "*She's its keeper. The net says so.*"

"*She's fifteen*," said Hask. "*And she can't walk without a crutch.*" He looked down at the stick in his hand. Plain. Dark. "*The Queen said stand in front of it. Every day. Till I can't.*"

"*I know what she said*."

"*Then you know I can't go*."

---

"*Big one*," said a voice.

They both turned.

Ivy Moss was sitting on the far side of the rim, where she always sat, with her bad foot tucked under her and her good one dangling over the deep. She wasn't looking at them. She was looking down. Listening.

"*It says go*," said Ivy.

Hask's face didn't change.

"*It says*—" She frowned. Tilted her head. "*It says, the big one knows the dark. It says, the big one's been in the deep places with a lamp on his head his whole life.*" She looked up. Across the pool. At Hask. With her strange light eyes. "*It says the smith needs someone who can hear rock settling.*"

Silas Hask stood on the rim for a long time.

Then he turned. And walked round the pool, slowly, to where Kit was sitting on the grass by the black stone. Seventeen. Lanky. With a burned hand from the forge and his helmet-lamp in his lap.

He held out the stick.

Kit looked at it. Then up at him.

"*Stand in front of it*," said Hask. "*Every day. Till I'm back.*"

"*Silas*—"

"*Nobody kneels at its edge*," said Silas Hask. "*Nobody cups their hands. Not while you're breathing.*"

Kit took the stick.

His hands were shaking. Hask's weren't. Hask let go of it slowly, one finger at a time, the way you'd let go of a rope you weren't sure would hold.

Then he turned round. And came back to Lex.

"*Coldwater*," he said.

---

Vharuun didn't like Coldwater either.

He said so. All the way there. A picture of black smoke. A picture of a hole in a hill with nothing in it. A picture of a dragon with soot on its nose, sneezing.

*Two days*, Lex told him. *Then the pool. Then sheep.*

The picture of the sneezing dragon came back. Larger.

*Four sheep*.

A pause.

The picture went away.

*[You're going to bankrupt Roole*,] said Ember.

*Roole can afford it*.

*[Roole's paying for six already. He wrote to you. He says the Keepers' quartermaster is threatening to resign.]*

---

Coldwater sat in the bottom of a long black valley at the eastern end of the Greyspine, where the mountains came down to the coal.

Lex had been here before. Two summers ago. To Unbind them. He'd stood by the Coldwater Stone in the square with his hand on it and his Root open and four thousand people in front of him, grey with coal-damp and Gloam, coughing. And he'd taken their Ceilings off. Every one. And watched them go up like mine-damp. Like a pit going up. Four thousand people levelling at once.

He remembered the coughing best.

It was quieter now.

He heard it as Vharuun came down over the lip of the valley. Not gone. Never gone. A dry rattle from a doorway. A wet hack from a man sitting on a step. But fewer of them. Much fewer. And the ones there were sounded different. Shorter. Lighter. As if something in the chests they came out of had more room round it.

"*Mal*," said Hask.

He'd heard it too. He was sitting behind Lex on the dragon's neck, enormous, his pick across his knees. Looking down at the black valley where he'd been born.

"*Since Lantern Night*," he said. "*It made room. In me. In all of us.*" His voice was very rough. "*Not a cure. It doesn't cure. But you can breathe further.*" He was quiet. "*My da never breathed further than halfway in his life.*"

---

The town was half empty.

That was the other thing. Half the houses shuttered. Half the chimneys cold. The long grey terraces climbing the valley sides in rows, and every second door shut, with grass coming up through the step.

"*They went*," said Hask. When Lex looked. "*After you. After the Ceilings came off.*" He shrugged. A small heavy movement. "*Hewers who'd been capped at nineteen all their lives. Level forty in a year. Level fifty. Why would they go back down a hole?*"

"*And the rest*?"

"*The rest stayed*," said Hask. "*Because it's ours.*" He looked at the pithead. At the great black wheel of it, turning slowly against the grey sky. "*Because somebody has to dig the coal or the whole of Caldor goes cold in the winter.*" He climbed down off the dragon. Heavily. "*And because some of us like it. Down there. In the dark. It's quiet.*"

---

They went to the Crown and Pick first.

Hask said they had to. He said you didn't go down a Coldwater pit without asking at the Crown and Pick, not if you wanted to come back up. He said it the way a sailor would say you didn't sail without throwing a coin in the harbour.

It was a low dark room with a fire at one end and a bar at the other and old men in between. Six of them. Sitting at a long table with their pots of ale. Grey-faced. Coal in the lines of their hands. Every one of them coughing, a little. Every one of them going quiet when Hask ducked in under the lintel.

They looked at him.

Then at Lex. At the mark on his cheek.

Then back at Hask.

"*Silas*," said one of them. The oldest. White-haired. With one eye clouded and a hand missing two fingers. "*Thought you were dead.*"

"*Not yet*, *Ned*."

"*Thought you'd gone off with a moth-woman and got yourself wings*."

"*I did*," said Hask. "*They fell off.*"

The old man looked at him for a long time.

Then he pushed a pot of ale across the table.

"*Sit down, lad*," said Old Ned. "*Your da would want to know you're breathing.*"

---

They asked about the Old Gallery.

Hask asked. Lex let him. He sat at the end of the long table with a pot of ale he didn't drink and listened.

The old men went quiet.

"*Nobody goes down the Old Gallery*," said Ned at last. "*You know that, Silas. Been sealed since my da's time.*"

"*I know*."

"*Then you know why*."

"*Tell him*," said Hask. And nodded at Lex.

---

Ned looked at Lex for a long moment with his one good eye.

"*It sings*," he said.

Lex waited.

"*Bottom of the Old Gallery*," said Ned. "*Two hundred fathom. Deepest working in the Greyspine. Deeper than anyone's dug since.*" He turned his ale pot round on the table. Slowly. "*My granda worked it. And his. And they all said the same. You put your ear to the rock down there, at the very bottom, at the end of the last seam, and you can hear it.*" He tapped the table with his three-fingered hand. Once. "*A note. One note. High. Like a harp-string. Like a wet finger round a glass.*"

"*Like the whole mountain was humming one thing*," said another old man. Quietly. "*Over and over. Forever.*"

"*Men went down there and came up singing it*," said Ned. "*Couldn't stop. My granda's brother. Hummed it for thirty years. In his sleep. At his own wedding. Hummed it on his deathbed.*" He drank. "*So they sealed it. Bricked up the head of the gallery. Put a stone over it with a hammer cut in it, the old way, so nobody'd ever go down.*"

He looked at Hask.

"*And now you want to*," said Old Ned.

"*Now I want to*," said Silas Hask.

---

"*Why*?"

Hask didn't answer. He looked at Lex.

Lex put down his ale pot.

"*Because something's coming along it*," he said. "*The note. The thing that hums. It's a thread. The deepest one in the world.*" He looked round the table. At the old grey faces. "*And something's climbing it. Iron. From the edge of the world. It'll pass under here.*"

Nobody said anything.

"*It might be under here now*," said Lex.

Old Ned put his pot down. Very slowly.

"*That's why*," he said.

"*Why what*?"

"*Why it changed*," said the old man. "*The note.*" He looked at the others. They didn't look back. They were looking at their ale. "*We hear it, sometimes. Up here. If the wind's right and the house is quiet. Faint. Through the floor. Through the rock.*" He swallowed. "*Always the same. One note. My whole life.*"

"*And now*?"

"*Now there's another sound*," said Ned. "*Under it. These three weeks.*" He looked at Lex with his one good eye. "*Like a hammer. A long way off. Every few hours.*"

He tapped the table. Once. Sharp.

*Clink*.

---

The stone with the hammer cut in it was still there.

At the end of a side-gallery a mile into the mountain. Down three shafts, by cage and by ladder. Through tunnels where Lex had to walk bent double and Hask had to walk on his knees. In the dark, with the lamps on their heads throwing small yellow circles on the black walls, and the air going thick and warm and close, and the sound of water dripping somewhere far off.

A wall. Brick. Old. Black with coal-dust. And set into it, at head height, a slab of grey stone with a hammer cut in it. Crossed out.

"*Like the moth*," said Lex. Very quietly. "*On the box. In the cellar.*"

*[People cross things out*,] said Ember. *[When they want to say don't. It's very old. It's older than letters.]*

Hask put his hand flat on the bricks.

He stood like that for a long time. His eyes shut. His big scarred palm on the black wall.

Then he took it away.

"*Hollow behind*," he said. "*Ten foot of brick. Then a drop. Long one.*" He opened his eyes. "*And something under the drop.*" His face was very strange. "*Something heavy, smith. Heavier than rock.*"

---

He opened the wall in four blows.

Lex watched him do it. He'd seen Hask break things before. He'd seen him break a Delve core and the Well scream. He'd seen him break the Moth Queen's wing on Lantern Night with one swing.

This was different.

Hask didn't swing hard. He hardly swung at all. He put the point of his pick against the bricks, here, there, very lightly, the way you'd touch a sleeping animal to see if it was breathing. Listening. With his head cocked.

*Fault-Finder*, the net called it. *He hears where things want to come apart.*

And then he leaned on the pick. Just a little. In one place.

And the wall fell down.

Not crashed. *Fell*. All of it. All at once. Ten feet of old brick sliding down into a heap of rubble at their feet in a soft dry roar, like a man sitting down who's been standing too long.

The dust came up round them. Black. Thick.

And through it, from the dark beyond, faint, so faint it was barely there at all—

A note.

---

Lex felt it before he heard it.

In his teeth. In his breastbone. In the bell on the cord round his neck, which didn't ring, but *shivered*, very slightly, the way a glass shivers on a shelf when someone sings near it.

A note. High. Clear. Thin. Like a harp-string. Like a wet finger round the rim of a glass. Going on and on and on. Never rising. Never falling. Never stopping. One note.

And under it—

*Clink*.

---

The Old Gallery went down at a slant.

Steep. Steeper than any working Lex had ever seen. Cut straight into the rock by men who'd been following a seam with picks and lamps, hundreds of years ago, and hadn't cared how steep it went so long as the coal kept coming. There were steps, in places. Hacked into the floor. Worn smooth. And an old iron chain pinned to the wall to hold on to, rusted almost through.

They went down.

Hask first. With his pick. With his head cocked. Listening. Lex behind, with his fire burning low and gold in one hand and the other on the rusted chain.

The note got louder.

Not louder. *Closer*. The way a sound gets closer when you're inside the thing that's making it. It wasn't in his ears anymore. It was in the rock. In the air. In him. Every step down, it went deeper into his chest, until he felt it humming in his ribs like a struck bell.

*[It's beautiful*,] said Ember. Very quietly. Wondering. *[Lex. Isn't it.]*

It was.

That was the worst thing. It was the most beautiful sound he'd ever heard. Pure and clear and patient. Like the world holding its breath.

He understood, going down, why old men's brothers came up humming it and never stopped.

---

The gallery ended in a crack.

Two hundred fathoms. Lex didn't need Hask to tell him. He could feel it. The weight of the mountain over his head. The whole Greyspine, pressing down.

The old men had cut the gallery down and down, following the coal, until the coal ran out. And then they'd stopped. Lex could see the marks of their last picks on the rock. Old. Rounded. Black.

And then, after they'd gone, something had come up from underneath.

---

The floor of the gallery was broken.

Split. Torn open. A crack, running across the bottom of the working from wall to wall, a yard wide. Its edges jagged and fresh and sharp, the rock round them shattered like glass. Not old. Not rounded. *New*. Weeks old. Days.

And coming up out of the crack, through the broken floor, like the rim of a great buried wheel—

Iron.

---

Black. Plain. Heavy. A curve of it. Rising up out of the crack at a slant and going back down into the rock on the far side. As thick as a man's thigh. Smooth. Perfectly smooth. No rust. No pitting. No hammer-marks. As if it hadn't been forged at all, but had simply *decided* to be iron, all at once, and been iron ever since.

A ring.

Lex could see that. Even though only the top of it showed. Even though the rest of it was buried in the rock, in the dark, two hundred fathoms down. He could feel the shape of it. Through the Stone. A great black circle, ten feet across, standing on its edge in the heart of the mountain.

And through the middle of it—

He didn't have to see that either. He could hear it.

The note.

---

He knelt at the edge of the crack.

He put his lamp down on the rock beside him and looked over. Down. Into the dark.

And saw it.

---

It was very thin. Thinner than a hair. Thinner than a thought. Running across the bottom of the crack, far down, straight as a ruler, from the dark on one side to the dark on the other. Silver. Bright. Brighter than anything had a right to be at the bottom of a coal mine two hundred fathoms under the Greyspine.

Humming.

The last thread.

He could see it because something was on it. The way he'd seen it at the Knot. The way he'd seen Elric's grey one.

Iron.

The great black ring stood round it. Closed. Tight. And beyond it, further west, deeper in the rock, another. And another. Going back into the dark under the mountains, toward the edge of the world. A line of black rings strung on a silver thread. Like beads on a wire.

And on the other side, east, toward the Knot—

Nothing. Yet.

Just the thread. Silver. Bare. Humming. Running off into the dark.

---

*Clink*.

He felt it this time. Not just heard. Felt it come up through the rock and into his knees. A shock. A jolt. Like standing on an anvil when someone strikes it.

And east of the great ring—just past it, just a little way along the thread, in the dark—

Another one closed.

Small, at first. A hoop of black. Snapping shut round the silver like a bracelet. And then *growing*. Swelling. Fattening. Spreading out into the rock round it with a sound like a slow groan, a creak, the sound of stone giving way. The new ring getting thicker. Heavier. Ten feet across.

The rock round it cracked.

Lex felt the whole floor of the gallery shudder.

---

"*He's here*," said Hask. Behind him. Very quietly. "*Isn't he.*"

"*He's here*."

"*Under us*."

"*Right under us*."

Hask came and knelt beside him. At the edge of the crack. And looked down at the silver thread and the black iron and the dark.

"*How do we stop him*?"

Lex didn't answer straight away.

He looked at the great black ring. At the iron. Smooth and perfect and finished.

He thought about a field of gold. About heat that wouldn't sink in.

*[It's not the same*,] said Ember. Quietly. *[Lex. It's not gold. It's not kept. It's iron. It's made.]*

*Everything made has a flaw*, Lex thought.

*[Yes*.]

*Everything*.

*[Yes*,] said Ember. *[Even crowns. Even queens. Even the Moth Queen had one.]*

He looked at Silas Hask.

"*Find it*," said Lex.

---

Hask didn't ask what. He didn't need to.

He climbed down into the crack.

Slowly. Carefully. His big hands finding holds in the shattered rock. His boots on the edges. Down, a yard, two yards, until he was crouched on a shelf of broken stone beside the great black ring, with the silver thread humming below him and his lamp throwing a small yellow circle on the iron.

He put his hand on it.

Lex saw his face change.

"*Cold*," said Hask. Very quietly. "*Smith. It's so cold.*"

"*Can you hear it*?"

Hask didn't answer.

He took his hand away. And took his pick off his back. The pick he'd swung at Delve cores, and at the Moth Queen's wing, and then held out, on a rubble-heap, on Lantern Night, for a moth to fly onto.

He put the point of it against the iron.

And listened.

---

It took a long time.

Lex knelt at the top of the crack and waited. He held his fire low and steady so Hask could see. The note hummed in his ribs. His knees ached on the rock.

Hask moved the point of the pick. A finger's width. Listened. Moved it again. Up the curve of the ring. Down. Round. Very slowly. Very patiently. The way a hewer works a seam he doesn't trust.

*Clink*.

Another ring. Further east. The floor shuddered. Dust came down from the roof of the gallery in a fine black rain.

Hask didn't look up. He moved the pick.

And stopped.

---

"*There*," he said.

Lex leaned over.

He couldn't see anything. Just iron. Black. Smooth. Perfect. Where the point of Hask's pick rested, a little below the top of the curve, on the eastern side.

"*There*," said Hask again. His voice was very strange. "*It's thin. Here. Just here. Like a—like a place where whoever made it stopped for a breath.*" He looked up. His face in the lamplight was grey and wet. "*It's not much, smith. It's the size of a fingernail. It's the only one.*"

"*It's enough*," said Lex.

---

He went down.

Into the crack. Onto the shelf of broken stone. Beside Hask. Beside the ring. So close to the silver thread that the note wasn't in his ribs anymore, it *was* his ribs, it was his teeth, it was the blood going round in him.

He took Aurelian's hammer out of the loop on his belt.

Small. Old. Plain. A smith's hammer, the kind you'd use for fine work. For setting rivets. For tapping a rune into a blade. Nine hundred years old. The hammer that had woven the world.

He'd tapped a Moth Queen's flaw with it.

He looked at the place where Hask's pick rested. At the black iron. At the spot the size of a fingernail where someone, nine hundred years ago, or nine weeks ago, had stopped for a breath.

"*Ready*," said Hask.

Lex lifted the hammer.

*Tink*.

---

It was a very small sound.

Smaller than *clink*. Higher. Lighter. A sound like a spoon on a cup. Like a fingernail on a bell.

For a moment nothing happened.

And then the ring cracked.

---

From the point where the hammer touched. Outward. A thin bright line, running through the black iron like a crack running through ice. Up the curve. Down the curve. Round. All the way round. Faster than he could follow it. Through the iron and down into the rock where the rest of the ring was buried and on round and back up the other side.

The whole ring rang.

Not *clink*. Not *tink*. A great deep groaning boom, like a bell the size of a mountain being struck once. It went through the rock. Through the gallery. Through Lex. He felt his teeth jar in his head.

And the ring fell apart.

Into pieces. Into black shards. Sliding off the silver thread and falling away into the dark of the crack, ringing as they fell, clattering, chiming, down and down and down.

And the thread, where it had been, was bare.

---

The thread *sang*.

The note changed. For the first time. For the first time in nine hundred years, in the whole history of the Old Gallery, in every story every old man in the Crown and Pick had ever told. It went up. Just a little. A half-step. Like a string that's been held down suddenly let go.

And far off, along it, east, in the dark, somebody grunted.

---

Lex heard it.

It came up the thread. Up through the rock. Up through the iron rings that were still there, east of the broken one. A sound. A voice. Low. Heavy. Rough. Like a cart going over gravel.

Not words.

Just a grunt. The sound a man makes when someone steps on his foot.

And then, after it—

"*Huh*."

---

"*Smith*," said a voice.

It came out of the iron.

Out of all of it. Out of the rings still standing to the east, out of the shards still falling into the dark below, out of the rock itself. Low. Flat. Heavy. A voice that had said perhaps four thousand words in its whole life before the drinking and perhaps four hundred since, and meant every one.

"*You broke it*," said Caddoc of the Iron Crown.

Lex didn't answer.

"*Nobody's broken one of my rings*," said the voice. "*Not in nine hundred years. Not ever.*" A pause. "*Huh.*"

He didn't sound angry. That was the strange thing. He sounded *interested*. The way a mason sounds when someone shows him a crack in a wall he built.

"*How*?" said Caddoc.

"*It had a flaw*," said Lex.

A long silence.

"*Yes*," said Caddoc. "*It would have. I was tired, there. Just there. I'd been climbing a long time.*" Another pause. "*I stopped for a breath.*"

---

"*Go back*," said Lex.

He said it to the iron. To the thread. To the voice. Crouched on a shelf of broken rock two hundred fathoms under the Greyspine with a nine-hundred-year-old hammer in his hand.

"*Go back*," he said again. "*Down the thread. To the grey. To your brother. Tell him no.*"

The iron was silent.

"*I can't*," said Caddoc.

"*You can*."

"*No*." The voice was very flat. Very patient. "*I gave my word. To my brother. Nine hundred years ago. On the shingle. In chains. I said I'd go where he went and do what he asked.*" A pause. "*I don't break my word, smith. I'm not like the rest of them. I've never broken it once.*"

"*He's going to keep the world*," said Lex. "*All of it. Every thread. Every child.*"

"*I know*."

"*And you don't care*?"

Another silence.

"*I care*," said Caddoc. "*I'm not cruel, smith. I'm only certain.*" A pause. "*I'm certain I gave my word. I'm certain I'll keep it. I'm certain I'll come down this thread and into your knot on the night you light your lanterns and take your Key to my brother.*" The voice didn't change. "*Those are the things I'm certain of. The rest I leave to him.*"

---

*Clink*.

Lex felt it.

Not east. Not along the thread toward the Knot. *Here*. Right here. On the bare stretch of silver where the broken ring had been.

A hoop of black. Snapping shut round the thread. Exactly where the old one had stood.

And growing.

Fast. Faster than the others. Swelling. Fattening. Out. The iron spreading through the rock like ink through water, like frost across a pane. A foot thick. Two. Ten feet across.

Whole. Smooth. Perfect.

No flaw.

---

"*It's my road*, *smith*," said Caddoc of the Iron Crown. "*And I'm on it.*"

---

The rock moved.

Not the ring. The *rock*. All round them. The walls of the crack. The floor of the gallery above. Lex felt it go. Through the Stone. A great slow shudder, running up through the mountain from below, from the thread, from the iron.

And the iron came up.

Not rings. *Spikes*. Black. Sharp. Thrusting up out of the broken floor of the crack like the teeth of a trap. Out of the walls. Out of the rock all round them. Slowly. Inexorably. Growing toward each other. Closing.

Like a fist.

"*Out*," said Hask.

---

They climbed.

Lex didn't remember most of it. He remembered his hands on the broken rock. He remembered the iron coming up beside his face, black and cold and smooth, so close he could feel the cold coming off it. He remembered Hask above him, reaching down, his big scarred hand closing round Lex's wrist like a vice, hauling.

He remembered the gallery floor bucking under them as they came up out of the crack.

He remembered running. Up the steep slant of the Old Gallery. In the dark. With the lamp on his head swinging and the rusted chain under his hand and the note screaming in his ribs, not humming now, *screaming*, high and wild and wrong, and the iron coming up through the floor behind them, *clink, clink, clink*, spike after spike after spike.

He remembered Hask stopping.

---

Halfway up the gallery.

Just stopping. Turning round. In the narrow dark. With his back to the slope and his face to the iron coming up the gallery behind them.

"*Hask*—"

"*Go*," said Silas Hask.

"*Hask*, *come on*—"

"*It's following the thread*," said Hask. "*Not us. The thread runs under the gallery. All the way up. It's going to come up under the whole mountain.*" He hefted his pick. "*Go. I'll find the faults. Slow it.*"

"*You can't*—"

"*I'm a Coldwater hewer*," said Silas Hask. "*I've been listening to rock settle my whole life.*"

And turned his back on Lex. And put his pick to the floor of the gallery. And listened.

---

Lex didn't go.

He stood behind him in the dark with his fire in his hand and watched.

The iron came up the slope. A spike. Black. Sharp. Bursting up through the floor five yards below them. Then another. Four yards. Three.

And Hask's pick touched the rock in front of the spike.

Lightly. Very lightly. Listening.

And then he leaned on it.

And the floor of the gallery fell in.

---

Not all of it. One slab. A great wedge of rock between Hask's boots and the oncoming iron. It cracked along a line Lex couldn't see and slid away, down, into the dark, taking the spike with it. Carrying it down. Crashing into the crack below.

The next spike came up. Further back. Slower.

Hask found the fault in front of it.

Another slab went.

"*It's following the grain*," said Hask. Very quietly. Not looking round. "*The iron. It grows along the lines in the rock. Where the rock's already weak. So I break the rock first.*" He moved the pick. Listened. "*And it has to go round.*"

Another slab.

The iron came on. Slower now. Much slower. Having to go round. Having to find another way.

---

It caught him on the fifth one.

Lex saw it happen. Saw the spike come up out of the wall of the gallery, not the floor, sideways, fast, where Hask wasn't looking. Black. Thin as a blade. And Hask's arm was in the way.

His right arm. His pick-arm. Holding the pick out to the rock.

The spike went past it.

Not through. Past. Along the outside of the forearm, from the wrist to the elbow. Fast. Clean. The way a knife goes past an apple when you're peeling it.

Hask made a sound.

Not a scream. A hewer's sound. A grunt. The sound a man makes when the cage-chain slips and catches his hand.

And kept working.

---

Lex burned it.

Not with fire. He'd learned that. You couldn't burn Caddoc's iron any more than you could burn the Moth Queen's light or a dust-man made of grey. He'd tried, at the crack, and it had done nothing.

He used the Stone.

He put his hand flat on the wall of the gallery beside the spike and *asked*. Not the iron. The rock round it. *Please. Hold it. Just here. Just for a moment.*

And the old rock of the Greyspine, which had been here long before any Crowned king drank from any pool, which had been here before the world was woven and would be here after, closed round the spike like a fist.

And held it.

Not for long. Lex felt the iron push. Felt it grow. Felt the rock groan and crack and begin to give.

Long enough.

Hask turned round.

---

"*Go*," said Lex.

This time Hask went.

They went up the rest of the Old Gallery together. Hask first, with his pick in his left hand now and his right arm held against his chest, the blood running black down it in the lamplight. Lex behind, with his hand on the wall, asking the rock to hold, and hold, and hold.

They came out through the broken wall at the top. Past the stone with the hammer cut in it and crossed out.

And behind them, from the bottom of the gallery, from two hundred fathoms down, from the iron—

*Clink*.

And then nothing.

---

The iron had stopped.

Lex felt it stop. Through the Stone. All at once. As if someone had put down a tool.

It didn't come up any further. It didn't come after them. It stood in the bottom of the Old Gallery in a forest of black spikes, the way it had stood in the crack round the thread. Finished. Perfect. Still.

And far down, under it, the thread hummed.

One note. The old one. The half-step gone. As if it had never been.

"*He's not following*," said Hask. Panting. Leaning against the wall of the side-gallery with his arm against his chest.

"*No*."

"*Why not*?"

"*Because he doesn't need to*," said Lex. "*We're not on his road.*"

---

They didn't talk on the way up.

Three shafts. Ladder and cage. Through the tunnels, bent double, on their knees. Hask's blood dripping on the black floor. Lex holding his fire up so they could see.

When they came out of the pithead into the evening, the whole of Coldwater was standing in the yard.

---

Lex didn't know how they knew.

The note, maybe. They'd all heard the note, all their lives. Faint. Through the floor. On quiet nights. And today it had changed. Gone up a half-step, for the first time in nine hundred years, and then come down again.

And then the mountain had shuddered.

They were standing in the pithead yard in the grey evening light. Hundreds of them. Men in their pit-clothes. Women with their shawls over their heads. Children. Old men from the Crown and Pick. Old Ned at the front, with his one good eye and his hand with two fingers missing.

Silent.

Looking at the cage as it came up.

At Silas Hask, stepping out of it. With his arm against his chest and his pick in his left hand and the blood running black down his sleeve.

---

Nobody said anything.

Hask stood in front of the cage and looked at them. At the town he'd been born in. That had coughed his father to death, and his mother, and his sister. That he'd left to go and break cores and get himself wings.

A woman came out of the crowd.

Old. Small. Bent. With a shawl over her head and a basin in her hands. She came across the black yard to Hask and stopped in front of him and looked up. A long way up.

"*Give it here*," she said.

He looked at her.

"*Your arm*," said the old woman. "*Give it here, Silas Hask. I've been binding hewers' arms since before your mam was born. I bound your da's when the cage-chain took the skin off it.*" She set the basin down on the ground. "*I'll bind yours.*"

He held it out.

---

She cut his sleeve away.

The wound was long. Wrist to elbow. Clean-edged. Deep. Not bleeding as much now. And round it, along both edges, the skin was strange. Darkened. Grey-black. As if the cold of the iron had gone into it and stayed.

The old woman looked at it for a long time.

"*That'll scar*," she said.

"*I know*."

"*That'll scar black*," she said. "*Like the blue scars. Like coal-tattoos. Like the miners get when the dust goes in a cut and the skin closes over it.*" She looked up at him. "*Only that's not coal.*"

"*No*," said Hask. "*It's iron.*"

She washed it. She bound it. She tied off the bandage with a knot Lex didn't know, a miner's knot, quick and hard.

Then she patted his arm. Once. Gently. On the bandage.

"*Your da'd be proud*," she said. "*Going down the Old Gallery.*" She picked up her basin. "*Daft. But proud.*"

---

The crowd didn't go home.

They stood in the pithead yard in the falling dark, hundreds of them, quiet. Waiting. Lex didn't know for what.

And then somebody started to sing.

He didn't see who. Somewhere in the middle of the crowd. A man's voice. Low. Rough. Cracked with coal-damp. Singing something slow. A hymn. A miners' hymn. The kind they sang going down at the start of a shift and coming up at the end of it, to count each other.

Another voice joined it. A woman's.

Then another.

Then all of them.

---

Lex had never heard anything like it.

Hundreds of voices. In a black valley at the end of the Greyspine, in the dark, under the great slow-turning wheel of the pithead. Rough voices. Cracked voices. Voices that coughed between lines and came back in. Voices that had breathed coal-dust and Gloam for generations and only lately, since Lantern Night, since a Well came home and made room, been able to breathe further than halfway.

Singing.

Not loud. Not beautiful, the way the note was beautiful. But *more*. More than one note. More than one voice. A hundred notes, and none of them quite the same, and all of them going the same way.

Hask was standing by the cage with his bandaged arm against his chest. His face in the dusk was grey and wet.

He was singing too.

---

*[He heard this*,] said Ember. Very quietly.

*Who*?

*[Caddoc. In the frost. In the message. He said he could hear men singing above him in the dark.]* A pause. *[This is what he heard.]*

Lex stood in the pithead yard and listened.

To the hymn. To the hundreds of rough cracked voices. And under it, faint, through the ground, through the black rock, through two hundred fathoms of mountain—

One note. Pure. Clear. Patient. The last thread, humming, the way it always had.

And every few hours, far off, a hammer.

*Clink*.

---

*[It broke*,] said Ember.

Lex didn't answer.

*[Lex. It broke. The ring. You broke it.]* A pause. *[He grew it back. But it broke. Nobody's ever broken one of his rings. He said so.]*

*He grew it back*.

*[Everything's a crack first*,] said Ember. Very softly. *[Ask any smith.]*

Lex stood in the dark with the hymn going up round him and his hand on the bell, which wasn't ringing, and thought about that.

They flew back to the pool in the morning.

Hask didn't say much. He sat behind Lex on Vharuun's neck with his bandaged arm across his knees and looked down at the green of the Blightmarch coming up under them. At the pool. At the small black house on the slope with the lantern in its window.

At the boy standing on the grey rim beside a square black stone. Seventeen. Lanky. With a long plain stick in his fist. Standing in front of the pool.
