@@PART Part Two: The Grey Country
# The White Road

There were no days in the Court, so Antho made some.

"*A day*," he said, "*is from when Pim wakes up to when he can't hold the room anymore.*"

They were sitting in the dust at the side of the white road. Between a gold washerwoman with a basket of gold linen on her hip and a gold boy holding a gold hoop. The washerwoman's eyes had followed them for a mile before they sat down. They were still following. Antho could feel them. On the back of his neck. Like a draught.

"*And a night*," said Antho, "*is when he makes the room and we sleep in it.*"

"*That's not a day*," said Lex. "*That's a Pim.*"

"*It's what we've got*."

His brother looked at the dim grey-gold light that came from nowhere. That hadn't changed since they stepped out of the Veil. That wasn't going to change.

"*Two Pims*, *then*," said Lex. "*Since the door.*"

"*Two Pims*," agreed Antho.

He looked at the basket on the ground beside the boy. Twelve cups. Each one heavy with nothing. Each one with an iron lid that sighed.

They'd drunk two.

---

He'd done the sums a dozen times. He did them again, sitting in the dust. He couldn't help it. He'd been doing sums in his head since the shingle, since the Ferryman, since Seraphine sat on the rim of the pool with a Hush-cup in her lap and said *thirty-six days*.

A cup was three days. For one.

There were two of them who needed it. Him and Lex.

If they each drank only while they walked—only in the *day*—and slept at night in Pim's room, a cup did them both for a day and a half.

Twelve cups. Eighteen days.

If Pim held the nights.

He looked at Pim.

---

The boy was sitting with his back against the gold boy's legs and his eyes shut. Small. Thin. In his blue coat with the brass buttons. His face very white. His hands open on his knees.

He'd held the room for the whole of the first night. Antho had lain awake in it and felt it. Round the four of them, in the dust, at the side of the road. A yard across. Quiet. Close. Pushing back against the full heavy air of the Court the way a cupped hand pushes back against water.

He'd held it till Antho thought he must be asleep. And then longer. And then, when Antho finally sat up and looked, he'd been sitting exactly where he'd sat down, with his eyes open, staring at nothing, holding it.

*Sleep*, Antho had said.

*Can't*, Pim had said. *If I sleep it goes.*

---

"*Pim*," said Antho now.

The boy opened his eyes.

"*How long can you*—"

"*Long enough*," said Pim.

"*That's not an answer*."

Pim looked at him. With his grey-violet eyes. Very steady.

"*Long enough*," he said again.

And shut them.

---

They walked.

Down the white road. Between the kept. With the hum all round them, one note, low and soft and endless. With the palaces glittering on the edge of the sky.

Closer, now. A little. Antho could see shapes. A tall clear one, catching the light like a spire of ice. A dark one, squat and heavy. A gold one, domed, shining dully. And in the middle, the tallest, white as bone, with a roof like a frozen waterfall.

The hall.

He didn't know how he knew. He'd never seen it. He'd only heard it described. By his mother, who'd dreamed it. By Seraphine, who'd read about it. By Lex, who'd heard it from Elric, who'd felt it from the Knot.

He knew.

---

The kept didn't end.

That was the thing Antho couldn't get used to. He'd thought they'd thin out. He'd thought there'd be a few hundred near the Veil, where Oszric had walked out among them in the first years. A crowd. And then the road would go on alone.

It didn't. They went on. Mile after mile. On both sides of the white road, in rows, three and four deep, standing in the dust. Cooks and grooms and maids and gardeners. Soldiers in gold armour with gold spears. Scribes with gold quills. A man with a gold fiddle tucked under his chin, mid-note. A woman with a gold baby at her breast.

All of them gold. All of them still. All of them with their eyes moving.

All of them humming.

---

He was a Threshold Warden.

He felt doors. Every kind. Every door in every room, every gap between one breath and the next, every place at the back of a person's eyes where they kept the things they didn't say.

And the last one.

He felt for it. He couldn't help it. He walked down the white road between nine thousand gold people and felt for the last door in every one of them.

It wasn't there.

Not one. Not in the washerwoman. Not in the boy with the hoop. Not in the fiddler or the soldier or the woman with the baby. Not in the baby.

Nine thousand people. Nine thousand places where a door should be.

Gold. Smooth. Bricked up so perfectly you couldn't even see the seam.

---

It got worse the further they went.

He didn't tell Lex. He didn't tell anyone. He walked down the white road and felt for the doors and found nothing and felt the Clarity in him tremble. Not drop. Not like the old days. He was at a hundred and he stayed at a hundred. But it *trembled*. Like a candle-flame in a room where someone keeps opening and closing a window.

He'd felt Bet Dale's missing door at the Lantern Keep and it had made the floor lurch under him.

This was nine thousand Bet Dales.

He walked through the middle of them with his hands at his sides and his eyes on the white stone and tried very hard not to feel for anything at all.

---

"*You're doing it*," said Lex. Beside him. Quietly.

"*Doing what*?"

"*The thing you do*. *When you're listening to something I can't hear.*" His brother didn't look at him. He was looking at the road. "*You've been doing it since the groom. Since the first one.*"

Antho didn't answer.

"*Doors*," said Lex.

"*Yes*."

"*They haven't got any*."

"*No*."

Lex walked beside him for a while without saying anything.

"*Stop*," he said at last.

Antho looked at him.

"*Stop looking for them*," said Lex. "*You can't open them. Not here. Not yet. You said so. To the four at the edge.*" His voice was very quiet. "*So stop feeling for them. Just for now. Just till we've got the crown.*"

"*I can't*."

"*I know you can't*." Lex didn't look at him. "*Try anyway. For me.*"

---

He tried.

He walked down the white road between the kept and thought about other things. About Seraphine. About her list. About reason six hundred and five, which was *he checks*.

About his father. In a bed under a boarded window. With a Lantern beside him, three panes of glass and one of horn. Breathing. In. Catch. Out.

About a gold king walking out of a door at the edge of the world.

*I'll be at your house*.

That didn't help.

---

Hask was breathing.

Antho noticed it on the third Pim. Walking behind the big man on the white road. Watching his back. The scarred leather jerkin. The two long scars down it where something had once torn through.

Rising and falling.

Slowly. Deeply. All the way down. Every breath.

He'd been coughing all spring. Antho had heard him at the pool. A short dry rattle. Twice. Then a breath. Careful. Further than halfway. Mal had made room in his lungs and it was better than it had been, but it was still there. The grey. The catch.

It wasn't there now.

Hask walked down the white road between the kept, breathing like a man who'd never been down a mine in his life.

---

"*It's not the room*," said Hask. When Antho came up beside him. Before Antho could ask. "*Mal's room. In my chest. That's still there. I can feel it.*" He looked at the road ahead. "*It's this place. It's full. The air. Everything. It fills me up. All the way.*"

"*Like she did*."

"*Like she did*," said Silas Hask.

They walked for a while.

"*I could stay*," said Hask.

Antho didn't say anything.

"*I won't*," said Hask. "*I said I wouldn't. I keep my word. I'm not like*—" He stopped. Didn't say whose name. "*But I could. I could find a place at the side of this road and sit down in the dust and breathe. All the way. Forever.*" His jaw was very tight. "*Nobody'd keep me. The gold won't stick. It thinks I'm one of theirs.*"

"*You're not*."

"*No*," said Hask. "*I'm a Coldwater hewer with a black scar on my arm and a stick waiting for me by a pool.*" He breathed in. All the way down. Out. "*I'm just telling you. In case you see me slowing down. In case I stop.*"

"*If you stop*?"

"*Pick me up*," said Silas Hask. "*And carry me. The way the smith carried Kell.*"

---

They stopped when Pim stumbled.

He didn't fall. Antho caught his arm. He'd been walking behind Lex with the basket on his back and his eyes half shut, and his foot had come down on the edge of a slab and turned, and he'd gone sideways.

Antho caught him.

The boy was shaking. Antho could feel it. Through the blue coat. Through the thin arm. A deep fine tremor, like a wire pulled too tight.

"*Night*," said Antho.

"*Not yet*—"

"*Night*," said Antho again. "*You're making it night. Now. Sit down.*"

---

They sat down in the dust at the side of the road.

Behind a row of gold soldiers. Ten of them. In gold armour, with gold spears, standing to attention. Their gold eyes moving. Down. To the four people sitting at their feet.

Pim shut his eyes.

And the room came.

---

Antho felt it. Soft. Close. Quiet. Spreading out from the boy the way warmth spreads out from a hearth. Round the four of them. A yard. Two. The full heavy air of the Court pressing against the edge of it.

And not coming in.

He felt his own chest loosen. The room Mal had given him from the cup—it was thinning. He could feel it. A day and a half was nearly done. But Pim's room was round him now. Holding. And he could breathe.

He lay down on his back in the dust.

And looked up at the soldiers.

---

They were looking back.

Ten gold faces. Ten pairs of gold eyes. Looking down at him from inside their gold helms. Young, most of them. Some of them very young. Seventeen. Eighteen. Boys. With gold spears and gold armour and gold freckles on their gold noses.

The one nearest Antho's head had a scar across his chin. Gold. Old. A boy's scar. From a fall, maybe. Off a wall. Out of a tree.

His eyes were moving.

Slowly. So slowly. From Antho's face. To Pim's. To Lex's. To Hask's. And back.

And the hum came out of him.

Antho could hear it. Close. So close. One note. Low. Soft. Coming out of a gold throat that couldn't open, a gold chest that couldn't breathe. Not loud. Barely there.

But there.

---

"*What are they saying*?" said Lex. Quietly. Lying on his back beside Antho. Looking up at the same gold faces.

"*I don't know*."

"*It sounds like a word*."

"*It sounds like a note*."

"*It sounds like a word*," said Lex, "*with all the letters worn off.*"

Antho listened.

He lay in the dust in Pim's room and listened to the gold soldier with the scar on his chin hum one note, over and over, through a throat that couldn't open.

And thought, *Lex is right*.

*It's a word*.

*It's a word that's been said so many times for so long that it's worn smooth. Like a coin rubbed smooth in a pocket. Like a step worn hollow by feet.*

*I can't hear what it was*.

---

*[It's a hum*,] said a voice.

Antho turned his head.

Lex was lying very still. His eyes unfocused. Listening to something only he could hear.

"*Ember says it's a hum*," said Lex. "*It says it's not a word. It says it's just the sound kept things make. Like the hum a kettle makes before it boils. Like the hum a hive makes.*" He was quiet. "*It says it doesn't think we should listen too hard.*"

"*Why not*?"

Lex didn't answer for a moment.

"*It says*," he said slowly, "*if it is a word, and we find out what it is, we'll have to do something about it.*"

---

Antho didn't sleep much.

He lay in Pim's room in the dust behind the gold soldiers and listened to the hum and felt for doors he couldn't find and tried not to. Lex slept. Eventually. On his back with his hand on the bell and his mouth open. Hask slept sitting up, against the legs of a gold soldier, with his pick across his knees, breathing all the way down.

Pim didn't sleep.

He sat cross-legged in the middle of them with his eyes shut and his hands open on his knees and his face white, holding the room.

Antho watched him.

He'd known him for four years. Since a winter night at the Lantern Keep, when he'd opened the door to a knock and found a boy on the step. Twelve, maybe. Starved. Beaten. Blue with cold. With a scar on his elbow from a man called Kessick who'd tried to beat the voices out of him.

Silent.

He'd been silent for twelve years. Not because he couldn't talk. Because he'd learned, very young, that talking got you hit. And that silence—real silence, the kind that went all the way down—was a place you could hide.

He'd made a room inside himself. Out of being hurt. And lived in it.

And now he was holding it open. Round three grown men. In the middle of a country where nothing had moved for nine hundred years.

---

"*Pim*," said Antho. Very quietly. So as not to wake the others.

The boy didn't open his eyes.

"*You can tell me*," said Antho. "*If it's too much. If you can't. You can tell me and we'll drink a cup. We've got twelve.*"

"*Eighteen days*," said Pim. Not opening his eyes. "*If I hold the nights. Nine if I don't.*"

"*I know*. *But*—"

"*I can hold it*."

"*You're shaking*."

"*I know*," said Pim.

He didn't say anything else.

Antho lay in the dust and looked at him. At the small white face. At the hands, open on his knees, trembling very slightly. At the blue coat buttoned to the chin. At the pocket over his heart, buttoned too, with a folded piece of paper inside it. A drawing of a cat. No words.

He didn't argue.

He'd learned, a long time ago, from his father and his mother and a Moth Queen and a Well, that you didn't argue with someone who'd decided to hold a door.

You just held it with them.

---

He reached out.

Across the dust. Slowly. And put his hand on the boy's knee. Next to his open hand. Not holding it. Just there.

And opened himself.

He wasn't a room-maker. He wasn't the Hollow. He didn't have the little piece of the seventh that Pim had made out of being hurt.

But he was a door.

That was what he was. The whole of what he was. He'd stood in every kind of door. He'd held the door of the Hollowmaw for eight years with a hunger pushing on the other side. He'd held a door open for a whole Well, on Lantern Night, with his whole heart.

A room is just the space inside a door.

He held it.

---

He felt Pim feel it.

The boy's hand moved. Just slightly. On his knee. His fingers touching Antho's.

The trembling eased.

Not stopped. Eased. Like a wire with someone else's hand on it, taking some of the pull.

"*Oh*," said Pim. Very quietly. Not opening his eyes.

"*I can't make it*," said Antho. "*Only you can make it. But I can hold the edge. While you rest.*"

The boy didn't say anything for a long time.

Then his shoulders came down. Just a little. From round his ears.

And Antho felt the room settle. Round them. Round all four of them. Not Pim's alone now. A boy's quiet, held up at the edges by a man who'd stood in every kind of door.

Pim's head drooped. Forward. Onto his chest.

He slept.

The room held.

---

Antho didn't sleep at all.

He sat in the dust behind the gold soldiers with his hand on Pim's knee and held the edge of the room and listened to the hum.

One note. Low. Soft. From the boy with the scar on his chin. From all ten soldiers. From the washerwoman a hundred yards back and the boy with the hoop. From all nine thousand of them, on both sides of the white road, from the Veil to the palaces.

A word with all the letters worn off.

He listened to it till the boy woke.

He didn't hear what it was.

He thought he was beginning to.

---

They saw the glass palace on the fourth Pim.

They'd seen it for days. A spire of ice on the edge of the sky. Catching the dim grey-gold light and throwing it back in flashes, like a mirror turned in the sun.

On the fourth Pim it wasn't on the edge of the sky anymore.

It was *there*.

Off the road. To the south. A mile, maybe. Rising out of the grey-gold dust like a frozen waterfall turned upside down. Glass. All of it. Towers and spires and domes and bridges. Clear as water. Glittering. Every surface a mirror. Every pane catching every other pane, so that the whole palace seemed to be made of light bouncing off itself forever.

And it was full of faces.

---

Antho saw them from the road.

He couldn't not see them. In every pane. In every tower. Pale. White-haired. Grey-eyed. Turning. Following.

Thousands. A palace of thousands of faces, all the same face, all looking out of the glass at four people on the white road.

"*She's home*," said Lex. Very quietly.

"*She came home with Mam's blood*," said Antho. "*In her hand.*"

"*She's watching us*."

"*She's always watching*," said Antho. "*It's what she is.*"

---

They didn't go near it.

They kept to the road. To the north side of it. As far from the glass palace as the white stone would let them. Between the kept and the dust. With their heads down.

The faces followed them.

All the way past. For a mile. Two. Turning in every pane of every tower as they went. Thousands of pale faces, all turning together, the way a field of sunflowers turns to follow the sun.

Nobody came out.

No hand reached out of the glass. No voice called. No frost wrote messages on the panes. The Glass Queen sat in her palace of mirrors and watched them go by, and did nothing at all.

"*Why isn't she*—" said Lex.

"*I don't know*."

"*She could*. *She came out of a stream at Harrow. She came out of your Lantern.*"

"*I know*."

"*Then why*—"

"*I don't know*," said Antho again. "*Maybe she's waiting. Maybe she's frightened. Maybe Father's mirror-ward is still in her head.*" He looked at the palace. At the faces. "*Maybe she's just looking.*"

---

*[She's lonely*,] said Ember.

Lex told him, later. That night. In Pim's room.

*[Arion said it. In the cottage. Nine hundred years of her own face. And then the baby looked at her.]* A pause. *[I think she's looking at us because we're the first new thing she's seen since the baby.]*

*She's dangerous*, Lex had thought.

*[Yes*,] Ember had said. *[Lonely things often are.]*

---

The iron palace came next.

North of the road. Closer than the glass. Squat. Heavy. Dark. A great block of black iron rising out of the dust like a fist. No towers. No windows. A single door, huge, at the front, standing open.

Empty.

Antho could feel it. As they passed. A Threshold Warden feels doors. That one was open and there was nothing behind it. No one. Not a servant. Not a guard. Not a kept groom with a gold brush.

"*Caddoc's*," said Lex.

"*He's on the thread*."

"*He left it open*."

"*He doesn't care*," said Antho. "*He's not coming back. He's going to the Knot. And then he's going to his brother with the Key.*" He looked at the open iron door. "*He doesn't need a house. He's got a road.*"

---

And there was something else.

Beyond the iron palace. North. Far off. Rising out of the dust at the very edge of what Antho could see.

A line.

Silver. Thin. Thinner than a hair. Straight. Going *up*. Out of the plain. Into the dim grey-gold of the sky. At a slant. Up and up and up, until it was too thin to see and was only a brightness, a suggestion, going on into nothing.

And on it, near the ground, as far as Antho could make out—

Rings.

Black. Small at this distance. Strung on the silver line like beads. Going up. Into the sky. Further and further. Too many to count.

---

"*The last thread*," said Lex.

He'd stopped walking. He was standing on the white road with his face turned north and his hand over his eyes, though there was no sun.

"*That's where it starts*," he said. "*Here. On this side. That's where it ties into the Court.*" He lowered his hand. "*That's where he started climbing.*"

Nobody spoke.

"*It goes up*," said Lex. "*Look. Up. Not across. Up into the sky.*" His voice was very strange. "*And under the world. And down to the Knot. And he's on it. Somewhere. Ringing it with iron.*"

"*Can we cut it*?" said Hask. "*From here. At the bottom.*"

Lex didn't answer for a long time.

"*It's his road*," he said at last. "*He's on it. He said so. At Coldwater. You saw the ring grow back.*" He looked at the silver line going up into nothing. "*If we broke it here, he'd ring it here. If we broke it a mile up, he'd ring it a mile up.*" A pause. "*It doesn't matter where you cut a man's road. He's still on it.*"

---

They walked on.

The silver line stayed with them. To the north. Going up into the sky. Antho kept looking at it. He couldn't help it. A thin bright line with black beads on it, climbing into nothing.

*Clink*, he thought. *Clink. Clink.*

He couldn't hear it. Not from here.

He heard it anyway.

---

The hall was ahead now.

White. Bone-white. Taller than the glass palace. Taller than anything. With a roof like a frozen waterfall, glittering. And doors. Tall. Glass. Standing open at the end of the white road, where the road ran up a long shallow flight of steps and stopped.

They didn't go near it either.

They didn't need to. The crown wasn't in the hall. The crown was in a tower. A mile off the road. North. In a forest of thorns.

Antho looked north.

Nothing. Dust. The iron palace. The silver line going up into the sky. Flat grey-gold plain going away to the edge of everything.

No thorns.

"*Mam said near the palaces*," he said. "*A mile off. North. Past the hall.*"

"*Past the hall*," said Lex.

They looked at the hall. White. Tall. With its doors open.

"*How far past*?"

"*She didn't say*," said Antho. "*She dreamed it. Dreams aren't maps.*"

---

They stopped for the night behind the last of the kept.

There was a last. Antho hadn't believed there would be. But the rows of gold people ran out, a mile short of the hall. The last one was a girl. Small. Seven or eight. In a white dress with a gold sash, gold now, all of it. Holding a little gold bunch of flowers in both hands. As if she'd been about to give them to someone.

Her eyes moved. Slowly. To the four people sitting down in the dust behind her.

Pim shut his eyes.

The room came.

Antho put his hand on Pim's knee and held the edge of it.

---

He woke in the dark that wasn't dark.

He didn't know what woke him. He'd been asleep, for once. Properly. Pim had held the room for most of the night and then dropped into sleep sitting up, and the room had held, and Antho had held the edge of it, and somewhere in there he'd slept too.

He woke.

And the room was wrong.

---

Thin. That was the first thing. The room was thin. Not gone. Still there. Still round them. But thin. Stretched. Like a bubble that's been blown too big. Like a cloth pulled tight over a drum.

Held only at the edges.

By him.

Only by him.

---

He sat up.

Lex was asleep. On his back. With his hand on the bell. Hask was asleep. Sitting up. Against the gold girl's legs. With his pick across his knees.

The basket was there. On the dust. In the middle of where they'd been sitting. Twelve cups. No, eleven. They'd drunk another that day. Eleven cups, each with an iron lid that sighed.

And beside it, folded, a piece of paper.

Pim wasn't there.

---

Antho picked up the paper.

He knew what it was before he unfolded it. He knew. He unfolded it anyway. In the dim grey-gold light that came from nowhere, in a room that was getting thinner every second because only one person was holding it now.

A drawing.

In pencil. Small. Careful.

Not a cat.

A girl. Seven. Eight. In a grey dress too big for her, with the sleeves rolled up. An apron. A wooden spoon in her hand. Standing at the side of a road.

Her eyes were drawn very large. Very dark. Looking out of the paper.

And next to her, smaller, a boy. In a coat with brass buttons. Holding out his hand.

No words.

---

He looked back down the road.

East. Toward the Veil. Toward the kept. Toward the rows of gold on both sides of the white stone, running back and back into the grey-gold dim, mile after mile, all the way to the door at the edge of the world.

Toward a kitchen girl with a wooden spoon, at the side of the road, near the beginning. Whose eyes hadn't moved slowly like the others.

Whose eyes had moved *fast*.

Darting. From Pim's face to the basket. Back and forth. The way a child's eyes move when she's seen something she wants very badly.

*He looked back*, Antho thought. *Twice*.

---

"*Lex*," he said.

His brother woke the way he always woke now. All at once. Bolt upright. Hand on the bell.

He saw Antho's face.

He saw the paper.

He saw the empty place in the dust where the boy had been sitting.

"*Where*—"

"*Back*," said Antho. His voice was very calm. He didn't feel calm. He felt the room round them thinning like ice on a pond in spring, and he felt the Court pressing on the edge of it, and he felt a boy of fourteen walking alone down a white road between nine thousand gold people with no room round him at all.

"*He's gone back*," said Antho Vey. "*For the girl.*"
