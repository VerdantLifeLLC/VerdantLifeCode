# The Last Pages

He told her one a day.

That was the rule. His rule. He'd made it the morning after Seraphine did her sums, lying on his pillows under the boarded window with the Lantern burning beside him and his voice a thread.

*One a day*, he'd said. *Like Antho with the stone. Like I did at the Keep, last summer, before the moths. You don't pour it all in at once. It runs out the other side.*

*Ember could*—

*Ember's in Lex*, he'd said. *And Lex is beyond the edge of the world. In a country where a day is ten.* He'd looked at her. *I'll do it myself. I'm the one who was there.*

So he did.

Every morning. When she'd got him propped up and fed him what he'd eat, which wasn't much, and wiped his face, and sat down on the edge of the bed with her hand in his.

One.

---

The first was mulled cider.

She'd heard it before. At the Keep. He'd told her last summer. She didn't mind. She wanted to hear it again. She wanted to hear all of them again. She wanted to hear them until she knew them the way you know the shape of your own hands.

*A stall in the market*, he said. *Below the Spire. The day after the steps. You made me buy you a cup of something hot because it was raining and you said Wardens owed the city a cup of something hot for all the Kindlings they'd ruined.*

*Did I*.

*You did*. *It was mulled cider. I'd never had it. I'd been alive for a hundred and ninety-nine years and I'd never had mulled cider.* His mouth twitched. *I drank it in one go because I didn't know you were meant to sip it. And then I walked into a pillar.*

*You didn't*.

*I did*. *The big one by the fountain. With the Founder's face on it.* He closed his eyes. *You laughed so hard you had to sit down on the fountain's edge. In the rain. And I stood there with my forehead bleeding and thought, I'm going to marry this woman if it kills me.*

She laughed.

She couldn't help it. She sat on the edge of the bed and laughed with her hand over her mouth and tears in her eyes, the way she must have laughed thirty-two years ago, on the edge of a fountain in the rain.

"*It nearly did*," said Arion. "*Kill me. Your mother had a very good arm.*"

---

The second was the walk to the Vale.

The third was the cottage. The first night. Wenna standing in the doorway in her apron with her arms folded, looking at him. *Who's this*.

*She put me in the barn*, said Arion. *For a month. I slept in the straw with the goat.*

*We had a goat*?

*You always had a goat*, said Arion. *Harrow women always have a goat. It's in the rule somewhere. In the part that doesn't need saying.*

---

He got tired by the end of each one.

She watched it happen. Every morning. He'd start with his eyes bright and his voice stronger than it had any right to be, and by the end the words would be coming slower, and further apart, and his hand in hers would go slack, and he'd stop. In the middle, sometimes. In the middle of a sentence.

And sleep.

She'd sit with him. Holding his hand. Until he woke. And then he'd open his eyes and look at her and say, *where was I*.

And she'd tell him.

And he'd finish it.

---

The days went.

Long ones. High summer. Hot. The birch wood dark and full and dusty and the stream running low. She kept the boards on the windows. She kept them on even though it was hot, even though the cottage was dim all day and stifling at night, even though Wren said the glass lady hadn't been seen since midsummer.

*She's gone home*, said Wren. *With your blood. Seraphine thinks she's not coming back.*

*Seraphine thinks*, said Seren.

She kept the boards on.

---

The news came every day.

Ravens. Riders. Keepers on foot. Farmers fleeing east on carts with their children on top. Elowen, every few days, on her grey warhorse, with dust to her knees, standing in the clearing not getting down, telling them.

Where he was. What he'd done. How many.

He walked five miles a day. Less. He stopped at every village. He sat on the edge of every well. He listened to everyone who came.

And kept the ones who asked.

---

"*He went round the Blightmarch*," said Elowen.

It was the thirty-first day. High summer. The worst of the heat. She'd come up the track at noon with her face burned red and her lips cracked, and Seren had brought her water, and she'd drunk it standing by her horse in the gold-patched clearing.

"*Round it*," she said again. "*Not across. Round. The long way.*" She wiped her mouth. "*Forty miles out of his way. South. Through the marsh country.*"

Seren looked at her.

"*Why*?"

"*Because the gold won't take*," said Elowen Dunmore. "*On the green. On Mal's ground.*"

---

Seren sat down.

On the oak ramp. In her bare feet. With the jug of water in her lap.

"*Seraphine went to look*," said Elowen. "*She flew down from the Keep on the dragon. To the edge of the circle. Where his road came up to it from the west.*" She looked at the birch wood. "*She said his road comes up to the edge of the green. A furlong wide. Gold. Every blade of grass. Every stone.*"

"*And*?"

"*And it stops*," said Elowen. "*At the edge. Right at the edge. Where the old trench was. Where they dug the word.*" She shook her head. "*She said it's like a tide line. Gold on one side. Green on the other. Sharp as a knife. Not a speck of gold on the green. Not one.*"

"*He tried to walk on it*," said Seren. Slowly.

"*He put his foot on it*," said Elowen. "*One foot. Seraphine found the print. Right at the edge. In the green. Just inside the line.*" A pause. "*It's not gold. It's just a footprint. In grass. Pressed down.*" She looked at Seren. "*And then he took his foot back. And turned south. And walked forty miles round.*"

---

Seren didn't say anything for a long time.

She sat on the ramp with the jug of water in her lap and thought about a pool in a green field. Full of room. With three small gold lights in the bottom of it.

*It makes room*, she thought. *It's the room. It's the Well of new things. The Well of empty on purpose.*

*And he can't fill it*.

*He drank it once*, she thought. *Nine hundred years ago. He drank it dry. And it starved, and it screamed, and it became the thing under the lid.*

*And now it's home. And green. And full of room. And he puts his foot on it and the gold won't take.*

"*He's afraid of it*," she said.

Elowen looked at her.

"*He's afraid of it*," said Seren again. "*The little one. Mal.*" She looked up. "*It's the only thing in the world he can't keep.*"

---

She told Arion that evening.

He listened with his eyes half shut. On his pillows. With the Lantern burning. When she'd finished, he didn't say anything for a long time.

"*Room*," he said at last.

"*Room*."

"*Aurelian's note*," said Arion. "*Seraphine read it to me. Unwrite it when there is someone on this side who can make room for what is on the other.*" He breathed. In. Catch. Out. "*He's on this side now. Oszric. In the world. And the one thing in the world that can make room is the one thing he walks forty miles to avoid.*"

"*Is that good*?"

"*I don't know*," said her husband. "*I think it might be. I think it might be the most important thing anyone's told me since I woke up.*" He turned his head on the pillow. "*Write it down.*"

"*In the grey book*?"

"*Lex has the grey book*." A faint smile. "*Write it on the wall. Write it on the boards over the window. Write it somewhere, love. Somebody's going to need it.*"

---

She wrote it on the boards.

That night. By the light of the Lantern. With a stick of charcoal from the hearth. In her square careful Harrow hand, on the oak planks nailed over the west window.

*The gold won't take on Mal's green.*

*He walked round*.

She stood back and looked at it.

It looked very small. Two lines of charcoal on a board.

She left it there.

---

He wrote the letters in the afternoons.

When he could. When his hand would hold the pencil. She'd prop the box on the blanket beside him, the oak one Brannoc had made, with the lid that sighed, and put a board across his knees for a desk, and a sheet of paper on the board, and the pencil in his hand.

And he'd write.

Slowly. So slowly. A line. A rest. Another line. His hand shaking so badly that sometimes the letters went off the edge of the page and he had to start again.

She didn't read them. She never asked.

But she counted them. She couldn't help it.

Twelve. *For Lark. On her twelfth birthday*.

Thirteen.

Fourteen.

---

"*What do you tell a fourteen-year-old*?" she said once.

He was lying with his eyes shut. The pencil in his slack fingers. The letter half done on the board across his knees.

"*That everyone she knows is an idiot*," said Arion. Not opening his eyes. "*Including her. Especially her.*"

Seren laughed.

"*That's what I needed*," said her husband. "*At fourteen. Someone to tell me I was an idiot. Nobody did. I was the heir. I was a Vey. I had the Root waiting for me.*" His mouth twitched. "*I'd have been a much better man if someone had thrown a pan at me at fourteen.*"

"*I'll throw one at her*."

"*Please do*," said Arion Vey. "*But miss. Your mother always missed.*"

---

Fifteen. Sixteen.

He wrote sixteen in one afternoon. All of it. Without stopping. His hand steadier than it had been in weeks. When he'd finished he lay back on the pillows grey-faced and breathing hard, and she took the pencil out of his fingers, and folded the letter, and sealed it with grey wax, and wrote on the outside, in her own hand, because his wouldn't do it anymore:

*For Lark. On her sixteenth birthday*.

"*That's the one*," said Arion.

She looked at him.

"*Sixteen*," said her husband. Very quietly. "*Her Kindling. If she still has one. If the world's still the world.*" He looked at the boards over the window. "*Antho was sixteen. When it broke. When everything broke.*" He breathed. "*I wasn't there. I'd sealed him and gone. I was in a crystal in Avaloria, holding the breaches.*" A pause. "*I never got to tell him anything. At sixteen. Before.*"

Seren held his hand.

"*So I told her*," said Arion. "*Everything I'd have told him.*"

---

Seventeen.

Eighteen.

---

Wren left on the fifty-third day.

It wasn't a surprise. They'd been talking about it for weeks. In the blue-door cottage, at night, with the Lantern burning. Wren and Seren and Arion. Brannoc, sometimes. Kestrel.

*When he's a week off*, Arion had said. The first time. Lying on his pillows. *When he's a week off, you take her and you go.*

*I'm not leaving you*.

*You're not leaving me*, said Arion. *You're taking her. That's different.*

*Lex said*—

*Lex said fog the glass*, said Arion. *And you have. Every night. For two months.*

*But the glass lady's gone*—

*The glass lady's gone*, said Arion. *And the King's coming. And you can't fog a king.*

---

He was eight days off.

That was what Elowen said. On the fifty-second day. In the clearing. With the dust to her knees.

*Ashbourne*, she said. *He's at the edge of the Vale. At the top of the downs. Looking down.* She was very pale. *He's stopped. He's sitting on the hilltop. On a stone. Looking down at the Vale. He's been there two days.*

*What's he doing*?

*Looking*, said Elowen. *Just looking. Like a man looking at a view. Like a man who's walked a long way and come over the top of a hill and seen the place he's going.*

---

Wren packed in the night.

Seren helped her. In the other cottage. Across the stream. By the light of a candle in a holder with no glass. The baby asleep in her cradle under the soaped window.

Not much. Wren was a tracker. Trackers didn't carry much. A blanket. A spare shirt. The baby's things, which were more than everything else put together. Her bow. Her knives. A little food.

A box.

Oak. Plain. With a lid that sighed.

Seren had carried it across the stream herself. That evening. From under Arion's bed. She'd set it on the table in Wren's kitchen and Wren had looked at it and gone very still.

*He said give it to you*, said Seren. *When the time came. He said you'd keep it safe. He said you're very good at keeping things.*

Wren had put her hand on the lid.

*How many*?

*Eighteen*, said Seren. *He's working on nineteen.*

---

They walked across in the morning.

Wren and the baby. Kestrel behind them, with her pack and her red hair and her amber eyes very bright. Brannoc, from the village, who'd come up at dawn and stood at the edge of the clearing with his arms folded and his gold fingertips catching the first light and said nothing at all.

Across the stepping stones. Up the oak ramp. Through the blue door.

Arion was awake.

---

He was propped up. Seren had done it before dawn. Three pillows. His white hair combed. A clean shirt. His spectacles on, folded face down on the blanket beside his hand, the way he always left them now.

The Lantern burned on the table. Three panes of glass and one of horn.

He looked at them come in.

At Wren. At the sling on her chest. At the baby in it, awake, looking round with her grey-green eyes, frowning. At the box under Wren's arm.

"*Good morning*," said Arion Vey.

---

Wren didn't say anything.

She came across the room and knelt down by the bed. With the baby on her chest. And looked at him.

"*She wants to say goodbye*," said Wren. Very steady. "*Don't you, love.*"

Lark looked at her grandfather.

At the old man on the pillows. At his walnut face. At the white wisp of his hair. At his grey eyes.

And reached out.

One square fist. Out of the sling. Toward his face.

He bent his head. Slowly. So slowly. As far as he could. Toward her.

And she grabbed his nose.

---

He laughed.

The real laugh. Startled out of him. It hurt. Seren saw it hurt. It caught in his chest halfway and turned into a cough and he went on laughing through it with his granddaughter hanging on to his nose by one square fist and refusing, absolutely refusing, to let go.

"*Ah*," he said. Through the cough. Through the laugh. "*Ah. There you are.*"

Brannoc, in the doorway, made a sound.

---

"*She's got good taste*," said Wren. Her voice was shaking now. "*She always goes for the nose.*"

"*She gets that from you*," said Arion.

"*She gets that from Lex*."

"*She gets it from both of you*," said her grandfather. Very quietly. With her fist still on his nose. "*She gets everything from both of you. That's the point.*" He looked at the baby. At her frown. At her fist. "*She can be anything. That's what you made her. Both of you.*"

Lark let go.

She did it all at once, the way she always did. Done. Finished. Her hand coming off his face and back into the sling and her face screwing up, red and fierce, because she didn't like being finished.

She liked the doing.

---

"*Wren*," said Arion.

"*I know*."

"*Keep moving*," said Arion Vey. "*That's all. Don't stop. Don't stay anywhere long. Don't let him learn your face.*" His grey eyes were very steady. "*You know how. You're a tracker. You've been running on roofs since you were fourteen.*"

"*I'm not a Harrow*."

"*No*," agreed Arion. "*But she is.*" He looked at the baby. "*Twenty generations of women who never stayed. It's in her. In her blood. The Veil never learned it.*" A faint smile. "*She'll keep you moving, Wren. You'll see. She'll know when it's time to go before you do.*"

---

Wren held the box out.

"*I'll keep it safe*," she said. "*I promise. Every one. On every birthday.*"

"*Thank you*."

"*Nineteen*," said Wren. "*You're on nineteen.*"

"*I'll send it after you*," said Arion. "*With a raven. And twenty. And twenty-one.*" His mouth twitched. "*If my hand holds. If the ravens can find you. Which they won't, if you're doing it properly.*"

"*Then how*—"

"*Then Seren will keep them*," said her father-in-law. "*Till you come home.*" He looked at her. "*You will come home. Afterward. When it's over.*"

"*I'll try*," said Wren.

Arion looked at her for a long moment.

"*Good*," he said. "*That's all anyone's got.*"

---

She kissed him.

On the forehead. Quickly. Fiercely. Like a seal stamped. The way Seren did.

Then she stood up. With the baby on her chest and the box under her arm and her bow on her back and her left hand, gold from the knuckles to the nails, flat on the baby's back.

And turned. And went to the door.

And stopped.

---

"*Arion*," she said. Not turning round.

"*Mm*."

"*Lex is going to come back*," said Wren Ashdown. "*Through the door at the edge of the world. With a crown. Thinking it's the end of summer.*" Her voice cracked. "*And I won't be here. And Lark won't be here.*"

"*No*."

"*Tell him*," said Wren.

"*Tell him what*?"

She stood in the doorway with her back to the room for a long time.

"*Tell him I ran*," she said at last. "*Tell him I ran the whole way. Tell him she laughed.*"

And went out.

---

Seren watched them go from the ramp.

Across the clearing. Past the gold birch. Past the gold woodpile. Past the cart. Wren with the baby on her chest. Kestrel beside her. Into the birch wood. Up the hill. North. Toward the Greyspine.

Not on the wind. Walking. A tracker's walk. Fast and light and steady.

At the top of the hill, at the edge of the trees, Wren stopped.

And turned round.

And Seren saw her lift one hand. The left. Gold from the knuckles. And hold it up. Not waving. Just holding it up. In the morning light. So it shone.

And then she turned. And was gone into the trees.

---

Brannoc stood beside her on the ramp.

He didn't say anything for a long time.

"*She didn't look back*," he said at last.

"*She did*," said Seren. "*Once. At the top.*"

"*Harrow women never look back*."

"*She's not a Harrow*," said Seren. "*She's an Ashdown.*" She swallowed. "*Ashdowns look back. Once. At the top of the hill. So they remember the way home.*"

---

Nineteen.

He wrote it that afternoon. And twenty, the next. His hand worse every day. The letters going off the page. Starting again. Going off again.

She didn't help. He didn't ask.

She sat on the edge of the bed and watched him write. And folded each one, when he'd finished, and sealed it with grey wax, and wrote on the outside in her own square hand.

*For Lark. On her nineteenth birthday*.

*For Lark. On her twentieth birthday*.

And put them in a drawer. In the table. By the bed. With the gold rose in its gold jug on the shelf above, behind the chipped blue cup.

---

She told him one a day.

No. He told her. One a day. She kept forgetting which way round it went. Some days she felt she was the one doing the telling. Holding his hand. Saying, *and then what*. Saying, *where was I*. Saying, *the pillar, love, you were at the pillar*.

The wedding was the forty-fourth day.

She'd been waiting for it. She'd known it was coming. In the order he was telling them. After the barn and the goat and the pan and the lamp in the window.

*The snow*, he said. *It was snowing. The chapel at Ashbourne. Sister Agathe's predecessor. Old Brother Wyn. He was ninety-three and deaf in one ear.*

*Who came*?

*Nobody*, said Arion. *Your mother had died that winter. Before the spring. And I didn't have anyone. I had the Spire. I had Wardens. I couldn't tell any of them.* A faint smile. *So it was you and me and Brother Wyn and Brannoc.*

Seren went still.

*Brannoc*?

*He was the smith*, said Arion. *He was thirty. He'd shod the horse that brought me down from Highcrest. I asked him to stand witness because there was nobody else.* His eyes were closed. *He cried. All the way through. He cried so loudly Brother Wyn had to stop twice.*

---

She went down to the village that evening.

She didn't decide to. She found herself on the Ashbourne road in the dusk with her bare feet in the dust, walking. Three miles. To the Forge. To the long hall with the twenty anvils and the great chimneys at each end and Quill on his nest of spoons.

Brannoc was at the anvil.

Alone. The students gone home. Gone east, most of them, with their families, ahead of the King. He was working by the light of the forge. With a bar of white-hot iron in his bare gold-tipped fingers. Turning it. Looking at it.

He looked up as she came in.

"*You cried*," said Seren. "*At my wedding.*"

He put the bar down.

"*He told you*."

"*He told me*." She came across the hall. Stopped on the other side of the anvil. "*He said you cried so loudly the priest had to stop twice.*"

Brannoc looked at her for a long moment. In the forge-light. With his bald head and his soot-stained beard and his gold fingertips.

"*Three times*," he said. "*He's being kind.*"

---

She cried then.

In the Ashbourne Forge. Across the anvil from the smith who'd stood witness at a wedding she didn't remember, in a chapel in the snow, thirty-one years ago. She cried with her hands over her face and her shoulders shaking.

He came round the anvil and put his enormous arms round her and held her.

"*I don't remember*," she said. Into his shirt. "*I don't remember it. You were there. You cried. And I don't remember.*"

"*I know*," said Brannoc Holt.

"*He's telling me*. *One a day. And I'm so glad. And it's not mine.*" She was shaking. "*It's his. It's all his. He's giving it to me and I'm holding it and it's not mine.*"

Brannoc didn't say anything for a long time.

"*You wore a blue dress*," he said at last.

She went still.

"*Blue*," said Brannoc. "*Very blue. Bluer than anything. Your mam had made it. Before she died. She'd finished it that winter. She'd been sewing it all autumn.*" His voice was very rough. "*You walked down the aisle of that chapel in the snow in a blue dress your dead mam had made you. And you were so happy. And so sad. And you laughed at Brother Wyn when he got your name wrong.*"

Seren couldn't breathe.

"*That's mine*," said Brannoc Holt. "*Not his. Mine. I was there. I saw it.*" He held her. "*You can have it. It's yours.*"

---

She walked home in the dark.

Three miles. Along the river path. Barefoot. Under the stars.

At the place where the path came out of the alders and you could see the Vale behind you, she stopped.

And turned. And looked back.

Ashbourne. Dark now. Mostly. Empty, mostly. A few windows lit.

And beyond it. Over the top of the downs. At the head of the Vale.

A glow.

---

Gold. Low. On the hilltop. Where the road came down from the shires into the Vale.

Not a fire. Not a lamp. Not the moon; the moon was behind her.

Like metal in a crucible. Just before it pours.

He was sitting up there. On his stone. Looking down. She could feel it. From three miles off. The way you feel the heat of a forge from across a yard.

Looking at the Vale.

At Ashbourne. At the river. At the birch wood at the far end of it, and the clearing, and the two cottages.

At her.

---

She stood on the river path and looked back at him.

She didn't look away. She was a Harrow woman. Harrow women didn't look back.

She looked back anyway. Straight at the glow. Straight at the hilltop. For a long time.

*Gold for the eldest*, she thought. *Who feared to grow old*.

*I'm here*, she thought. At him. Across three miles of dark. *I'm here. In a cottage at the end of the Vale. With a blue door. With my husband in a bed under a boarded window. Dying.*

*Come for tea*.

*I'll say no*.

---

She went home.

Over the stepping stones. Up the ramp. Through the blue door.

He was awake.

He was always awake now, when she came in. Lying on his pillows with his eyes open, looking at the boards over the window. At the two lines of charcoal on them in her square hand.

*The gold won't take on Mal's green*.

*He walked round*.

"*Where were you*?" he said.

"*At the Forge*." She sat down on the edge of the bed. Took his hand. "*Brannoc told me about the dress.*"

His face changed.

"*The blue one*," he said.

"*The blue one*."

"*I'd forgotten*," said Arion. Very quietly. "*I'd forgotten the dress.*" He closed his eyes. "*That's the trouble with being old. You think you've got all of it. And then someone tells you something and you realize you only ever had your side.*"

"*Brannoc had his*."

"*Brannoc had his*," said Arion Vey. "*Thank God for Brannoc.*"

---

Twenty-one.

He wrote it on the sixty-first day.

It took him all afternoon. A line. A rest. A line. She sat beside him and watched his hand move across the page. So slowly. Shaking. The letters going off the edge. Him stopping. Breathing. Starting again.

He didn't let her see it.

He turned the board a little, on his knees, so the page was facing away from her. She didn't mind. It wasn't hers.

At the end, he stopped.

He held the pencil over the page for a long time. She watched him. His hand shaking. The point of the pencil an inch above the paper.

Then he wrote one more word.

And put the pencil down.

---

"*Done*," said Arion.

She took the board off his knees. Folded the letter without reading it. Sealed it with grey wax. Wrote on the outside, in her square careful hand:

*For Lark. On her twenty-first birthday*.

And put it in the drawer. With the others. Nineteen. Twenty. Twenty-one.

She closed the drawer.

"*Done*," said Arion again. Very quietly. To the boards over the window. "*All of them.*"

"*All of them*."

"*That was the last thing*," said her husband. "*Not the Knot. Not Elric. This.*" He breathed. In. Catch. Out. "*I didn't know it was the last thing until I wrote the last word.*"

"*What was it*?" said Seren. "*The last word.*"

He looked at her.

"*It's hers*," he said. "*Not mine. Not yours.*" A faint smile. "*You'll have to wait till she's twenty-one and ask her.*"

---

She lay down beside him.

On top of the blankets. With her bare feet cold and her head on the edge of his pillow and her hand on his chest. Over his heart.

In. Catch. Out.

In.

The Lantern burned on the table. Three clear squares on the walls and one soft blur.

"*Seren*," said Arion.

"*Mm*."

"*He's on the hill*."

"*I know*. *I saw him.*"

"*He's coming down tomorrow*," said Arion. "*Or the next day. Elowen says.*" A pause. "*He's coming here.*"

"*I know*."

"*For tea*."

"*I know*," said Seren Harrow. "*I bought more. From Hollis Penn. The good kind. In a tin.*"

He laughed.

Very quietly. In the dark. A short cracked sound that caught in his chest and turned into a cough and turned back into a laugh.

"*The good kind*," he said.

"*He's a king*," said Seren. "*He'll expect it.*"

---

He slept.

She didn't.

She lay beside him in the dark with her hand on his chest and counted. In. Catch. Out. And listened. To the stream. To the birch wood. To an owl.

To something else.

Very far off. At the very edge of hearing. At the head of the Vale, on a hilltop, three miles and more away.

Footsteps.

Heavy. Slow. Unhurried.

*Thud*.

Getting up off a stone.

*Thud*.

Starting to walk.

*Thud*.

Down the hill.

---

She didn't wake him.

She lay very still with her hand on his heart and listened to the Gilded King come down into the Vale.

*Thud*. *Thud*. *Thud*.

Not hurrying.

Not late.
