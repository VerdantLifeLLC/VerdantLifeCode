# The Rose

The rose hadn't wilted.

Seren checked it every morning. She couldn't help it. She came out from behind the curtain at dawn, barefoot, with her hair down and her shawl round her shoulders, and went to the table under the west window where the jug stood. And looked.

It was the same. Dark red. Velvet. Every petal where it had been the day Lex brought it across the stream. Not a curl at the edge. Not a brown spot. Not one fallen leaf.

Seven weeks.

"*It's watching you*," said Arion. From the bed. Behind the curtain. He was always awake before her now. He said he didn't sleep much anymore. He said there wasn't time.

"*It's a rose*."

"*It's watching you*," said her husband. "*I've been lying here for an hour watching it watch you. It turns. Very slightly. When you come out.*" A pause. "*Toward you. Like a sunflower.*"

Seren looked at the rose.

It was facing her.

She was almost sure it had been facing the window when she went to bed.

---

She'd started talking to it.

She didn't tell anyone. Not Lex. Not Wren. Arion knew, because Arion knew everything that happened in the room, lying behind his curtain with his grey eyes half shut. But he didn't say anything. He just listened.

She talked to it the way Ivy talked to the pool. The way her mother used to talk to the fire, at the end of summer, in twenty-two cottages. Not expecting an answer. Just talking. Because something was listening, and it seemed rude not to.

*Good morning*, she'd say. *It's raining.*

Or, *Lark has a tooth coming. She bit Wren.*

Or, *He's tired today. He couldn't finish his letter. It's the ninth one. Her ninth birthday. He says he can't think what to tell a nine-year-old because he can't remember being nine.*

The rose didn't answer.

But it turned. Very slightly. Toward her voice.

---

"*It's her*," said Arion. That evening. From the chair. She'd got him up for an hour, the way Ilsa said. He was sitting at the west window with a blanket over his knees and his spectacles on and a letter in his lap that he wasn't writing. Looking at the rose in its jug on the table beside him.

"*Rosie*."

"*It must be*." He took his spectacles off. Folded them. Put them face down on the table. He always put them face down now. "*Ysenne looks out of glass. Why shouldn't Rhosyn look out of roses?*"

"*Then why doesn't she say anything*?"

His mouth twitched.

"*Perhaps she's shy*," said Arion Vey. "*Perhaps she's been asleep in a cocoon for nine hundred years and has forgotten how to start a conversation.*" He looked at the rose. "*I'd sympathise. I was a hundred and eighty-eight before anyone taught me.*"

---

Lark came over in the afternoon.

Wren brought her. Across the stepping stones with the baby on her hip and a basket on her arm and a look on her face Seren had started to see more often lately. Lighter. Looser. Like a girl who'd remembered something she used to be good at.

*She ran*, Lex had told her. *Over the roofs. With the baby. In the gold.* And then, quieter. *She's been sleeping, Mam. Right through. For the first time since Lantern Night.*

Wren put the baby on the floor on a sheepskin by the hearth and sat down at the table and ate an apple and talked about nothing. About the cottage. About the soap on the windows. About Correspondence, who'd got into the lean-to that morning and eaten half a bridle.

Lark lay on her back on the sheepskin and kicked.

And then she rolled over.

---

Seren saw it happen.

One moment the baby was on her back. Kicking. Glaring at the ceiling. The next she'd flung one square fist across her body, and her knee after it, and heaved, with a grunt like a smith lifting an anvil—

And was on her front.

Her face in the sheepskin. Her bottom in the air. Her fists on either side of her head, flat on the fleece.

She lifted her head. Wobbling. Furious. Triumphant.

And looked straight across the room at the table under the west window. At the jug. At the rose.

"*Aah*," said Lark.

---

"*She rolled*," said Wren. Very quietly. The apple halfway to her mouth. "*She's never rolled. She's been trying for a week.*"

"*She rolled to look at it*," said Arion.

They both looked at him.

He was sitting in the chair by the window with his blanket and his letter and his spectacles face down on the table. Watching his granddaughter. Watching her watch the rose.

"*She's been trying to see it*," he said. "*All week. Every time Wren brings her over. She lies on her back and she can't see the table. So she tries to turn over. And she can't.*" A faint smile. "*And today she did.*"

Lark, on her front on the sheepskin, reached out one square fist. Toward the table. Toward the jug. Opening. Closing.

"*She can't have it*," said Wren. "*It's got thorns.*"

"*She knows*," said Arion. "*She's not reaching for it. Look.*" He leaned forward. Stiffly. "*She's just reaching.*"

---

Seren knelt down on the sheepskin beside her granddaughter.

She'd never done this before. Not with Lark. She'd held her. She'd fed her porridge, once, which had gone everywhere. She'd sung her the Lantern Song a dozen times.

She'd never knelt beside her and told her anything.

"*That's a rose*," said Seren. Very quietly. Into the baby's ear. Into the dark fluff of her hair. "*A long way away, there's a lady who grows them. She grew that one for her sister.*"

Lark reached for it.

"*Her sister's dead*," said Seren. "*A long time ago. Nine hundred years. She was your grandmother's grandmother's grandmother. Twenty times over.*" She swallowed. "*Her name was Hesper. She said no. When everybody else said yes. She said no and she put down her crown and she walked away.*"

The baby frowned at the rose.

"*And she taught her daughter a rule*," said Seren Harrow. "*And her daughter taught hers. All the way down. To me.*" She put her hand on Lark's back. Small. Warm. Rising and falling. "*And now I'm going to teach you.*"

---

She'd never said it out loud. Not to anyone. Not even to her boys. Her mother had made her promise. *Never. Not to anyone. Not even to your own babies.*

Her mother was dead. Her mother had paid a whistler to send a crown across a river so that her daughter could stay. So that her daughter could put down roots, and Kindle her sons, and let the net learn their faces.

The rule was meant for women who ran.

Seren had stopped running.

"*We break the ground*," she said. Into her granddaughter's hair. "*We never take the crown. We never let a moth in.*"

---

Lark didn't understand. Of course she didn't. She was five months old. She had vowels and a grip like a vice and she'd just rolled over for the first time in her life.

But she turned her head. Wobbling. Away from the rose. And looked up at Seren.

With her grey-green eyes. Not quite focused. Frowning.

As if she were trying very hard to remember it.

---

"*You've never told the boys that*," said Arion. Later. When Wren had gone home with the baby. When it was dusk, and the fire was low, and Seren was sitting on the floor by his chair with her back against it and her head against his knee.

"*No*."

"*Why her*?"

Seren didn't answer for a long time.

"*Because she's a girl*," she said at last. "*It was always mother to daughter. Mam never had a son. Her mam never had a son. Not once. Twenty generations. All girls.*" She looked at the fire. "*And then I had two boys. And I didn't know what to do. I didn't know if it was meant for boys. So I didn't tell them. And then I went into the dark.*"

"*And now there's Lark*."

"*And now there's Lark*," said Seren. "*The first Harrow girl since me.*" She tipped her head back against his knee. "*She ought to have it. Even if she can't remember. Even if she's too little. She ought to have heard it once. From someone who knew what it meant.*"

Her husband's hand came down. Slowly. Onto her hair. And stayed there.

"*She'll remember*," said Arion.

"*She's five months old*."

"*She'll remember*," said Arion Vey. "*Not the words. The voice. The way you said it.*" His thumb moved in her hair. "*That's how rules go down, love. Not in words. In voices.*"

---

The rose spoke at midnight.

---

She was awake. She was always awake at midnight. She was lying on top of the blankets beside him behind the curtain, with her hand on his chest and her bare feet cold, counting his breaths.

In. Out. In. A little catch. Out.

In.

And from the table under the west window, very faint, very soft, like the rustle of leaves in a garden at night when there's no wind—

"*...Hes?*"

---

Seren didn't move.

She lay very still. On top of the blankets. Her hand on Arion's chest.

He'd stopped breathing.

No. He hadn't. He was holding it. She felt him hold it. Under her palm. The way you hold your breath in the dark when you've heard a step on the stair.

"*...Hes?*" said the voice again.

Thin. Papery. Green. If a voice could be green. Like a stem bending. Like a leaf unfolding very slowly. Like a girl who hasn't spoken to anyone in a very long time, and isn't sure she still knows how.

"*...is someone there?*"

---

Seren sat up.

Slowly. Carefully. Arion's hand found her wrist in the dark and held it. Not stopping her. Just there.

She slid off the bed. Pushed back the curtain. Stood up in the dark room in her nightdress with her bare feet on the cold flags.

The fire was down to embers. Red. Faint. Enough to see by.

The rose was on the table. In its jug. Under the window.

It had turned all the way round. Facing the curtain. Facing her.

And it was moving.

Very slightly. The petals. Lifting. Falling. Lifting. Like breath. Like something breathing.

---

"*No*," said Seren. Very quietly. "*Not Hes.*"

The rose went still.

"*I'm sorry*," said Seren. "*She's gone. A long time ago.*" She swallowed. "*I'm one of hers. Her girl. Twenty times down.*"

Silence.

Then the petals moved. Lifted. Fell.

"*...you sound like her*," said Rhosyn.

---

Seren sat down at the table.

She didn't light a candle. She didn't know why. It seemed wrong, somehow. To bring a light. She sat down in the dark, in her chair under the west window, across the table from the rose in its jug, and put her hands flat on the wood.

Behind her, she heard the curtain move. Heard the bed creak. Heard Arion sit up. Slowly. With a small sharp breath when his hip caught.

He didn't come out. He couldn't, without help. He sat on the edge of the bed behind the curtain in the dark and listened.

"*My name's Seren*," said Seren. "*Seren Harrow. Seren Vey, now. I married.*"

"*...Harrow...*"

"*That's what she called herself*. *Hesper. After. When she walked away from the shingle. To harrow. To break the ground before sowing.*"

"*...Harrow...*" The petals trembled. "*...she named herself...*"

"*She named herself*."

"*...she never named anything...*" The voice was barely there. "*...when we were girls...she wouldn't name her pony...she said it had a name already and nobody had asked it...*"

Seren laughed.

She didn't mean to. It came out of her in the dark. Small. Startled.

"*That sounds like her*," she said. "*That sounds like every Harrow woman who ever lived.*"

---

"*...is it you*?" said the rose. "*...who gave Mae the lamp...*"

Seren went still.

"*...Orrin told us*," whispered Rhosyn. "*...a Harrow woman knelt down in front of her and gave her a lamp...and she took it...and she was warm...*" The petals were shaking. "*...was it you...*"

"*Yes*," said Seren.

The rose didn't speak for a long time.

"*...thank you*," it said.

---

Seren hadn't expected that.

She sat in the dark with her hands flat on the table and felt something come up in her throat she didn't have a name for.

"*She tried to take my sons*," she said. "*She tried to take my granddaughter. She dimmed a whole city. She drank a thousand lights. She*—"

"*...I know*."

"*Then why*—"

"*...because she was cold*," said Rhosyn. "*...her whole life...right in the middle...she never knew why...*" The voice was very small. "*...she brushed Hes's hair when the nurses were too busy...she sat up all night with her when she had the fever...she told her a story about a girl who married a bear...*" The petals trembled. "*...she was the kindest of us...before...and the coldest...and nobody ever gave her anything...not once...not in nine hundred years...*"

Seren didn't say anything.

"*...and you did*," said the rose. "*...at the end...*" A long silence. "*...so thank you...*"

---

"*Did she have a good life*?" said Rhosyn.

Seren looked at the rose.

"*Hes*," said the voice. "*...after...in your world...did she...was she...*"

It couldn't finish.

Seren sat in the dark and thought about it.

She didn't know. Not really. Nobody did. Hesper had been dead for nearly nine hundred years, and the Harrow women didn't write things down, and the songs only told you the rule and where it went wrong.

But she knew some things. The things you know about the women in your line, the way you know the shape of your own hands.

"*She walked east*," said Seren. "*From the shingle. With the crown in a bundle on her back and a pair of boots she stole from the kitchen.*"

"*...the kitchen boots...*" A sound. Almost a laugh. "*...Cook's...Cook was so angry...*"

"*She found a valley*," said Seren. "*In the hills. Where nobody had ever heard of the Crowned. She built a cottage with her own hands. She never Kindled. She never let the net look at her.*" She swallowed. "*She had a daughter. I don't know who the father was. Harrow women never say.*"

"*...a daughter...*"

"*And she taught her the rule*. *We break the ground. We never take the crown. We never let a moth in.*" Seren looked at the rose. "*And when the daughter was grown, Hesper packed up and moved on. To another valley. Another cottage. And another, after that. All her life.*"

"*...she never stayed...*"

"*She never stayed*," said Seren. "*Thirty houses, Mam said. Maybe more. She never stayed anywhere long enough for anyone to learn her face.*" She was quiet. "*And she never left a light on. Not in any window. Not once. Not in her whole life.*"

---

The rose didn't speak.

Seren waited. In the dark. With her hands on the table.

"*...was she happy*?" said Rhosyn at last. So faint it was barely a rustle.

Seren closed her eyes.

*Was she happy*.

She thought about her mother. At the end of summer. Packing. Twenty-two cottages. Thirty years. Singing the old verses with the fire low and the cottage half bare round them. Throwing a frying pan at a Warden-Architect's head. Walking three hundred miles west with her feet bleeding through her boots to pay a whistler to a river for a crown.

Whistling all the way across.

"*Mam used to say*," said Seren slowly, "*that Harrow women are happy the way hedges are.*"

The rose waited.

"*Not showy*," said Seren. "*Not like roses. Not like gardens. Nobody plants a hedge to look at it.*" She opened her eyes. "*But they're there. Every season. In the snow and the rain and the wind. Full of birds. Full of nests. Keeping the field.*" She looked at the rose. "*Mam said that's the best kind of happy there is. The kind that keeps something.*"

---

The rose was crying.

Seren knew. She didn't know how. Roses can't cry. They haven't got eyes. But the petals were moving, very slightly, trembling, and there was a sound in the dark like rain on leaves.

"*...a hedge...*" whispered Rhosyn. "*...she was always going to be a hedge...even when we were girls...she was the one who sat at the edge of things...*"

"*She kept the field*," said Seren. "*For nine hundred years. Her and all her girls. Keeping something.*"

"*...keeping what...*"

Seren didn't answer.

She didn't need to. She knew. And so did the rose.

---

"*It's on the chair*," said Seren.

The rose went still.

"*The crown*," said Seren. "*Her crown. My mother sent it to you. Through the Ferryman. Thirty-two years ago.*" She leaned forward. Over the table. Toward the jug. "*It's in a tower. In the middle of your thorns. On a chair you grew for her.*"

Silence.

"*...how do you know*?" whispered Rhosyn.

"*I dreamed it*."

"*...Harrow women dream...*" The petals shivered. "*...Hes did...she used to dream of the pool...before...she'd wake up crying...*"

"*Is it there*?"

The rose was silent for a long time.

"*...yes*," said Rhosyn.

---

"*...I grew it for her*," said the rose. "*...the tower...in the first year...when we still had something left...*" A small sound. "*...the others laughed...Ysy laughed hardest...she said Hes would never come...she said Hes had made her choice...*" The petals trembled. "*...I said, in case...*"

"*In case she changed her mind*."

"*...in case she wanted somewhere to sleep...*" A long silence. "*...she never came...*"

"*But the crown did*."

"*...the crown did...*" whispered Rhosyn. "*...the grey man brought it...carried it up the stair...I felt it come into the Court...like a cold hand...like a no...*" The voice was very small. "*...I woke...I hadn't woken in four hundred years...I went to the tower and I stood at the bottom of the stair and I couldn't go up...*"

"*Because it said no*."

"*...because it said no...to me...*" The rose was shaking. "*...because I drank...*"

---

"*...so I grew thorns*," said Rhosyn. "*...round and round...a mile...so nobody would find the stair...so Oz would never...*" A pause. "*...he thinks it's lost...he thinks Hes took it into your world and it's gone...rotted...buried...he's never once thought of my tower...he never thinks of anything of mine...*"

"*And it kept you asleep*."

The rose went very still.

"*...how do you know that*?"

"*The Ferryman told us*. *My mother hoped it would. A crown that says no. In the middle of the Court. Every night. While you slept.*" Seren looked at the rose. "*She thought, maybe they'll sleep more soundly. Maybe they won't wake.*"

"*...we did*," said Rhosyn. Very softly. "*...sleep more soundly...thirty years...it was like...like a hand on a forehead...*" A long silence. "*...like Mae's hand...when Hes had the fever...*"

---

Behind Seren, behind the curtain, the bed creaked.

"*Why the roses*?" said Arion.

The rose went still.

His voice came out of the dark behind the curtain. Thin. Dry. A thread. But very clear. The voice of a man who'd questioned ten thousand witnesses in two hundred years and knew which question to ask.

"*Every night*," said Arion. "*Since the first one. Little ones. Red. Through the crack. With no notes.*" A pause. "*If you're hiding your sister's crown from your brother, why send roses through a crack he can see? Why let him notice?*"

The rose didn't answer for a long time.

"*...who's that*?" it whispered.

"*My husband*," said Seren.

"*...the old man...*" The petals lifted. "*...Mae's letters...the clever one...*"

"*Everyone keeps saying that*," said Arion. "*Answer the question.*"

---

"*...because I can't stop*," said Rhosyn.

Seren went still.

"*...nine hundred years*," whispered the rose. "*...nothing but thorns...everything I touched...everything I grew...black...hooked...dry...hungry...*" The petals were trembling. "*...and then I sat outside her tower...and I thought about her...on the edge of the cabbage bed...with her knees up...talking to a puddle...*" A small sound. "*...and a rose came...*"

Nobody spoke.

"*...the first one in nine hundred years*," said Rhosyn. "*...and I sent it to you...to whoever was there...to ask...*" The voice cracked. "*...and then there was another one...the next night...I didn't mean it...I was just sitting...thinking about her...*" A long silence. "*...and another...and another...every night...I can't make them stop...every time I think of her a rose comes...*"

"*So you send them through*," said Arion.

"*...where else would I send them*?" said the Thorn-Crowned. "*...there's no one here to give them to...*"

---

Seren heard her husband breathe out.

Slowly. Behind the curtain. In the dark. The way he breathed out when a ledger finally balanced.

"*Thank you*," he said. Very quietly. "*That's all I needed to know.*"

"*...is it*?"

"*You can't fake summer*," said Arion Vey. "*And you can't stop it, either. Not once it starts.*" A pause. "*That's the trouble with it.*"

---

"*If one of hers came*," said Seren. "*Through the crack. Into your country.*"

The rose went very still.

"*To fetch it*," said Seren. "*The crown. Off the chair. If one of Hesper's came. Could they reach it?*"

"*...it wouldn't say no to them*," said Rhosyn slowly. "*...not to her blood...her blood never drank...*" A pause. "*...but they'd never find it...the thorns...a mile of them...they'd tear anyone to pieces...*"

"*Unless you opened them*."

Silence.

"*Would you*?" said Seren.

---

The rose didn't answer for a long, long time.

Seren sat in the dark. Behind her, she could hear Arion's breathing. In. Out. Catch. Out. Outside, the stream. An owl in the birch wood. Across the water, faint, a baby crying, once, and stopping.

"*...why do you want it*?" said Rhosyn at last.

Seren told her.

The Key. The last thread. Caddoc climbing it, with his iron, toward the Knot. Lantern Night. The net with nobody's hand on it. *Only a hand that has refused a crown*.

The Veil.

---

"*...you'd bring it down*," whispered Rhosyn. When she'd finished. "*...the edge...the Veil...*"

"*If we have to*."

"*...and we'd come through*..." The petals trembled. "*...all of us...Oz and Ysy and Cad...and me...and the household...nine thousand...gold...*" A long silence. "*...and the dead...*"

"*Yes*."

"*...you'd let us in*."

"*We'd stop him picking up the net*," said Seren. "*Your brother. We'd stop him keeping the whole world. Every thread. Every child.*" Her voice was shaking. She didn't let it stop her. "*And then we'd have to deal with the rest. On the ground. Like anyone.*"

---

"*...he wants your girl's baby*," said Rhosyn.

Seren didn't answer.

"*...he told us*," whispered the rose. "*...in the hall...when we woke...he said, I'll put a crown on her and keep her and she'll be ours forever...*" The petals were very still. "*...he said, an heir, Rosie...wouldn't that be nice...*"

"*He can't have her*."

"*...I know...*" A pause. "*...I've seen forever...*" Another pause. Longer. "*...there are nine thousand of them on the white road...my gardeners are there...Tam and old Wyn and little Bess who used to carry the watering cans...I walk past them...every time I leave the hall...I don't look...I haven't looked in nine hundred years...*"

Seren closed her eyes.

"*...they hum*," whispered Rhosyn. "*...did you know...the kept...they hum...one note...all of them...I've never let myself listen long enough to hear what it's saying...*"

---

"*...I'll open them*," said the rose.

Seren opened her eyes.

"*...my thorns*," said Rhosyn. "*...if one of hers comes...I'll open them...I'll take them to the door...*" The petals lifted. "*...I can't open the door...I can't go up the stair...it says no to me...*" A pause. "*...but I can take them to the bottom of it...*"

"*Why*?"

"*...because I listened*," said the Thorn-Crowned. "*...and then I drank anyway...*" The voice was barely there. "*...and the song breaks there...doesn't it...your song...the one Hes's girls sing...I heard it in my sleep sometimes...in the dreams...*" A long silence. "*...gold for the eldest...glass for the vain...iron for the one who never laughed...*"

"*Thorns for the gardener who listened*," said Seren. Very quietly. "*And drank.*"

"*...it doesn't rhyme*," whispered Rhosyn.

"*No*."

"*...it's meant not to...*"

"*Yes*."

"*...I'd like it to rhyme*," said the rose. "*...just once...before the end...*"

---

And then the room went warm.

Not warm like a fire. Warm like a forge. Like an oven door opened. A heat, rolling across the dark room from the table under the west window, from the jug, from the rose. Heavy. Golden. Seren felt it on her face. On her hands on the table.

The rose went rigid.

Every petal. Every leaf. Stiff. Still.

"*...no*," whispered Rhosyn. "*...oh, no...*"

---

"*Rosie*," said a voice.

---

It came out of the rose.

Not the papery green whisper. Something else. Something *deep*. Like a bell. Like a mountain. Like a great warm voice that filled a room the way a fire fills a grate. Rough. Rough as a road. As if it had been asleep for nine hundred years and had only lately remembered how to use itself.

"*Who are you talking to*, *Rosie*?" said the Gilded King.

---

Seren didn't move.

She sat in the dark at the table with her hands flat on the wood and the heat rolling over her and looked at the rose.

It wasn't moving anymore. Not breathing. Not trembling. Rigid. Every petal stiff.

And it was glowing.

Very faintly. Very deep. A gold light, coming up from somewhere inside it. From the stem. From the heart of the flower. Like a coal at the bottom of a grate.

"*Oz*," whispered Rhosyn. Very small. Very far. "*...Oz, I wasn't...I was only...*"

"*I know what you were only*," said Oszric. Warmly. Gently. "*You were only sending flowers. Every night. Through my crack. To nobody.*" A pause. "*I've been counting them, Rosie. Forty-nine. Did you think I wouldn't count?*"

"*...Oz*—"

"*Hush*," said the Gilded King. "*Let me talk to her.*"

---

The heat turned.

Seren felt it. Felt it turn toward her. Like a great warm face leaning close across the table. Like the open mouth of a forge.

"*Hesper's girl*," said Oszric of the Crowned.

She didn't answer.

"*You have her voice*," said the voice in the rose. Wondering. Warm. "*Rosie's right. Exactly her voice. I'd know it anywhere. She used to argue with me at dinner when she was twelve and I'd laugh so hard I fell off my chair.*" A pause. "*She stopped arguing with me later. She stopped speaking to me at all.*"

"*She said no to you*," said Seren.

"*She said no to all of us*." The voice was gentle. "*She was very good at it. It was the only thing she was ever good at. She couldn't ride. She couldn't dance. She couldn't sit still through a whole dinner.*" A pause. "*But she could say no.*"

"*So can I*."

"*I know*," said the Gilded King. "*You said it to my steward. At your door. And to my sister, in the hall, through a hundred lanterns.*" The heat leaned closer. "*And you said it to my other sister. On a hill. With a lamp in your hands.*"

Seren's hands were flat on the table. White at the knuckles.

"*You gave Mae a lamp*," said Oszric. "*And she was warm. Orrin says. For the first time.*" The voice didn't change. Didn't harden. Didn't break. "*And then she came apart.*"

---

"*I'm sorry*," said Seren.

She didn't know she was going to say it. It came out of her in the dark. Small. True.

The heat went still.

"*Are you*?" said Oszric.

"*Yes*," said Seren. "*I'm sorry she's dead. I'm sorry she was cold. I'm sorry nobody gave her anything for nine hundred years.*" She swallowed. "*I'm not sorry I gave her the lamp. I'd do it again.*"

The Gilded King was silent for a long time.

"*So would she*," he said at last. Very quietly. "*Hesper. Exactly that. She'd say exactly that.*" A pause. "*I find I don't know what to do with it. I never did.*"

---

"*Your husband*," said Oszric.

Behind the curtain, the bed creaked.

"*The old man*," said the voice in the rose. "*The clever one. Mae's letters.*" The heat turned. Toward the curtain. "*He's very old. I can feel it from here. Like a candle at the end of its wick.*"

"*He's mine*," said Seren.

"*Of course he is*. *I'm not trying to take him.*" The voice was very gentle. "*I'm trying to give him to you. Forever. Exactly as he is.*" A pause. "*I could. You know I could. One pinch. On the tongue. And he'd be at your window every evening for a thousand years.*"

"*No*."

"*You said that to Orrin*," said the Gilded King. "*At your door. I heard.*" The heat leaned closer. "*You'll change your mind. They all do. At the bedside. At the very end. When his breath catches and doesn't start again. When you're holding his hand and you feel it go slack.*" A pause. "*They all say yes, then. Every one. I've never known anyone who didn't.*"

"*Then you've never known a Harrow*," said Seren.

---

The heat didn't move.

And then it laughed.

Deep. Warm. Delighted. Like a bell. Like a great warm fire roaring up a chimney. The whole room shook with it. The jug rattled on the table.

"*Oh*," said Oszric of the Crowned. "*Oh, I'm going to like you. I'm going to like you so much.*" The laughter faded. "*I'll come and see you. Soon. When the crack's wider. When my sister's been through your windows and brought me back what I need.*" A pause. "*I'll come to your door. I'll knock, the way my steward does. And you'll say no. And I'll come in anyway.*" The heat was very close now. "*And we'll have tea.*"

---

"*And Rosie*," said the Gilded King.

The rose had gone very still. Not rigid now. *Still*. The way a thing goes still when it's waiting to be hit.

"*Come home*," said Oszric. Very gently. "*Come back to the hall. Stop sending flowers.*" A pause. "*You were always sending flowers to people who didn't want them, Rosie. Even when we were little. Even to Father, at the end, when he couldn't smell anymore.*"

"*...he could smell them*," whispered Rhosyn. "*...he told me...he said they were the last thing...*"

"*Come home*," said the Gilded King.

And the rose turned gold.

---

Seren watched it happen.

From the heart. From the centre of the flower, where the glow had been. Outward. Petal by petal. The dark wine-red going to gold. Not fading. Not dying. *Turning*. Like a drop of ink in water. Like frost across a pane. The petals. The leaves. The black hooked stem. Down. Into the jug. Into the water in the jug, which went gold too, and stopped, a gold disc of water with a gold stem standing in it.

It took the time of a breath.

And then there was a gold rose in a jug on the table under the west window. Every petal perfect. Every leaf. Every thorn. Shining dully in the red light of the embers.

Still.

Kept.

---

The heat was gone.

The room was cold. Colder than it had been. The fire was down to a few red coals.

Seren sat at the table and looked at the gold rose.

"*Rosie*," she said. Very quietly.

Nothing.

"*Rosie*. *Are you there?*"

Nothing.

The rose didn't turn. It would never turn again.

---

She went behind the curtain.

Arion was sitting on the edge of the bed. Where he'd been sitting for an hour, in the dark, with his hands on his knees. He looked up at her as she came.

She sat down beside him.

Neither of them said anything for a long time.

"*He kept it*," she said at last. "*The rose. Her rose. The one she grew for Hes.*"

"*Yes*."

"*He keeps everything*. *Even his sister's flowers.*"

"*Especially his sister's flowers*," said Arion. Very quietly. "*Those most of all.*"

---

"*Will she be all right*?" said Seren.

He didn't answer straight away.

"*He loves her*," he said at last.

Seren put her face in her hands.

"*I know*," she said. Through her fingers. "*That's what frightens me.*"

---

She lay down beside him.

On top of the blankets. With her hand on his chest. Over his heart. Counting.

In. Out. In. A little catch.

Out.

She didn't sleep.

At dawn she got up and went to the table and picked up the jug.

It was heavy. The water was gold. The rose was gold. Everything was gold, all the way through, and cold. Cold as a coin. Cold as a ring left on a windowsill in winter.

She held it for a long time.

Then she took it to the shelf above the hearth. The one Arion had reached for, the morning he fell. And set it there. At the back. Behind the chipped blue cup with the crack down the handle.

Where she'd see it every time she made tea.

Where she'd remember.

---

"*Seren*," said Arion. From the bed. Behind the curtain.

"*I know*."

"*He said*—"

"*I know what he said*." She stood at the hearth with her back to the room and her hands on the shelf. "*He said when the crack's wider. He said when his sister's brought him back what he needs.*" Her voice was very steady. "*He said he'd come to my door.*"

"*For tea*."

"*For tea*," said Seren Harrow.

She turned round.

"*Then I'd better make sure there's some*," she said.

And went to put the kettle on.
