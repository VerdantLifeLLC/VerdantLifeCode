# Blood on the Glass

Antho had never left anyone behind on purpose before.

He'd been left. He'd been left at sixteen, in a burning cottage, with his mother walking into the dark and his brother screaming in the yard. He'd left, at sixteen, running into the Greyspine with a hunger in his head. But he'd never stood in a doorway and looked at someone he loved and decided, with his whole heart, to go.

He did it at Harrow on the third morning after midsummer.

His father was in the bed. Propped on three pillows. With the Lantern on the table beside him, three panes of glass and one of horn, burning. His face on the pillow was a walnut. His hair a white wisp. His breath short. In. Catch. Out.

His eyes the same.

"*Go on*," said Arion. "*You're letting the draught in.*"

---

Antho didn't go.

He stood in the doorway of the blue-door cottage with his pack on his back and Ashka steaming in the clearing behind him and looked at his father.

He'd known him for four years.

That was the thing. That was the thing that kept catching in his chest like a fishbone. Four years. Since Avaloria. Since a gold room and a man in crystal and a voice saying *my sons*. Four years, out of thirty. And for the first twenty-six he'd thought his father was a story. A Warden who'd gone away. A name his mother wouldn't say.

And for three of the four, his father had lived at the Lantern Keep. With him. Keeping the door. Reading in the library while Antho counted the lanterns. Sitting at the long table at breakfast with his porridge going cold while he argued with Seraphine about the Founders.

Antho had never said anything.

Not *thank you*. Not *I'm glad*. Not *I used to lie awake in the cave and wonder what you sounded like*.

He'd thought there'd be time.

---

"*Antho*," said his father.

"*I know*."

"*You've got the face*," said Arion. "*The one your brother gets. Like someone's handed you a bill you can't pay.*" A faint smile. "*You're both very bad at hiding it. You get it from me.*"

"*I never*—"

"*I know*," said his father.

Antho stopped.

"*I know*," said Arion again. Very gently. From the pillow. "*You never said. Any of it. Three years at the Keep and you never said a word.*" His grey eyes were very steady. "*Neither did I. I'm your father. I'm the one who's meant to.*" A pause. "*I wrote it all down instead. Forty pages. For your mother. I never wrote a single page for you.*"

"*You didn't have to*."

"*No*," agreed Arion. "*You were right there. Every morning. Across the table.*" He breathed. In. Catch. Out. "*I used to watch you count the lanterns, Antho. Every night. From the top of the passage stair. You never knew I was there.*"

Antho didn't say anything.

"*One thousand, one hundred and thirteen*," said his father. "*Every night. Out loud. To the door.*" A ghost of a smile. "*I used to count with you. Under my breath. I never lost count once.*"

---

Antho came back into the room.

He didn't decide to. His feet did it. They took him back across the flags to the bed, and he knelt down beside it, with his pack still on his back, and took his father's hand.

It was so cold.

"*I used to lie awake*," said Antho. "*In the cave. In the Greyspine. With the Murmur in my head.*" He didn't look up. "*And I used to wonder what you sounded like. Mam never said. Nobody did. I used to make up voices. Lying in the dark. Trying to hear it.*"

His father didn't move.

"*None of them were right*," said Antho Vey. "*When I heard it. At Avaloria. In the gold room.*" His voice cracked. "*None of them were anywhere near.*"

"*What did you make up*?"

"*Deep*," said Antho. "*Like a bell. Like a mountain. Like a king in a story.*" A wet sound that might have been a laugh. "*And then you opened your mouth and you sounded like a librarian telling off a choir.*"

His father laughed.

The real laugh. The wide startled one. It came out of him short and cracked and broken off in the middle by the catch in his chest, and he coughed, and Antho held his hand through it, and when it was over his father was still smiling.

"*Your mother said that*," said Arion. "*On the steps. In the rain. The first time.*"

"*I know*. *She told me.*"

"*Page four*," said his father.

---

"*Go*," said Arion. "*Please. I'll be here.*"

"*You'll try*."

"*I'll try*," agreed his father. "*Very hard. I've never tried so hard at anything in my life. Not the Root. Not the crystal. Not the map.*" His hand tightened. As much as it could. "*I'll be in this bed with that Lantern burning and I'll breathe in and out until you walk back through that door. That's all I'm going to do. That's my whole job now.*"

Antho bent his head.

And put his forehead down on his father's hand. On the blanket. And stayed there.

"*Thank you*," he said. Into the blanket. Very quietly. "*For counting.*"

His father's other hand came down. Onto his hair. Black. Long. Wild. The way it had always been.

"*Thank you for letting me*," said Arion Vey.

---

His mother walked him to the door.

She stood on the oak ramp in her bare feet with her bandaged hand held against her chest and looked at him. At Ashka. At the clearing, gold in patches, the birch tree at the edge of it standing shining in the morning sun like a thing made by a jeweller.

"*Bring your brother back*," she said.

"*I will*."

"*Bring yourself back*." She reached up. With her good hand. And put it on his face. His thin face. His wild beard. "*I lost you once. For thirteen years. On the other side of a door.*" Her eyes were very bright. "*Don't you dare make me do it again.*"

"*I won't*."

"*You'll try*," said Seren.

"*I'll try*," said Antho. "*Very hard.*"

She looked at him for a long moment.

"*You get that from him*," she said. And kissed him. On the forehead. Quickly. Fiercely. Like a seal stamped.

---

They stopped at the pool on the way.

They had to. For the last cups. Ivy had filled two more since midsummer. That made fourteen. Fourteen Hush-cups, each with an iron lid that sighed, each one heavy with nothing, wrapped in straw in a basket on Pim's back.

Pim wouldn't let anyone else carry it.

"*It's mine*," he said. When Lex offered. Three words. A speech. "*Ivy gave them to me.*"

Ivy Moss was sitting on the rim. Where she always sat. She didn't get up when they came. She looked at Pim for a long time with her strange light eyes.

Then she held out her hand.

There was something in it. Small. Folded. A piece of paper.

Pim took it. Unfolded it. Looked at it.

Antho saw over his shoulder. A drawing. In pencil. Of a cat. A small one. Sitting very straight. With its tail curled round its feet. Looking out of the paper with a fierce determined expression.

No words.

Pim folded it up again. Very carefully. And put it inside his blue coat. In the pocket over his heart. And buttoned the pocket.

He didn't say anything.

He reached into his other pocket. And took out a piece of paper. Folded. And held it out.

Ivy took it. Unfolded it.

A cat. In pencil. A little bigger. Sitting on the rim of a pool. With its feet over the edge.

No words.

Ivy looked at it for a long time.

Then she folded it up. And held it against her chest. And looked back at the pool.

"*It says come back*," she said. Not to anyone. To the deep. "*Mal. It says, little room-maker, come back.*"

"*I will*," said Pim.

---

Hask came with them.

He didn't trust dragons. He'd said so. Twice. He'd walked two days to Harrow so he wouldn't have to fly.

He got on Vharuun's back behind Lex at the pool without a word, and sat there with his pick across his knees and his hands white on the haft, and his eyes shut.

"*It's three days to the Veil on foot*," said Lex. Turning round. "*Less than one on Vharuun.*"

"*I know*," said Silas Hask. Not opening his eyes.

"*You could walk*. *We'd wait.*"

"*I know*."

"*Then why*—"

"*Because the old man's got four months*," said Hask. "*Less. He said so.*" His jaw was very tight. "*I'm not wasting three days of them because I don't like heights.*"

Lex looked at him for a long moment.

Then he turned round. And laid his hand on the dragon's neck.

*Gently*, he thought. *Please. Very gently.*

Vharuun went up so smoothly that Hask didn't open his eyes until they were over the downs.

---

Antho flew with Pim.

On Ashka. With the boy in front of him, small and thin in his blue coat, and the basket of Hush-cups strapped between them. With his arms round Pim and the basket both.

Pim didn't say anything. For hours. He looked down. At the green of the Blightmarch going away behind them. At the western shires. At the bare grey of the Sundered Lands coming up ahead, cracked and dead and endless, with the wind coming off it cold.

And then, somewhere over the bare lands, with the Veil a grey smudge on the edge of the world ahead—

"*Antho*."

"*Pim*."

"*What's it like*?"

Antho didn't need to ask what.

"*I don't know*," he said. "*Nobody does. Nobody living.*" He looked ahead. At the grey. "*The Ferryman's been. Hesper never went. Maelis came out of it.*" A pause. "*Ysenne says it's dust and glass and no sun.*"

Pim was quiet for a while.

"*Is it like the Hollow*?" he said. "*Before. When it was hungry.*"

Antho thought about it.

He thought about eight years in a cave with a voice in his head. About a crack in him, opening onto a dark that wanted. About the cold. About the *need*.

"*No*," he said slowly. "*The Hollow was empty. It wanted to be filled.*" He looked at the grey smudge on the horizon. "*I think the Court's the opposite. I think it's full. All the way. And it doesn't want anything at all.*"

Pim didn't say anything.

But Antho felt him lean back. Just a little. Against his chest.

---

Greyfall at dusk.

Roole met them on the landing field below the walls with a lantern in his good hand and his stiff arm held close. He watched the dragons come down. He watched Hask climb off Vharuun's back, grey-faced, and stand on the grass with his hand on the dragon's flank, breathing.

"*You came*," said Roole.

"*We said we would*."

"*People say a great many things*," said Commander Roole. He looked at them. All four. Lex and Antho and Pim and Hask. "*Come and see it. While there's light.*"

---

The gold had grown.

Antho saw it from the walls of Greyfall in the last red light of the sun. A great soft stain spreading out from the foot of the Veil across the bare grey ground. Not a mile across now. Five. Six. Reaching almost to the fort. Shining dully in the dusk like a lake of old brass.

The tongue of it running west to the crack was wider. Much wider. And at the end of it—

"*It's bigger*," said Lex. Very quietly. Beside him. "*Isn't it.*"

"*Since midsummer*," said Roole. "*Three nights ago. We felt it from here.*" His hatchet face was very still. "*Something came out of the west on the wind. In the middle of the night. A light. Like a falling star going the wrong way. Up off the ground and into the crack.*" A pause. "*And the crack opened.*"

"*How wide*?"

Roole looked at him.

"*Come and see*," he said.

---

Nan Ashby was still there.

Antho saw her as they came down off the walls and out across the bare ground toward the gold. Sitting on the grey at the very edge of it. Where she'd sat every day since the spring. With her shawl round her shoulders and her basket beside her.

Talking.

She looked up as they came. At Lex. At the mark on his cheek.

"*You came back*," she said.

"*I said I'd find out*," said Lex.

"*And did you*?"

He knelt down beside her. On the grey. At the edge of the gold. And looked across it. At the field. At the forty gold sheep, heads down, grazing. At the one tall thin gold shape among them. Mid-step. Mid-whistle. His crook in his hand.

His eyes moving.

"*There's a way*," said Lex. "*Not with him. Not yet. He's set. He's finished.*" He swallowed. "*But I found the room in front of it. In a girl. In Ashbourne. Before it set.*" He looked at Nan Ashby. "*So there's a way. Somewhere. There has to be. Everything's a crack first.*"

She looked at him for a long time.

"*That's more than anyone else has said*," said Nan Ashby. And turned back to her son. And went on talking. About the hens. About the byre roof. About the smith who'd come back.

The gold boy's eyes moved. Slowly. From his mother. To Lex.

And stayed there.

---

The crack was a door.

Not a door like a door. Not oak and iron. But a *door*. Antho felt it as soon as they came round the end of the tongue of gold and saw it. A Threshold Warden feels doors. That was what he was.

It was taller than a man now. As tall as two men. A dark seam at the foot of the grey, running up from the ground like a tear in a curtain. And wide. Not a finger. Not a hand. Wide enough for a man to turn sideways and squeeze through. Wide enough for a thin man to walk.

Dark.

Grey-gold dust sifting out of it. Not a trickle now. A *flow*. Like sand out of a sack with a slit in it. Pouring out onto the ground in a soft glittering heap and the wind picking it up and carrying it east.

And thorns.

Black. Hooked. Thick as a man's wrist. Dozens of them. Hundreds. Growing out of the dark of the crack and down into the ground on either side and up the edges of it like ivy up a wall. Gripping. Pushing. Widening.

And on the edges of the crack, on the grey of the Veil itself, where the thorns gripped it—

Red.

---

Antho went closer.

He couldn't help it. He was a Threshold Warden. He went up to the door.

The red was on the edge. On the lip of the crack, where the grey of the Veil met the dark. A stain. Dark. Dried. A hand's breadth across. As if someone had reached through the crack from the other side and laid a hand there. A wet red hand. And held it against the edge of the world until the edge gave.

Glass-shards glittered in it. Tiny. Clear. Like frost in a bloodstain.

*I've got it*, Ysenne had said. Fading. Laughing. *A little. Enough.*

His mother's blood.

On the edge of the world.

---

He put his hand out.

Not to the stain. To the Veil beside it. The grey. The edge.

It didn't push.

He'd known it wouldn't. Lex had told him. But knowing and feeling were different things, and he stood at the foot of the edge of the world with his hand an inch from the grey and felt nothing. No push. No *not you*. Nothing.

And on the other side of the crack, through the dark, faint—

He felt it.

---

Doors.

Not this one. Others. Beyond. On the far side. In the grey country.

A great many doors. Thousands. Standing open. Every one. Wide open. Hanging on their hinges in a country where there had been no one to keep out and no one to keep in for nine hundred years.

And behind them—

Nothing. No rooms. No hallways. No warmth. No one coming home.

Just *full*. Like the gold. Like Bet Dale's door, bricked up so smooth you couldn't see where it had been.

A country of open doors with nowhere to go.

He took his hand back.

---

"*Antho*," said Lex. Behind him.

"*I know*."

"*It's big*."

"*It's big enough for a thin man*," said Antho. He didn't turn round. He was looking at the crack. At the thorns. At the red. "*Not big enough for a king. Not yet. Not quite.*"

"*He's big*," said Lex. "*Mam says. Ysenne said. Taller than Brannoc. Broader.*"

"*He's not coming through that*," said Antho. "*Not yet.*"

"*And when we open it*?"

---

Nobody said anything.

The wind blew. Off the Veil. Cold. Carrying gold.

They stood at the foot of the edge of the world in the last of the dusk. Four of them. Two brothers. A boy with a basket on his back. A hewer with a pick. And behind them, Roole, with his lantern, with his stiff arm, with his face like a hatchet.

"*It's Harrow blood that opens it*," said Antho at last. Slowly. "*A finger's depth did that.*" He nodded at the crack. "*Mam's blood. In a glass hand. Did this.*" He looked at his own hand. "*Ours. On the edge. To get four people through.*"

"*It'll widen*," said Lex.

"*It'll widen*."

"*Wide enough for him*."

"*I don't know*," said Antho. "*Maybe. Probably.*" He looked at his brother. "*It doesn't close, Lex. Seraphine said. Once blood's opened it, it stays.*"

"*So we go through*," said Lex. Very quietly. "*To get the one thing that can stop him. And the door we go through is the door he comes through after us.*"

"*Yes*."

"*Into the world*. *While we're gone.*" Lex's voice was very flat. "*Toward Harrow. Toward Father. Toward Wren and Lark.*"

Antho didn't answer.

He didn't need to.

---

"*Don't go*," said Roole.

They turned.

The Keeper-Commander was standing on the grey with his lantern held up. His face in its light was hard and plain and lined, the way it always was. His voice didn't change. It never did.

"*Don't go*," he said again. "*That's what I'd say. If I were you. If I were sensible.*" He lowered the lantern a little. "*Stay. Sit tight. Wait for him to come down the thread on Lantern Night. And fight him in the Knot. On your own ground. With the door shut.*"

"*And if we lose*?" said Lex.

"*Then you lose*," said Roole. "*And he picks up the net. And keeps it.*" He looked at the crack. "*And we go back to what we were. Ceilings and Wardens and children Kindled into the same class as their mothers.*" A pause. "*I was a Warden in that world for twenty-six years. I know how to live in it.*"

"*Do you want to*?"

The hatchet face didn't change.

"*No*," said Daxian Roole. "*I don't.*" He looked at the crack for a long time. At the thorns. At the red. "*Fenn didn't either. He straightened every leaning Stone in the west because he didn't want to.*"

---

"*If he comes through*," said Antho. "*After us. When it's wider.*"

Roole looked at him.

"*Greyfall's the first thing he'll see*," said Antho. "*You'll be the first.*"

"*I know*."

"*Get out*," said Antho. "*When he comes. Don't stand in front of him. Don't fight him. You can't.*" He looked at the gold, spreading. At the field. At the boy. "*Get your Keepers out. Get Nan Ashby out. Get everyone out. And send a raven to Harrow.*"

Roole didn't answer for a long moment.

"*Fenn stood in front of the Mothwing*," he said at last. "*At the south edge. All night. With his staff held crosswise.*" His voice didn't change. "*I said the same thing to him. Get out. Don't stand in front of them. You can't.*"

"*And*?"

"*And he said*, *somebody has to stand somewhere, Dax*," said Roole. "*And then he died.*" He looked at Antho. "*I'll get them out. I promise. I'll send the raven.*" A pause. "*And then I'll stand somewhere.*"

---

They camped at the foot of the Veil.

Not inside the gold. On the grey. In a hollow out of the wind, a hundred yards from the crack. Roole went back to Greyfall. He said someone should be on the walls. He said he'd come back at dawn.

They didn't light a fire.

There was no wood. There was nothing to burn in the Sundered Lands. Lex sat with his hand open on the ground and let a little heat come up out of it, black, no light, so they were warm without anything to see by.

They ate cold bread. They didn't talk much.

Pim sat with the basket of Hush-cups in his lap and his hand on the lid.

Hask sat a little apart. On a stone. With his pick across his knees. Looking at the crack. Gold dust drifting past him on the wind, catching on his sleeve, his hair, his scarred bandaged arm. Sliding off.

Lex lay on his back on the grey with his hands behind his head and looked up at the sky.

Antho sat beside him.

---

"*You could stay*," said Antho.

Lex didn't move.

"*I mean it*," said Antho. "*You've got a wife. A daughter. A father who might not*—" He stopped. "*I can go. With Pim. With Hask. We can get it. You don't have to come.*"

"*You need Harrow blood*," said Lex. "*Two of us. Seraphine said.*"

"*Seraphine said more blood*. *She didn't say two people.*" Antho looked at the crack. "*I can bleed for both of us. I've got plenty. I'm very tall.*"

Lex laughed. Short. Cracked.

"*You're not that tall*."

"*I'm taller than you*."

"*You're not*," said Lex. "*We measured. At Ashbourne. Against the forge door. When you came back from the Hollowmaw.*" He turned his head. "*I'm half an inch taller. Brannoc marked it with a nail.*"

"*Brannoc cheated*."

"*Brannoc's never cheated in his life*."

They looked at each other in the dark.

"*I'm coming*," said Lex.

---

"*Why*?" said Antho.

His brother didn't answer for a long time.

He lay on his back on the cold grey ground at the edge of the world and looked up at the sky. The stars were out. Hard. White. Close. Fewer than over the Vale. Much fewer. As if the edge of the world thinned them out.

"*Because you went alone*," said Lex at last. "*At sixteen. Into the Greyspine. With the Murmur.*" His voice was very quiet. "*And then again. Into the Hollow. To keep the door. Into the Drowned Country. Down to the Heart.*" He swallowed. "*You always went alone. Every time. You said it was yours. You said nobody else could.*"

Antho didn't say anything.

"*And I went alone*," said Lex. "*Up the Spire. Into the Wellworlds. To the Knot. Down the Old Gallery.*" He laughed. Short. "*We're both terrible at it. Going with people. We're the worst. Wren says.*"

"*Wren's right*."

"*Wren's always right*. *It's very annoying.*" Lex turned his head. "*I'm not letting you go into a country with no sun and no room on your own, Antho. I'm not letting you open a door at the edge of the world and walk through it without me.*" He looked at his brother. "*Not this time. Not ever again.*"

---

Antho didn't say anything for a long time.

He sat on the grey with his knees drawn up and his arms round them. The way his mother sat. The way Hesper had sat on the edge of a cabbage bed, nine hundred years ago, talking to a puddle.

"*Brannoc cheated*," he said at last.

Lex laughed.

Properly. On his back on the cold ground at the edge of the world. With his hands over his face. Laughing.

And Antho, after a moment, did too.

---

*[I'm going to put that in the main text*,] said Ember. Very quietly. In the back of Lex's head. Antho didn't hear it. Lex told him, later. *[Not a footnote. The main text. Chapter forty. "The brothers argued about who was taller at the edge of the world, and laughed, and it was the last time either of them laughed for a while."]*

*That's very cheerful*, thought Lex.

*[I'm a historian*,] said Ember. *[I write what happens.]*

---

Dawn came grey.

Roole came with it. On foot, from Greyfall, with his lantern unlit and three Keepers behind him and a raven on his shoulder. One of the old ones. One-eyed. Ragged.

"*He'll stay*," said Roole. Nodding at the raven. "*On the walls. Watching. If anything comes through after you, he'll go east. To Harrow. Fast.*"

"*Thank you*."

"*Don't thank me*," said Daxian Roole. "*Come back.*"

---

They stood at the foot of the crack.

The four of them. In the grey light. In the cold wind off the edge of the world. With the gold dust pouring out of the dark past their boots and the thorns gripping the edges of the Veil on either side and the red stain on the lip of it, dark, glittering with glass.

Pim with the basket on his back. Fourteen cups. Fourteen days.

Hask with his pick.

Lex with Aurelian's hammer on his belt, and Malachi's bell on its cord, and the grey book in oilcloth inside his shirt.

Antho with nothing. No Lantern. He'd left it by his father's bed. Three panes of glass and one of horn.

"*How much*?" said Lex.

Antho looked at the crack. At the stain. At the edge.

"*I don't know*," he said. "*Enough.*"

---

He took out his knife.

A plain one. Short. The one he used for bread. He'd thought about using something else. Something grander. Something with a rune on it.

He'd decided against it.

*It's just blood*, he'd thought. In the night. On the grey. *It's just Harrow blood. Mam's blood. Hesper's. Twenty generations of it. It doesn't need a rune. It never did.*

He drew the blade across his palm.

---

It didn't hurt much. It stung. A thin line of fire across the middle of his left hand. And then the red came up. Welling. Bright.

He held his hand out.

He handed Lex the knife.

His brother looked at it. Then at Antho's palm. Then at his own.

He drew the blade across his palm. Not flinching. A smith's hand. Calloused. Scarred. A thin line of red welling up across the old burns.

They looked at each other.

"*Together*," said Lex.

"*Together*," said Antho.

---

They put their hands on the Veil.

Either side of the crack. On the grey. Flat. Palms open. Bleeding.

And the edge of the world didn't know them.

---

Antho felt it.

Felt it the way he felt every door. Felt the grey under his palm, the edge, the last thread, stretched tight across the end of everything for nine hundred years. Felt it *look* at him. The way the net had always looked at him. The way it had read him at his Kindling and called him ERROR.

Felt it look at the blood on his hand.

And not know.

Hesper's line. The tenth. The sister who wouldn't drink. Who poured back instead. Whose daughters had never Kindled and never stayed and never let a moth in, for nine hundred years, so that the net never once got a proper look at them.

The edge didn't know.

And so the edge did the only thing a door can do, when a hand it has never seen before is laid on it, and there is no reason in the world to keep that hand out.

---

It *opened*.

Not like the crack. Not a seam. Not a tear.

Like a door.

Antho felt it go. Under his hand. Under Lex's. The grey on either side of the crack *drawing back*. Folding. Parting. Like a curtain drawn by a hand. Like a door swinging on its hinges, slowly, silently, inward, into the dark.

The crack widened.

A man's width. Two. Three. The thorns on the edges creaking, stretching, snapping, as the grey drew back from them. The dust pouring out faster. Faster. A flood of it. A river of gold glittering past their boots, past their knees, and the wind taking it, and the sun coming up behind them over the bare lands and catching it, so that for one moment the whole edge of the world was a wall of grey with a great tall gold-lit door in it, pouring light.

---

And it was wide.

As wide as a cart. As wide as a barn door. As wide as the great doors of the Hall of Lanterns, where every king of Caldor had hung a lantern on their crowning day.

Wide enough for four people to walk through side by side.

Wide enough for a king.

---

Antho took his hand off the Veil.

He looked at his palm. Red. Cut. Bleeding. A thin line of fire.

He looked at the door.

Dark. Tall. Wide. The grey drawn back on either side of it like curtains. The thorns hanging off the edges, snapped, broken, dangling. And beyond it, through it, in the dark—

Light.

Grey-gold. Dim. Coming from nowhere. The colour of a candle seen through a dirty window.

And a smell.

Dust. Old dust. The dust of a closed room. Of a hall nobody has walked through in nine hundred years.

And under it, very faint—

Roses.

---

"*Well*," said Lex. Beside him. Very quietly. Looking at the door. With his bleeding hand held against his chest. "*That's done.*"

"*That's done*," said Antho.

They looked at each other.

Behind them, Roole made a sound. Small. Not a word.

Pim shifted the basket on his back.

Hask lifted his pick.

And the four of them walked through the door at the edge of the world.
