# The Crossing

The Veil was thick.

Lex hadn't known that. Nobody had. Nobody living had ever walked into it. You stood at the foot of the edge of the world and looked at the grey and it looked like a curtain. A wall. A sheet of something hanging from nowhere.

It wasn't a sheet.

It was a *weave*.

---

He was inside it before he understood.

Three steps through the door. Four. The bare grey ground of the Sundered Lands under his boots and the cold wind at his back and Roole's lantern behind him, and then the grey closed in on either side and above, and the wind stopped.

Not died. *Stopped*. As if a door had shut on it.

And he was in a corridor.

Grey. All round. Soft. Close. Not stone. Not cloth. *Thread*. He could see it. Even without the Root. Even without the gold in his eyes that let him see the net, that he'd given up two years ago and missed every day since. He could see this.

Threads. Grey. Thousands of them. Millions. Woven so close together they made a wall. Running up and down and across and through each other in patterns too fine to follow. On either side of him. Above. Below his boots. A great soft grey corridor of thread, going on ahead of him into a dim grey light.

And all of them humming.

One note. High. Clear. Like a harp-string.

The last thread.

He was walking through the inside of it.

---

*[Oh*,] said Ember.

Very quiet. Very small. In the back of his head.

*Ember*?

*[I'm here*.] A pause. *[It's—Lex. It's very loud. In here. The note.]*

*I can hear it*.

*[No. You can hear it with your ears. I can hear it with—]* It stopped. *[I'm made of him. Of your father. He held the net for two hundred years. I'm made of someone who held the net.]* A pause. *[It's all round me. In here. It's like standing inside a bell.]*

*Are you all right*?

*[I'll put it in a footnote*,] said Ember. Faintly. *[A long one.]*

---

They walked.

Single file. The corridor wasn't wide. Lex first. Then Antho. Then Pim, with the basket on his back. Then Hask, at the end, with his pick, ducking because the grey came down low in places and he was very tall.

The light didn't change. Grey. Dim. Coming from nowhere. From everywhere. From the threads themselves.

The floor didn't change. Grey. Soft. Thread underfoot like moss.

The note didn't change.

He didn't know how long they walked. He tried to count steps and lost count somewhere past six hundred. He tried to count breaths. He tried to count anything.

Everything in the corridor was the same. Every step. Every breath. Every thread.

*[It's the last one*,] said Ember. Very quietly. *[The last thread. Aurelian tied it. It's—Lex, it's as wide as the world. It's as long as the edge. We're walking through the thickness of it.]*

*How thick*?

*[I don't know*,] said Ember. *[I don't think anyone's ever measured.]*

---

And then the panel came up.

He didn't call it. He hadn't called his panel in months. He didn't need to. He knew what was on it.

It came up anyway. On its own. Hanging in the grey air in front of his face, in the gold letters it always used. Flickering.

He stopped walking.

The letters were breaking up. As he watched. The gold going thin. Grainy. Like sand. Like letters written on a beach with a stick, and the tide coming in.

:::error
L▓X V▓Y
▓▓▓▓▓: ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓
NOT OF THIS NET

And then it was gone.

---

"*Lex*," said Antho. Behind him. Very quietly. "*Did yours*—"

"*Yes*."

"*Mine said ERROR*," said Antho. "*At the end. Before it went.*" A short sound. Not quite a laugh. "*Like my Kindling. ERROR. Like it didn't know what I was.*"

"*Mine said not of this net*."

"*That's what it said about her*," said Antho. "*Maelis. When she came through. Last spring. UNKNOWN ENTITY. NOT OF THIS NET.*" He was quiet for a moment. "*We're the unknown ones now.*"

Lex looked at the grey corridor going on ahead.

"*Pim*?" he said.

"*Nothing*," said Pim. From behind Antho. "*Mine didn't come.*"

"*Hask*?"

A long silence from the back.

"*Mine came*," said Silas Hask.

They all turned round.

---

He was standing at the end of the line. Ducking under the low grey. With his pick in his good hand and his bandaged arm against his chest. His face very strange.

"*What did it say*?" said Lex.

Hask didn't answer for a moment.

"*It said*, *welcome back*," he said.

Nobody spoke.

"*Not in gold*," said Hask. "*Not like the others. Grey. Grey-gold.*" He swallowed. "*Like her wings. Like the dust off them.*" His big scarred hand tightened on the pick. "*It said, welcome back, champion.*"

---

The corridor ended.

Lex didn't see it end. One moment there was grey ahead. Grey threads, humming. The next, the grey was thinning. Going pale. Going soft. Like mist burning off a field. And there was light beyond it.

Not daylight. Not sun.

Grey-gold.

---

He stepped out of the Veil.

And the world pushed.

---

He'd been warned. The Ferryman had warned them. Seraphine had warned them. *Anyone with room in them, it pushes. Slowly. Firmly.*

He'd imagined a wind. A weight. A hand on his chest.

It wasn't any of those.

It was like stepping into deep water.

All at once. All over. From every side. Pressure. Not hard. Not crushing. *Full*. As if the air itself were thicker. Heavier. As if there were no space in it. As if every breath he took was a breath into a room already full of something, and he had to push the something aside to fit his breath in.

His ears popped.

His chest went tight. He breathed in and the breath stopped halfway. Not because it hurt. Because there wasn't *room*. Because his lungs pushed out and the air pushed back.

He staggered.

Antho caught him. From behind. A hand on his shoulder. And Lex heard his brother gasp, the same gasp, the same stopped breath, as he stepped out of the grey into the light.

"*Oh*," said Antho. "*Oh. That's*—"

He didn't finish.

He didn't have the breath.

---

Pim stepped out behind them.

And didn't stagger.

Lex saw it. Saw the boy step out of the thinning grey with the basket on his back and stop. And stand there. Small. Thin. In his blue coat with the brass buttons. With his eyes shut and his face very white.

And round him—

Lex felt it. The way you feel a draught from an open door. A *space*. Round the boy. A small round room of quiet. Pushing back. Not hard. Just—holding. A yard wide. Two. Spreading out from him the way warmth spreads out from a hearth.

The pressure stopped at the edge of it.

Lex felt it stop. Felt the full heavy air of the grey country press against the edge of Pim's room and not come in. Felt his own chest, inside the edge, loosen. Felt his breath go past halfway. Further.

He breathed.

"*Pim*," said Antho. Hoarsely. Breathing too.

The boy didn't open his eyes.

"*Not long*," said Pim. Very quietly. "*Can't hold it walking. Only sitting.*" His small face was screwed up with effort. "*Drink.*"

---

Lex reached into the basket.

His hands were shaking. He took out a cup. Horseshoe iron. Heavy with nothing. With an iron lid on it that sighed when he lifted it.

Empty. He could see the bottom. The broken circle of the Hollow cut on the inside of the curve.

He lifted it to his mouth.

And drank.

---

There was nothing in it.

There was nothing in it at all. He tipped the cup and nothing came out. No water. No taste. No weight on his tongue.

And then he felt it go in.

Not down his throat. *In*. Into his chest. Into the tight full space where his breath had stopped. Spreading. Like warmth from a hearth. Like light coming into a room when someone opens the shutters.

Room.

He felt it open. In him. A space. Small. Round. Quiet. Pushing back against the full heavy air from the inside. Not hard. Just holding. Making room for his breath. For his heartbeat. For the blood in his veins.

He breathed in.

All the way. All the way down. Further than he had since he stepped out of the grey.

*Like holding your breath*, Ivy had said. *Right before you laugh*.

It was exactly like that.

---

*[...that's Mal*,] said Ember. Very faintly. Wondering. *[Lex. That's a bit of Mal. In you.]*

*I know*.

*[It's warm*.]

*I know*.

*[It's like being home*,] said Ember. Very small. *[In the middle of here.]*

---

He held the cup out to Antho.

His brother took it. Drank. Lex watched his face. Watched it go from grey and tight to something else. Something softer. Watched his shoulders come down from round his ears.

"*Oh*," said Antho. "*Oh, Mal.*"

He put the lid back on the cup. Carefully. It sighed.

"*Three days*," he said. "*One cup. Three days for one person.*" He looked at the basket. "*We've drunk one cup between us. Two people. A day and a half each.*"

"*Thirteen left*," said Lex.

"*Thirteen left*," said Antho. "*Thirty-nine days for one. Nineteen for two.*" He looked at Pim. "*If Pim holds the nights.*"

Pim opened his eyes.

The room round him faded. Lex felt it go. Felt the full heavy air of the grey country press in again, all round, from every side.

But it didn't press *him*. Not now. Not inside. Inside him there was room. A small round space, warm and quiet, that the pressure pushed against and couldn't get into.

"*Fourteen days*," said Pim. "*Not twelve.*"

---

Hask was standing apart.

He'd stepped out of the Veil last. Ducking. And he hadn't staggered either.

He was standing on the grey-gold ground with his pick in his good hand and his face turned up to the dim light. Breathing.

All the way down.

"*Hask*," said Lex.

"*It doesn't push*," said Silas Hask. Not looking round. "*Not me.*" He breathed in. Out. In. Slow. Deep. "*It's like the mine. Like the bottom of the Old Gallery. Close. Heavy. Full.*" He looked down at his hands. "*I know this. I know what this feels like. I had it in my chest for a season.*"

He turned round.

His face was grey. Wet.

"*I could breathe here*," said Hask. Very quietly. "*Smith. I could breathe here all the way down.*"

Nobody said anything.

"*No grey-lung*," said Silas Hask. "*No catch. No rattle. Nothing.*" He breathed again. All the way down. "*It's the first time since I was nine.*"

---

Lex looked at him for a long time.

He thought about a pithead yard at dusk. Hundreds of rough voices. Coughing between the lines. Singing anyway.

He thought about a man who'd broken twenty-nine cores and made twenty-nine holes because every level eased the cough.

He thought about the Moth Queen. Who'd found a dying hewer and filled his chest with light so he could breathe.

"*Hask*," he said.

"*I know*," said Silas Hask. "*I know what it is. I know what it costs.*" He looked at the grey-gold country. At the dust. At the dim light that came from nowhere. "*I'm not staying.*"

He shouldered his pick.

"*I'm just saying*," he said. "*It's very nice. Breathing.*"

---

Lex looked at the Court.

He hadn't. Not yet. He'd been breathing. He'd been drinking. He'd been watching his brother and Pim and Hask. He hadn't looked.

He looked now.

---

It was flat.

That was the first thing. Flat as a table. Flat as a frozen lake. A plain of grey-gold dust running away from the foot of the Veil in every direction as far as he could see, without a hill, without a fold, without a tree. Grey-gold. Soft. Fine as flour. Lying in drifts and ripples the way snow lies, the way sand lies in the bottom of a riverbed, undisturbed.

Nothing had disturbed it. Not for nine hundred years.

No wind. That was the second thing. No wind at all. Not the faintest breath of air. The dust lay where it had fallen and stayed there.

And the light.

Grey-gold. Dim. Coming from nowhere. Not from the sky; there was no sky, not really, just a paler grey-gold overhead, like the inside of a shell. Not from a sun; there was no sun. From everywhere. From the air. From the dust itself. The colour of a candle seen through a dirty window.

It didn't change. He could tell, somehow, that it never had. Not in nine hundred years. No morning. No evening. No night.

Just this.

---

And a road.

It started at his feet. Right at the foot of the Veil. Where the crack had been. Where the door was now. A road. White. The white of old bone. Broad. Paved with great flat slabs of stone, laid edge to edge, without a crack between them.

Running away from the Veil. Straight. Straight as a ruler. Across the grey-gold plain. Toward the far edge of everything.

And at the far edge, where the plain met the paler grey-gold of the sky—

Towers.

Small, at this distance. Very far. But towers. Spires. Domes. Glittering. Some of them clear, catching the dim light like ice. Some of them dark. Some of them gold, shining dully even from here. Some of them white, like the road. Like bone.

Palaces.

---

"*Glass*," said Antho. Very quietly. Beside him. "*And iron. And gold.*"

"*And bone*," said Hask.

"*Ysenne's*," said Antho. "*Caddoc's. Oszric's.*" He looked at the white towers. "*And the hall. The one in the middle. With the cocoons.*"

"*How far*?"

Antho looked at the road. At the white line running away across the plain.

"*I don't know*," he said. "*There's nothing to measure by.*" He shaded his eyes, though there was no sun to shade them from. "*Days. Several.*"

"*And the thorns*?"

His brother didn't answer for a moment.

"*I can't see them*," he said. "*Not from here.*" He looked north. Along the plain. Flat. Grey-gold. Empty. "*Mam dreamed them off the road. Near the palaces. A mile off. North.*"

"*Days*," said Lex.

"*Days*."

He looked at the basket on Pim's back. Thirteen cups.

---

There was something on the road.

At his feet. On the first slab. Right at the foot of the Veil. Where the door was.

Small. Grey-gold. Lying in the dust that had drifted over the edge of the white stone.

He knelt down.

It was a moth.

---

It was dead. It had been dead a long time. Months. It lay on its back on the white stone with its legs folded up and its wings spread out on either side of it, shredded, torn, most of the dust gone off them. The painted eyes on the wings faded almost to nothing.

It was very small. Smaller than the ones that had come to the Keep. Smaller than the one that had darkened the lantern at the wedding.

Beside it, a glass bottle.

Small. The size of a thumb. Lying on its side on the white stone. Empty. Its cork gone. A crust of something dark round the neck of it.

Red-dark.

---

*[Oh*,] said Ember. Very softly. *[Oh, Lex. That's the one.]*

*The one*?

*[The last one. The one she left on the desk. In the Spire. With your mother's blood.]* A pause. *[It carried it all the way here. Forty nights. Over every village between Highcrest and the edge.]* Another pause. *[And then it died. On the road. On the other side.]*

Lex looked at the little dead moth for a long time.

*It went past the lanterns*, he thought. *Every one. Every window. Every village. It went past them all.*

*[It had been told to take it home*,] said Ember.

He reached out.

And picked it up.

---

It weighed nothing. Less than nothing. A scrap of dry dust and silk in his palm. It didn't come apart. He'd thought it might. It lay in his hand on its back with its legs folded and its shredded wings spread and its faded painted eyes looking up at nothing.

Antho was watching him.

"*What are you doing*?"

"*I don't know*," said Lex.

He didn't. He knelt on the white road at the foot of the Veil with the little dead moth in his hand and didn't know what he was doing.

Then he did.

He took the grey book out of his shirt. Out of its oilcloth. And opened it. To the back. Past the eleven thousand Vhessans. Past Kell. Past the twenty-three from the rim. Past a blank page with three lines on it in a shaking hand he didn't read, because they weren't for him yet.

To the inside of the back cover.

And laid the moth there. On the leather. With its wings spread.

And closed the book.

---

"*Lex*," said Antho.

"*It brought her blood here*," said Lex. "*Mam's blood. All the way. It's the reason the crack opened. It's the reason they woke. It's the reason any of this.*" He put the book back in its oilcloth. Back in his shirt. Against the bell. "*I know.*"

"*Then why*—"

"*Because it went past the lanterns*," said Lex Vey. "*Every one. It wanted them so much. And it went past.*" He stood up. "*Somebody should write that down.*"

His brother looked at him for a long moment.

Then he nodded.

---

They started walking.

Down the white road. Into the grey-gold. Away from the Veil.

Lex looked back once.

The door was there. Behind them. A tall dark gap in the grey. The edge of the world, drawn back on either side like curtains. And through it, very faint, a long way off, a smudge of other light. Thin. Pale. Morning light. The light of the Sundered Lands at dawn.

The world.

It looked very small.

---

The dead were at the edge.

Lex didn't see them at first. He thought they were dust. Drifts of it. Swirls. Lying against the foot of the Veil on either side of the road, where the grey met the plain.

Then one of them moved.

Not on the wind. There was no wind. It *moved*. Lifted. Rose up off the plain in a slow soft spiral of dust and stood there. In the air. A shape. Vague. Thin. A column of dust about the height of a man.

Bronze.

Not grey-gold like the rest. Bronze. A dull reddish-brown glitter in the dim light.

And beside it, another rose. White. Bone-white. And another. Copper. Green-tinged. And another. Coral. Pink. Pale.

Four.

---

"*Four came apart*," said Antho. Very quietly. Stopped on the road beside him. "*Against the edge. Coming through. The Ferryman said.*"

"*Bronze and bone and copper and coral*," said Lex.

"*The cousins*."

The four columns of dust stood at the foot of the Veil. Swaying. Very slightly. Not toward them. Not away. Just swaying. The way a candle-flame sways in a room with no draught.

They didn't have faces. They didn't have anything. Just dust. Coloured dust, in the shape of nothing, standing.

But Lex felt them look.

---

*[They're not dead*,] said Ember. Very faintly. *[Lex. They're not—they're not anything. They came apart. Against the edge. But there's no river here. There's no Ferryman. There's nowhere for them to*—]

It stopped.

*Nowhere to go*, Lex thought.

*[Nine hundred years*,] said Ember. *[Standing at the edge. Where they came apart. In pieces.]* A pause. *[Waiting for a door.]*

---

Antho stepped off the road.

Lex didn't stop him. He watched his brother walk across the grey-gold dust toward the four swaying columns at the foot of the Veil. Slowly. With his hands open at his sides. The way he walked toward a frightened animal. The way he'd walked toward a little grey girl in the Drowned Country, two years ago, and promised to come back.

He stopped in front of them.

The bronze one swayed. Toward him. A little.

"*I'm sorry*," said Antho Vey.

The dust didn't move.

"*I'm a Threshold Warden*," said Antho. "*I feel doors. Every kind.*" He looked at the four columns. "*I can feel where yours should be. All four. I can feel the shape of them. The place.*" His voice cracked. "*There's nothing there. There's no door. There's never been one. Not for nine hundred years.*"

The coral one leaned. Toward him. A little more.

"*I can't make one*," said Antho. "*Not yet. Not here. There's no room.*" He swallowed. "*But there will be. Someday. There's a pool in a green field. Full of room. It's still very little.*" He put his hand out. Open. Toward the four columns of dust. "*When it's bigger. When it's grown. I'll come back.*"

He didn't touch them. He held his hand out in the air. A foot away.

"*I promise*," said Antho.

---

The four columns stood for a long moment.

Then they sank. Slowly. Down. Back into the dust at the foot of the Veil. Bronze and bone and copper and coral. Lying in their drifts. Very still.

Antho came back to the road.

His face was white. He didn't say anything.

"*You promised the grey girl*," said Lex. Quietly. "*In the Drowned Country. You promised you'd come back.*"

"*I went back*," said Antho. "*With a lantern. And her name.*" He looked down the white road. Toward the palaces. "*I keep my promises. Eventually.*"

---

They walked.

Hours. Or what would have been hours. There were no hours here. The light didn't change. Lex couldn't tell how long they'd been walking except by how tired his legs were and how many times Pim shifted the basket on his back.

The road ran on. White. Straight. The plain on either side of it grey-gold. Flat. Empty.

And then not empty.

---

The first one was standing at the side of the road.

A man. Lex saw him from a long way off. A small dark shape at the edge of the white stone. Standing very still.

As they came closer, it wasn't dark.

It was gold.

---

He was a groom.

Lex knew it at once. From the clothes. A leather jerkin. Breeches. Boots. A cap. A horse-brush in one hand, held up. The other hand out in front of him, palm flat, as if he were reaching to stroke a horse's neck.

There was no horse.

He was gold. All of him. From his cap to his boots. Every fold of his jerkin. Every hair of his beard. The bristles of the horse-brush. A thin gold scar across the back of his reaching hand.

Standing at the side of the white road. Mid-reach.

And his eyes—

Lex stopped.

His eyes moved.

---

Slowly. So slowly. The way the hand of a clock moves. Off the place in front of him where a horse should have been. Across. To the road.

To Lex.

And stayed there.

---

"*The household*," said Antho. Very quietly.

Lex didn't answer.

He was looking past the groom. Further down the road. On both sides.

There was another one. A woman. In an apron. Gold. With a basket on her hip.

And another. A boy. Gold. Carrying a bucket.

And another.

And another.

And another.

---

They lined the road.

All the way. As far as he could see. On both sides. Down the long white line of it toward the far glittering palaces. Rows of them. Ranks. Standing at the edges of the white stone in the grey-gold dust. Shoulder to shoulder. Hundreds. Thousands.

Gold.

Cooks with ladles. Maids with brooms. Gardeners with trowels. Grooms with brushes. Nurses with babies in their arms, gold babies, gold blankets. Old men with sticks. Children. So many children. A girl with a skipping rope, frozen mid-skip, the rope a gold arc over her head.

All gold. All still. All standing at the side of the road where they'd been standing for nine hundred years.

All with their eyes moving.

All turning, slowly, so slowly, to look at four people walking down the middle of the white road.

---

And there was a sound.

Lex heard it as they came among the first of them. Faint. So faint it was barely there. Under the silence. Under the stillness.

A hum.

One note. Low. Soft. Like a finger run round the rim of a glass. Coming from every side. From every gold throat. Thousands of voices that couldn't speak, holding one note together.

He didn't know what it was saying.

He didn't think he wanted to.

---

*[Nine thousand*,] said Ember. Very faintly. *[Aurelian counted. In the note. Seraphine found it. Nine thousand who didn't choose.]*

Lex walked down the white road between them.

He didn't look at them. He tried not to. He looked at the road. At his boots. At the white stone.

He could feel them looking at him.

Every one.

---

"*Lex*."

Pim. Behind him. Very quietly.

Lex turned round.

The boy had stopped. In the middle of the road. With the basket on his back. Looking at something at the side of it.

A girl.

Small. Seven. Eight. In a grey dress too big for her, with the sleeves rolled up, and an apron, and a wooden spoon in her hand. A kitchen girl. Gold. Standing at the edge of the white stone with her free hand at her side and her mouth a little open. As if she'd been about to say something.

Her eyes were moving.

Not slowly. Not like the others.

*Fast*.

Darting. From Pim's face to the basket on his back to his face again. Back and forth. Back and forth. The way a child's eyes move when she's seen something she wants very badly and isn't allowed to have.

Pim stood in the middle of the road and looked at her.

She looked back.

---

"*Pim*," said Antho. "*We have to keep moving.*"

Pim didn't move.

"*Pim*. *Thirteen cups.*"

The boy looked at the gold girl for a long moment more.

Then he turned. And walked on. Down the road. Without a word.

But Lex saw him look back.

Twice.

And the gold girl's eyes followed him. All the way. Until the road bent—it didn't bend; the road never bent—until they were too far for Lex to see.

---

They didn't talk for a long time after that.

They walked. Down the white road. Between the gold. With the hum all round them, one note, low and soft and endless. With the light never changing and the dust never moving and the palaces glittering on the edge of the sky, no closer.

Lex walked with his hand on the bell.

It didn't ring.

He'd thought it might. Here. Of all places. In a country full of nine thousand people who'd been standing at the side of a road for nine hundred years, unable to speak, unable to die, unable to go home.

It didn't ring.

He didn't understand why.

*[Because they're not sad*,] said Ember. Very quietly. When he asked. *[They're not anything. They're kept.]* A pause. *[You have to have room to be sad, Lex. They haven't got any.]*

---

And then the light changed.

---

It had never changed. Not since they stepped out of the grey. Not in nine hundred years, Lex was sure of it.

It changed now.

Ahead. Down the road. Far down. Toward the palaces. A brightness. On the white stone. Faint at first. A glow. Gold. Warmer than the grey-gold. Brighter. *Moving*.

Coming toward them.

---

"*Off the road*," said Hask.

Lex looked at him.

The big man had stopped. In the middle of the white stone. His pick in his hand. His face, in the brightening light, gone the colour of ash.

"*Off*," said Silas Hask. "*Now. Into them. Into the gold. Get down.*"

"*What is it*?"

"*I know that light*," said Hask. "*I had her light in me for a season. I know what Crowned light feels like.*" His voice was very low. "*That's not her. That's bigger. That's hotter.*" He looked at the brightness coming down the road. "*That's him.*"

---

They went off the road.

Into the gold. Between the kept. Into the ranks of them at the side of the white stone. Lex pushed through. Between a gold cook and a gold groom. Between a gold nurse and a gold old man with a stick. Into the dust behind them. Down. Flat. In the grey-gold. With Antho beside him and Pim beside Antho and Hask, enormous, last, crouching behind a row of gold gardeners with their trowels raised.

"*Pim*," said Antho. Very quietly. "*Room. Small as you can. Just us.*"

The boy shut his eyes.

Lex felt it. A quiet. A space. Round the four of them, lying in the dust behind the gold. Not wide. A yard. Less. Pressed in close. A room so small it was barely a room at all.

But a room.

The gold round them didn't move. The kept didn't move. Their eyes moved. Slowly. Down. To the four people lying in the dust at their feet.

And the light came down the road.

---

It was heat first.

Lex felt it before he saw anything. Heat. Rolling down the road ahead of the light like the heat from an opened forge. Heavy. Golden. Dry. It poured over the gold ranks at the side of the road and over the dust and over the four of them lying in it and he felt it on the back of his neck like a hand.

Then the light.

Bright. So bright. In a country where nothing had been bright in nine hundred years. A gold glare, filling the road, filling the air, throwing the shadows of the kept out across the dust behind them, long and black, the first shadows in the Outer Court since the world was woven.

And then the footsteps.

---

Heavy. Slow. Unhurried.

*Thud*. *Thud*. *Thud*.

Coming down the white road. Toward them. Toward the Veil.

Lex lay in the dust behind a gold cook with a ladle and didn't breathe.

He saw him through her legs.

---

He was enormous.

That was all Lex could think. Enormous. Taller than any man. Taller than Hask. Broader than Brannoc. A great gold beard that came down to his chest and gold hair to his shoulders and skin like old ivory. A robe of gold and white that swept the stone behind him. A crown of gold leaves, crooked, on his head.

And his eyes.

Furnaces. White and gold and roaring. Like the open mouth of a forge. Like the sun, if the sun were angry. Lex couldn't look at them. He looked at the white stone instead. At the great gold-shod feet coming down it.

*Thud*. *Thud*.

Past the groom with the brush.

Past the nurse with the baby.

Past the cook with the ladle.

*Thud*.

And stopped.

---

Right there. On the road. A yard from the cook. From the four of them lying in the dust behind her.

Lex didn't move. He didn't breathe. He lay in Pim's tiny room with his face in the grey-gold dust and his hand on the bell and felt the heat of the Gilded King pour over him like the heat off a forge.

The King didn't look round.

He stood on the white road with his back to them. Looking ahead. Toward the Veil. Toward the tall dark door at the edge of the world, drawn back on either side like curtains, with a smudge of thin pale morning light beyond it.

And breathed in.

Long. Slow. Deep. Through his nose.

The way a man breathes in when he comes into a kitchen and smells bread.

---

"*Harrow*," said Oszric of the Crowned.

His voice was deep. Like a bell. Like a mountain. Warm.

"*I can smell it*," he said. To the road. To the air. To nobody. "*On the edge. On the door. Fresh.*" He breathed in again. "*Two of you. Hesper's line. Vey on the father's side.*" A pause. "*And something else. Something small. Something that makes rooms.*"

Lex felt Pim go rigid beside Antho.

The King didn't turn round.

"*And one of my sister's*," said Oszric. Softly. "*The hewer. Her champion. I can smell her on him still. Like smoke in a coat.*"

Behind the row of gold gardeners, Lex heard Hask stop breathing.

---

The Gilded King laughed.

Low. Warm. Delighted. The sound rolled out across the plain and the kept and the dust like a bell.

"*Oh*," he said. "*Oh, you opened it. Didn't you. With your own hands. With your own blood.*" He breathed in. Deep. "*Wide enough for a cart. Wide enough for a king.*" A pause. "*Thank you, cousins. I've been waiting so long.*"

He didn't turn round.

He didn't look for them. He didn't need to. He stood on the white road with his back to the four people lying in the dust behind his kept, and his furnace eyes on the door at the edge of the world.

"*Welcome home*," said Oszric. "*Make yourselves comfortable. Go wherever you like. Look at whatever you want.*" Warmly. Kindly. "*Rosie will be so pleased to have company.*"

And then—

"*I'll be at your house*," said the Gilded King.

---

He walked on.

*Thud*. *Thud*. *Thud*.

Down the white road. Toward the Veil. Toward the door. The heat going with him. The light going with him. The long black shadows of the kept swinging round across the dust as he passed, like the hands of nine thousand clocks.

Lex lifted his head.

He watched him go. A great gold shape on the white road. Getting smaller. Brighter. Walking toward the tall dark door in the grey.

He watched him reach it.

He watched him stop. Just for a moment. On the threshold. With the thin pale morning light of the world on his face.

And step through.

---

The light went with him.

All of it. All at once. The road went grey-gold again. The kept went dim. The shadows vanished. The heat drained out of the air like water out of a basin.

And the door at the edge of the world stood open. Tall. Dark. Wide.

Empty.

---

Nobody moved.

They lay in the dust behind the kept. The four of them. In Pim's tiny room. With the hum all round them, one note, low and soft and endless. With the gold eyes of a cook and a groom and a nurse and a row of gardeners looking down at them.

Lex lay with his face in the dust and his hand on the bell.

*I'll be at your house*.

Harrow. The birch wood. The stream. Two cottages. A blue door. A hornlight, gold now, on a sill.

His father in a bed under a boarded west window, with a Lantern burning beside him, three panes of glass and one of horn. Breathing. In. Catch. Out.

His mother beside him.

His wife, fogging every pane.

His daughter.

---

*[Lex*,] said Ember.

He didn't answer.

*[Lex*. *Lex, breathe.]*

He breathed.

It went all the way down. All the way. Past halfway. Past the catch. Into the room Mal had made in him, warm and quiet and round.

He breathed again.

Then he got up.

---

"*Lex*," said Antho. From the dust. Hoarsely. "*Lex, he's*—"

"*I know*."

"*He's going to*—"

"*I know where he's going*," said Lex Vey.

He stood in the grey-gold dust behind the kept with his bleeding hand clenched at his side, and looked back down the white road. At the door. At the thin pale smudge of the world beyond it.

Then he turned round.

And looked the other way. Down the road. Toward the palaces. Toward the glittering towers on the edge of the sky. Toward a forest of thorns, a mile off the road, north, that none of them could see yet.

"*Thirteen cups*," he said.

Antho looked at him.

"*Thirteen cups*," said Lex again. "*Thirty-nine days for one. Nineteen for two. If Pim holds the nights.*" He looked at his brother. "*He walks. He doesn't hurry. Roole says it's two hundred miles from the Veil to Harrow. More.*" His voice didn't shake. "*He'll walk it the way Caddoc climbs. Not hurrying. Not late.*"

"*Lex*—"

"*Wren's there*," said Lex. "*With the wind. Mam's there. With her no. Brannoc and Kestrel and Elowen and the Ashari.*" He swallowed. "*Father's there.*"

"*Lex*—"

"*We've got a crown to get*," said Lex. "*And then we've got to get it home before he gets to the door.*"

---

He stepped back onto the white road.

Between the kept. Past the cook with her ladle, whose gold eyes followed him. Onto the white stone.

And started walking.

West. Away from the door. Away from the world.

Toward the palaces.

He didn't look back.

Behind him, after a moment, he heard his brother's boots on the stone. And then Pim's, lighter. And then Hask's, heavy.

And all round them, from every side, from nine thousand gold throats, one note. Low. Soft. Endless.

He still didn't know what it was saying.

He was going to find out.
