# Lantern Night

Lark wouldn't go to sleep.

Wren tried. At dusk. In the cottage. With the fire banked and the candle in the east window and the baby in her cradle under the loft window with the horseshoe nail turning slowly on its red ribbon overhead.

"*Sleep*," said Wren.

"*No*," said Lark.

She was sitting up in the cradle. She'd learned to do that in the summer, in a barn above Thistledown, and she'd been doing it ever since, at every opportunity, with the expression of someone who has discovered that the world is much more interesting from higher up.

She was looking at the window.

At the soaped glass. At the faint gold glow coming through it from outside. From the clearing. From the ring of lanterns round the grave by the stream.

"*Sleep*, *love*," said Wren. "*It's late.*"

"*No*," said Lark.

And pulled herself up. On the edge of the cradle. On her square fists. And stood there. Swaying. With her face pressed to the soaped glass.

Looking at the lights.

---

Wren gave up.

She wrapped the baby in every blanket she owned and carried her down the ladder and out of the door and across the stepping stones in the dark, with the stream running black and loud under her feet.

To the clearing.

To the lanterns.

---

There were more of them now.

The twenty from the morning were still there. Still lit. Round the grave in the snow. Iron and tin and clay and a turnip. Hollis Penn had stayed to keep them filled. He was sitting on the cart's tailboard with a jug of oil and a taper, going round them one by one, every hour, his breath smoking in the cold.

And others.

People had come up the track all afternoon. From Ashbourne. From Thistledown. From the farms in the Vale that hadn't emptied when the King came down. In ones and twos. With lanterns.

They didn't say much. They came into the clearing in the dusk and set their lanterns down. In the snow. Round the grave. Along the stream. On the oak ramp of the blue-door cottage. In the windows.

And lit them.

*For Arion*. *From the Thistledown mill.*

*For Arion Vey*. *He fixed our roof. Once. Thirty years ago. I never paid him.*

*For the Warden-Architect*. *From the Ashbourne Forge. All of us.*

That one was a lantern Wren had never seen. Iron. Big. Clumsy. With twenty-three different hands' work in it, every rivet a different size. The students. Who'd gone east with their families when the King came down, and come back for Lantern Night. They'd made it in the long hall in the afternoon, Tilda told her. With Brannoc swearing. With Tilda holding the tongs in her one good hand.

---

The whole clearing was lit.

Wren stood at the edge of it with the baby on her chest and looked.

Hundreds. Gold. Small. In the snow. On the ramp. Along the stream, so the black water was full of little gold reflections, rippling. In every window of both cottages. Round the gold birch, which shone. Round the gold woodpile. Round the gold cart. Up the gold path through the birch wood, where somebody had set them all the way to the top of the hill, so you could see the line of them going up and up into the dark like stars on a string.

And in the middle, round a grey stone by the stream, the first twenty.

*ARION VEY*.

*He tried*.

---

Lark laughed.

Wren felt it. Through the blankets. The jerk. The breath. The small body going stiff with delight.

And then the laugh. Not loud. Not the wind laugh. Small. Astonished. Like the morning. Like someone who has never seen so many lights in one place and thinks they've all been lit for her.

"*It's your birthday*," said Wren. Very quietly. Into the dark fluff of hair. "*You're one. Did you know?*"

Lark didn't know. Lark was looking at the lights.

"*A year ago tonight*," said Wren, "*I was lying on a heap of black rubble at the bottom of a hill with my back against a stone. And the whole world was coming home round us. And your da was holding a Moth Queen off with fire. And your grandma was walking toward her with a lamp.*" Her voice cracked. "*And you came. In the middle of all of it. And screamed once. And stopped.*"

Lark reached out.

One square fist. Toward the nearest lantern. On the snow. A tin one. With a little gold flame behind its glass.

"*No*," said Wren. Catching the fist. "*Hot.*"

"*No*," agreed Lark. Seriously.

And reached for the next one.

---

Seren was by the grave.

She'd been there since dusk. Sitting on a stool Brannoc had brought out from the cottage. In her bare feet. In the blue dress. With a shawl round her shoulders and her hands in her lap.

With a bundle in her lap. Wrapped in a cloth. Square. Small.

She hadn't unwrapped it.

Wren went and sat down beside her. On the snow. With the baby.

Seren didn't look at her. She was looking at the grave. At the grey stone. At the twenty lanterns round it.

"*He'd have hated this*," she said.

Wren looked at her.

"*All of it*," said Seren. "*All these people. All these lanterns. All this fuss.*" A small crooked smile. "*He'd have stood at the window with his hands behind his back and said, it's very kind, but really, there's no need.*"

"*And then*?"

"*And then he'd have gone round every one*," said Seren Harrow. "*And read every tag. And remembered every name. And written them all down in a grey book in a column.*" Her voice cracked. "*And cried. Where nobody could see.*"

---

Old Mag was on the cart.

Beside Hollis Penn. On the tailboard. Wrapped in three shawls with her cat in her lap. She'd refused to go home at dusk. She'd said she was ninety-six and she'd go home when she liked.

She was looking at the baby.

"*There's that face*," she said. Cackling. Softly. "*The cheated-at-market face. She's got it now. Look.*"

Lark was frowning at a lantern that Hollis Penn had just refilled. As if she suspected it of something.

"*She's one*," said Wren.

"*She's one*," agreed Old Mag. "*And she's going to be impossible. You'll see.*" The one black eye glittered. "*She's going to say no to everyone. Teachers. Kings. Dragons. Her own grandchildren.*"

"*She already has*," said Wren. "*Said it to a king. Her first word.*"

Old Mag looked at her.

Then she laughed. So loud the cat jumped off her lap.

"*Good*," she said. "*Oh, good. That's my girl.*"

---

Kestrel came at midnight.

Up the track. Through the birch wood. On foot. Alone. With her red hair wild and full of snow and her boots worn through and her face burned red and raw by four days of wind.

With a small fire in her hand.

Not burning. Just lit. Held up. Like a lamp. Like a torch in a doorway.

She stopped at the edge of the clearing.

She looked at the lanterns. Hundreds of them. Gold. In the snow.

She looked at the grave.

She looked at Wren.

"*I'm late*," said Kestrel Varn.

"*You're not late*," said Wren.

"*I am*. *I know I am.*" Kestrel looked at the grave again. At the grey stone. "*I could feel it. On the road. Two nights ago. Something going out. Like a fire banked down to nothing.*" She swallowed. "*I walked faster. I didn't stop. I walked through the passes in the dark.*" Her voice broke. "*I'm late.*"

Wren stood up.

With the baby. And went across the snow. And put her arm round Kestrel.

"*You walked the whole way*," said Wren. "*Holding a fire up. So I'd remember.*"

"*You were supposed to see it*," said Kestrel. Into her shoulder. "*From the air. So you'd know the way home.*"

"*I saw it*," said Wren Ashdown. "*I looked back. Once. At the top. I saw it.*"

---

Kestrel set her fire down.

At the foot of the grave. In the snow. Not in a lantern. Just a flame. Small. Gold. Burning on nothing. The way unlatticed fire burns, that nobody ever taught to need a wick.

*For Arion*, said Kestrel. *From the walk.*

---

And then the west went white.

---

Wren saw it first.

She didn't know why. She was standing at the edge of the clearing with her arm round Kestrel and the baby on her chest, and she was facing west, and she saw it.

Over the birch wood. Over the gold path going up the hill with its string of lanterns. Over the Vale. Over the downs.

The horizon.

It had been dark. Midnight dark. Winter dark. Stars over it.

It went *pale*.

---

Not light. Not dawn. Not fire. Not gold.

*Pale*.

A line. Along the whole western edge of the sky. From north to south. As far as she could see. Low down. Where the downs met the stars. Going pale. Grey. Silver. The colour of a curtain seen from very far away, through a window, in the dark.

Moving.

---

"*What*—" said Kestrel.

Everyone in the clearing had turned.

Seren on her stool. Old Mag on the cart. Hollis Penn with his jug of oil. Brannoc and Hedda by the ramp. Tilda. The students. The people from Ashbourne and Thistledown and the farms.

All of them. Turned. Facing west.

Looking at the pale line on the edge of the sky.

---

It was falling.

Wren didn't know how she knew. It was two hundred miles off. Further. At the edge of the world. She couldn't see it. Not really. Just a paleness. A line.

But she saw it fall.

Slowly. The way a cloth falls when someone lets go of the corners. The way a curtain falls when the rope that holds it up is cut. Down. From the top. The pale line on the horizon sinking. Sinking. Drawing down out of the stars like a blind being lowered.

And as it went, behind it—

---

Gold.

Faint. So faint. A glow. Low. On the very edge of the world. Where the pale curtain had been. Warm. Dull. Like old brass. Like a coin at the bottom of a well.

Spreading.

Along the whole western sky. From north to south. As the curtain came down.

The grey country.

On the other side of nothing at all.

---

And then the wind.

---

It came from the west.

Wren felt it coming before it got there. She was a Mistwalker. She felt wind the way other people feel heat. A pressure. A movement. A great soft rolling shove of air, coming across two hundred miles of winter dark from the edge of the world.

It hit the clearing.

Not hard. Not a gale. *Soft*. Enormous and soft. Like a sigh. Like the breath a sleeper lets out when they finally lie down after standing a very long time.

It went through the birch wood. Through the clearing. Through the lanterns.

They didn't go out.

Not one. Wren watched them. Every flame in the clearing bending. Leaning. All together. East. Away from the wind. The way grass leans.

And then standing up again.

Not one went out.

---

And the wind smelled of something.

Dust. Old dust. The dust of a closed room. Of a hall nobody had walked through in nine hundred years.

And under it, faint—

*Roses*.

---

Wren's panel came up.

She hadn't called it. She never called it. She hadn't looked at it since the summer. It came up on its own, in front of her face, in the dark, in the clearing, in gold letters.

And everyone else's.

She saw them. All round the clearing. Every face lit. Gold. Panels hanging in the air in front of them. Seren's. Brannoc's. Old Mag's, in front of her one black eye, which she was squinting at as if it had insulted her.

The same words. In every one.

---

---

Nobody said anything.

The clearing was silent. Hundreds of people. Hundreds of lanterns. Standing in the snow in the dark at the end of the Vale, looking at gold words hanging in the air.

*A hand that wore a crown that said no*.

Wren looked at it.

*Elric*, she thought. *It was Elric. He wore it. Lex said he would. He said he'd worked it out*.

*He went first*.

---

"*He did it*," said Seren.

Very quietly. From her stool by the grave. With the bundle in her lap.

"*Elric*," she said. "*He wore her crown. And turned it. And went.*" She was looking at the panel in front of her face. At the gold words. "*Arion said he would. In the spring. At the bottom of the world.*" Her voice was very steady. "*He said, you'd go first. Wouldn't you.*"

"*And the Veil's gone*," said Brannoc. Hoarsely.

"*And the Veil's gone*," said Seren Harrow.

She looked west. At the gold glow on the edge of the sky.

"*And they're coming*," she said.

---

Nobody answered.

The panels faded. One by one. The gold words going out like lanterns snuffed. Until there was only the clearing again. The snow. The dark. The hundreds of small gold flames, standing up straight again after the wind.

And in the west, on the very edge of the world, that glow.

Gold. Faint. Warm.

Like metal in a crucible. Just before it pours.

---

Lark laughed.

Wren looked down.

Her daughter was looking west too. Over Wren's shoulder. Out of the blankets. At the gold glow on the edge of the sky.

And laughing.

Not frightened. Not crying. Laughing. The way she'd laughed at the gold dust in the cellar. At the Glass Queen in the stream. At the biggest man in the world, kneeling on the floor of her grandmother's cottage, holding out his hand.

"*Oh*, *love*," said Wren. "*Oh, it's not for you. It's not a lantern.*"

Lark laughed harder.

"*No*," said Lark.

---

She didn't sleep.

Wren didn't try to make her. She sat by the grave with Seren and the baby in her lap, in the snow, wrapped in every blanket, and Lark sat up in her arms and looked at the lights.

The lanterns in the clearing. The string of them going up the gold path. The gold glow on the edge of the world.

She didn't make a sound. She looked. With her grey-green eyes enormous. For hours.

At three in the morning she frowned at the west and said, very firmly, "*No.*"

At four she put her fist in her mouth.

At five she fell asleep. Suddenly. All at once. The way she did everything. Mid-frown. Heavy and warm against Wren's chest.

And at six—

---

Wren heard them before she saw them.

Wings. Over the birch wood. In the grey before dawn. The great slow beat of something enormous. And the quicker beat of something smaller beside it.

From the west.

She stood up.

---

They came down in the high pasture above the birch wood.

Wren didn't see them land. She was already running. Up the hill. Through the birches. The green ones. Bare. With snow in the forks. Not the gold path. Past it. Beside it. With the string of lanterns going up beside her on the left, gold, and the baby asleep on her chest.

She came out at the top of the wood.

And stopped.

---

Vharuun. In the snow. Enormous. Bronze and copper and old gold. With his great head down on his forelegs and his eyes shut and his breath steaming.

Ashka beside him. Smaller. Blue-grey. Curled.

And two figures.

Standing in the snow between the dragons. In the grey light. Barefoot. Both of them.

One thin. Tall. With long black hair and a wild beard. With a gold patch on the back of his left hand.

One big. Broad-shouldered. With dark curly hair cut badly and a grey-gold handprint on his left cheek.

And a basket on his back.

---

He turned round.

He saw her.

He didn't move. He stood in the snow in the high pasture in the grey before dawn and looked at her. At the woman with red hair standing at the top of the birch wood with a baby asleep on her chest.

His face—

Wren had seen it do a lot of things. In seven years. On a hundred rooftops. In the gold room at Avaloria. On the rubble of Duskhold with their daughter coming.

She'd never seen it like this.

Empty. That was the first thing. Something gone out of it. Something that had always been there, behind his eyes, the way a fire's always there at the back of a room.

And then full. All at once. Of something else.

---

She went to him.

Across the snow. Not running now. Walking. The tracker's walk. Fast and light and steady.

She stopped in front of him.

"*It's still her birthday*," said Wren Ashdown.

He looked at her.

"*She didn't go to sleep*," said Wren. "*Not till five. She sat up all night looking at the lanterns.*" Her voice cracked. "*She said no to the Veil. At three in the morning. Very firmly.*"

Lex made a sound.

"*It's still her birthday*," said Wren again. "*Till she wakes up. That's the rule. I just made it.*" She held out her arms. With the baby in them. "*You're not late.*"

---

He took her.

Slowly. Carefully. The way he'd taken her the first time, on the rubble, three minutes old. Both hands. Under her. Lifting her off Wren's chest.

She didn't wake.

She lay in his arms. Heavy. Warm. Asleep. With her fist curled against her mouth and her fluff of dark hair standing up like a bird's crest and her face screwed up in the frown she frowned even in her sleep.

He looked at her for a long time.

"*Happy birthday*," said Lex Vey.

Very quietly. So as not to wake her.

She didn't wake.

But her fist moved. In her sleep. Out from her mouth. Up. And found the front of his shirt. And closed round it.

And held on.

---

Antho came and stood beside them.

He didn't say anything. He looked at the baby. Then at Wren.

Then he put his arms round both of them. Round Lex and the baby. Round Wren. All three. In the snow. In the high pasture. In the grey before dawn.

And held on too.

---

"*He went first*," said Lex.

Wren looked at him.

"*Elric*," said Lex. Hoarsely. Not looking up from the baby. "*He wore the crown. He turned it. The net offered him everything. Every thread. First. Forever.*" He swallowed. "*And he said no. Himself. Out loud. Not me. Not ever.*"

"*I know*," said Wren. "*It was in the notice. A hand that wore a crown that said no.*"

"*And then he went*," said Lex. "*With the thread. With the Veil. Into the place where it was made.*" His voice was very strange. "*He said it felt like sitting down. After standing for a long time.*"

Wren didn't say anything.

"*He said beside*," said Lex. "*At the end. He said Father saved him a seat. Beside.*"

---

"*And Caddoc*," said Antho.

Wren looked at him.

"*Fell*," said Antho. "*With the thread. When it went. He was standing on it. At the edge.*" He looked west. At the gold glow on the edge of the sky, fading now, a little, as the grey came up in the east. "*He let us go first. Took the chains off. Said he wasn't cruel.*" A pause. "*Then he nodded at the crown. And stepped back.*"

"*Nodded*?"

"*Hesper's*, he said," said Antho Vey. "*And nodded. The way he nodded at her on the shingle.*"

---

They went down.

Through the birch wood. Lex with the baby. Wren beside him. Antho behind, with the basket now, because Lex couldn't carry both.

Down. Toward the clearing. Toward the stream. Toward the lanterns.

Lex stopped at the edge of the trees.

He looked at it.

At the clearing at Harrow. In the grey dawn. Full of small gold flames. In the snow. On the ramp. Along the stream. In every window. Round the gold birch. Up the gold path behind him. Hundreds of them.

At the people. Standing in the snow. Turning. Looking up at him.

At the grave by the stream. With twenty lanterns round it. And a small fire burning on nothing at its foot.

*ARION VEY*.

*He tried*.

---

"*They came*," said Lex.

"*They came*," said Wren. "*All afternoon. All night. From everywhere.*" She looked at the clearing. "*For Arion. From the Thistledown mill. He fixed our roof once. From the Kettle, he never paid his tab.*"

Lex didn't say anything.

He stood at the edge of the birch wood with his daughter asleep in his arms and looked at the lanterns for a long time.

*[They liked him*,] he thought. In the dry voice. In the shape.

*I know*.

*[Nobody ever knows till Lantern Night*.]

*I know now*, thought Lex.

---

Seren was standing up.

By the grave. Beside her stool. In the blue dress. In her bare feet. With a bundle in her arms.

She was looking at him.

He went down to her.

---

He stopped in front of her.

She looked at him. At his face. At the baby in his arms. At the basket on Antho's back.

At the empty place behind his eyes.

"*It's gone*," she said. Very quietly. "*Isn't it. All of it. You can't hear anything.*"

"*No*."

"*It went with him*," said his mother. "*In the lantern. On the boat. Gold.*" She swallowed. "*It said, tell him I'll be in the main text. Chapter forty.*"

"*I know*," said Lex. "*Antho told me.*"

Seren looked at him for a long time.

"*I said I'd wait*," she said. "*Till you were standing here.*"

She held up the bundle.

---

She unwrapped it.

Slowly. In the grey dawn. By the grave. With the lanterns all round in the snow and everyone in the clearing watching.

The cloth came off.

A lantern.

Small. Iron. Plain. With four panes of horn, scraped thin, so you could see light through them but nothing else. The way lanterns were made in the old days. Before glass. Before there was enough glass in the world for a Queen to live in.

He'd made it in the spring. At the little forge in the lean-to. Late. When he couldn't sleep. After Ysenne came through the window. After he made the first hornlight.

*In case I'm not back*.

---

Seren turned it over.

To look at the base. Where he always cut the word. Where only someone who picked it up would see it.

She looked at it.

And her face—

---

Wren saw it.

She'd seen Seren's face do a great many things. In the cellar. At the blue door. At the Hall of Lanterns. In the bed two nights ago, with her hand on a still chest.

She'd never seen this.

It didn't break. It didn't light. It *opened*. All of it. All at once. Like a door with the wind blowing through it. Like a window flung wide on the first warm morning of spring.

And then it crumpled.

Seren Harrow sat down in the snow by her husband's grave with the little iron lantern in her lap and put her face in her hands and laughed and cried at the same time, so hard she couldn't breathe.

---

Wren looked at the lantern.

At the base of it. Turned up. In Seren's lap.

Two words. Cut into the iron. In Lex's big clumsy careful hand. The hand of a man who'd learned his letters at a kitchen table at Harrow and then from nobody, and still made them the way you'd make a weld.

*TOO LATE*

---

She didn't understand.

Not at first. She stood in the snow with Lex beside her holding the baby and looked at the two words and didn't understand.

Then she did.

*Page nine*.

The letter. The forty-page one. That Seren had read eleven times, twelve, a hundred. That Wren had heard her read aloud by the fire, some nights, in the blue-door cottage, with Arion pretending to sleep.

*Don't ever leave a light on for me, Warden*.

*Too late*.

---

"*I read it*," said Lex. Very quietly. "*The letter. In the spring. Before I left. Mam was asleep and it was on the table.*" He looked at his mother. Sitting in the snow. "*I wasn't supposed to. I read page nine.*"

Seren lifted her face out of her hands.

"*I thought*," said Lex, "*if I wasn't back. If he—*" He stopped. "*I thought, somebody should leave a light on for him. On Lantern Night. In the west window. Facing the road.*" His voice cracked. "*Even though he told everyone not to. Even though he said there was no need.*"

"*Too late*," said Seren.

"*Too late*," said Lex Vey.

---

She held it up.

The little iron lantern. With its four horn panes. To him.

"*Light it*," she said.

---

He gave Wren the baby.

Carefully. Lark didn't wake. She went back onto Wren's chest with her fist still curled, and settled, and frowned.

Lex knelt down. In the snow. By the grave. By his mother.

He took the lantern from her.

He opened the little iron door on the side. There was a wick inside. Twisted cotton. Soaked in tallow. New. He'd put it in, in the spring, ready.

He held his hand over it.

And let the fire come up.

---

Small. Gold. The way it came every morning when he knelt at the hearth.

*Good morning*, said the fire.

He didn't hear it. Not in words. He never had. A warmth. A lift. A small bright gladness.

The wick caught.

And the lantern lit.

Soft. Gold. Blurred behind its four panes of horn. Like a lantern in a fog. Like a light left on in a mist.

---

He stood up.

He carried it to the blue-door cottage. Up the oak ramp. Through the door. Into the dim room. Past the table under the west window with the chipped blue cup on it. Past the empty bed with the curtain drawn back. Past the other Lantern, on the table beside the bed, three panes of glass and one of horn, still burning, because nobody had thought to put it out.

To the west window.

The boards were down. The glass was soaped. One pane wiped clear, where Wren had set her candle the morning before.

He set the lantern on the sill.

Beside the candle. Facing out. Facing west. Facing the road. Facing the gold path going up through the birch wood. Facing the grave by the stream. Facing the edge of the world, two hundred miles off, where the gold glow was fading as the sun came up behind him.

*TOO LATE*, said the base. Where only someone who picked it up would see.

---

He stood there a long time.

Wren came and stood beside him. With the baby.

Then Seren. In her blue dress. With snow on her bare feet.

Then Antho. With the basket on his back. Three pieces of iron and a crown in the straw.

They stood at the west window of the blue-door cottage, the four of them and the baby, and looked out.

At the clearing. At the lanterns. At the grave. At the people in the snow.

At the dawn coming up behind them, grey and then gold and then pale, the first light of the first day after the edge of the world came down.

---

"*It's a terrible lantern*," said Seren.

Lex looked at her.

"*It's terrible*," said his mother. Her face was wet. She was smiling. "*The horn's uneven. The hinge is crooked. You've put the door on the wrong side.*" She looked at it. "*He'd have said so. He'd have stood here with his hands behind his back and said, it's very kind, Lex, but the hinge.*"

"*And then*?"

"*And then he'd have cried*," said Seren Harrow. "*Where nobody could see.*"

---

Lark woke up.

All at once. The way she did everything. A jerk against Wren's chest. A breath. Her head lifting. Wobbling.

She looked round.

At the window. At the lantern on the sill. At the soft gold blur behind its horn.

At her father.

She looked at him for a long time. With her grey-green eyes. Frowning.

As if she were trying very hard to remember him.

And then she remembered.

He saw it. Wren saw him see it. The frown going out of her face. Not all at once. From the edges in. Like a field going from frost to grass.

And she reached for him.

Both fists. Out of the blankets. Opening and closing.

The way she reached for the nail.

---

He took her.

She grabbed his nose.

"*Ow*," said Lex Vey.

"*No*," said Lark. With enormous satisfaction.

And didn't let go.
