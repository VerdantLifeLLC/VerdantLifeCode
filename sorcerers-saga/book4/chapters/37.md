# The Last Turn

The crown settled on nothing, and the nothing became a man.

Lex watched it happen. Lying on his back on the black glass, bound in iron from his ankles to his shoulders, with his head turned toward the Key.

The strand of grey thread caught the crown in the air. Held it. Drew it down. Slowly. Toward the smudge above the Key. Toward the faint grey shape that had been bent over the black iron for three years like a man bent over a book.

And set it there.

Where a head would be.

And the smudge *changed*.

---

Not much. Not all at once. The grey gathering. Drawing in. Round the crown. The way mist gathers round a cold stone. The way breath gathers on a window.

A brow. Under the silver band. Lined. Furrowed.

A face. Long. Lean. With a scar running from the left temple down into a beard.

Eyes.

Storm-grey. Under heavy grey brows. Looking down at the Key. And then up. Across the black glass.

At Lex.

---

He wasn't solid. He'd never be solid again. He was grey. Grey smoke. Grey mist. The ghost of a man in a misted mirror. You could see the dark through him.

But he had a face.

For the first time in three years, Elric Thorne had a face.

And on it, a plain grey silver crown with seven blunt points. Sitting a little crooked. The way a crown sits on a man who's never worn one and doesn't know how.

"*Well*," said Elric. "*Doesn't suit me, does it.*"

---

Lex laughed.

He couldn't help it. It came out of him lying bound on the black glass at the bottom of the world with iron round his chest. Cracked. Wet. Sudden.

"*No*," he said. "*Not really.*"

"*I didn't think so*," said Elric. He looked up. Rolling his grey eyes toward the silver band on his brow, the way a man looks at a hat that's slipped. "*I always thought it would. When I was young. I used to lie awake in the village and imagine it.*" A dry rasp. "*A crown. On me. First.*"

"*And*?"

"*And it's cold*," said Elric Thorne. "*And plain. And it doesn't want to be worn.*" He looked at Lex. "*It's the best one I could have got.*"

---

The *no* poured out of it.

Lex felt it. All round. Out of the crown on Elric's grey brow, across the black glass, into the dark. Cold. Quiet. Patient. Not loud. A hand laid flat on a door.

*Not you. Not ever*.

Caddoc had stepped back.

Lex turned his head. The iron king was standing ten paces off. On the glass. With his square hands at his sides and his heavy face like a closed door.

He didn't come forward.

He couldn't. Lex could see it. The way Rhosyn couldn't climb the stair. Not because it hurt. Because it was right. The crown on Elric's brow was saying *no* to everyone who'd ever knelt at the edge of a pool and cupped their hands, and Caddoc of the Iron Crown had knelt there, on the last evening, nine hundred years ago, and drunk.

He stood on the glass and looked at the crown.

"*Hesper's*," he said.

"*Yes*," said Elric.

---

"*She gave it up*," said Caddoc. Very quietly. "*On the stone. At the edge of the pool. On the last evening. She put it down and poured back.*" His black eyes didn't leave the silver band. "*I watched her do it. I didn't understand.*"

"*Do you now*?"

The iron king didn't answer for a long time.

"*No*," he said. "*But I know she won.*"

---

"*Lad*," said Elric.

Lex looked at him.

The grey face. The storm-grey eyes. Looking down at him from above the Key, under the crown.

"*When I turn it*," said Elric, "*it's going to be bright. Very bright. Brighter than the lid lifting.*" A pause. "*Close your eyes.*"

"*Elric*—"

"*I know*."

"*You don't know what I was going to say*."

"*I know exactly what you were going to say*," said Elric Thorne. "*You were going to say, let me. You were going to say, I'm the Worldforger, I wove the net, I've refused a crown before, it should be me.*" A dry rasp. "*You were going to start working out how it could be you.*"

Lex didn't answer.

"*It can't*," said Elric. "*It mustn't. Whoever turns it last goes with what it unwrites. You've got a daughter who says no to kings.*" His grey face was very gentle. "*And it's her birthday.*"

---

"*And I'm already here*," said Elric. "*I'm already on it. I've been on it for three years.*" He looked down at the Key. At the shredded grey thread still wound round the black iron. "*I'm mostly thread. I'm nearly gone. Iron saw to that.*"

"*I'm sorry*," said Caddoc. From ten paces off.

"*I know you are*," said Elric. "*You keep saying.*"

---

"*And because I want to*," said Elric.

Lex looked up at him.

"*Your father knew*," said Elric Thorne. "*In the spring. In the chair. He said, you'd go first. Wouldn't you. Ahead. Before anyone.*" A pause. "*I said I hadn't thought of it like that. He said yes I had.*"

"*Had you*?"

"*Of course I had*," said Elric. "*Three years. Nothing else to do.*" His grey eyes were very bright. "*I've wanted to go first my whole life, lad. Through every door. Ahead of everyone. Ahead of him.*" He looked at the Key. "*And now I can. Into whatever's on the other side of this. Before the Veil. Before Iron. Before anyone.*"

He looked back at Lex.

"*Let me go first*," said Elric Thorne. "*Please. Just this once.*"

---

Lex lay on the black glass and looked at him.

At the grey face. At the scar. At the crooked crown.

He thought about a forge at Ashbourne. Smoke and an anvil and Brannoc swearing. A tall grey man with an ash staff and a raven on his shoulder ducking under the door, smiling. *You must be the smith*.

He thought about a year of lessons. Runes. Fire. *Mind the quench*.

He thought about the night at Caldris. The wards opening. His father carried out of a crystal in a sack.

He thought about the black glass. Two years ago. An old man with his hands on the Lockward's hilt. *Tell him*.

He thought about his father. In a chair. At the bottom of the world. With his hand held out and a thread wound round his fingers.

*You were first*.

"*Go on, then*," said Lex.

---

Elric smiled.

Lex had seen him smile a thousand times. In the forge. On the road. At the Spire. Charming. Avuncular. Warm. A teacher's smile. A friend's.

He'd never seen this one.

It was small. Crooked. A little embarrassed. The smile of a herring-fisher's son who's been told he can go first, and doesn't quite know what to do with it now he's been given it.

"*Thank you*," said Elric Thorne.

---

He put his hand on the Key.

Not a hand. A strand of grey thread. Thicker than the others. Gathering. Wrapping round the bow of the Key. The broad open loop of black iron sticking up out of the lid. The way a hand closes round a handle.

He looked at Lex one more time.

"*Close your eyes*," he said.

Lex didn't.

"*Stubborn*," said Elric. "*Like him.*"

And turned it.

---

The Key went *closed*.

It already was closed. It had been closed for three years. Barred. Turned closed in the Lock with a Lockward beside it.

It went *more* closed.

Lex felt it. Through the glass. Through his back. Through the iron round his chest. The Key turning. Past where it had stopped. Further. A quarter turn. A grinding. A deep groan in the lid of the world, like a great door being forced past its frame.

And the net *felt* it.

---

All of it. Every thread. Four million, one hundred and six thousand, three hundred and twelve. All at once.

Lex felt them feel it. The hum changing. Not louder. *Turning*. Like a field of barley when the wind first touches it. Like a crowd turning its head. Every thread in the world, every Stone, every knot, every Ceiling that wasn't there anymore and every level that was. Turning toward the centre. Toward the Knot. Toward the Key.

Toward the hand on it.

*It goes where the hand goes*, Elric had said. In the spring. *Whoever turns the Key, lad, the net offers them everything*.

---

And the net offered.

---

Lex saw it.

He'd seen it before. In a glass palace. In a pane. A man with his face and gold eyes and threads running into his hands and a crown of light on his brow.

He saw it now. Round Elric.

Light. Rising. Out of the black glass. Out of the dark. Out of the threads. Silver and gold. Pouring in from every direction. Round the grey shape above the Key. Winding round it. Into it. Making it bright. Making it *solid*.

A man.

Tall. Strong. Sixty. Storm-grey eyes. A scar from temple to beard. A grey cloak sewn with tiny brass storm-charms, catching the light. An ash staff, lightning-scarred, in his hand.

Elric. As he'd been. The day he walked into the Ashbourne forge.

And round his head, rising out of the threads, weaving itself, a circle. Of light. Bright. Gold. Thin. Like a halo in a church window.

Root.

The net's crown.

---

*[First*,] said the net.

Not in words. The net didn't have words. It had never had words. But Lex heard it anyway. Everyone at the bottom of the world heard it. The way you hear a bell in your teeth.

*[First. Of everything. Every thread. Every knot. Every child. Yours. Forever.]*

*[No one ahead. No one behind. No one to hold your coat.]*

*[First]*.

---

Elric looked at it.

At the crown of light. Rising round his head. Weaving itself. Bright. Gold. Warm. Everything he'd ever wanted. Everything he'd lain awake in a fishing village imagining. Everything he'd stolen a book for, and sixty threads for, and opened the wards of Caldris for.

He looked at it for a long time.

And the plain grey silver crown on his brow, under it, said—

---

*No*.

---

It didn't push. It didn't fight. It didn't shatter the crown of light or throw it off.

It just said no.

Quietly. Patiently. Steadily. The way it had said it on a chair in a dead tower for thirty-two years. The way it had said it in a box under twenty-two cottage floors. The way a girl of seventeen had said it on a shingle with her voice shaking.

*Not me. Not ever*.

And the crown of light, rising round Elric's head—

*Stopped*.

---

Hung there. In the air. Bright. Gold. Unfinished. A circle of light with a gap in it. Like a ring that doesn't quite close. Like the Hollow glyph. Like a door left open a crack.

Waiting.

For the hand to take it.

---

Elric laughed.

Lex heard it. The real laugh. Not the dry rasp. Not the thread-sound. A man's laugh. Full. Warm. Startled. The laugh of a teacher whose student has just said something unexpectedly clever.

"*Oh*," said Elric Thorne. "*Oh, that's very good. That's very good indeed.*"

He looked up at the crown of light. Hanging over his head. Bright. Unfinished. Waiting.

"*I wanted you*," he said. To it. To the net. To the four million threads, turned toward him like a field of barley. "*I wanted you my whole life. I'd have killed for you. I nearly did.*"

The crown of light hung there.

"*And now I've got her crown on*," said Elric. "*And it's saying no for me.*" He shook his head. Slowly. "*I don't even have to say it. It's saying it already. It's been saying it for nine hundred years.*"

He looked at Lex.

"*But I'd like to say it anyway*," said Elric Thorne. "*Just once. Out loud. Myself.*"

---

He looked up at the crown of light.

"*Not me*," said Elric.

His voice didn't shake.

"*Not ever*."

---

The crown of light came apart.

Gently. Like a dandelion. Like a breath on a cold window. The bright gold threads unweaving. Falling away. Drifting down round Elric like sparks from a forge. Back into the black glass. Back into the dark. Back into the net.

The net turned away.

Lex felt it. The four million threads, turning back. The hum settling. Not disappointed. Not angry. Nets don't feel things. Just—*settling*. The way water settles in a bucket when you stop carrying it.

Nobody's hand on it.

Still.

The crown in the road. Still lying there.

But nobody had picked it up.

---

And Elric went on turning the Key.

---

Past closed. All the way past. The grinding getting louder. The groan in the lid of the world getting deeper.

And then past *open*.

Lex felt that too. The place where the Key had stood two years ago, when Elric turned it on the longest night and the lid lifted and gold poured up out of the dark. The Key went past it. Past open. Further. Round.

Into the place where it was made.

---

Light.

Not gold. Not silver. Not grey.

*White*.

Pouring up out of the Lock. Out of the slot in the black glass where the Key stood. Up. Round the black iron. Round the grey shape of the man turning it. Up into the dark. Up into the threads.

So bright.

Lex didn't close his eyes. He'd been told to. He didn't. He lay on his back on the black glass and looked at it and it was like looking into the heart of a forge, into the white at the centre of the white, where iron stops being iron and becomes something else.

*Where it was made*, he thought.

*Aurelian made it here*. *Out of his own shadow. Ysmir said. The night he wove the world.*

*This is where things are made*.

---

And the last thread went slack.

---

He felt it. Through the glass. The one bright silver line running out from the edge of the lid into the dark. Taut as a harp-string. Humming the one high note it had been humming since the world was woven.

It stopped humming.

Not all at once. From the Knot. Outward. The note dying. Going flat. Going soft. The way a harp-string goes soft when someone loosens the peg. The silver going grey. Going pale. Going thin.

Going.

---

Unwriting.

From the Knot outward. Along the thread. Out into the dark. Under the Drowned Country. Under the Greyspine. Under Coldwater, where a forest of black iron spikes stood in the bottom of the Old Gallery round a silver line.

Lex felt the line go out of the iron. Felt the rings go loose. Felt them fall.

On. Under the Sundered Lands. Out. To the edge.

To the Veil.

---

He couldn't see it. He was at the bottom of the world.

He felt it anyway.

A curtain. Grey. Hanging from nowhere. Falling to nowhere. Stretched across the edge of everything for nine hundred years.

The thread that held it up, going slack.

And the curtain—

---

Caddoc was at the edge of the glass.

Lex didn't know when he'd moved. He'd been ten paces off. Standing. Looking at the crown.

He was at the edge now. Where the last thread came in. Where the iron rings had been, a moment ago, the last of them, black, closing round the silver.

The rings were gone. Falling. Into the dark. Ringing as they went. A long way down.

And the thread they'd been closed round was grey. And pale. And thin. And going.

Caddoc stood at the edge of the glass with one square hand on it.

On the thread. His road. The road he'd climbed for eight months, ring by ring, from the Court to the bottom of the world.

Unwriting under his hand.

---

He looked back.

Across the black glass. At the Key. At the white light pouring up out of the Lock. At the grey shape of a man in it, turning the Key, with a plain grey silver crown on his brow.

At Lex. Lying on his back. Bound in iron.

"*Smith*," said Caddoc of the Iron Crown.

Lex looked at him.

The iron king lifted his free hand. Slowly. And made a small gesture. A flick. The way you'd flick a fly off a sleeve.

And the chains round Lex let go.

---

All at once. Every coil. Every link. Unwinding off him. Falling away. Clattering on the black glass. Ringing.

He was free.

Across the glass, Antho's chains fell too. Lex heard them. Ringing. Clattering.

He sat up.

He looked at Caddoc.

"*Why*?" he said.

The iron king looked at him for a long moment.

"*I'm not cruel*," said Caddoc. "*I keep saying.*" A pause. "*Thorne said nobody who isn't cruel keeps saying it.*" The corner of his mouth twitched. "*I thought I'd better prove it.*"

---

The thread went.

Under his hand. The last of it. At the edge of the glass. Grey. Pale. Thin as a hair. Thinner than a thought.

Gone.

And Caddoc of the Iron Crown, standing at the edge of the black glass with his hand on nothing, looked across the lid of the world one more time.

At the crown on Elric's brow.

"*Hesper's*," he said.

And nodded.

Once.

The way he'd nodded at her on the shingle. Nine hundred years ago. In chains. As if she'd won a game he hadn't known they were playing.

And stepped back.

Off the edge.

Into the dark.

---

Lex didn't hear him fall.

There was nothing to hear. No cry. No shout. No crash. He stepped off the edge of the black glass into the dark where his road had been, and the dark took him the way the sea takes a stone.

Down.

Very far down.

Lex sat on the glass and looked at the place where he'd been. At the edge. At the dark.

*I'm not cruel. I'm only certain*.

*He kept his word*, Lex thought. *He gave it to his brother and he kept it. All the way down the thread. To the end.*

*And then the thread wasn't there anymore. And there was nothing left to keep it to*.

---

The white light was going.

Lex turned. Back to the Key.

The light pouring up out of the Lock was thinning. Dimming. Not going out. *Drawing in*. Back down into the slot in the black glass. The way water draws back down a drain.

And Elric was going with it.

---

He was still there. A man. Grey. Faint. With his hand—his strand of thread—on the bow of the Key. With the plain grey silver crown on his brow.

But he was thinning.

From the edges. From his feet. From his hands. The grey of him unwinding. The way the last thread had unwound. Strand by strand. Drawn down into the Key. Into the Lock. Into the white. Into the place where things are made.

*Whoever turns it last goes with what it unwrites*.

---

"*Elric*," said Lex.

He was on his knees. On the glass. He didn't know when he'd got there. On his knees by the Key with his hands out. Not touching. He couldn't. He'd go with it.

"*Lad*," said Elric.

He looked down at Lex. From the white. With his storm-grey eyes. With his face going faint.

"*It's all right*," he said. "*It doesn't hurt. Iron was right. It's very quick.*" He looked at his own hands. Thinning. Unwinding. "*It feels like*—"

He stopped.

He thought about it. Lex could see him think about it. The way Arion used to think about things. Carefully. From several angles.

"*It feels like sitting down*," said Elric Thorne. "*After standing for a very long time.*"

---

"*Tell Quill*—" he said.

Lex waited.

"*No*," said Elric. "*He's a raven. He won't care.*" A dry sound. A laugh. Fading. "*He never forgave me for the name.*"

"*He's got a nest of spoons*," said Lex. "*On the Forge chimney. He's very happy.*"

"*Good*," said Elric. "*Good. That's good.*" He was so faint now. Lex could barely see him. A grey shape. A crown. Two storm-grey eyes. "*Tell him I'm sorry about the name anyway.*"

"*I will*."

---

"*And your father*," said Elric.

His face was nearly gone. Just the eyes. Just the crown. Just the voice, thin as the last thread.

"*He's waiting*," said Elric Thorne. "*On the other side. He said. Where the light's on.*" The eyes were very bright. "*He saved me a seat.*"

"*In front*?" said Lex.

He didn't know why he said it. It came out of him. The old question. The old wound.

The grey eyes looked at him.

And crinkled. At the corners. The way they used to, in the Ashbourne forge, when Lex got a rune right.

"*Beside*," said Elric.

---

And went.

---

Not all at once. Not like a candle going out. The last of the grey drawn down into the Key, gently, the way the last of a tide draws back off a beach. The storm-grey eyes. The voice. The shape.

Gone.

Into the white. Into the place where things are made.

First.

---

And the crown fell.

Lex watched it. The plain grey silver circle, with nothing under it anymore. Hanging in the air for a moment above the Key. Where a head had been.

And dropping.

Onto the black glass. Beside the Lock. With a sound like a small bell struck once.

*Ting*.

Slightly off. A little crooked.

Like a bell with a bent clapper.

---

The Key broke.

Lex heard it. A crack. Sharp. Bright. Like ice. Like a pane in the first frost.

And the black iron in the Lock came apart.

Into three pieces. The way it had been made. Bow, bit and blade. Fitted together so cleanly you couldn't see the joins, nine hundred years ago, by a man with a little hammer.

Coming apart.

The blade first. Sliding up out of the slot in the glass. Then the bit. Then the bow. Three pieces of black iron, lying on the black glass beside the empty Lock.

And the Lock closed.

The slot in the glass. Where the Key had stood. *Healing*. Smoothing over. The way water closes over a dropped stone. Until there was nothing there at all but black glass, smooth and cold, a mile across, at the bottom of the world.

No Lock. No Key. No slot for a ward.

Done.

---

Silence.

---

Not silence. The threads. Four million, one hundred and six thousand, three hundred and eleven of them.

Humming.

One fewer than before.

The last one, gone.

---

Lex knelt on the black glass and didn't move.

Antho came. Across the glass. Barefoot. With the chains clattering off him behind. He knelt down beside Lex. And didn't say anything.

They knelt there together.

Two brothers. At the bottom of the world. In front of a smooth black glass where a Key had been. With three pieces of black iron and a plain grey silver crown lying on the glass in front of them.

And the Lockward. A few paces off. Where Caddoc had set it down. Gently. Grey. Notched like a mountain range. *STAY*.

---

"*He went first*," said Antho. Very quietly.

"*He went first*."

"*Father's waiting for him*."

"*Beside*," said Lex. "*He said beside.*"

Antho looked at him.

"*I don't know what it means*," said Lex. "*I think Father told him. On the way. When the Ferryman stopped.*" He looked at the smooth black glass. "*I think it was theirs. Not ours.*"

"*Then let them have it*," said Antho Vey.

---

Lex picked up the pieces.

He didn't know why. He reached out. On the black glass. And picked up the bow of the Key. The broad open loop of black iron. Cold. Heavy.

Then the bit. Notched. Toothed.

Then the blade.

He held them in his hands. Three pieces of black iron. A key that had been made to unweave the world, and had been turned for the last time, and had broken into the shape it was made in.

He was a smith.

He knew what you did with broken iron.

You kept it.

You kept it until you knew what it wanted to be next.

---

He put them in the basket. In the straw. Beside the last three Hush-cups.

Antho picked up the crown.

He held it in both his scarred hands. A plain grey silver circle. Seven blunt points. Cold.

"*It's quiet*," he said.

Lex looked at him.

"*The no*," said Antho. "*It's still there. I can feel it. Inside. Like before. Holding.*" He turned the crown in his hands. "*But it's quieter. It's not saying it to anyone now.*" A pause. "*It said it. To the net. The thing it was kept nine hundred years to say.*"

"*Is it done*?"

His brother looked at the crown for a long time.

"*No*," he said. "*I don't think so. I think it's resting.*" He put it in the basket. Gently. In the straw. Beside the three pieces of the Key. "*I think it might have one more no in it.*"

"*Yes*," said Antho.

"*And Mal's still little*."

"*Yes*," said his brother.

---

Lex stood up.

He picked up the basket. He put it on his back. It was heavy. Heavier than before. Three pieces of iron and a crown.

He looked at his brother.

"*It's her birthday*," he said.

Antho looked at him.

"*Lark*," said Lex. "*She's one. Tonight.*" He swallowed. "*I told her I'd try to be back.*"

His brother stood up too.

"*Then let's go and try*," said Antho Vey.

---

They walked back across the black glass.

To the soft dark mouth at the edge. To Mal's room. Shaking. Waiting.

At the lid of the world. A mile of smooth black glass. Cold. Empty. With a grey stone notched like a mountain range lying on it, where a square dark man had set it down gently because it deserved to be set down gently.

*STAY*, said the last rune. Near the hilt.

It had stayed. Three years. Longer than anyone thought it could.

He left it there.

Somebody should keep the Knot.

*[...I'm frightened...]*
