# What the Old Man Did

The first mirror-ward Arion Vey ever built was for a cat.

He'd been sixty-one. A junior Architect, with ink on his cuffs and opinions about load-bearing arches. There was a cat in the Spire library that kept getting into the copying-room and sitting on the wet manuscripts, and the Archivist had asked him, very politely, if he could do anything about it.

He'd built a ward on the copying-room door. Two mirrors. Facing each other. Small. Silver-backed. Angled very precisely, so that anything that came through the door saw itself, and then itself again, and again, and again, a corridor of cats going on forever into the glass.

The cat had stopped in the doorway. Stared. And sat down. And stayed there for an hour, looking at itself, utterly still, until the Archivist came and picked it up and carried it away.

*It's not a trap*, he'd told her. When she asked how it worked. *Not really. It just shows a thing what it is. Over and over. Until it can't look away.*

*That sounds like a trap*, said the Archivist.

*Most things that show you what you are*, he'd said, *are*.

---

He'd built a great many after that.

The Spire was full of them. Every door to the Root-chamber. Every window of the vaults. Every stair to the Warden-Architect's rooms. Two hundred years of mirror-wards, each one cleverer than the last, each one angled more precisely. Built against Gloam and shades and the reflection-spirits that drifted in sometimes off the Sundered Lands and tried to look out of the Spire's four thousand windows.

He'd never thought of them as weapons.

He'd thought of them as manners. A way of saying, at a door, *are you sure? Look at yourself first.*

---

"*Put the Lantern down on the floor*," he said. "*Glass side up. And then hand me my stick.*"

---

Antho didn't ask why.

That was the thing Arion would remember. Later. In the long nights, when he couldn't sleep and couldn't move and lay on his back under the west window counting his own breaths.

His son didn't ask.

His son, who had asked *why* about everything his whole life. Who had asked it of the Murmur and the Ferryman and the dark at the bottom of the world. Who had been mad for eight years and was still, even now, the most stubborn questioner Arion had ever met.

He didn't ask.

He picked up the Keeper's Lantern off the table. With a glass hand still reaching out of one of its panes. Long. Clear. Glittering. Groping at the air of the cottage like a blind thing.

And set it on the floor.

Glass side up.

---

The hand went up.

Of course it did. A hand coming out of a pane comes out at right angles to the pane. Arion had known it would. He'd known it the way he knew the angle of the sun on a floor. With the Lantern on its side on the flags, the pane facing the ceiling, the hand came out of it *upward*. Straight up. Toward the dark beams. Toward the black heat.

Toward nothing.

Away from the bed. Away from the baby. Away from all of them.

It stopped.

Hung there. A glass arm sticking up out of a Lantern on a cottage floor like a reed out of a pond. Fingers spread. Feeling the air.

"*Oh*," said Ysenne. From the pane. Puzzled. "*What did you*—"

"*Stick*," said Arion.

---

Antho put it in his hand.

His stick. Plain ash. Brannoc had cut it for him in the winter, when he first started needing the chair, and he'd used it to get from the chair to the bed and the bed to the chair and, once, the morning he fell, to reach for a cup he shouldn't have reached for.

It was heavy. It had never felt heavy before.

He held it across his knees.

"*Seren*," he said.

"*I'm here*."

"*Help me sit up*."

---

She didn't argue either.

She put her arm behind his back and lifted. And he felt his hip catch and his breath catch and something in his chest turn over like a fish. And then he was sitting. On the edge of the bed. With his bare feet on the cold flags and his wife's arm round him and his stick across his knees.

Looking down at the Lantern on the floor.

At the glass arm reaching up out of it.

At the face in the pane below it. Tiny. Pale. Turning. Looking up at him through her own arm.

"*Arion*," said the Glass Queen. "*What are you doing?*"

"*Talking to you*," said Arion Vey. "*Before I do anything else. It's manners.*"

---

The arm went still.

He watched it. He watched her. He'd been watching people for two hundred years, across tables, across courtrooms, across the long desk under the window in the Warden-Architect's study. He knew what it looked like when someone stopped to listen.

"*I knew your sister*," he said.

The face in the pane didn't move.

"*Not well*," he said. "*Not long. At the end.*" He shifted his grip on the stick. "*She sat on the floor of the Hall of Lanterns and told the Queen of Caldor the truest lie I ever heard. Every word true. Every word arranged.*" A faint smile. "*She came to my rooms in the Spire one night and offered me twenty years from her hands. And I took them. For an hour. And then I gave them back.*"

"*I know*," whispered Ysenne. "*Orrin told us. In the hall. When we woke.*"

"*She was very lonely*," said Arion. "*Your sister. Did you know?*"

The face in the pane didn't answer.

"*She was the loneliest person I've ever met*," said Arion. "*Two hundred and thirty-four years. Wardens and kings and Crowned. She was the loneliest of all of them. She told me so. In my study. With her lit eyes. She said, I've been cold my whole life, old man, right in the middle, and I've never known why.*"

"*She was always cold*," said Ysenne. Very quietly.

"*Yes*."

"*Even when we were girls*. *Even in summer.*" The tiny face tilted. "*She used to sit at the windows. Every window. Any window. Looking out.*" A pause. "*I used to sit at the mirrors. Looking in.*"

"*I know*," said Arion. "*You told Seren. In the Hall of Lanterns. Nine hundred years in a palace of mirrors. And she asked you how it had been.*"

The glass arm trembled. Just slightly. In the air.

"*And you said lonely*," said Arion Vey.

---

The cottage was very quiet.

Pim on the floor by the bed, cross-legged, with his eyes shut and his hands open and his face white, making room. Antho at the door, his hand flat on the wood. Hask behind him, enormous, with gold dust sliding off his shoulders onto the flags. Wren against the far wall with the baby on her chest and her gold-fingered hand flat on the baby's back.

Seren beside him. With her arm round him. Holding him up.

The Lantern on the floor. Blazing. Gold. With a glass arm sticking up out of it.

And the face in the pane. Looking up at him.

---

"*The child looked at you*," said Arion.

The face went still.

"*In the loft*," he said. "*In the window. The first night. Wren told us.*" He leaned forward. A little. On his stick. It hurt. "*And on the stepping stones. Just now. I heard it through the door. You said, you were the first one in nine hundred years.*"

"*She looked at me*," whispered the Glass Queen. "*Not at the glass. At me.*"

"*Why do you think that is*?"

The face didn't answer.

"*I'll tell you*," said Arion. "*Since I'm old and there isn't much time and I may as well be useful.*" He shifted his weight. His hip screamed. He ignored it. "*Everyone who looks into glass sees themselves. That's what glass is for. That's what you've always been. A thing people look into to see their own faces.*" He looked down at the tiny pale face in the pane. "*Nobody looks at the glass. Not ever. They look through it. Past it. At themselves.*"

The arm was very still.

"*She's five months old*," said Arion Vey. "*She doesn't know what she looks like. She's never seen herself. She hasn't got a self yet, not really. Not the kind you see in a mirror.*" A faint smile. "*So when she looks into glass, there's nobody there for her to see.*"

The face in the pane didn't move.

"*Except you*," said Arion.

---

He heard Seren's breath catch beside him.

He didn't look at her. He looked at the pane.

"*She's the only person in the world who can see you*," he said. "*Because she's the only person in the world who isn't looking for herself.*" He leaned on the stick. "*And you want to take her to your brother. So he can put a crown on her. And keep her.*"

"*Arion*—"

"*And then she'll be gold*," said Arion. Very gently. "*Five months old forever. With her eyes open. Looking at nothing.*" He tilted his head. "*Kept things can't see, Ysenne. You know that. You've walked past nine thousand of them. Their eyes move. They follow you. But they don't* see.*"

The arm trembled.

"*If he keeps her*," said Arion Vey, "*you'll never be looked at again.*"

---

Silence.

Total. The fire. The dust at the edge of Pim's room, hanging in the air, glittering. The baby breathing against Wren's chest.

And the glass arm, sticking up out of the Lantern on the floor. Trembling.

And then pulling back.

---

Slowly. An inch. Two. Down. Into the pane. The way a hand draws back from a stove.

"*I'm only looking*," whispered Ysenne. Very small. "*I told her. I only wanted to look.*"

"*I know*," said Arion.

"*Oz wants her*. *Not me. I don't want anything. I just look.*" The arm was half in the pane now. The fingers still out, spread. Still reaching. "*I'm supposed to bring him something. The child. Or the blood. Something. He sent me. I'm supposed to bring him back what he needs.*"

"*Then go home*," said Arion. "*Tell him you couldn't. Tell him the old man stopped you.*" A faint smile. "*He won't believe you. He'll think it's very funny. But tell him.*"

The fingers hung in the air above the pane.

"*I can't*," said Ysenne.

"*Why not*?"

"*Because he's my brother*," said the Glass Queen. "*And I've never once told him no.*"

---

And the arm came out again.

Fast. All at once. Not reaching up. *Bending*. At the elbow. At the wrist. The glass arm curling over like a striking snake, turning, the fingers swinging round toward the bed. Toward him. Toward the woman beside him.

Toward the baby, against the wall, behind them both.

Seren moved.

---

She didn't think. Arion knew she didn't. He'd known her for thirty-two years, twice, and she'd never once thought before she moved toward something that was trying to hurt someone she loved.

She let go of him.

And put out her hand.

And caught the glass wrist as it came round.

---

He heard it. A small sound. Sharp. Like a nail scraping a pane.

He saw her face.

Not pain. Harrow women don't show pain. *Surprise*. The face of a woman who has grabbed a bramble without looking and only now feels the thorns.

The glass fingers had closed on her palm.

Five of them. Long. Fine. Clear. Edged like broken glass, because they *were* glass, because that was all the Glass Queen was. Closed round Seren's square freckled hand.

And red was running into them.

---

Not dripping. *Running in*. Up. Into the glass. Into the fingers. The way water runs up into a dry cloth. Seren's blood, from the cuts in her palm, running up into the glass hand like dye into a lens.

The fingers went red.

Then the hand.

Then the wrist.

A glass arm filling with blood. Red. Dark. Swirling. Like wine poured slowly into a goblet.

And the face in the pane below it—

Arion saw it.

It closed its eyes.

The way you close your eyes when you taste something you've been hungry for for a very long time.

---

"*Oh*," whispered Ysenne. "*Oh. Hesper's.*"

---

He stood up.

He didn't know he was going to. He'd known for a month that he couldn't. Ilsa had told him. *Not without help. Not without the chair. Not with that hip.*

He stood up.

Off the edge of the bed. Onto the cold flags. With his bare feet and his bad hip and his wife's blood running up into a glass arm a yard from his face.

Something in his chest turned over. Hard. Like a fish on a line. Like a fist closing.

He didn't care.

He'd been careful for two hundred years. He'd been careful for so long. He'd sealed his sons in the dark and himself in crystal and written forty pages instead of saying a single word out loud, because it was careful, because it was right, because he was always right.

He wasn't careful now.

He took off his spectacles.

---

He'd put them on without thinking, when Antho put the Lantern down. The way he always did when something needed looking at. Folded on the table by the bed. He'd unfolded them and put them on.

He took them off now. In one hand. The left. And held them out.

Over the Lantern. Over the pane. Lens down.

Facing the glass.

---

A mirror-ward needs two mirrors.

That was the whole of it. That was all it had ever been, since the first one, since the cat. Two pieces of glass, facing. Each one showing the other. Each one showing the other showing it. Over and over. A corridor. Going on forever into the glass.

The Lantern's pane, facing up.

His spectacle lens, facing down.

---

And Ysenne looked.

---

He saw her look. In the pane. Her eyes snapping open. Her tiny pale face turning. Up. Toward the lens. Toward the thing hanging above her that shone.

Because she couldn't not look. Because she never had. Because she was the Glass Queen, and glass was a thing that showed you your face, and she'd sat in front of it for nine hundred years and looked.

And in the lens she saw herself.

And in the pane, under her, herself again. Seeing herself in the lens.

And in the lens, herself in the pane, seeing herself in the lens.

And again.

And again.

A corridor of Ysennes. Pale. White-haired. Grey-eyed. Glass-crowned. Going down into the pane and up into the lens, smaller and smaller, further and further, forever. Every one of them looking at the next one. Every one of them alone.

Nine hundred years of her own face.

---

The arm stopped.

In the air. Halfway out of the pane. Red to the elbow with Seren's blood. Its fingers still closed round Seren's hand.

Stopped.

"*Oh*," whispered the face in the pane. In the lens. In every pane and every lens, all the way down the corridor. A hundred thousand voices. All the same. All very small. "*Oh, no. No. Not this. Not again.*"

"*I'm sorry*," said Arion.

He was. He'd never meant anything more in his life.

"*Please*," said the Glass Queen. "*Please. Arion. Not this. I can't—I can't look away—I've been looking at it for nine hundred years, please, I'll go, I'll go home, I'll tell him no, please*—"

"*Let go of her hand*," said Arion Vey.

---

The fingers opened.

All five. All at once. The way a hand opens when it's been burned. Seren's hand came free. Red. Cut across the palm in five fine lines. Bleeding.

She didn't make a sound. She pulled her hand back against her chest and held it there with the other one and didn't make a sound.

The glass arm hung in the air above the Lantern. Red to the elbow. Swirling. Full.

And the face in the pane below it looked up into the lens above it, and down into the pane below it, and up, and down, and couldn't stop.

"*Please*," it whispered.

"*Antho*," said Arion.

---

His son was beside him.

Arion didn't know when he'd moved. He was just there. At his elbow. Holding him up. The way Seren had been.

"*The stick*," said Arion. "*I can't lift it. You'll have to help.*"

Antho didn't ask why.

He put his hand over Arion's on the stick. Young hand. Scarred. Burned across the knuckles from eight years of a hunger burning through it. Over old hand. Spotted. Thin. Shaking.

"*The pane*," said Arion.

"*It's my Lantern*," said Antho. Very quietly. "*You made it. Lex made it. In Malachi's valley. With HUSH twelve times and SHELTER.*" His voice didn't shake. "*It held the Murmur off for six years.*"

"*I know*."

"*I haven't needed it in three*," said Antho Vey.

And they brought the stick down together.

---

The pane broke.

Not loudly. A crack. Sharp. Bright. Like ice on a pond in the first frost, when a child stamps on it to see if it'll hold. The Lantern's pane, the one facing up, starring under the end of the ash stick. Splitting. Splintering into a hundred pieces.

And the corridor broke with it.

Every Ysenne. All the way down. All the way up. Gone. Shattered. A hundred thousand tiny pale faces flying apart into a hundred thousand splinters of light.

And the arm—

---

The arm came apart.

From the elbow down. Where it met the pane. Into shards. Clear. Glittering. Falling. Raining down onto the flags round the Lantern with a sound like a handful of coins thrown on a stone floor.

All except one.

The hand. The red one. The one that had held Seren's. Full of her blood. Swirling.

It didn't fall.

It *dropped*. Straight down. Into the broken pane. Through it. Into the Lantern. Into the flame. Into the light.

And was gone.

---

He heard her.

Very far away. Very small. Going down. Down through the glass. Through the light. Through every pane and every reflection between Harrow and the edge of the world, down, away, west, a thin high sound fading like a finger lifted off the rim of a glass.

Not a scream.

A laugh.

Small. Surprised. Delighted. A girl's laugh. In a room full of mirrors.

"*...I've got it...*" whispered Ysenne. Fading. "*...a little...enough...oh, Arion...I'm sorry...I've got it...*"

And then nothing.

---

He sat down.

He didn't decide to. His legs decided. They went out from under him the way they'd gone out from under him the morning he reached for the cup, and he sat down hard on the edge of the bed, and Antho's arm caught him, and Seren's.

The thing in his chest that had turned over like a fish closed like a fist.

He couldn't breathe.

---

Not further than halfway. Not even halfway. A quarter. Less. A breath that went in and caught and stopped and wouldn't go any further. Like a door jammed. Like a drawer that sticks.

He sat on the edge of the bed with his son on one side and his wife on the other and tried to breathe and couldn't.

"*Arion*," said Seren. Very close. Her hand on his face. Her cut hand. Wet. "*Arion. Breathe. Breathe. Look at me. Breathe.*"

He looked at her.

Her face. Square. Freckled. Flour on the cheek, because there was always flour on her cheek, even now, even here, even with her blood on her hand and gold dust drifting at the edge of a boy's silence.

*I turned back twice*, he thought. *And then I didn't.*

He breathed.

---

It went past the place where it stuck.

Not far. A little. Just past. Like a cart wheel jolting over a stone. And then out. And then in again. Shallow. Short. But in.

He sat on the edge of the bed and breathed. In. Catch. Out. In.

"*There*," said Seren. Her voice was breaking up. Like ice. "*There. There you are. There.*"

"*Here*," said Arion Vey.

He didn't know why he said it. It came out of him. A word. The word on the base of a little iron lantern across the stream, gold now, kept, on a sill.

"*Here*," he said again.

"*I know*," said his wife. "*I know you are.*"

---

They laid him down.

He felt them do it. Antho's arms under his shoulders. Seren's under his knees. Lifting him. Laying him back on the pillows. Pulling the blanket up. Very carefully. As if he were made of glass.

He supposed he was, now. A little.

He lay on his back under the boarded west window and looked at the dark beams and breathed. In. Catch. Out. And felt something he'd never felt before in two hundred and thirty-four years.

He felt *finished*.

Not dying. Not yet. He could tell. He'd sat by enough beds. He knew what it looked like from the outside, and he was beginning to learn what it felt like from inside. It wasn't that. Not tonight.

But finished. Like a ledger with the last column added. Like a letter with the last line written. Like a wall with the last stone set. Nothing else would go on it now. Nothing else would be built. It would stand, for a while. And then it wouldn't.

*It's like being very tired*, he'd told her. In his spectacles. In the dark. *At the end of a very good day.*

He'd been right. He was always right.

It was the worst thing about him.

---

"*Did she get it*?" he said. To the beams. Very quietly.

Nobody answered.

"*The blood*," said Arion. "*Seren's. In the hand. Did she get it?*"

"*Yes*," said Antho.

He was sitting on the floor by the bed. Arion could hear him. Could hear him breathing. Hear him picking up the shards of glass round the Lantern, one by one, very carefully, the way you'd pick up the pieces of something that mattered.

"*She got it*," said his son. "*The hand went into the Lantern. Into the light. West.*" A pause. "*She got enough.*"

"*Enough for what*?" said Wren. From the wall.

Nobody answered that either.

They didn't need to.

---

The dust stopped falling at dawn.

He heard it stop. Lying on his back with his eyes shut, breathing. He heard the soft dry hiss of it outside the boarded windows go quiet. Heard the wind change. Heard, far off, very faint, a voice like sand poured from a sack.

*Forgive me*.

Going west.

And then the birds.

---

Lex came across at sunrise.

Arion heard him. The stepping stones. The ramp. The door. And then his son in the doorway, stopping. With his hands black and his face grey and his eyes red-rimmed, as if he'd been holding black heat all night with no light at all.

Which he had.

He looked at the room.

At Pim, asleep on the floor by the bed, curled on his side like a cat, his small face white with exhaustion. At Hask, sitting against the wall with his eyes shut and gold dust round him on the flags in a ring, untouched. At Wren, on the floor beside Hask, with the baby asleep on her chest and her left hand flat on the baby's back, four fingers shining gold.

At Antho, cross-legged by the Lantern, with a little heap of glass shards in his lap.

At Seren, sitting on the edge of the bed, with her right hand wrapped in a clean strip of linen, already spotted red through. Holding his hand with her left.

At him.

"*Father*," said Lex.

"*Good morning*," said Arion Vey.

---

His son came and knelt by the bed.

He didn't say anything. He took his father's other hand. The one Seren wasn't holding. In both of his. And held it.

It was so cold. Arion could tell. He could see it in his son's face.

"*I stood up*," said Arion.

Lex looked at him.

"*Ilsa said I couldn't*," said his father. "*Without help. Without the chair. She was very clear about it.*" A faint smile. "*I stood up anyway. I was very careful about not being careful.*" He breathed. In. Catch. Out. "*I've been careful for two hundred years, Lex. I'd forgotten what it felt like not to be.*"

"*What did it feel like*?"

Arion thought about it.

"*Like coming round a corner*," he said. "*In the rain.*"

---

"*How long*?" said Lex.

He didn't say it to his father. He said it to the room. To his mother. To Antho. To nobody.

Nobody answered.

"*How long*," said Lex again. "*Ilsa will know. She'll come and put her hands on him and she'll know.*" His voice was shaking. "*Before I go. Before we cross. I need to know how long.*"

"*Before the year turns*," said Antho.

Lex looked at him.

"*The Ferryman said*," said his brother. Very quietly. Holding the glass shards in his lap. "*On the shingle. To Mam and me. Before the year turns.*" A pause. "*Before Lantern Night.*"

"*That's four months*."

"*Less*," said Arion.

They looked at him.

"*It's less*," he said. To the beams. "*I can feel it. I don't know how much less. I'm not the Ferryman.*" He breathed. "*But I was very old before tonight. And tonight I stood up.*"

---

Nobody said anything for a long time.

Seren held his hand. Lex held the other. The baby breathed against Wren's chest. Pim slept. Outside, the birds.

"*Go*," said Arion.

Lex's grip tightened.

"*Go*," said his father again. "*Both of you. Across. Through the crack. Get the crown. Bring it back.*" He turned his head on the pillow. Slowly. To look at his sons. One kneeling by the bed. One on the floor with a lap full of glass. "*She's got your mother's blood now. Ysenne. She'll take it to the Veil. To the crack. And the crack will open wider.*"

"*Wide enough for him*," said Antho.

"*Wide enough for him*," agreed Arion. "*Not yet. Not quite. But soon.*" He looked at Lex. "*He said he'd come to the door. For tea. Seren told me. Through the rose.*" A ghost of a smile. "*I'd like to be here when he does.*"

"*Father*—"

"*And I'd like you to be back by then*," said Arion Vey. "*With the crown. So when he knocks, there's someone at the door who can say no and mean it.*"

---

"*I won't go*," said Lex.

His father looked at him.

"*I won't*," said his son. "*Not now. Not like this. You can't*—you stood up. You stood up and you can't breathe and you might*—"

"*I might*," agreed Arion. "*I might tomorrow. I might next week. I might on Lantern Night with a gold king at the table.*" His voice was very gentle. "*I might whether you're here or not, Lex. That's the thing about it. You can't stop it by staying.*"

"*I can be here*."

"*You can*," said his father. "*And Caddoc comes down the thread. And the Gilded King turns the Key. And picks up the net. And keeps it.*" He looked at his son. "*And Lark grows up in a world where she's whatever the Stone says she is. Forever. With a Ceiling. With nothing new.*"

Lex didn't say anything.

"*Or he comes here*," said Arion. "*Through the crack. When it's wide enough. And puts a crown on her. And she's five months old for a thousand years.*"

His son's face broke.

"*Go*," said Arion Vey. "*Please. Go and get the crown. It's the only thing left that can say no.*" His hand tightened. As much as it could. Which wasn't much. "*And then come home. And tell me about it. I'll be at the window. Every evening. Looking west.*"

"*You can't get to the window*."

"*Then I'll be in the bed*," said his father. "*Looking at the boards over the window. Imagining west.*" A faint smile. "*I've got a very good imagination. I drew a map of the whole world from the inside with a pendulum.*"

---

Lex put his head down.

On the blanket. On his father's chest. The way a child puts his head down at the end of a long day.

Arion lifted his hand. Slowly. It took a long time. And put it on his son's hair. Dark. Curly. Cut badly, because he cut it himself, because he always had.

And left it there.

"*I'll try*," he said. "*To be here. I'll try very hard.*"

"*It's not enough*," said Lex. Into the blanket.

"*No*," said Arion Vey. "*It never is.*"

He stroked his son's hair. Very slowly. The way Seren used to, Ember said. Twenty-two years ago. In a cottage. In the night. When the boys were small and frightened of the noises in the walls.

"*But it's all anyone's got*," he said.

---

Antho mended the Lantern that afternoon.

Arion watched him. From the bed. Through the open curtain. His son at the table with the Keeper's Lantern in front of him and a little heap of glass shards and a strip of scraped horn that Lex had brought across from his own cottage.

He didn't throw the shards away. He laid them out on a cloth. Every one. Very carefully. In a pattern Arion couldn't see.

Then he fitted the horn into the empty frame where the pane had been.

He held it up. To the light from the open door.

Three panes of glass. Clear. And one of horn. Pale. Cloudy. You could see light through it. Nothing else.

"*It doesn't match*," said Antho.

"*No*," said Arion.

"*It'll never match*." His son turned the Lantern in his hands. Slowly. Looking at it. "*Three that show everything. One that shows nothing.*" A pause. "*I don't mind. I like it. It looks like it's been somewhere.*"

"*It has*," said Arion.

---

Antho put the Lantern down. On the table. By the bed. Where his father could see it.

And lit it.

The flame came up. Gold. Steady. Behind three panes of glass and one of horn. Throwing three clear squares of light on the walls of the cottage, and one soft gold blur.

"*Keep it*," said Antho.

Arion looked at him.

"*While we're gone*," said his son. "*Keep it lit. By the bed.*" He didn't look at his father. He looked at the Lantern. "*It held the dark off me for six years. In the cave. In the Keep. At the bottom of the world.*" His voice was very quiet. "*I thought it might hold it off you. For a few weeks. Till we're home.*"

Arion didn't say anything for a long time.

Then he reached out. Slowly. Across the blanket. And put his spotted hand flat on the iron of the Lantern. On the side with the horn. Warm.

"*Thank you*," he said.

"*It's yours*," said Antho Vey. "*It always was. You were the one who taught Lex the runes.*"

---

They left on the third day.

Arion didn't see them go. He couldn't get to the window. He heard it. The dragons in the clearing. Vharuun's great slow wingbeats. Ashka's quicker ones. Voices. Wren's, sharp, saying something to Lex that made him laugh, cracked and sudden. Seren's, from the doorway, saying something he couldn't hear.

Lark. Crying. Once. And stopping.

And then the wings. Lifting. Going.

West.

---

Seren came back in.

She shut the blue door behind her. And stood with her back against it. The way she stood at doors.

Then she came across the room. And sat down on the edge of the bed. With her bandaged hand in her lap.

"*They've gone*," she said.

"*I know*."

"*Lex said tell you*—" She stopped. "*He said tell you he'll be back before the year turns.*"

"*He doesn't make promises he can't keep either*," said Arion. "*Does he.*"

"*No*." Seren looked at the Lantern. At the three clear panes and the one of horn. "*He said tell you he'll try.*"

Arion closed his eyes.

"*Good*," he said. "*That's all anyone's got.*"

---

She lay down beside him.

On top of the blankets. With her bare feet cold and her head on the edge of his pillow and her bandaged hand on his chest. Over his heart. Counting.

In. Catch. Out.

In.

The Lantern burned on the table. Three clear squares of light on the walls. One soft blur.

"*Arion*," said Seren. Very quietly.

"*Mm*."

"*What you said to her*. *To Ysenne. About the baby.*" She was quiet. "*That Lark's the only one who can see her. Because she isn't looking for herself.*"

"*Mm*."

"*Was it true*?"

He thought about it. With his eyes shut. In the dark. Breathing.

"*I don't know*," he said at last. "*I think so. I hope so.*"

"*You don't know*?"

"*I'm two hundred and thirty-four*," said Arion Vey. "*I've just found out I don't know a great many things.*" A faint smile. "*It's very restful.*"

She laughed. Into his pillow. Cracked. Wet.

"*You were right, though*," she said. "*It worked. She let go.*"

"*I'm always right*," said her husband. "*It's the worst thing about me.*"

"*No*," said Seren Harrow. "*It's not.*"

He opened his eyes.

She was looking at him. In the Lantern-light. With her brown eyes very steady.

"*The worst thing about you*," said his wife, "*is that you stood up.*"

He didn't answer.

"*And it's the best thing too*," she said. "*I want that noted. In the grey book. Somewhere. Both.*"

---

He wrote it that night.

Under the first two lines. In the dark. By the light of the Lantern with one horn pane. In a hand so shaking it was barely writing at all.

*Arion Vey. Of the Spire. And of Harrow.*

*He turned back twice. And then he didn't.*

And under that—

*He stood up*.

---

He put the pencil back in the binding. And closed the book.

And lay back on the pillow with his wife asleep beside him and her hand on his chest, and the Lantern burning on the table, and the boards over the west window.

And imagined west.

He had a very good imagination.

He could see it all.
