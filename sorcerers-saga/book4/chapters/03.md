# The First Knot

He got home in the dark.

Vharuun came down in the high pasture above the birch wood at the end of a long cold day's flight, all the way from the Veil without stopping, and lay down on the wet grass and put his great head on his forelegs and closed his eyes and sent Lex a picture so clear and so sulky that Lex almost laughed out loud. A sheep. A whole flock of sheep. Running away over a hill. Uneaten.

"*Tomorrow*," Lex told him. "*I promise. Two. Three.*"

The eye opened. A slit of molten gold.

"*Four*," said Lex.

The eye closed.

He ran down the hill through the birches.

---

The window was lit.

He saw it from the top of the wood. A small gold square in the dark. The east window. The lantern. *HERE*.

And on the step below it, a shape.

He knew it before he was close enough to see. The way she sat. Knees drawn up. Something in her arms. Something else across her knees, catching the lantern-light. Long. Thin. Steel.

He came out of the trees into the clearing and stopped.

Wren looked up at him.

She was sitting on the doorstep in the cold with Lark asleep inside her coat, against her chest, wrapped in a shawl, only the dark fluff of her hair showing. And across her knees, unsheathed, one of her long knives. The leaf-blade one. The one he'd forged for her years ago with *TRUE* on the steel.

Her face was very white.

"*You're early*," she said.

"*Has anything come*?"

"*No*." She didn't move. "*Nothing. Nobody. It's been quiet all day.*" She looked at his face. "*What happened?*"

He sat down on the step beside her. Close. So their shoulders touched. And looked at his daughter's hair. At the dark fluff of it. At the small warm bulge of her under the shawl, rising and falling.

"*Something came through*," he said. "*At the crack. Dust. It said it was a steward. It said—*" He stopped.

"*Said what*?"

"*It said give my regards to the child*," said Lex.

Wren's hand tightened on the knife.

---

They sat there a long time.

He told her all of it. The gold. The boy. Nan Ashby with her basket. The heat that wouldn't sink in and the Hollow that had nowhere to put its hand. The crack, and the thorns, and the dust that hung in the air against the wind and bowed.

He told her about the Veil not pushing.

She didn't say anything to that. She just looked at him. For a long time. With her green eyes very steady. And then looked away, out across the dark clearing, at nothing.

"*You could have gone through*," she said.

"*I didn't*."

"*But you could have*."

"*Yes*."

She was quiet.

"*Don't*," said Wren. Very softly. "*Not without me. Not without telling me. Not ever.*"

"*I won't*."

"*Promise*."

"*I promise*."

She leaned her head on his shoulder. Lark stirred inside her coat. Made a small sound. A small creaking complaint. And settled again.

"*Your mam came over*," said Wren after a while. "*This afternoon. With a pie.*"

"*I know*."

"*How do you know*?"

"*Father told me*," said Lex. "*He said act surprised.*"

Wren laughed. It came out of her cracked and tired and sudden, like a laugh out of someone who hadn't expected to laugh again that day.

"*It was a good pie*," she said.

---

He took the rose across in the morning.

He'd kept it in a jug of water on the table overnight. It hadn't wilted. It hadn't dropped a petal. It stood in the jug in the grey light by the east window, dark red, velvet, as fresh as when he'd caught it, and the whole kitchen smelled faintly of summer.

Lark had looked at it for a long time from Wren's lap. Very seriously. With her square fists opening and closing.

"*No*," Wren had told her. "*It's got thorns. It's not for you.*"

"*Ah*," said Lark. Disapprovingly.

"*It's not for any of us*," said Lex. "*Ember thinks it's for Mam.*"

---

His mother was at the hearth again. Kneeling. Poking the fire. She looked round as the blue door opened.

She saw the rose.

And went white.

Not slowly. All at once. The colour went out of her face the way water goes out of a basin when you pull the plug. She knelt at the hearth with the stick in her hand and stared at the rose in Lex's hand and didn't move.

"*Where did you get that*?" she said.

"*At the Veil*."

"*Where at the Veil*?"

"*Growing*," said Lex. "*Out of the gold. Next to a boy who's been kept.*" He held it out. "*There was this on it.*"

He unfolded the grey silk. Carefully. On his palm.

*HES?*

---

His mother stood up.

Slowly. Like an old woman. Like someone much older than she was. She came across the room, past the long table, past his father in his chair at the west window, who had turned his head and was watching, very still.

She stopped in front of Lex.

She didn't take the rose. She looked at it. At the dark red of it. At the black stem. At the thorns.

Then at the scrap of grey silk.

She put out one finger. Square. Freckled. And touched the scratched letters. Very lightly. As if they might burn.

"*Rosie*," said Seren Vey.

---

"*Who's Rosie*?" said Lex.

His mother didn't answer.

She took the rose. At last. Out of his hand. By the stem. Below the thorns. She held it the way you'd hold something very old and very fragile, a bird's egg, a letter from the dead.

"*Thorns for the one who listened*," she said. Quietly. Not to him. To the rose. "*Mam sang it. At the end of summer. In the old verses. The ones she said I wasn't to sing to anyone, not ever, not even to my own babies.*" She swallowed. "*Gold for the eldest. Glass for the vain. Iron for the one who never laughed.*"

"*Thorns for the one who listened*," said his father. From the window. Very quietly.

His mother looked round at him.

"*She listened*," said Seren. "*To Hesper. When nobody else would. She listened and she said oh, Hes, are you sure. And she said oh.*" Her voice shook. "*And then she went down to the hall that night and drank anyway.*"

She looked back at the rose.

"*Rhosyn*," she said. "*That was her name. The fourth heir. Hesper used to call her Rosie.*" She touched the silk again. "*She had a garden. Roses on a white wall. Mam said they all died on the shingle. One petal at a time. While the Ferryman waited.*"

---

Nobody said anything.

The fire ticked. Outside, the stream ran. His father sat at the window with his hands on the arms of his chair.

"*She's asking for Hesper*," said Lex at last.

"*Yes*."

"*Hesper's been dead for nine hundred years*."

"*Rosie doesn't know that*," said his mother. "*Or she does, and she's asking anyway.*" She looked down at the rose in her hand. "*Or she's not asking for Hesper at all.*"

"*Then who*?"

His mother looked at him. With her Harrow eyes. Brown and steady and very tired.

"*Us*," said Seren Harrow. "*She's asking if anyone's still here. Anyone of Hesper's. She's asking if we lived.*" Her mouth twisted. "*She's knocking on the door, love. From the other side. And asking, very politely, if anybody's home.*"

---

"*It could be a trap*," said his father.

They both looked at him.

He hadn't moved. He sat in the wheeled chair at the west window with the blanket over his knees and his spotted hands on *STEADY* and *HOME*, and looked at the rose in his wife's hand with his grey eyes very sharp.

"*It could be*," he said again. "*A rose. For a Harrow woman. With her sister's name on it. Sent through a crack in the Veil by one of the four people who most want Harrow blood.*" His voice was very gentle. "*If I were Oszric, I'd send a rose.*"

Seren looked at him for a long moment.

"*You would*," she said. "*You'd send a rose. You'd write a letter forty pages long and leave it with your son for thirteen years.*" She turned the rose in her fingers. "*You're very clever, Warden. You're very good at traps.*"

"*I'm only saying*—"

"*I know what you're saying*." She came across the room. To the window. To his chair. And knelt down beside it, on the floor, and laid the rose across his knees. On the blanket. "*Smell it.*"

He looked at her.

"*Go on*," said his wife. "*Smell it.*"

He bent his white head. Slowly. Stiffly. Over the rose on his knees. And breathed in.

Lex saw his face change.

"*Summer*," said Arion Vey. Very quietly.

"*Summer*," said Seren. "*Nine hundred years in a grey country with no sun and no rain and nothing growing, and she grew that.*" She put her hand over his, on the blanket, beside the rose. "*You can't trap anyone with summer, love. It's not the kind of thing you can fake.*"

---

He left them like that.

His father with the rose on his knees. His mother kneeling beside the chair with her hand on his. Both of them looking at it. Not talking.

He went out of the blue door and down the ramp and across the stepping stones and up the hill to the high pasture, where a bronze dragon was waiting with an expression of infinite, ancient, sheep-related patience.

"*Four*," Lex promised him. "*At the Blightmarch. Hask's people will find them.*"

Vharuun opened his eyes.

*[He doesn't believe you*,] said Ember.

*He'll get them*.

*[He knows. He's just making a point.]* A pause. *[So am I. You're going down. To the Knot. Aren't you.]*

Lex didn't answer.

*[I don't like it*,] said Ember. *[Footnote three. Underlined twice.]*

---

The pool lay in the middle of the green.

It was smaller than he remembered. It always was. In his head it was enormous, the size of a lake, the size of the hole in the world it had been on Lantern Night when a starving Well came up out of the deep with its whole vast soft heart. But it was a pool. Round. Grey-rimmed. Thirty paces across. Perhaps less. Lying in the long new grass at the bottom of a shallow slope like water in a cupped hand.

Except it wasn't water.

You couldn't see into it. That was the first thing everyone noticed. It wasn't dark the way water is dark at night. It wasn't still the way a pond is still. It was *deep*. That was the only word. You looked into it and your eye just went down. And down. And kept going. And never found a bottom. Like looking up at a clear night sky, except down.

And very far down, so far you could only see them out of the corner of your eye, three small lights.

Gold. Warm. Curled together. Like three people sitting round a fire.

---

There was a stick on the rim.

Long. Plain. Dark. Like a shepherd's crook without the crook. Lying on the grey stone at the edge of the pool, beside a square black stone with two words cut into it in Brannoc's big careful hand.

*ISAURA KELL*
*She held on*

And standing beside the stick, between it and the pool, a man.

Big. Bigger than Brannoc, almost. Broad as a door. In a leather miner's jerkin with two long scars down the back of it where something had once torn through. A shaved head. A face like a slab of Coldwater coal. Big scarred hands, folded on top of the stick, which he held upright now, the end planted on the grass.

He was coughing.

Not badly. Not like last year. Not the long wet tearing cough that used to bend him double. A short dry one. A rattle. Twice. Then a breath. In. Slow. Careful. Further than halfway.

Then out.

"*Smith*," said Silas Hask.

---

"*Hask*."

"*You've come to look at it*."

"*I've come to go down*."

Hask looked at him for a long moment. With his ordinary brown eyes. Not gold anymore. Not moth-lit. Just brown. Just a man's.

"*Down*," he said.

"*Down*."

The big man didn't move out of the way. He stood between Lex and the pool with Kell's stick in his hands and his feet planted and looked at Lex the way a hewer looks at a seam he isn't sure of.

"*Why*?" he said.

Lex told him.

---

It didn't take long. Hask didn't need much. He'd been a Coldwater hewer for twenty-six years and a Delvebreaker for one and Maelis's champion for a season and he'd learned, somewhere in all that, to listen to the short version and fill in the rest himself.

When Lex had finished, he was quiet for a long time.

"*The King*," he said.

"*The King*."

"*She talked about him*." Hask looked down at the pool. "*Maelis. When I was hers. Not much. Once or twice. She said he was the eldest. She said he always went first.*" His big hands shifted on the stick. "*She said he kept things.*"

"*He does*."

"*I didn't know what she meant*," said Hask.

"*Neither did I*," said Lex. "*Until yesterday.*"

---

"*You'll want Ivy*," said Hask at last.

And turned his head. Toward the far side of the pool.

There was a girl there. Sitting on the grey rim. Small. Pale. Fourteen. Fifteen, now, perhaps. Thin as a stick. With a crutch lying on the stone beside her, and one foot, the twisted one, tucked up under her skirt, and the other dangling over the edge of the pool, over the dark, swinging a little.

She was talking.

Not loudly. Not to anyone Lex could see. Just talking. Steadily. Quietly. Looking down into the deep. The way Nan Ashby had talked to her son.

"*She does that every morning*," said Hask. "*Since before dawn. Tells it things.*" A thin smile. Barely there. "*What the sheep are doing. What Kit burned for breakfast. What Brigid said to Absalom about his helmet-lamp. It likes the stupid things best.*"

"*It always did*," said Lex.

He went round the rim.

---

Ivy Moss looked up as he came.

She had very light eyes. Grey, almost colourless, like water in a tin cup. They looked at him and through him and somewhere slightly behind him, the way they always did, as if she were listening to something just over his shoulder.

"*Good morning, Master Lex*," she said.

"*Good morning, Ivy*."

"*It knew you were coming*." She turned back to the pool. "*It said the smith's coming. It said the smith's got a question in him like a stone in a shoe.*"

"*It said that*?"

"*Not in words*." Ivy swung her foot over the deep. "*It doesn't really do words. Not yet. It does—*" she frowned, searching "*—shapes. Feelings. A sort of leaning. It leans toward things.*" She tilted her head. "*It's leaning toward you now. Can you feel it?*"

He could.

He'd felt it since he came over the rise. A soft pull. A tilt. Like the whole green slope was very slightly downhill toward him, the way a bed slopes toward someone heavy sitting on the edge of it.

*[...smith...]*

It came up out of the pool. Out of the ground. Out of everywhere. Small and soft and young. Not loud. Not in his ears. Somewhere behind them. The way the Murmur had once come to Antho, all those years, through a crack.

But not hungry now. Not broken.

Just glad.

*[...smith...you came...]*

"*Hello, Mal*," said Lex.

---

He knelt on the rim beside Ivy.

He told it. Not out loud. He didn't need to. He just thought it, looking down into the deep, and felt the pool listen. The way the fire listened in the morning. The way Vharuun listened, with his hand on the warm scales.

The Knot. The Key. The man who'd been holding it for three years at the bottom of the world.

*I need to go down*, he thought. *To where you used to be. Under the lid. Before you came up. The Knot was right there. Beside you.*

The pool was quiet for a long moment.

*[...down...]*

*Down*.

*[...where I was HUNGRY...]*

Lex felt it flinch. Very slightly. Like a child flinching from the memory of a burn.

*I know*, he thought. *I'm sorry. I wouldn't ask. Only there's someone down there. Holding something. And something's coming for him.*

*[...the grey one...]*

Lex went still.

*[...the grey one...wound round the cold thing...]* The voice was very small. *[...I used to feel him...when I was coming up...he was very tired...]* A pause. *[...he said sorry to me once...when he first went in...he said sorry I didn't know you were a person...]*

*He said that*?

*[...nobody ever...]* The pool tilted. Softly. *[...except the old man who sang...and the Warden...and the big one with the stick...and the grey one...]*

A long silence.

*[...all right, smith...]* said Mal. *[...I remember where I used to sit...I'll make you a room...]*

---

"*It's going to do it*," said Ivy Moss.

She'd gone very still. Her colourless eyes fixed on the pool. Her dangling foot stopped swinging.

"*Do what*?" said Hask. He'd come round the rim behind them. Kell's stick in his fist.

"*Make room*," said Ivy. "*Down. All the way down.*" She looked at Lex. "*It's going to make a room for you to go in. Like a bubble. Like a hand cupped round you. It's going to carry you down to where it used to be and hold you there.*" She swallowed. "*It says it'll bring you back. It promises. It says it's not hungry anymore and it won't keep you.*"

"*Do you believe it*?"

Ivy Moss looked at him with her strange light eyes.

"*It's never told me a lie*," she said. "*Not once. It doesn't know how. It's too young.*" She turned back to the pool. "*But it's frightened, Master Lex. It's frightened of the place it used to be. It's going to go there anyway. For you.*"

---

He took off his boots.

He didn't know why. It seemed right. His mother went barefoot everywhere now. She said she liked to feel the ground.

He took off his coat and folded it on the grass. He kept the bell. He kept Aurelian's little hammer, in its loop on his belt, where it always was. He kept the grey book, in its oilcloth, inside his shirt, because he'd promised.

He sat on the rim with his bare feet over the deep.

*[I want it noted*,] said Ember, *[that I am extremely, comprehensively, footnote-four-in-red-ink against this.]*

*Noted*.

*[If you die down there your wife will kill me*.]

*You're a voice in my head*.

*[She'll find a way*,] said Ember. *[She's very resourceful.]*

Lex almost smiled.

Then he slid off the rim.

---

It didn't feel like falling.

It felt like being held.

He went into the dark the way you go into warm water. Slowly. Softly. The deep closing over his feet, his knees, his chest, his head. Not wet. Not cold. Not anything. Just—there. All round him. Close. Like a hand closing round a moth. Like a cupped palm.

And then going down.

He couldn't tell how fast. He couldn't tell how far. There was nothing to see. Dark above. Dark below. Dark all round, soft and close and warm, and in the middle of it, a space. A small round room of nothing, just big enough for a man sitting with his knees drawn up.

Him.

He sat in it. In the dark. In the room Mal had made. And went down.

*[...don't be frightened...]* said Mal. Very close now. All round him. *[...I've got you...]*

*I'm not frightened*.

*[...I am...]* said Mal.

---

He felt it when they got there.

A change. In the dark. A pressure, all at once, from every side. Not pushing in. Pushing *against*. The way the air feels before a storm. The way a forge feels when you open the door and the heat comes out at you like a hand.

*Threads*.

He couldn't see them. He'd lost that. He'd lost it two years ago when he took his hand off the net, and he'd missed it every day since the way you'd miss a sense. Like going deaf. Like losing the taste of bread.

But he could feel them. Down here. So many of them. So thick. So close. Running past him in the dark on every side, above and below, in every direction, more of them than there were stars, all humming. All singing. All pulling in toward one point. Like the spokes of a wheel pulling in toward the hub.

And at the hub—

The room Mal had made opened.

Just a little. A crack. Like an eye.

And there was light.

---

He was on black glass.

He knew it. He'd knelt on it before. A disc of it, smooth and dark and cold, a mile across, at the bottom of the world. The lid. The great black seal Aurelian had put over the starving Well nine hundred years ago, which Lex had watched tilt and lift and crack open, with gold pouring up out of it, on the worst night of his life.

It was closed now. Healed. Smooth. The Well that had been under it was a pool in a green field three hundred miles away with a girl on a crutch telling it about sheep.

But the glass was still here. And in the middle of the glass—

The Key.

---

It stood up out of the black like a sword out of a stone.

A key. Taller than a man's forearm. Black iron. In three pieces, fitted together, so cleanly you couldn't see the joins unless you knew where to look. The bow at the top, a broad open loop. The bit in the middle, notched and toothed. The blade below, sunk into the glass. Into the Lock.

Turned closed.

And beside it, in the glass, a second slot. Narrow. Jagged. And in the slot, a second thing. Dark grey. Almost black. Notched like a mountain range. With a thin line of gold runes down its face.

The Lockward.

He'd been there when it was forged. At the Hollowmaw, with the whole world coming apart. Dorran Vask striking. Wren's breath in the fire. Hedda's runes. His father's Wellscript. His own blood in the quench. And a rune at the very end, near the hilt, that meant only one thing. *STAY*.

And round both of them—round the Key and the Lockward together, binding them, holding them, wound so tight you couldn't see the black iron through it in places—

A thread.

Grey.

---

He could see it.

He didn't know how. He couldn't see the others. The thousand thousand threads pulling in all round him, singing in the dark. He could feel them, but he couldn't see them.

He could see this one.

Thin. Grey. Not silver. Not gold. *Grey*. The grey of an old man's hair. The grey of a cloak sewn with tiny brass storm-charms. Wound round and round the Key and the Lockward, tight, tighter, a hundred times, a thousand, until it was more like a cocoon than a thread. A grey cocoon on a black iron key, at the bottom of the world, at the centre of everything.

And at the top of it, where the thread ran up out of the binding and away into the dark—

A shape.

Not a man. A suggestion of one. A long grey smudge on the air, like smoke. Like the ghost of a figure in a misted mirror. A head, maybe. Shoulders, maybe. Hunched. Bent over the thing it held.

Very still.

---

"*Elric*," said Lex.

The shape didn't move.

He stood on the black glass in his bare feet with the threads singing all round him and the room Mal had made still open at his back, a soft dark round mouth in the air, waiting.

"*Elric*," he said again. "*It's Lex.*"

Nothing.

He came closer. Across the glass. Slowly. The way you'd come up on a sleeping animal.

"*You called me*," he said. "*You said wake up. You said they're coming for the Key.*" He stopped. A few paces off. Close enough now to see the grey thread clearly. How fine it was. How tight. How in places, where it was wound thickest, it had worn thin. Frayed. Like an old rope on a well-winch. "*I'm awake. I'm here.*"

The shape didn't move.

And then it did.

---

It lifted its head.

That was all. The smudge on the air that might have been a head came up. Slowly. Very slowly. The way an old man lifts his head from a book when someone says his name, and has to come back from a long way off to remember where he is.

And looked at him.

There was no face. There was nothing that could have been a face. Just grey. Just smoke.

But it looked at him. He felt it.

"*Well*," said a voice.

---

It wasn't loud. It wasn't anything. It was a voice made of thread, if a thread could talk. Thin. Dry. Rasping. Like wind through a gap in a wall.

But Lex knew it.

He'd have known it anywhere. He'd heard it on the first day. In the Ashbourne forge, with the smoke and the anvil and Brannoc swearing, and a tall grey man with an ash staff and a raven on his shoulder ducking under the door, smiling, saying *you must be the smith*.

He'd heard it lie to him for a year.

He'd heard it, at the end, on the black glass, with old hands on the Lockward's hilt, say *tell him*.

"*Well*," said Elric Thorne. "*Took you long enough, lad.*"

---

Lex didn't know what to say.

He stood on the black glass and looked at the grey smudge on the air and all the things he'd thought about on the way down went out of his head. All the questions. All the careful words.

"*Three years*," he said.

"*Three years*," agreed Elric. "*Nearly. Give or take. It's hard to tell, down here. There's no sun. There's no dinner.*" A faint dry sound. A rasp. It might have been a laugh. "*I've been counting threads. It passes the time. There are four million, one hundred and six thousand and some. I keep losing count around the eight-hundred-thousandth.*"

"*You never said anything*."

"*There was nothing to say*." The grey smudge tilted. "*I was holding a key. That's all. It doesn't take conversation. It takes—*" A pause. "*—stubbornness. Mostly. And not thinking about it too much.*"

"*Arion tried to reach you. And Seraphine. The first winter.*"

The smudge went still.

"*I know*," said Elric. Very quietly. "*I heard them.*"

"*Then why didn't you*—"

"*Because I didn't know what to say to him, lad*," said Elric Thorne. "*Not then. I still don't.*" A long dry silence. "*I've had three years. And I still don't.*"

---

*[Lex*,] said Ember. Very small. *[Ask him. Ask him what's coming.]*

Lex swallowed.

"*You said they're coming for the Key*."

"*I did*."

"*Who*?"

The grey smudge didn't answer at once.

It turned. Slowly. Away from him. Toward the edge of the black glass. Toward the dark beyond, where the threads came pouring in from every direction, thousands upon thousands of them, singing.

"*Look*," said Elric. "*There. You can't see them, can you. Any of them. You gave that up.*" A pause. "*But you can see me. Because I'm one. And you can see that.*"

Lex looked.

And saw it.

---

One thread.

Among the thousands. Among the millions. Running away from the Key. From the Knot. From the black glass. Out into the dark, sideways, away, so long and so straight that it seemed to go on forever. Silver. Bright. Brighter than the others. Thicker. Tauter. Like the top string of a harp, tuned higher than all the rest.

He could see it. Like the grey one. Because something was on it.

Far away. So far it was only a speck. A pinprick. At the very edge of seeing.

A ring.

Black. Small. Round. Closed round the silver thread like a bead on a wire.

And beyond it, further, another. And beyond that another. And another. A line of them. Black rings on a silver thread. Strung out into the dark. Coming closer.

---

"*The last thread*," said Elric.

Lex didn't speak.

"*Aurelian tied it last*," said the voice of thread. "*When he'd finished. When the whole net was woven and the lid was on and the Nine were rowed out. He came down here, to the Knot, and he tied one more thread. Out to the edge. To hold it.*" A pause. "*The Veil, lad. That's what the Veil is. It's not a wall. It's not a curtain. It's a thread. The last one. Stretched out at the edge of the net to keep the world in and the grey out.*"

"*And it ties in here*."

"*Right here*." The smudge tilted toward the Key. "*Into the Knot. Into the middle. Same as every other thread.*" A dry rasp. "*Which means, if you were on the other end of it. Out at the edge. And you wanted to come in.*"

"*You could climb it*," said Lex.

"*You could climb it*," agreed Elric.

---

*Clink*.

Lex heard it.

Far off. So far it was barely a sound. Down the long silver line of the last thread, out in the dark, at the very edge of hearing. A small hard sharp sound. Like a link of chain closing. Like a hammer on cold iron. Like a smith, a very long way off, striking one blow.

*Clink*.

And a new ring. Black. Small. Round. Closing round the silver thread. A little closer than the last.

"*That's him*," said Elric. Quietly. "*Every few hours. I can feel it, every one. Like a stitch in my side.*" The grey smudge didn't move. "*He's ringing it as he comes. With iron. So it won't throw him off.*"

"*Who*?"

"*I don't know his name*," said Elric. "*I know what he feels like. Heavy. Patient. Cold. Like an anvil. Like a gaol door.*" A pause. "*He's very sure of himself. He's not in a hurry. He knows exactly how long it'll take and he's going to take exactly that long.*"

*Iron for the one who never laughed*.

"*Caddoc*," said Lex.

"*Is that his name*?" said Elric. "*Huh.*" A rasp. "*It suits him.*"

---

"*How long*?"

The grey smudge was quiet for a moment.

"*I've been working it out*," it said. "*I've had time. Six weeks of it. Counting rings. Counting the gaps.*" A pause. "*He's slow, at the edge. The thread goes down there, steep, through the grey. He's faster now. He'll be faster again when he's under the world, where it runs flat.*" The smudge tilted. "*The end of autumn, lad. Give or take a day. The night you light your lanterns.*"

"*Lantern Night*."

"*Lantern Night*," said Elric Thorne. "*He'll come down that thread and onto this glass. And he'll take the Lockward out, because I'm three years old in here and very tired and he's an anvil. And he'll take the Key.*"

---

"*And then*?"

"*And then he'll take it to the one who wants it*," said Elric. "*And they'll turn it.*"

"*Arion says the net would ask them what they wanted*."

The grey smudge went very still.

"*Does he*," said Elric. Very quietly.

"*He says nobody's holding it. Since I let go. He says it would take a hand.*" Lex swallowed. "*He says it would give them what they asked for.*"

A long silence.

"*He's right*," said Elric Thorne. "*He was always right. That was the worst thing about him.*" A dry rasp. Not a laugh. "*I've been reading, lad. Down here. Three years. There's nothing else to do. Aurelian left notes. He always left notes. In the knots. In the way the threads cross. You can read them if you've got long enough and nothing else to look at.*"

"*What do they say*?"

"*They say the Key unwrites*," said Elric. "*Whatever it's turned against. And they say that when it's turned in the Knot, the net feels it. All of it. Every thread. And the net, being a net, and having nobody's hand on it, does what nets do when someone picks up the end.*"

He paused.

"*It goes where the hand goes*," said Elric. "*Whoever turns the Key, lad, the net offers them everything. Every thread. Every knot. Every Ceiling. Every Stone. The Root. The whole of it.*" A rasp. "*You left a crown lying in the road. And they're coming to pick it up.*"

---

Lex couldn't breathe.

He stood on the black glass in his bare feet and thought of a boy in a field. Gold. Mid-step. His eyes moving.

He thought of a net with a crown on it. A gold crown. Every thread kept. Every level fixed. Every Ceiling back, and never, ever lifting. Every child Kindled into the same class as their mother and their grandmother and their grandmother's grandmother, forever, no growth, no change, nothing new.

Kept.

*[Lex*,] said Ember. *[Lex, breathe.]*

He breathed.

"*Can we stop him*?" he said. "*On the thread. Before he gets here.*"

"*I don't know*," said Elric. "*You can try. I'd try.*" A pause. "*He's very heavy, lad. And very sure.*"

"*Can we move the Key*?"

"*It's in the Lock. The Lockward's in it. I'm in it.*" The smudge didn't move. "*If you take the Lockward out to move the Key, the Key's free. If the Key's free, he can feel it. All the way down the thread.*" A rasp. "*And so can anyone else who wants it.*"

"*Then what*—"

"*Then there's only one other thing the Key can do*," said Elric Thorne.

---

He said it very quietly.

"*It could be turned against the last thread*. *Before he gets here. Unwrite it.*" A pause. "*No thread, no climbing. He'd just—drop. Wherever he was. Back into the grey. Or into nothing.*"

Lex stared at him.

"*Unwrite the Veil*," he said.

"*Unwrite the Veil*."

"*Then the Veil's gone*."

"*Then the Veil's gone*," said Elric. "*And the grey country's on the other side of nothing at all. And they walk in.*" The smudge tilted. "*On foot. Like anyone. No road to the Key. No crown in the road.*" A dry rasp. "*Just four very old, very hungry people, and a country full of gold, walking into your world across the Sundered Lands.*"

---

"*And whoever turns it*," said Lex slowly.

"*Gets offered the crown*," said Elric. "*Same as anyone. The net doesn't care why you're turning it. It just feels a hand.*"

"*So if I turned it*—"

"*You'd be Root again*," said Elric. "*Crowned. Whether you wanted it or not. With every thread in your hands and nobody to give it back to.*" A pause. "*And then you'd be the thing they're coming for, lad. You. Not the Key. You.*"

Lex didn't say anything.

"*There's a way round it*," said Elric. "*Aurelian thought of it. He always thought of everything, damn him. It's in the knots. Down there, by the Lockward. In the last crossing he made before he went up.*" The smudge was very still. "*Only a hand that's already refused a crown can turn it and stay itself.*"

---

*[Lex*,] said Ember. Very quietly. *[Lex. The crown. The seven-pointed one. The hollow in the box.]*

He knew.

He'd known since the word *crown*. He'd known since a cellar at Harrow, a box with a moth carved in its lid and crossed out, a hollow pressed into the oak at the bottom. A circle. Seven points.

*I've taken the crown where it belongs*.

"*Hesper's*," he said.

The grey smudge looked at him for a long moment.

"*Is that her name*?" said Elric. "*Aurelian didn't write it. He just wrote*—" a dry rasp "*—the tenth. The one that never touched the pool. The only crown that can say no.*" A pause. "*He wrote, if it must be turned again, let the hand wear the tenth crown.*"

"*It's gone*," said Lex. "*Wenna took it. My grandmother. My mother's mother. Thirty years ago. She left a letter. She said she'd taken it where it belongs.*"

"*Then ask your mother where it belongs*," said Elric Thorne.

---

The room behind him moved.

Lex felt it. The soft dark mouth Mal had made, open in the air at his back. Tilting. Leaning. A small anxious pull.

*[...smith...]* Very faint. Very small. *[...smith, I can't stay...it's the HUNGRY place...I can feel it in the glass...]*

*A moment*, thought Lex. *Just a moment more.*

*[...smith...]*

---

"*It's calling you*," said Elric. "*The little one. Isn't it.*"

"*Yes*."

"*Go on, then*." The grey smudge bent back over the Key. Over the Lockward. Over the thread wound round them, frayed and worn and tight. "*Don't keep it waiting. It's frightened of this place. It's right to be.*"

Lex didn't move.

"*Elric*."

"*Lad*."

"*Is there anything you want*?" He didn't know why he said it. It came out of him. "*Anything I can—bring. Or tell anyone. Or*—"

The smudge was still for a long time.

"*Quill*," said Elric at last. "*Is he*—"

"*He's at the Forge*," said Lex. "*On the chimney. He's got a nest. Made of spoons.*"

A sound. Dry. Rasping. Very soft.

"*He always liked spoons*," said Elric Thorne.

---

"*And your father*," said Elric.

Lex waited.

"*Is he*—" The voice stopped. Tried again. "*I felt something. A year ago. On the threads. Something very old going very fast. Like a candle burning down from both ends.*"

"*He's old*," said Lex. "*Older than anyone. He's got a year. Maybe two.*" He swallowed. "*He's happy. My mother's home. She doesn't remember him. She's courting him. She brought him a pie.*"

The grey smudge didn't move. Didn't speak. For so long that Lex thought it had gone back into its silence. Into its three years. Into its counting.

And then, very quietly—

"*Don't tell him*," said Elric. "*That I asked.*"

"*He asked about you*."

The smudge went very still.

"*He asked how you sounded*," said Lex. "*He asked me to tell him exactly. The words. How you said them.*"

A long, long silence.

"*Then bring him*," said Elric Thorne. Very quietly. Not looking up. Bent over the Key, over the Lockward, over his own frayed grey thread. "*Before he goes. If he'll come. If the little one can carry him.*" A pause. "*I'd like to say it to his face. Whatever it is. I've had three years. I'll have worked it out by then.*"

---

*[...SMITH...]*

The room pulled. Hard. Lex felt it take hold of him. Soft. Warm. Frightened. Like a hand round a moth.

"*Go*," said Elric.

Lex went.

---

He felt the dark close round him. Felt the black glass go out from under his bare feet. Felt himself lift. Rising. Fast now. Faster than coming down. Mal pulling him up and away from the hungry place as fast as it could go.

And behind him, below him, far down at the bottom of the world, at the centre of everything, on a long silver thread that ran out into the dark—

*Clink*.

A ring of black iron. Closing. One more.

He counted, all the way up. He couldn't help it.

By Lantern Night, give or take a day, there would be a great many more.
