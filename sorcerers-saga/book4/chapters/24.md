# The Glass Palace

Antho came back alone.

Lex saw him from a long way off. Down the white road. Between the kept. A tall thin shape in the grey-gold dim, walking. Not running. Walking. Slowly. The way a man walks when there's no longer any point in running.

Alone.

Lex was on his feet before Hask could say anything.

---

"*She took him*," said Antho.

He stood in the middle of the road in front of them with his hands hanging at his sides. His face was grey. His eyes were very dark. There was a red mark round his right wrist, Lex saw, as if something had gripped it very hard and then torn away.

"*Who*—"

"*Ysenne*." Antho's voice was very flat. "*Out of a basket. Out of a washerwoman's basket of gold linen. A hand.*" He looked down at his own hands. "*I had his arm. I had it. And it slipped.*"

Lex didn't say anything.

"*He'd found the girl*," said Antho. "*The kitchen girl. Ilda. She'd been walking after him. For four days. A finger's width every hour.*" His voice cracked. "*He made room in her. He made her talk. She said mam. She cried.*" He swallowed. "*And it took everything he had. And he was empty. And she was waiting.*"

---

*[Lex*,] said Ember.

*I know*.

*[The glass palace. A mile south of the road. Full of faces.]* A pause. *[She said come and see my house.]*

Lex looked south.

There it was. Rising out of the grey-gold dust like a frozen waterfall turned upside down. Towers. Spires. Bridges. Clear as water. Glittering. Every pane catching every other pane.

Every pane with a face in it.

They weren't looking at the road anymore.

They were looking at him.

---

"*Cups*," said Lex.

Antho blinked at him.

"*How many*?" said Lex.

"*Eleven*. *I've had one since I went after him. I'll need another soon.*" Antho looked at the basket on the ground. Where Pim had left it. With a folded drawing beside it, weighed down with a stone. "*Sixteen days. Fifteen. If someone holds the nights.*"

"*Nobody can hold the nights*," said Lex. "*Not without Pim.*"

"*I can hold the edges*. *A little.*"

"*Then fifteen*," said Lex. "*Fewer.*" He bent down. Picked up the basket. Slung it over his own shoulder. It was heavy. Heavy with nothing. "*We'll do the sums later.*"

"*Lex*—"

"*She's got Pim*," said Lex Vey.

He started walking.

South. Off the road. Into the dust. Toward the glass palace.

He didn't look back to see if they were following. He didn't need to. He heard Antho's boots. And Hask's.

---

It took an hour to reach.

It looked closer than it was. Everything in the Court did. There was nothing to measure by. No trees. No hills. Just flat grey-gold dust, and the palace rising out of it, and the faces.

The faces watched them come.

Every pane. Every tower. Thousands of them. All the same face. Pale. Sharp-cheeked. Long-necked. White hair. Grey eyes. Turning as they walked. Following.

Not smiling.

Lex had seen Ysenne smile. In the Hall of Lanterns. In the window at Harrow. In the stream.

She wasn't smiling now.

She looked—

*[Curious*,] said Ember. *[She looks curious, Lex. Like a cat watching a mouse she isn't sure she wants to eat.]*

---

There was a door.

At the foot of the palace. In the middle. Tall. Glass. Clear as water. Standing open.

Through it, Lex could see the inside.

Light.

That was all. Light. Bouncing. Glass walls and glass floors and glass ceilings, every surface clear and every surface shining, the dim grey-gold light of the Court coming in through a thousand panes and bouncing off a thousand more. Endless. Dizzying. A corridor of light going on into the palace forever.

And in every surface, the face.

---

They stopped at the door.

"*It's a trap*," said Hask.

"*It's a trap*," agreed Antho.

"*She's got Pim*," said Lex.

Nobody said anything else.

---

They went in.

---

The light hit Lex like a wave.

He'd known it would be bright. He hadn't known it would be like this. He stepped through the glass door and the light came at him from every side. From the walls. The floor. The ceiling. From the face in every pane, which was looking at him. From his own face in every pane, which was looking back.

He couldn't tell which way was forward.

He couldn't tell which way was up.

He stood on a glass floor that showed him his own face looking up from below, in a glass corridor that showed him his own back going on ahead forever, with glass walls that showed him Antho and Hask behind him, and Antho and Hask in front of him, and Antho and Hask to the left and to the right and above, a thousand Anthos and a thousand Hasks, all turning their heads, all looking for the way.

And among them, everywhere, the Glass Queen.

Watching.

---

"*Smith*," she said.

From everywhere. From every pane. Small and far and singing, like a wet finger round the rim of a glass. A thousand voices. All the same voice. All speaking at once.

"*You came*," said Ysenne. "*I thought you would. Arion would have. He came down to the bottom of the world to say sorry to a thread.*"

"*Give him back*," said Lex.

"*He's safe*," said Ysenne. "*He's asleep. He's very tired. He gave too much to the little cook.*" The thousand faces tilted. All together. "*I put him in my best room. With nothing in it. He seemed to like that.*"

"*Give him back*."

"*Come and get him*," said the Glass Queen. "*If you can find him.*"

---

And the palace showed him.

---

He didn't know it was happening at first. He was looking at the glass wall in front of him. At his own face in it. Grey. Tired. With the grey-gold handprint on his cheek and his dark hair cut badly and his eyes red-rimmed.

And then it wasn't his face.

It was his face. But older. A little. Not much. And *different*.

His eyes were gold.

---

Not ember-gold. Not the gold they went when he used the Root, before he gave it away. *Gold*. All the way through. Iris and white and pupil. Bright. Steady. Like two coins.

He was standing in a room he didn't know. Tall. White. Full of light. And round him, in the air, all round him, so thick he could barely see through it—

Threads.

He could see them. In the glass. Thousands. Millions. Silver and gold. Running out from him in every direction like the spokes of a wheel. Running into his hands. Into his chest. Into his eyes.

The net.

All of it. In his hands. Every thread. Every knot. Every Stone. Every child.

Him in the middle. Holding it.

And on his head—

---

"*It suits you*," said Ysenne. Softly. From everywhere. "*Doesn't it.*"

Lex couldn't speak.

On the head of the man in the glass was a crown.

Not gold leaves. Not glass. Not iron or thorns or silver wire. Light. A circle of it. Thin. Bright. Made of the same threads that ran into his hands. Woven. Round his brow. Like a halo in a church window.

Root.

---

"*That's what the Key would give you*," said the Glass Queen. "*If you turned it. You. Instead of Oz. Instead of the thread in the knot.*" The faces in the glass were very still. "*The net. All of it. Back in your hands. The way it was for the little man with the hammer. The way it was for your father.*"

"*I gave it away*," said Lex.

"*You did*. *And look what happened.*" A sigh. Like breath on a cold pane. "*Nobody holding it. A crown in the road. And Oz coming to pick it up.*"

The man in the glass with gold eyes lifted his hand. Slowly. And the threads moved with it. All of them. Every thread in the world, bending to follow his hand like grass bends to follow the wind.

"*You could stop him*," said Ysenne. "*With that. You could stop him with a thought. Undo his gold. Undo his gilt. Every grandmother in every chair. Every boy in every field.*" A pause. "*You could unring every one of Cad's rings. You could close the door at the edge of the world with a word.*"

Lex stared at the man in the glass.

"*And your father*," said the Glass Queen.

---

He went cold.

"*You know he's dying*," said Ysenne. Very gently. "*You know there isn't long. The Ferryman said before the year turns.*" The faces in the glass looked at him. "*Do you know how long the net held him? The first time? Two hundred years. Forty-five years old, for two hundred years. Because he had the Root. Because the net held him.*"

"*Stop*."

"*It could hold him again*," said the Glass Queen. "*If you had it. You could put your hand on the net and find his thread and hold it. The way Aurelian held the Hollow. The way your father held the breaches from inside the crystal.*" A pause. "*He wouldn't have to go. Not this year. Not next. Not for a hundred.*"

The man in the glass with gold eyes was smiling. Lex's smile. His father's.

"*You could keep him*," said Ysenne.

---

He stood in the glass corridor and looked at himself.

At the gold eyes. At the threads. At the crown of light.

He thought about his father in a bed under a boarded window. With a Lantern beside him. Breathing. In. Catch. Out.

He thought about his father's hand on his hair. *I'll try. I'll try very hard.*

He thought about his father on the floor of the blue-door cottage, for an hour, watching the sun move across the flags.

He thought about *I stood up*.

---

*[Lex*,] said Ember.

Very quietly. Very close.

*[Lex, don't*.]

*I wasn't going to*.

*[You were thinking about it*.]

*I was thinking about it*, Lex admitted. *I'm allowed to think about it.*

*[Yes*,] said Ember. *[You are.]* A pause. *[He'd hate it. You know he'd hate it. Kept. In the net. For a hundred years. In a bed.]*

*I know*.

*[He'd say*—]

*I know what he'd say*.

---

"*Kept*," said Lex. Out loud. To the man in the glass. To the faces. To the Glass Queen. "*You said keep him. That's the word you used.*"

The faces went still.

"*That's your brother's word*," said Lex. "*Not mine. Not my father's.*" He looked at the man in the glass with gold eyes. "*He held the net for two hundred years and it kept him forty-five and he hated every day of it. He watched everyone he knew grow old and die. He watched my mother walk into the dark.*" His voice was very quiet. "*And when he finally let go of it, he was happy. For the first time. With a year left and a wife who brings him pies.*"

"*Smith*—"

"*He doesn't want to be kept*," said Lex Vey. "*He wants to be finished. There's a difference.*"

He turned his back on the glass.

"*Old Mag said that*," he said. "*Wren told me. To your steward. In her kitchen.*"

---

The man in the glass with gold eyes didn't move.

The faces didn't move.

And then the glass behind Lex cracked.

Not broke. *Cracked*. A thin bright line, running down the pane where the man with the crown of light had been. From top to bottom.

And when Lex looked back, the man was gone. There was only his own face. Grey. Tired. With a handprint on its cheek.

"*Huh*," said the Glass Queen. Very softly. From everywhere. "*Arion said you'd say that. I didn't believe him.*"

---

Antho was on his knees.

Lex hadn't seen him go down. He turned round and his brother was on the glass floor behind him with his hands flat on it and his head bowed and his black hair hanging over his face. Shaking.

"*Antho*—"

"*Don't look*," said Antho. Hoarsely. Not lifting his head. "*Don't look in the glass. Not mine. Please.*"

Lex looked.

---

It was a cave.

In the glass wall beside his brother. A cave in the Greyspine. Cold. Dark. With a fire in it, burned down to red. With a bone flute on a rock. With a young man sitting against the wall, very thin, with his black hair wild and his beard wild and his hands burned.

And his eyes.

Black. All the way through. Iris and white. Black as the Hollow. Black as the dark under the lid.

The young man in the glass was talking. To nobody. To the cave wall. His lips moving. Fast. And Lex could hear it, faint, through the glass, the way you hear voices through a wall.

*[...hungry...so hungry...little brother smells of warm iron...]*

---

"*Eight years*," said Ysenne. Gently. From everywhere. "*I saw them in you. Through the glass. The first time you looked into my brother's gold.*" The faces tilted toward Antho. "*Eight years with a hunger in your head. Talking to you. Wanting through you.*"

Antho didn't move.

"*You still dream about it*," said the Glass Queen. "*Don't you. Every night. Seraphine doesn't know. You wake up at three in the morning and lie very still and check. Check that it's gone. Check that it's not there.*" A pause. "*Clarity a hundred. And you still check.*"

"*Stop*," said Lex.

"*I'm only showing him what's there*," said Ysenne. "*That's all I ever do.*"

---

And the cave changed.

The young man in it stood up. Slowly. And came toward the glass. Toward Antho, kneeling on the other side of it. With his black eyes and his burned hands and his wild hair.

And as he came, he went gold.

From the feet up. Slowly. The way the boy in the field had gone, the way Bet Dale had gone. His boots. His legs. His chest. His burned hands, reaching. His face. His black eyes.

Gold.

A gold young man. Standing in a gold cave. With his hand out. Toward the glass. Toward the door.

*Kept*.

"*He could keep you*," said Ysenne. Softly. "*Oz. If you asked. He could keep you exactly as you are now. Clarity a hundred. Forever. It would never come back. You'd never have to check again.*" A pause. "*You'd never have to wake up at three in the morning and lie very still.*"

---

Antho lifted his head.

Slowly. His face was grey and wet and his eyes were very dark. Not black. Grey. His father's grey.

He looked at the gold young man in the glass.

At his own face. Kept. With its hand out. Reaching for a door it would never open.

"*I'd never dream again*," he said.

"*No*."

"*I'd never wake up*."

"*No*," said Ysenne. "*Never. Isn't that what you want?*"

Antho looked at the gold young man for a long time.

"*No*," said Antho Vey. "*I want to wake up at three in the morning and check.*"

The faces went still.

"*Because it's not there*," said Antho. "*Every time. Every night. I wake up and lie very still and check and it's not there.*" His voice was shaking. He didn't let it stop him. "*And then I go back to sleep. And Seraphine's asleep beside me with ink on her nose and her spectacles on the floor. And I think, it's not there. It's gone. I'm here.*" He wiped his face with the back of his burned hand. "*That's the best part of my whole day.*"

---

The glass in front of him cracked.

A thin bright line. Top to bottom. Through the gold young man in the gold cave.

And the young man was gone.

---

Hask hadn't moved.

He was standing at the end of the corridor. Enormous. Still. His pick in his good hand. His bandaged arm against his chest.

Looking at the glass.

Lex went to him.

He knew before he got there. He didn't need to see. But he saw.

---

A window.

Small. Square. In a grey terraced house in a black valley. With rain on it. And a candle on the sill. And a face on the other side of it, looking out.

A girl. Eight. Thin. Pale. With her brother's broad face and her brother's dark eyes and grey on her lips.

She was whistling.

Two notes. High. Low. Over and over. Through the glass. Through the rain.

*Here I am. Here I am.*

---

"*Nell*," said Silas Hask.

His voice was barely there.

The girl in the window kept whistling. Two notes. High. Low. Looking out at the rain. At the street. At someone who wasn't there.

"*She used to do that*," said Hask. "*At the end. When she couldn't get out of bed. Kit's mam would carry her to the window. And she'd whistle. Two notes. So I'd know. Coming home from the pit. So I'd look up and know she was still*—" He stopped.

"*Hask*—"

"*Every night*," said Silas Hask. "*Six months. I'd come up the street and look up and she'd be in the window. Whistling.*" His big scarred hand was white on the pick. "*And then one night I came up the street and looked up and the window was dark.*"

---

"*He could keep her*," said Ysenne. Very softly. "*If she were here. If she'd lived long enough. Oz could have kept her in that window. Whistling. Forever.*" A pause. "*You'd never have come up the street to a dark window.*"

Hask didn't say anything.

"*But she's dead*," said the Glass Queen. "*Isn't she. Dead and gone. Down a river. On the other side.*" The faces were very gentle. "*There's nobody to keep.*"

"*No*," said Hask.

"*So you can only look*," said Ysenne. "*Like me. At what's there. At what's gone.*" A sigh. "*Forever.*"

---

Hask looked at the girl in the window for a long time.

At the rain. At the candle on the sill. At the grey on her lips.

At her mouth. Whistling. Two notes.

And then he put his lips together.

And whistled back.

---

Two notes. High. Low. Rough. Cracked. A Coldwater hewer's whistle, from a chest full of grey that had finally, in this full heavy country, found room to breathe all the way down.

*Here I am*.

The girl in the window stopped.

She looked out. Through the rain. Through the glass. As if she'd heard. As if someone had whistled back from the street.

And smiled.

---

And the glass cracked.

Top to bottom. A thin bright line. Through the window. Through the rain. Through the girl's smile.

She was gone.

Hask stood in front of the cracked glass with his lips still pursed.

"*She smiled*," he said. Very quietly. "*She never smiled. Not at the end. She was too tired.*"

"*I know*," said Lex.

"*She smiled at me*," said Silas Hask.

---

"*Oh*," said Ysenne.

From everywhere. Very small. Very quiet. Puzzled.

"*That's three*," she said. "*Three cracked. In three breaths.*" The faces in every pane were turning. Looking at the three cracked panes in the corridor. "*Nobody's ever cracked one. Not in nine hundred years. I've shown Oz his father in the bed a thousand times and he's never*—"

She stopped.

"*How*?" she said.

---

Lex didn't answer.

He didn't know. He stood in the glass corridor among the cracked panes and the faces and thought about it.

*He wants to be finished*.

*I want to wake up and check*.

*Here I am*.

*[Because they looked at what was there*,] said Ember. Very quietly. *[And said yes to it. All of it. Even the dark window. Even the cave. Even the bed.]* A pause. *[That's the one thing glass can't show. What you'd do with what you see.]*

---

He lit the black heat.

---

He'd been holding it back. He didn't know why. Some idea that he'd need it later. That it was the last thing. The only thing that worked on her.

He let it go now.

Out of his hands. Out of his chest. Into the glass corridor. Not flame. Not light. *Heat*. Black. Rolling off him in waves. Filling the corridor. Rising into the glass ceiling. Spreading out through the glass walls into the glass rooms beyond.

Eating the light.

---

The palace went dark.

Not all at once. From him. Outward. Like ink spreading through water. The glass walls going dim. Dimmer. The light that bounced from pane to pane, endless, dizzying, stopping. Swallowed. Drunk.

And the faces went with it.

Every pane. Every tower. One by one. The pale sharp-cheeked face in every surface going dim, going black, going out like a candle snuffed. Until there was nothing in the glass at all. No light. No reflection. No Queen.

Just dark.

And heat.

And somewhere, far off, deep in the palace, a voice. Small. Shocked.

"*...oh...I can't...I can't see...*"

---

"*Antho*," said Lex.

He couldn't see either. Not a thing. He stood in the black with the heat pouring off him and couldn't see his own hand.

"*Here*."

"*Find him*."

A pause. In the dark.

"*I can't see*," said Antho.

"*You don't need to see*," said Lex. "*You're a Threshold Warden. You feel doors.*" He swallowed. "*This is a palace. It's full of doors. Rooms and doors and stairs and corridors.*" His voice was shaking with the effort of holding the heat. "*She said she put him in her best room. With nothing in it.*"

Silence.

"*A room with nothing in it*," said Antho. Slowly.

"*Pim makes rooms*. *Even empty. Even asleep. Even with nothing left.*" Lex's hands were burning. "*Find the quietest room in the palace. That's where he'll be.*"

---

Antho found it.

Lex didn't know how. He followed. In the black. With one hand on his brother's shoulder and the other pouring heat. With Hask behind him, his hand on Lex's shoulder, breathing all the way down.

Antho walked.

Slowly. With his hands out in front of him. Not touching the glass. Feeling. For the doors. For the spaces between one room and the next. For the place where a wall stopped and a way began.

Left. Right. Up a stair Lex couldn't see, glass steps under his boots that rang faintly like bells. Along a corridor. Through an arch. Down. Left again.

"*There*," said Antho.

---

Lex felt it before Antho said it.

Quiet.

Not the dark. Not the black heat. *Quiet*. A space. Ahead of them. In the black. Small. Round. Still. The way the cellar at Harrow had been still. The way a room is still when someone you love is sleeping in it.

Pim.

---

"*He's in there*," said Antho. Very quietly. "*Through that door. It's the only room in the palace with nothing in it.*" A pause. "*Not even glass.*"

"*How can it not have glass*?"

"*I don't know*," said Antho. "*But it hasn't. The floor's stone. The walls are stone. There's a door and there's nothing.*" His voice was very strange. "*It's the only room in her whole palace where she can't see herself.*"

---

They went in.

And Lex let the black heat go.

---

Not all of it. Most. He let it drain back. Slowly. Into the glass corridor behind them. Into the palace. Letting the light come back, a little. Dim. Grey-gold.

There was light in the room.

Not much. A little. Coming in through the door behind them. Enough to see by.

It was small. Stone. Grey. Plain. With no window. With no glass at all. Just four walls and a floor and a ceiling. Bare.

Pim was lying in the middle of the floor. On his side. Curled up like a cat. In his blue coat. Asleep.

And sitting beside him—

---

She was real.

Not a face in a pane. Not a hand out of a basket. Not a reflection. *Real*. Glass. All of her. Clear and cold and faintly glittering, like frost, like ice over deep water. Sitting on the stone floor beside the sleeping boy with her long glass legs folded under her white gown and her long glass hands in her lap.

You could see through her. Lex could see the stone wall behind her, bent, distorted, through her chest.

She was looking at Pim.

Not at them. She didn't look up when they came in. She sat on the stone floor in the only room in her palace with no glass in it and looked at a sleeping boy, very still, the way you'd look at a fire on a cold night.

"*He's asleep*," said Ysenne. Very quietly. "*Don't wake him.*"

---

Nobody moved.

Lex stood in the doorway with his hands still warm and the light coming dim and grey-gold past him. Antho beside him. Hask behind.

"*Give him back*," said Lex. Quietly. So as not to wake the boy.

"*I asked him*," said the Glass Queen.

She still didn't look up.

"*When he woke*," she said. "*The first time. In here. I asked him.*" Her glass hands moved in her lap. "*I said, make room in me. The way you did in the little cook. Please. I'll give you back. I'll give you all back. Just make room in me first.*"

Nobody spoke.

"*He said he couldn't*," said Ysenne. "*He said he was empty. He said he'd left it all in Ilda.*" A pause. "*And then he tried anyway.*"

---

Lex looked at the boy on the floor.

Curled up. White-faced. Breathing very slow. More asleep than Lex had ever seen anyone asleep.

"*He tried*," said the Glass Queen. Wonderingly. "*He put his hand on my knee. Like this.*" She reached out. Slowly. And laid her long glass fingers on the stone floor beside Pim's hand. Not touching it. "*And he got quiet. As quiet as he could. With nothing left.*"

She was silent for a long moment.

"*And for a breath*," said Ysenne, "*there was room.*"

---

Lex didn't say anything.

"*In me*," said the Glass Queen. "*For one breath. Nine hundred years and for one breath there was a place in me that wasn't my face.*" Her voice was very small. "*It was so quiet. It was so empty. I didn't know anything could be that empty.*" She looked at the boy. "*I didn't see myself. For one breath. In my own palace. I didn't see anything at all.*"

"*And then*?" said Antho.

"*And then he fell asleep*," said Ysenne. "*Like that. In the middle. Like a candle going out.*" She looked at her hand on the stone beside his. "*And I sat here. And I didn't want to look at anything. For the first time. I didn't want to look in any glass. I just wanted to sit here.*"

She looked up.

At last.

Her ice-grey eyes. In her clear glass face. On Lex.

"*So I made this room*," said the Glass Queen. "*While he slept. Out of stone. With no glass in it.*" She looked round at the bare grey walls. "*It's the first thing I've ever made that I can't see myself in.*"

---

Lex didn't know what to say.

He stood in the doorway of a stone room in a glass palace and looked at a Crowned queen sitting on the floor beside a sleeping boy.

"*Take him*," said Ysenne.

He stared at her.

"*Take him*," she said again. "*He's yours. He came with you. He'll want to wake up with you.*" She drew her glass hand back. Into her lap. "*I only wanted to look.*"

"*And you're letting us go*."

"*I'm letting you go*," said the Glass Queen.

"*Why*?"

She looked at the sleeping boy for a long time.

"*Because the baby looked at me*," she said. "*And Arion broke my mirror and said kept things can't see. And this one made room in me with nothing left.*" She looked up at Lex. "*And because I've watched you for an hour, smith. In every pane. And you cracked three of them in three breaths.*" A small strange smile. Not a girl's. Not delighted. Tired. "*I'd like to see what you do next.*"

---

Hask picked Pim up.

He came past Lex into the stone room and bent down, enormous, and slid his good arm under the boy, and lifted him. The way he'd lifted Lark out of the cellar. One finger at a time.

Pim didn't wake.

He lay against Hask's chest in his blue coat with his small white face turned into the scarred leather. Breathing very slow.

The Glass Queen watched them.

"*Where are you going*?" she said.

Nobody answered.

"*Not back to the door*," said Ysenne. "*You'd have gone back already. You came here for something. You came past my house and past Cad's house and past the hall.*" Her ice-grey eyes moved. To the north. Through the stone wall. As if she could see through it. "*There's nothing north. Nothing but dust.*"

Lex didn't say anything.

"*And Rosie's thorns*," said the Glass Queen.

---

The room was very quiet.

"*You're going to Rosie*," said Ysenne. Slowly. "*Aren't you. To her thorns. That she grew in the one hour she was awake. Thirty-two years ago.*" Her face was very still. "*I always wondered why. Oz said she was being Rosie. Growing things nobody wanted. I said, she's hiding something.*"

"*Ysenne*—"

"*I don't care*," said the Glass Queen. "*I don't. Whatever it is. Oz can have his crown in the road and Cad can have his rings and Rosie can have her secret.*" She looked down at her hands. "*I've had a room with nothing in it. For an hour. That's more than I've had in nine hundred years.*"

She looked up.

"*But you should know*," she said. "*Before you go.*"

"*Know what*?"

---

"*Rosie will betray you*," said Ysenne.

Lex didn't move.

"*She betrays everyone*," said the Glass Queen. "*Not because she wants to. Because she's Rosie. Because she listens to everyone and agrees with everyone and loves everyone and then does what Oz says.*" Her voice was very flat. "*She listened to Hes. In the garden. She said oh, Hes, are you sure. She cried.*" A pause. "*And then she went down to the hall and drank.*"

"*That was nine hundred years ago*."

"*She's the same*," said Ysenne. "*We're all the same. That's what drinking does. It keeps you. It keeps you exactly what you were the night you drank.*" She looked at Lex. "*She'll open her thorns for you. She'll take you to whatever she's hiding. She'll cry. She'll mean it.*"

"*And then*?"

"*And then Oz will ask her where you are*," said the Glass Queen. "*And she'll tell him.*"

---

Nobody said anything.

"*She won't want to*," said Ysenne. "*She'll hate it. She'll grow a thousand roses afterward and send them all to nobody.*" Her glass hands were still in her lap. "*But she'll tell him. She always tells him. She can't not.*"

"*Why are you telling us*?" said Antho.

The Glass Queen looked at him.

"*Because I always tell the truth*," she said. "*That's what glass is.*" A pause. "*And because the room-maker tried.*"

---

They left her there.

On the floor of the stone room. In the only room in her palace with no glass in it. Sitting very still with her hands in her lap and her ice-grey eyes on the place where the boy had been lying.

Lex looked back once. From the doorway.

She wasn't looking at anything.

Not at the walls. Not at the floor. Not at him. Her eyes were open and she was looking at nothing at all, the way you look at nothing when you're listening very hard to something only you can hear.

To the quiet.

---

They went out through the dark palace.

Lex lit the black heat again. A little. Enough. Antho led. Through the doors he could feel. Down the glass stairs that rang like bells. Along the corridors. Past the three cracked panes.

Nobody stopped them. No faces. No voices. No hands out of the glass.

They came out through the tall glass door into the grey-gold dim.

And Lex let the black heat go.

---

The palace lit up behind them.

He felt it. The light coming back. Bouncing. Pane to pane. Endless.

He didn't look back.

But Antho did. And told him, later.

*The faces were back*, Antho said. *In every pane. Thousands of them. The way they were when we came.*

*Watching us*?

*No*, said Antho. *That's the thing. They weren't watching us.*

*Then what*—

*They had their eyes shut*, said Antho. *Every one. Every face in every pane. All of them. Eyes shut.*

*Like they were listening*.

---

Pim woke on the white road.

Hask was carrying him. Walking north. Toward the edge of the plain. Toward nothing. Toward where Seren had dreamed a forest of thorns.

The boy stirred. Lifted his head off Hask's chest. Blinked.

Looked round. At the grey-gold dust. At Lex and Antho, walking ahead. At Hask's face, very close, looking down at him.

"*Ilda*," he said.

"*She's back on the road*," said Hask. "*Walking. A finger's width an hour.*"

Pim was quiet for a moment.

"*The glass lady*," he said.

"*She let us go*."

"*I tried*," said Pim. "*I didn't have any left. I tried anyway.*"

"*I know*," said Silas Hask. "*She told us.*"

Pim looked up at him.

"*Did it work*?"

Hask didn't answer straight away.

He walked on, north, across the grey-gold dust, with the boy against his chest, breathing all the way down.

"*She made a room with no glass in it*," he said at last. "*Out of stone. While you slept. So she could sit in it.*"

Pim didn't say anything.

He put his head back down on Hask's chest. And shut his eyes.

But Lex, looking back, saw him smile.

---

They walked north.

Away from the white road. Away from the kept and their hum. Away from the palaces. Across a plain of grey-gold dust that went on to the edge of everything without a hill or a fold or a tree.

Ten cups now. Antho had needed another, coming out of the palace.

Fifteen days. Fewer. Lex didn't do the sums. He didn't want to know.

He walked with the basket on his back and his hand on the bell and his eyes on the flat grey-gold horizon to the north.

*Rosie will betray you*.

*She'll open her thorns. She'll cry. She'll mean it.*

*And then Oz will ask her where you are. And she'll tell him.*

---

He saw them on the second Pim.

A line. On the horizon. Dark. Low. Running east and west as far as he could see.

He thought it was a hill. The first hill in the Court.

It wasn't a hill.

As they came closer, it rose. Taller. Darker. Not a line. A *wall*. Black. Hooked. Tangled. Taller than the palace doors. Taller than the hall. Running round in a great ring, a mile across, maybe more, in the middle of the empty plain.

Thorns.

And in the middle of the ring, rising above them, very small, very thin, grey—

A tower.

---

They stopped at the edge of the thorns.

Black stems. Thick as a man's wrist. Thorns as long as a finger. Not a leaf. Not a bud. Hooked into each other so tight there wasn't a gap anywhere you could have put your hand.

And they moved.

Lex saw it. As the four of them came up to the edge. The stems. Shifting. Very slightly. Creaking. Leaning. Toward them.

The way plants lean toward a window.

"*Hello*," said Lex. To the thorns. Very quietly.

The thorns didn't answer.

But in the middle of the wall in front of him, at the height of his chest, on the end of one black hooked stem—

Something red.

Small. Closed. A bud.

And as he watched, it opened.
