# The Council of Lanterns

Highcrest had gone blind.

Lex saw it from the air. Coming in on Vharuun's neck over the downs at noon, with his father in Brannoc's chair strapped in front of him and his mother behind him with her arms round his waist, and the golden city rising on its hill in the spring sun the way it always did. Tier on tier. Roof on roof. The white towers. The Spire.

It didn't shine.

That was what he noticed. Highcrest always shone. In the morning, in the afternoon, at sunset. A thousand windows catching the light and throwing it back. You could see it from twenty miles off on a clear day. A city made of sparks.

It was dull now. Grey-white. Flat. Every window on every tier blank and milky, like a blind man's eyes.

Soaped.

---

"*All of them*," said Elowen Dunmore.

She met them on the landing field below the Crown Terrace, with Oathkeeper on her hip and two knights behind her and her face set in the expression of a woman who had not slept properly in a week and was not going to discuss it. She looked at the dragon. At the old man in the chair. At Seren. At Lex.

"*Every window in the city*," she said. "*The Queen's order. Six days ago. After Seraphine's letter.*" She turned and began to walk. Fast. They followed. Lex pushing his father's chair over the cobbles. "*Soap. Chalk. Whitewash, where people didn't have soap. Paper pasted over, where they didn't have whitewash.*" She glanced back. "*The Spire took three days. It's got four thousand panes. They had to do it from ropes.*"

"*And mirrors*?"

"*Covered. Turned to the wall. Broken, some of them, by people who were frightened.*" Elowen's mouth was very tight. "*There were three sightings in the first two days. A woman in the glass. White hair. Grey eyes. Smiling.*" She walked faster. "*One was in the bathhouse on Copper Street. There was a riot.*"

"*Was anyone hurt*?"

"*A man broke his nose running out of the steam-room with no towel*," said Elowen. "*Otherwise no.*" A pause. "*He's very embarrassed.*"

*[I'd be embarrassed*,] said Ember.

*You don't have a nose*.

*[Hypothetically*,] said Ember. *[If I had a nose. And a towel. And had lost the towel.]*

---

They went up through the city.

It was strange. Lex had walked these streets a hundred times. He'd run across these roofs with Wren in the rain. He'd seen the city dimmed, last autumn, every warm thing gone out of it and the people standing in the street like scarecrows.

This was different.

The people were all right. They were out. Busy. Shouting at each other across the market squares. Hollis Penn's bakery on the corner of Fennel Street had its door propped open and its window chalked white, and someone had written on the chalk in big letters, with a wet finger: *NO GLAZE TODAY*. *UNGLAZED BUNS 2 FOR A COPPER*.

"*He thinks he's very funny*," said Elowen.

"*He is very funny*," said Seren.

"*He's been doing it for six days*. *Glaze is a reflective surface. Did you know that? I didn't. I do now.*" Elowen's jaw tightened. "*He came up to the palace on the second day to tell us. He'd seen her in a tray of iced cakes.*"

---

Nobody could see themselves.

That was the strange thing. That was what Lex kept noticing, all the way up through the tiers. Men coming out of barbers' shops with odd tufts sticking up at the back where the barber couldn't check. Women with their hair pinned crooked. A girl at a fountain with her hands cupped, about to drink, who looked down into the water and saw it was covered with a sheet of oiled cloth and laughed and made a face.

A whole city that couldn't see its own face.

*[It's oddly freeing*,] said Ember. *[Don't you think? Look at that man. His collar's inside out. He has no idea. He's whistling.]*

*It's frightening*.

*[It's both*,] said Ember. *[Most things are.]*

---

The Hall of Lanterns was the oldest room in the palace.

It had been a chapel once, before the palace grew round it. A long low stone room with a vaulted roof and narrow windows set high in the walls. And everywhere, on every surface—hanging from the vault on chains, standing in rows along the walls, set into niches in the stone—lanterns.

Hundreds of them. Iron and brass and copper and silver. Old. Some of them very old. Every king and queen of Caldor for eight hundred years had hung a lantern here on their crowning day. And every one of them had glass.

They were lit.

And every pane was soaped.

The light in the hall was strange. Soft. Clouded. A warm gold fog. Like being inside a lantern yourself. Lex stood in the doorway with his hands on his father's chair and looked up at the vault, at the hundreds of milky gold blurs hanging from it, and felt something in his chest go quiet.

"*Aldric's*," said his father softly. Looking up. At one lantern in particular. A plain iron one, near the door. "*I stood here when he hung it. I was standing right there. By that pillar.*" A pause. "*He was nineteen. He dropped it.*"

"*Dropped it*?"

"*On his foot*," said Arion Vey. "*He swore so loudly the Archbishop fainted.*" His mouth twitched. "*It's still dented. Look. There. On the bottom.*"

---

They were all there.

Lex hadn't been sure they'd come. He'd sent ravens. His mother had sent Correspondence, with a note tied to her collar, which seemed to have worked, since the goat was standing in the corner of the hall eating a tapestry.

The Queen, at the head of the long table. In a plain blue dress, with her silver circlet. Small and sharp-eyed and twenty-four, with a soft mouth she kept biting.

Roole, in his Keepers' grey. Three days on the road from Greyfall. His stiff arm held close.

Antho and Seraphine. Antho grey with tired. Seraphine with a stack of books in front of her a foot high and ink on her fingers.

Brannoc. Enormous. Bald. Soot-bearded. Standing behind the end of the table with his arms folded because he said the chairs weren't made for men his size and he'd break one.

And Kestrel Varn, with her red hair cropped short and her amber eyes bright and her feet up on the table. Unlatticed. Untitled. The only living person who could command Flame without ever having touched a Stone.

And at the far end, on a stool that someone had found for her, very small, very old, in red robes, with a face like a dried apple and two black eyes like a crow's—

Grandmother Asha of the Ashari.

"*Smith*," she said. As Lex came in. Grinning her toothless grin. "*I came on a camel. A camel. Two hundred miles. At my age. You'd better have something worth hearing.*"

---

Wren wasn't there.

She was on Fennel Street. At her mother's. With Lark.

*I'm not bringing her into a room full of lanterns*, she'd said. On the landing field. With the baby in the sling against her chest and her face set. *Not with that woman in the glass. Not with everyone looking. Mam's got no mirrors and she's soaped her windows and she's been waiting three months to hold her granddaughter without a hundred people round.*

*Wren*—

*Tell me after*, Wren had said. *Tell me everything. Every word.* She'd kissed him. Hard. *I'll be on the roof if you need me.*

He'd watched her go. Down the steps from the landing field. Into the city. A small figure with red hair and a baby on her chest.

She'd been walking fast. Almost running.

He'd noticed that. He didn't know why.

---

They sat down.

His father at the Queen's right hand, in the wheeled chair, with a blanket over his knees. His mother beside him. Then Lex. Then the others. The goat went on eating the tapestry. Nobody stopped her.

"*Well*," said Lysandra. "*Tell me.*"

So he told her.

---

It took a long time.

He told it the way his father had taught him, years ago, in a cold room at the top of the Spire. *Start at the beginning. Go to the end. Then stop. And don't leave anything out because you think it's too small.*

The bell. The voice. The gold at the Veil. A boy in a field, mid-step, his eyes moving. A rose. *HES?* The crack, with dust coming out of it and thorns going in.

The pool. The black glass. The Key, and the Lockward, and a grey thread wound round them, frayed and tight. The last thread. Running out into the dark. Black iron rings coming closer.

*Clink*.

Lantern Night.

The net with nobody's hand on it. The crown in the road.

---

Antho told the next part.

The knock. The dust. The voice in the walls. An old woman who said no, and an old man who said yes for her. A gold face on a pillow. A tear.

A door that wasn't there anymore.

He told it very quietly. Looking at the table. When he got to the part about the door, his voice stopped. Just for a moment. And Seraphine, beside him, put her hand on his under the table, and he went on.

When he'd finished, nobody said anything for a long time.

---

"*Two in Ashbourne*," said Brannoc. Into the silence. His voice was very rough. "*Old Wim. And the widow Tobbin.*" He looked at his enormous hands. "*Both of them in their chairs by the fire. Both of them gold.*"

"*Who chose*?" said the Queen.

"*Wim's daughter*. *And the widow's son.*" Brannoc's jaw worked. "*Neither of them will talk about it. Wim's daughter won't let anyone in the house. The Tobbin boy sits outside his mother's door all day and won't eat.*" He looked up. "*He's twenty-six. He's a Brawler now. Level forty. He cleared the rock-wyrms out of the Greyspine last month with his bare hands.*"

He swallowed.

"*He sits outside the door*," said Brannoc Holt, "*and cries like a child.*"

---

"*It's spreading*," said Roole. "*The gold at the edge. Not just from the crack. From him too. From the dust-man.*" He shifted his stiff arm. "*There are reports from Westerling. From the crofts out past it. From two villages in the western shires.*" His hatchet face didn't change. "*Old people. Gold. In their beds. Their chairs.*" A pause. "*A baby. In Marrowby. Sick with the croup. Her mother couldn't bear the coughing.*"

The Queen's mouth went very tight.

"*How many*?"

"*Thirty-one*," said Roole. "*That we know of. In three weeks.*"

"*And every one of them chose*?"

"*Someone chose*," said Antho quietly. "*Every time. Someone at the bedside. Never the one in the bed.*"

---

"*Can they be brought back*?" said Lysandra.

She looked at Lex.

They all looked at Lex.

He didn't answer straight away. He sat at the long table in the soft gold fog of a hundred soaped lanterns and thought about a field. About heat that wouldn't sink in. About the Hollow putting out its hand and finding nowhere to put it.

"*I don't know*," he said. "*I tried. With the boy. Fire won't touch it. Stone can't feel it. The Hollow—*" He stopped. "*There's no room in it. That's what it is. It's full. All the way through. It's not that it's hard. It's that it's finished.*"

"*Pim made room*," said Antho.

They looked at him.

"*In Bet*," said Antho. "*For a moment. Just a breath. A crack too small to see. And she cried.*" He looked at Lex. "*It closed again. But it opened. It opened, Lex.*"

Lex looked at his brother for a long moment.

"*Then there's a way*," he said.

"*There's a crack*," said Antho. "*It's not the same thing.*"

"*Everything's a crack first*," said Lex Vey. "*Ask any smith.*"

---

"*I don't understand*," said Kestrel.

Everyone looked at her. She'd taken her feet off the table. She was leaning forward on her elbows, her amber eyes very bright, frowning.

"*Why*?" she said. "*Why does he want to? Why go round the world turning grandmothers into statues? What does he get?*"

"*Nothing*," said Seren.

Kestrel looked at her.

"*He doesn't get anything*," said Seren. Quietly. "*That's not what it's for. It's not a weapon. It's not a trick.*" She looked down at her hands. "*He thinks it's a kindness.*"

"*A kindness*."

"*My mother used to tell a story*," said Seren. "*About a king who couldn't bear to lose anything. Who kept everything he ever loved in a gold room with no doors. His dog. His horse. His wife. His children.*" She was quiet. "*She said he walked through it every evening. Talking to them. And they couldn't answer. And he never noticed.*"

Nobody spoke.

"*He's not cruel*," said Seren Harrow. "*He's frightened. He's the most frightened man who ever lived. And he wants everyone else to be safe from the thing that frightens him.*"

"*Which is what*?" said Kestrel.

"*Endings*," said Seren.

---

"*And the Key*," said Lysandra.

She'd been quiet a long time. Listening. Biting her soft mouth. She looked at Arion now. Down the table. At the old man in the wheeled chair at her right hand.

"*You were Warden-Architect*," she said. "*For two hundred years. You knew the net better than anyone alive.*"

"*I knew the old one*," said Arion. "*Lex wove the new one.*"

"*The edge didn't change*," said Lysandra. "*Did it? The Veil. That's still Aurelian's.*"

His father was quiet for a moment.

"*No*," he said. "*The edge didn't change. Lex wove from the middle out. The Veil was there before he started and it was there when he finished.*" His spotted hands moved on the arms of the chair. "*He tied his new threads into it. The same way Aurelian did. The Veil's the last thread. Everything else hangs from it, at the edge, like a net hangs from its top rope.*"

"*And it runs all the way to the Knot*."

"*All the way down*."

"*Where*?" said the Queen. "*Where does it run? Under what?*"

---

His father didn't answer straight away.

He looked up. At the vault. At the hundreds of soaped lanterns hanging there, gold and blurred.

"*There's a map*," he said. "*In the Spire. In my old rooms. Behind the bookcase.*" A faint smile. "*I drew it a hundred and forty years ago. When I was young and arrogant and thought I could understand everything if I just measured it carefully enough.*" He looked down. "*The great threads. The main ones. Where they ran under the world. I followed them with a pendulum and a compass for eleven years.*"

"*And the last thread*?"

"*The deepest*," said Arion. "*And the straightest. It comes in from the edge under the Sundered Lands. Very deep. Too deep to feel, most of the way.*" He closed his eyes. "*And then it rises. It comes up under the Greyspine. Under the old mountains. Under the coal country.*"

Lex went very still.

"*Under Coldwater*," said his father. "*Right under it. Two hundred fathoms. Closer to the surface there than anywhere else in the world.*" He opened his eyes. "*The miners used to say the deep seams sang. They said if you put your ear to the rock at the bottom of the Old Gallery you could hear a note. One note. Very high. Like a harp-string.*"

"*The last thread*," said Lex.

"*Wound tight*," said Arion. "*Humming.*" He looked at his son. "*If Caddoc is climbing it, he'll come under Coldwater on his way to the Knot. And if there's anywhere in the world you could reach him, Lex—*"

"*It's there*."

"*It's there*," said his father.

---

"*Then we stop him there*," said Kestrel. "*We go down and we break the iron and we cut him off.*"

"*Perhaps*," said Arion.

"*Perhaps*?"

"*He's ringing the thread as he comes*," said his father. "*With iron. So it won't throw him off. If you break a ring, he'll make another.*" He looked at Kestrel. "*Iron for the one who never laughed. He's the most patient of them. He'll have thought of it.*"

"*So we try anyway*," said Lex.

"*You try anyway*," agreed Arion. "*And if it doesn't work—*"

He stopped.

"*Then there's the crown*," said Seraphine.

---

She'd been writing all through it. In a book. In her small neat hand. She stopped now and looked up and pushed her spectacles up her nose.

"*Only a hand that's refused a crown can turn the Key and stay itself*," she said. "*That's what Elric said. That's what Aurelian left in the knots.*" She tapped the page. "*Hesper's crown. The tenth. The only one that never touched the pool.*" She looked down the table. At Seren. "*If we had it. If someone wore it. They could turn the Key. Against the last thread. Before Caddoc gets to the Knot.*"

"*And unwrite the Veil*," said Lex.

"*And unwrite the Veil*."

"*And the net would offer them the crown*," said Lex. "*And they'd say no.*"

"*Because they'd already be wearing one that said it for them*," said Seraphine. "*Yes.*"

"*And then the Veil's gone*," said Lysandra.

Her voice was very quiet.

Everyone looked at her.

"*And then there's nothing between us and them*," said the Queen of Caldor. "*No wall. No edge. Nothing. A country full of gold and four Crowned walking out of it across the Sundered Lands toward us.*" She looked round the table. "*Am I right?*"

Nobody answered.

"*Am I right*?" said Lysandra.

"*Yes*," said Arion Vey. "*You're right.*"

---

The Queen sat back in her chair.

She looked up at the vault. At the hundreds of lanterns. At her grandfather's, by the door, with the dent in the bottom.

"*So we have three roads*," she said. "*All of them bad.*"

"*All of them bad*," agreed Roole.

"*We can try to stop him on the thread*. *At Coldwater. And he'll probably just make another ring.*" She held up one finger. "*We can find Hesper's crown and turn the Key and bring the Veil down ourselves. And they walk in.*" A second finger. "*Or we do nothing. And on Lantern Night Caddoc comes down into the Knot and takes the Key to his brother. And his brother turns it. And picks up the net.*" A third finger. "*And keeps it.*"

She looked at the three fingers.

"*And then every Ceiling comes back*," said Lysandra. "*And every child is Kindled into the same class as her mother. And nothing ever changes again. Ever. Anywhere.*" She lowered her hand. "*Forever.*"

Nobody said anything.

"*I was a princess in a world like that*," said the Queen. Quietly. "*For twenty years. I'm not going back.*"

---

"*Then we do all of it*," said Lex.

She looked at him.

"*Coldwater first*," said Lex. "*Now. This week. I'll take Hask. He knows the seams. He knows the Old Gallery. If there's anything to break, his pick will find the flaw in it.*" He looked down the table. "*And we look for the crown. At the same time. In case.*"

"*And the third road*?"

"*Last*," said Lex. "*Only if the others don't work. Only if there's nothing else.*" He swallowed. "*Only if it's that or the crown in the road.*"

The Queen looked at him for a long moment.

"*Agreed*," said Lysandra.

---

"*And where*," said Roole, "*is the crown*?"

Everyone looked at Seren.

She hadn't said anything for a long time. She'd sat at the long table beside her husband's chair with her square hands folded on the wood and listened. Lex had watched her listen. She did it the way she did everything. Completely. Without moving. The way you'd listen for a moth at a window.

"*West*," she said.

The hall was very quiet.

"*My mother took it west*," said Seren Harrow. "*The summer before she died. To the Ferryman. She paid him with the memory of the man she loved.*" Her voice was very steady. "*And she wrote, after, I've taken it where it belongs.*"

"*Where does it belong*?" said Lysandra.

"*I dreamed it*."

---

She told them.

The dream. The country with no sun. The white road. The gold people in rows. The thorns, miles of them, round and round.

The tower in the middle. Grown, not built. Grey and dead.

The chair in the window.

"*It's on the other side*," said Seren. "*Beyond the Veil. In the Court.*" She looked down the table at Seraphine. "*In a tower in the middle of the thorns.*"

"*Rhosyn's thorns*," said Seraphine. Very quietly.

"*Rosie's thorns*."

"*She grew them round it*," said Seraphine. "*To hide it. From the others.*"

"*Or to keep it*," said Arion.

They looked at him.

"*I only say it*," said his father mildly. "*Someone should. She sent a rose to a Harrow woman with her sister's name on it. And now the Harrow woman dreams of a crown in her thorns.*" His grey eyes were very steady. "*If I wanted the only crown in the world that could stop my brother's plan, and I couldn't touch it myself, and I needed someone with Hesper's blood to come and fetch it for me—*"

"*Summer*," said Seren.

He looked at her.

"*You smelled it*," said his wife. "*You said it yourself. You can't fake summer.*"

"*No*," said Arion Vey. "*But you can grow it in the middle of a trap.*"

---

And then the lanterns flickered.

All of them. All at once. Every lantern in the hall. Hanging from the vault. Standing along the walls. In the niches in the stone. Hundreds of them. Their soft gold blurs behind the soaped glass going dim. Bright. Dim.

As if a wind had come through the hall.

There was no wind.

---

"*Oh, Arion*," said a voice. "*You always were the clever one.*"

It came from the lanterns.

From all of them. From every soaped pane. Small and far and singing, like a wet finger round the rim of a glass. A hundred tiny voices, all the same voice, all speaking at once.

The goat stopped eating the tapestry.

"*Mae said so*," said the voice. "*She said you were the cleverest man in that world. She said it was a shame you were so honest. She said it was very inconvenient.*"

Lex was on his feet. They were all on their feet. Elowen's sword was out. Kestrel's hands were burning. Brannoc had picked up a chair.

"*Oh, sit down*," said the Glass Queen. "*I can't come through. I'm only looking.*"

---

He could see her.

Not clearly. Not in any one lantern. The soap was thick. But in every one of them, behind the milky glass, very faint, there was a shape. Pale. Narrow. A long face. White hair. And two pale points of light, very faint, like stars seen through cloud.

Hundreds of her. Hanging from the vault. Looking down.

"*You soaped them*," said Ysenne. Amused. "*How clever. Who thought of that? It was you, wasn't it, Arion? Or your scribe. The one with the spectacles.*" The hundred faint faces turned. All together. Toward Seraphine. "*I like your spectacles. I sat in them for a whole evening last week while you were reading. You read very fast. I couldn't keep up.*"

Seraphine went white.

"*But it's only soap*," said the Glass Queen. Gently. "*And soap is thin. And I'm very, very patient.*" A pause. "*Like a frost.*"

---

"*What do you want*?" said Lysandra.

The Queen of Caldor didn't sit down. She stood at the head of the long table in her plain blue dress with her silver circlet and looked up at the vault. At the hundred faint faces. Her soft mouth very straight.

"*Oh*," said Ysenne. "*You're the one who practices being stern. In the mirror. With the silver back.*" The faint faces tilted. "*You've got much better at it. I've been watching. You bite your lip less.*"

Lysandra's face didn't move.

"*What*," she said, "*do you want*?"

---

"*I've come with terms*," said Ysenne.

She said it lightly. Pleasantly. Like a girl announcing that she'd brought a cake.

"*From Oz. He asked me to say them. He's very good at terms. He always was. When we were children he used to draw up treaties with Cad over the last biscuit.*" A small laugh. "*Cad always got it. Cad's very stubborn. But Oz always made him sign.*"

"*Say them*."

"*The smith's child*," said the Glass Queen.

Lex's hands closed.

"*Freely given*," said Ysenne. "*That's important. Oz was very particular. He says he doesn't want her taken. He says he doesn't want her frightened. He wants her brought to him by someone who loves her. And set down in his arms.*" A pause. "*And then he'll put a crown on her.*"

"*No*," said Lex.

"*Let me finish*," said Ysenne. Mildly. "*It's rude to interrupt. Didn't Arion teach you that? He taught Mae. She said he made her wait until he'd finished a whole page.*" The hundred faint faces turned toward him. "*She'll be a queen, smith. Not a princess. A queen. Of everything. A whole country of gold. Nine thousand people. Four palaces. And all of us.*" Her voice was very soft. "*She'll never be cold. She'll never be hungry. She'll never be sick, or old, or frightened. She'll never die.*"

"*She'll never grow up*," said Wren's voice.

---

Everyone turned.

She was in the doorway. Wren. With her red hair wild and her face white and her breath going hard, as if she'd run all the way up from Fennel Street. Which she had. Lex could see it in the way she stood.

She didn't have the baby.

"*Mam's got her*," said Wren. Before he could ask. "*In the cellar. With the door shut and no light.*" Her green eyes went up. To the vault. To the hundred faint faces. "*I saw the lanterns flicker from the roof. Every lantern on the palace. All at once.*" Her jaw set. "*I thought I'd come and hear the terms.*"

"*Oh*," said Ysenne. Delighted. "*It's you. The one who breathes on things.*"

"*She'll never grow up*," said Wren again. Very flat. "*That's what you meant. Isn't it. She'll be three months old forever. With milk on her chin. Kept.*"

The faint faces didn't answer for a moment.

"*She'll be perfect*," said the Glass Queen. "*Forever. Exactly as she is.*"

"*She's not finished*," said Wren Ashdown. "*She's not supposed to be perfect. She's supposed to grow.*"

---

"*And the rest of you*," said Ysenne. Lightly. As if Wren hadn't spoken. "*Oz says you can have your world. Just as it is. He'll turn the Key and he'll pick up the net and he'll leave it exactly as you made it, smith. No Ceilings. If you like. Levels. Classes. All of it.*" A pause. "*He doesn't care about any of that. He never did. He only wants the child. And the blood. And the Key. And then he'll leave you alone.*"

"*He'll keep everything*," said Antho. Quietly. "*Every thread. Every knot. He'll keep the net exactly as it is.*"

"*Yes*. *Isn't that what I said?*"

"*Nothing new*," said Antho. "*Ever again. Nothing different. Nothing growing. Nothing changing. A net that never moves.*"

"*Safe*," said Ysenne.

"*Kept*," said Antho Vey.

---

"*And you can die*," said the Glass Queen. Softly. "*All of you. In your own time. In your beds. The way you seem to like it so much.*" A sigh, like breath on a cold pane. "*He won't stop you. He won't come round with his pouches. He'll leave you to your endings, since you're so fond of them.*"

The hundred faint faces turned. Slowly. All together.

Toward the old man in the wheeled chair at the Queen's right hand.

"*Even you, Arion*," said Ysenne. Very gently. "*He'll let you go. If that's what you want. He says he'll even send flowers.*"

---

"*No*," said Lex.

He said it before anyone else could. He said it standing up at the long table in the Hall of Lanterns with his hands burning and his voice very quiet.

"*No*," he said again. "*Not her. Not freely. Not any other way. Not for the net. Not for the world. Not for anything.*" He looked up at the vault. At the hundred faces. "*Tell him no.*"

The Glass Queen was silent for a moment.

Then she laughed.

---

It was the small, surprised, delighted laugh. A girl's laugh. In a room full of mirrors.

"*Oh, smith*," she said. "*That's what Hesper said. Exactly that. In exactly that voice.*" The faint faces glittered. "*Not me. Not ever.*"

"*Good*."

"*Is it*?" Ysenne's voice was very soft. "*Look where it got her. A hedge. Nine hundred years of hedges. Running from cottage to cottage with a crown in a box. Never staying. Never Kindling. Never letting anyone learn her face.*" A pause. "*Her daughters died in ditches, smith. Some of them. In the snow. In the night. Running.*"

"*Twenty generations*," said Seren.

The hundred faces turned.

---

His mother was standing. Beside his father's chair. With one hand on his shoulder. Small and square and barefoot on the stone floor of the Hall of Lanterns, looking up.

"*Twenty generations*," said Seren Harrow. "*Of women who never took the crown. Who never let a moth in. Who kept the rule. In ditches and hedges and cottages and snow.*" Her voice was shaking. She didn't let it stop her. "*Twenty generations, from her to me. Twenty women who stayed people.*"

The faces didn't answer.

"*You've been nine hundred years in a palace of mirrors*," said Seren. "*Looking at your own face.*" She tilted her head. "*How's that been?*"

---

The Glass Queen was silent.

For a long time. So long Lex thought she'd gone. The lanterns still flickered. Dim. Bright. Dim. The faint faces still hung there behind the soap.

And then, very quietly, almost too quietly to hear—

"*Lonely*," said Ysenne.

---

Lex moved.

Not with fire. Not the white and gold roar of it. He'd learned that much. You can't burn a reflection any more than you can burn dust.

With the other thing.

He'd learned it on a rubble-heap in the dark, a year ago, from an old smith with a bald head who'd told him, *fire's not about light, lad. Light's just what fire gives away. Fire's about heat.*

Black heat.

Fire with no light.

---

He let it go up.

Out of his hands. Out of his chest. Into the air of the Hall of Lanterns. Not a flame. Not a glow. A *heat*. Pouring off him like the heat off a forge when you open the door. Rising into the vault. Spreading. Filling the hall.

And as it rose, it ate the light.

Not put it out. *Ate* it. The way black heat did. The soft gold blurs of the lanterns, hanging from the vault, going dim. Dimmer. Not because the flames died. The flames were still there. Lex could feel them. Burning hotter than ever. But the light coming off them was going into the black heat like water going into sand. Swallowed. Drunk.

Until the hundreds of lanterns in the Hall of Lanterns were burning hot and bright and *dark*. Every one. Glowing in the vault like coals in a banked fire. Heat without light. Fire without a face.

And in the glass, behind the soap, there was nothing.

No light. No reflection.

No Queen.

---

Grandmother Asha cackled.

She was sitting on her stool at the end of the table with her red robes round her and her toothless mouth wide open, laughing so hard she had to hold her knees.

"*Sand*," she said. "*Smith. Sand.*"

"*It worked*," said Lex.

"*Of course it worked*." The old fire-woman wiped her eyes. "*Fire with no light casts no shadow. Shows no face. Makes no picture in any glass.*" She grinned at him. "*Every Ashari girl knows that. We used to frighten the Wardens with it. They'd come into our tents with their lamps and their mirrors and we'd sit round the fire in the dark and they couldn't see a thing.*"

"*You might have mentioned it*," said Lex.

"*You might have asked*," said Grandmother Asha.

---

He let the black heat go.

Slowly. Carefully. The light came back into the lanterns one by one. Soft gold blurs behind the soap. Hundreds of them. Hanging from the vault.

Empty.

Nobody in them. No faint pale faces. No points of light.

The hall was very quiet.

---

Nobody said anything for a long time.

Then the Queen of Caldor sat down. Slowly. In her chair at the head of the long table. And put her hands flat on the wood. And looked at them.

"*Lonely*," she said.

Nobody answered.

"*She said lonely*." Lysandra didn't look up. "*Nine hundred years in a palace of mirrors and she said lonely.*" She bit her soft mouth. Hard. "*I didn't want to feel sorry for her.*"

"*Nor did I*," said Seren.

---

Wren came across the hall.

She came round the table to where Lex was standing and stood in front of him. Close. She was still breathing hard. Her face was white.

"*You said no*," she said.

"*Of course I said no*."

"*Not of course*." She looked up at him. Her green eyes very bright. "*She offered everything, Lex. The net as it is. No Ceilings. All of it. Everything you ever fought for.*" She swallowed. "*For one baby. For our baby.*"

"*No*," said Lex.

She looked at him for a long moment.

Then she put her forehead against his chest. In the middle of the Hall of Lanterns. In front of the Queen and the Keepers and the old fire-woman and the goat.

"*I ran*," she said into his shirt. "*Do you know that? All the way up from Fennel Street. Over the roofs. I haven't run like that since*—" She stopped. "*I didn't even think about it. I just ran.*"

He put his arms round her.

"*I know*," he said.

---

They found the frost at the end.

When the others had gone. When the council was over and the decisions were made and Roole had gone to write his reports and Kestrel had gone to find something to eat and Grandmother Asha had gone to shout at her camel.

Seraphine found it.

On the lantern by the door. King Aldric's. The plain iron one, with the dent in the bottom where he'd dropped it on his foot at nineteen.

On the inside of the soaped glass. On one pane. In the fog of soap.

Letters.

Not written. Not scratched. *Frost*. Fine white crystals, feathery, delicate, picked out on the inside of the pane in a narrow slanting hand. The way frost writes on a window on a winter morning, if frost could write.

---

Seraphine read them out loud.

Her voice was very quiet.

"*Smith*," she read. "*Cad says to tell you. He's under your mountains now. The black ones. With the coal in.*" She swallowed. "*He says he can hear men singing above him in the dark.*"

Lex went cold.

"*He says*," read Seraphine Ilvane, "*don't bother.*"

---

The frost faded.

Slowly. As they watched. The fine white crystals going soft. Melting. Running down the inside of King Aldric's lantern-pane in thin wet lines, like tears.

Until there was nothing there but soap.

Lex stood in front of it for a long time.

"*Coldwater*," he said.

"*Coldwater*," said his father. Behind him. Very quietly. From the wheeled chair. "*He's early.*"
