# The Iron Road

The thorns opened north.

Lex saw it happen. Behind Rhosyn. Behind the tower. The black wall at the far side of the clearing, covered in dead roses, *parting*. Stems unhooking from stems. Creaking. Groaning. Drawing back like a curtain.

A path. Narrow. Dark. Going away north through the thorns.

"*Go*," said Rhosyn.

She was still on her knees. In the dust. With her hands flat on the ground now. Thin. Grey-green. The fingers spread like roots.

"*Rhosyn*—"

"*Go*." Her voice was like a stem snapping. "*North. Away from them. They're coming from the road. From the east. From the hall.*" She didn't look up. "*I'll close it behind you. I'll hold them. As long as I can.*"

"*Come with us*," said Antho.

The Thorn-Crowned laughed.

It was a terrible sound. Small. Cracked. Dry as a dead leaf.

"*Where*?" she said. "*Through the door? Into your world? With Oz in it?*" She shook her head. "*I'd come apart against the edge. Like the cousins. Or he'd find me. He always finds me.*" She looked up at last. Her green eyes very wet. "*And I always go when he calls.*"

---

*Scrape. Scrape. Scrape*.

Louder now. Closer. All round. From outside the ring of thorns. From the east and the south and the west. The ground shivering.

And a new sound under it.

*Crack*.

Like a branch breaking. Like a dry stem snapping under a boot.

*Crack. Crack. Crack.*

"*They're coming through*," said Hask. Very quietly.

---

Lex looked east.

At the wall of thorns on the far side of the clearing. Black. Hooked. Tangled. Taller than a house. Covered in dead roses.

It was moving.

Not parting. *Shuddering*. The stems shaking. Bending inward. As if something were leaning on the other side of them. Something heavy. Something that didn't care about thorns.

And through the gaps, low down, between the black stems—

Gold.

---

A hand. Gold. Pushing through the thorns. Slowly. A finger's width at a time. The thorns scraping across it, hooking into it, catching on it.

Not tearing it. Nothing tore the gold.

The thorns broke instead.

*Crack*. A stem as thick as a wrist, snapping against the back of a gold hand like a dry twig.

And behind the hand, an arm. A gold sleeve. A gold shoulder. A face.

A gardener. With a gold trowel in his other hand. Pushing through the thorns with his eyes moving, slowly, slowly, toward the clearing. Toward the woman on her knees in the dust.

Toward her.

---

Rhosyn saw him.

Lex saw her see him. Saw her thin grey-green face go white.

"*Tam*," she whispered.

The gold gardener came on through the thorns. A finger's width. A finger's width. The black stems breaking against him.

His eyes were on her.

"*Tam*," said the Thorn-Crowned. "*Oh, Tam. I'm sorry. I'm sorry.*"

And she pushed her hands into the dust.

---

The thorns came up.

Not slowly. Not like the path opening. Fast. *Hard*. Out of the ground in front of the gold gardener. Black. Thick as a man's arm. Hooked with thorns as long as a hand. Rising. Twisting round him. Round his arms. His legs. His chest. Wrapping. Binding. Pulling him back.

Not hurting him. Nothing hurt the gold.

*Holding* him.

The gardener stopped.

Caught. Wrapped in black thorns from his gold boots to his gold throat. Still pushing. A finger's width. A finger's width. Against the thorns.

The thorns held.

---

And behind him, more.

Lex saw them. Through the gaps in the eastern wall. Gold. Dozens. Hundreds. Pressing through the thorns. Gardeners and grooms and cooks and maids. Gold hands. Gold faces. Gold eyes, moving.

All of them coming toward the woman on her knees.

Rhosyn pushed her hands deeper into the dust.

The thorns came up. Round every one. Black. Binding. Holding.

Her face was grey.

Not grey-green. *Grey*. Like ash. Like the inside of a dead tree. Every thorn that came up out of the ground came out of *her*. Lex could see it. Could see her thinning. Could see her sinking further into herself, the way a fire sinks when you keep raking the coals out of it.

"*GO*," screamed the Thorn-Crowned.

---

They went.

Lex didn't want to. He wanted to stay. He wanted to stand in the clearing beside her with his fire and his hammer and Aurelian's little bell round his neck and hold the thorns with her.

He couldn't hold the thorns. Nobody could. Only her.

And there were nine cups in the basket on his back. And a crown in the straw between them. And a world on the other side of the Veil with a King walking through it.

He went.

---

North. Into the path. Into the dark of the thorns. Antho behind him. Hask behind Antho, with Pim in his arms again, the boy limp and white against his chest.

The thorns closed behind them.

Lex heard it. The long dry creak. The groan. The path sealing shut a yard behind Hask's heels.

And through it, behind it, faint, from the clearing—

*Crack. Crack. Crack.*

And over that, dry and soft as sand—

"*Majesty*," said Orrin. "*Majesty, please. Don't. You'll wear yourself out.*"

And Rhosyn's voice. Very small. Very far.

"*...I'm trying...*"

---

The path wound.

North. Through the thorns. Lex couldn't see more than a few yards ahead. Black stems on every side. Dead roses. Thin grey threads of light coming down through the tangle overhead.

He ran.

The basket bumped on his back. Nine cups. A crown. Heavy with nothing.

Behind him, Antho was running. Breathing hard. Further than halfway. Not much further.

Behind Antho, Hask. Heavy. Steady. Breathing all the way down.

Pim didn't make a sound.

---

The thorns ended.

All at once. One moment black stems. The next, nothing. Grey-gold dust. Flat. Empty. Going away north to the edge of everything.

Lex came out of the thorns at a run and stopped.

And looked back.

---

The ring of thorns stood behind him. A mile across. Black. Covered in dead roses. And in the middle, rising above it, small and grey and thin—

The tower.

And round the ring, outside it, on every side but this one—

Gold.

---

Thousands.

He couldn't count them. He didn't try. A ring of gold round the ring of thorns. Three deep. Ten deep. More. The household. The kept. Cooks and grooms and maids and gardeners and soldiers in gold armour and children with gold hoops. Pressed against the black wall of thorns on every side. Pushing.

A finger's width. A finger's width.

*Scrape. Crack. Scrape.*

The thorns breaking against them. And new thorns rising. And breaking. And rising.

And above them all, standing on nothing, a thin bent shape of grey-gold dust with two dim sparks for eyes. Looking down into the ring. Into the clearing. At the woman on her knees.

---

And something else.

Lex saw it. To the south. On the near side of the ring. Where the gold was thickest.

A gleam.

Clear. Cold. Glittering. Like ice. Like frost on a window.

Walking. Through the kept. Between them. They parted for it, the way the thorns parted for Rhosyn. A tall shape. Thin. Long-necked. White-haired. In a long white gown that poured off her like water and not like water.

Glass.

---

"*Ysenne*," said Antho.

He was standing beside Lex. At the edge of the thorns. Breathing hard. Looking south.

"*She said she didn't care*," said Lex. "*In the stone room. She said Oz could have his crown in the road.*"

"*She said she's never once told him no*," said Antho.

---

They watched her walk into the thorns.

They opened for her. Lex saw it. The black wall at the south of the ring, parting. A path. Not because Rhosyn opened it. Because Rhosyn couldn't hold it anymore. Because Rhosyn was on her knees in the clearing with every thorn she had wrapped round a thousand gold servants, and there was nothing left for the south wall but to sag and part.

The Glass Queen walked in.

And the gold followed her. The kept. Pressing through the gap behind her. A finger's width. A finger's width.

---

Lex couldn't see the clearing. The thorns were too high.

He didn't need to.

He heard it.

Very faint. Very far. Coming through a mile of thorns on the dead still air of the Court.

A voice. Small. Like a wet finger round the rim of a glass.

"*Rosie*," said Ysenne.

Silence.

"*Rosie, stop*. *Please. You're so thin. You've given it all away.*" A pause. "*Look at you. You've got nothing left.*"

And then, very small, very far, like a stem bending—

"*...Ysy...*"

"*I'm sorry*," said the Glass Queen. "*He asked. You know he asked. Orrin carried it all the way from the world. He felt the crown go, Rosie. He felt it from the other side of the edge.*" Her voice was shaking. Lex had never heard it shake. "*He said bring her home.*"

"*...I always go...*" whispered Rhosyn. "*...when he calls...*"

"*I know*," said Ysenne. "*So do I.*"

---

And then a sound Lex would hear for a long time.

Not a scream. Not a crack. Not a scrape.

A *chime*.

Small. Clear. High. Like glass touching glass. Like a link of a chain made of ice closing round a thin grey-green wrist.

And another.

*Chime*.

---

The thorns stopped moving.

All of them. All at once. The whole mile-wide ring. The stems that had been rising out of the ground to bind the kept. The stems that had been shuddering against the gold. The black wall round the clearing.

Still.

And then, slowly, from the inside out, they began to sag.

Like a hedge in winter. Like a vine when the root's been cut. The black stems going slack. Drooping. Falling in on themselves with a long low dry sigh that went on and on and on.

And the gold came through.

---

"*She's got her*," said Hask.

Nobody answered.

"*Her sister*," said Hask. Very quietly. "*Her own sister. In chains.*"

"*She said she'd never told him no*," said Antho. "*Not once. In nine hundred years.*"

"*She told Rhosyn she was sorry*."

"*Yes*," said Antho Vey. "*They're all sorry. Every one of them. Every Crowned. Sorry for nine hundred years.*" He looked at the sagging thorns. At the gold pouring through. "*And they all still go when he calls.*"

---

Lex turned away.

He made himself. He turned his back on the ring of dying thorns and the gold pouring through it and the chime of glass chains, faint, from the clearing he couldn't see.

And looked north.

At the dust. Flat. Grey-gold. Going away to the edge of everything.

And at the line.

---

Silver.

Thin. Bright. Rising out of the plain a mile off. At a slant. Up. Into the dim grey-gold of the sky. Up and up and up, until it was too thin to see.

With rings on it. Black. Strung like beads. Going up.

The last thread.

"*That's where it starts*," said Lex.

---

They went to it.

He didn't decide to. His feet did. He walked north across the grey-gold dust with the basket on his back and his brother beside him and Hask behind with Pim, and the silver line got closer, and thicker, and brighter.

And then they were there.

---

It came out of the ground.

Not out of a hole. Not out of a stone. Out of the dust itself. As if the dust were only a skin over something, and the thread had been sewn through it from underneath.

Silver. Bright. Not thin now. Not up close. As thick as Lex's arm at the base. Thicker. Humming. One note. High. Clear. Like a harp-string. Like a wet finger round a glass. The note from the Old Gallery. The note from the bottom of the world.

It rose out of the dust at a slant. North-east. Up. Into the sky. Taut. So taut Lex could feel it in his teeth.

And round it, a yard from the ground, a ring.

Black. Iron. Ten feet across. Smooth. Perfect. No rust. No mark.

The first ring.

And above it, ten yards up, another. And above that, another. And another. Going up. Smaller and smaller. Into the sky. Into the dim. Into nothing.

Caddoc's road.

---

"*He started here*," said Lex.

He put his hand out. Not touching the iron. Near it. A foot away.

Cold. Like the iron at Coldwater. Cold all the way through.

"*He stood here*," said Lex. "*When he woke. When his brother said, the Key. He came out here and stood at the foot of the last thread and put a ring on it and started to climb.*"

"*And he's still climbing*," said Antho.

"*He's under the world*," said Lex. "*Under Coldwater, by now. Past it. Under the Drowned Country.*" He looked up. At the line. At the rings going up into nothing. "*He's months ahead of us. Weeks. However long it's been out there.*"

"*Can we cut it*?" said Hask.

---

Lex looked at the silver thread.

At the iron ring round it.

At the hammer on his belt. Small. Old. Plain. The hammer that had woven the world.

"*Find it*," he said.

---

Hask put Pim down.

Gently. On the dust. One finger at a time. The boy didn't wake.

Then he took his pick off his back. The pick that had broken Delve cores, and a Moth Queen's wing, and the floor of the Old Gallery under Coldwater. He put the point of it against the black iron of the first ring.

And listened.

It took longer than at Coldwater. Much longer. Lex stood beside him with his hand on the bell and watched. Hask moved the point of the pick. A finger's width. Listened. Moved it again. Round the ring. Up. Down. Round.

"*It's better*," said Hask at last. Very quietly. "*Than the one under Coldwater. Tighter. Thicker.*" He moved the pick. "*He wasn't tired, here. This was the first. He was fresh.*"

"*Is there one*?"

Hask didn't answer.

He moved the pick. Listened. Moved it.

And stopped.

"*There*," he said. "*Small. Smaller than a fingernail. Right at the bottom. Where the ring touches the dust.*" He looked up at Lex. "*He bent down. To close it. Right at the start. His back would have been stiff. From the silk.*"

---

Lex took Aurelian's hammer off his belt.

He knelt in the dust. At the foot of the last thread. Where the first ring touched the ground. Where a man nine hundred years old had bent down, stiff-backed from a cocoon, to close a circle of iron round a silver line.

He lifted the hammer.

*Tink*.

---

The ring broke.

The way the one at Coldwater had. A bright line, running round the black iron from the point where the hammer touched. Round. All the way round. And the whole ring rang, a great deep groaning boom, and fell apart into black shards and slid off the thread and lay in the dust.

And the thread sang.

The note went up. A half-step. The way it had in the Old Gallery.

And nothing happened.

---

No grunt. No voice. No *huh*. No new ring growing back.

Nothing.

The silver thread hummed. A half-step higher. Above it, ten yards up, the second ring sat round it. Black. Smooth. Perfect. Holding.

And above that, the third. And the fourth. Going up into the sky.

Lex knelt in the dust with the hammer in his hand and looked up at them.

"*He didn't notice*," said Antho. Very quietly.

"*No*."

"*He's too far up*," said Antho. "*Too far along. He's not standing on this one anymore. He's not standing on any of these.*" He looked up at the rings. "*He's past them all. He's under the world.*"

"*It doesn't matter*," said Lex.

"*What doesn't*?"

"*Where you cut a man's road*," said Lex Vey. He stood up. Slowly. "*If he's already past it.*"

---

He looked at the thread.

Silver. Bright. Humming. Rising out of the dust and going up into the sky, toward the edge of the world, toward the Veil, toward the bottom of everything where an old friend of his father's was wound round a Key in the dark.

He could feel it. The way he'd felt it at Coldwater. The way he'd felt it at the Knot. One note. Going on and on.

*It's not a wall*, Elric had said. *It's a thread. The last one.*

He put his hand on it.

---

Not near it. *On* it.

He didn't know why. He hadn't meant to. His hand went out the way it went out to the fire every morning, and closed round the silver thread at the foot of the first broken ring.

It was cold.

Not like iron. Not like the gold. Cold the way a harp-string is cold, the way a wire is cold, thin and taut and singing.

And it was *alive*.

---

He felt it.

All of it. All at once. Not with the Root; he'd given that up. Not with his eyes. With his *hand*. The way a smith feels a blade he's forging. The way you feel the grain in a piece of iron when you've worked it for twenty years.

The last thread.

Running up from his hand. Out of the Court. Through the Veil. The weave of it. The thickness. The grey corridor they'd walked through, which was only the inside of this, only the thread seen from the middle. On. Under the Sundered Lands. Deep. Under the Greyspine. Under Coldwater, where a black ring stood in a forest of black spikes at the bottom of the Old Gallery.

On. Under the Drowned Country. Down.

To a black glass disc a mile across. To a Key. To a grey thread wound round it, frayed and worn and tight.

And somewhere between—

---

*Clink*.

He felt it. Through his hand. Up his arm. Into his chest. A ring closing. Far away. So far. Under the world. On the thread. Between Coldwater and the Knot.

Closer to the Knot.

Much closer.

*Clink*.

---

And a voice.

Low. Flat. Heavy. Rough as gravel. Coming down the thread. Into his hand.

"*Smith*," said Caddoc of the Iron Crown.

---

Lex didn't let go.

"*You're at the bottom*," said the voice. Not surprised. Interested. The way a mason sounds when he finds a man looking at the foundations of a wall he built. "*At the start. Where I began. I can feel you.*" A pause. "*You broke my first ring.*"

"*Yes*."

"*Huh*," said Caddoc. "*That was a good one. I was fresh. I took a long time over it.*"

"*It had a flaw*."

"*They all have flaws*," said Caddoc. "*I bent down. My back was stiff.*" Another pause. "*It doesn't matter. I'm not on it.*"

"*I know*."

"*I'm close now*," said the voice. Very even. Very calm. "*I can feel the knot. I can feel the man in it. He's very tired.*" A pause. "*I can feel the bar. The ward. The Key under it.*"

Lex's hand tightened on the thread.

"*How close*?"

"*Close*," said Caddoc of the Iron Crown. "*Your lanterns. The night you light them for the lost.*" A pause. "*Not long.*"

---

Lex went cold.

*Not long*.

He'd been counting Pims. Nine cups. Thirteen days. He'd been counting from midsummer. From the edge of the world. From a door his brother opened with his own blood. *Lantern Night*, he'd thought. *Months. The end of autumn. We've got time. We've got the whole summer.*

"*How long have I been here*?" he said.

The thread was quiet.

"*Here*?" said Caddoc. "*In the Court?*"

"*Yes*."

A long pause.

"*There's no time in the Court*," said the voice. Slowly. As if explaining something obvious to a child. "*You know that. Everyone knows that. That's why we slept so long.*" Another pause. "*A day in the Court is a long time outside. Ten days. More. I don't know. I've never counted.*"

Lex's hand was white on the silver thread.

"*Ten days*," he said.

"*Near enough*."

"*For every day here*—"

"*Ten days there*," said Caddoc. "*Yes.*" A pause. "*You didn't know.*"

---

Lex let go of the thread.

He didn't mean to. His hand opened. On its own. And fell away. And he was standing in the dust at the foot of the last thread with his hand hanging at his side and the cold of the silver still in his palm.

*Ten days*.

*For every day*.

He'd been in the Court for—he didn't know. He didn't count. Eleven Pims. Twelve. Thirteen. He'd lost count somewhere after the glass palace.

*A hundred and thirty days*.

*A hundred and forty*.

*Your lanterns. The night you light them for the lost. Not long.*

---

"*Lex*," said Antho.

He didn't answer.

"*Lex*. *What did he say?*"

Lex turned round.

His brother was standing in the dust a yard off. Looking at him. With his face grey and his eyes dark and his hand, his left, with the thin red line across the palm, hanging at his side.

"*It's been months*," said Lex.

Antho went very still.

"*Out there*," said Lex. "*Not days. Not weeks. Months.*" His voice was very strange. He couldn't make it sound like his own. "*A day here is ten there. He said. Caddoc. He said it like it was nothing. Like everyone knew.*"

"*Months*."

"*It's autumn*," said Lex. "*Out there. Now. The end of it.*" He looked east. Toward the white road. Toward the Veil. Toward a door at the edge of the world they'd walked through three days after midsummer. "*It's nearly Lantern Night.*"

---

Nobody said anything.

Hask was standing over Pim. Enormous. Still. His pick in his hand. His face like a slab of coal.

Antho was standing in the dust. Not moving. Not breathing, almost.

"*Father*," said Antho.

Lex closed his eyes.

"*He said before the year turns*," said Antho. Very quietly. "*The Ferryman. Before Lantern Night. And it's*—"

"*I know*."

"*And the King*," said Antho. "*He's been walking. For months. Five miles a day. Less.*" His voice was going. Breaking up. Like ice on a pond. "*He's been walking for months, Lex. Toward Harrow. Toward*—"

"*I know*," said Lex Vey.

---

*[Lex*,] said Ember.

Very small. Very far. Like a voice at the bottom of a well.

*[Lex, I can feel him*.]

*Who*?

*[Your father*.] A pause. *[I've been able to feel him the whole time. Even here. A thread. Thin. Going out of me. Back through the edge. Back to him.]* Another pause. *[It's been going thinner. Every Pim. I thought—I thought it was this place. The pressure. The no room.]*

Lex didn't move.

*[It's not this place*,] said Ember. Very quietly. *[It's him. It's been months, Lex. Out there. And he's been going.]*

*How thin*?

Ember didn't answer.

*Ember*. *How thin?*

*[Very*,] said Ember.

---

He started walking.

East. Not north. Not back toward the thorns. East. Toward the white road. Toward the Veil. Toward the door.

He didn't look to see if they were following.

"*Lex*," said Antho. Behind him. "*The kept. They're between us and the road. Thousands of them. Orrin. Ysenne.*"

"*I know*."

"*We can't go straight back*. *We'll walk right into them.*"

"*Then we'll go round*," said Lex.

"*Round*—"

"*North*," said Lex. "*Then east. Then south to the road. Near the door. Where the kept are thinnest.*" He didn't stop walking. "*Wren taught me. On the moors. You don't go through the herd. You go round it and come at the gate from the side.*"

"*That's days*."

"*It's days here*," said Lex. "*It's weeks there.*" His voice didn't shake. He didn't let it. "*Every Pim we spend in here is ten days out there. Every hour is most of a day.*" He looked back at his brother. "*So we don't stop. We don't sleep. We drink the cups. All of them. We walk till we get there.*"

"*And Pim*?"

Lex looked at the boy. Lying in the dust. White. Limp. Breathing very slow.

"*Hask carries him*," said Lex.

Silas Hask bent down without a word.

---

They walked.

North first. Away from the white road. Away from the ring of thorns, sagging, dying, with gold pouring through it and a Glass Queen in the middle putting chains on her sister. Away from the silver line going up into the sky.

Then east.

The plain didn't change. Grey-gold. Flat. Endless. The light didn't change. The dust didn't move.

Lex didn't stop.

---

He drank a cup when his chest went tight.

Lifted the iron lid—it sighed—and tipped the empty cup to his mouth and felt the room go in. Warm. Round. Mal.

He passed it to Antho. Antho drank. Passed it back.

Eight.

He didn't do the sums. He didn't need to. He knew what eight meant. Eight cups, two people, no Pim holding the nights. Twelve days.

A hundred and twenty days, out there.

Too many. Far too many. They didn't have a hundred and twenty days. Not out there. Not on the other side of the door, where it was the end of autumn and the leaves were coming down and an old man was lying in a bed under a boarded window, breathing.

In. Catch. Out.

Going thin.

---

He thought about Wren.

He didn't mean to. He walked east across the grey-gold dust with the basket on his back and a crown in the straw and his hand on the bell, and he thought about Wren.

Months.

She'd have been fogging windows for months. Every night. Breathing on the glass. Holding the wind.

And the King walking toward her.

And Lark—

*She'll be nearly a year old*, he thought.

He stopped walking.

---

Just for a moment. In the dust. In the middle of the grey-gold plain.

*She'll be nearly a year old*.

*She was five months old when I left. She had one tooth. She'd just rolled over.*

*She'll be sitting up. She'll be crawling. She might be standing. Holding on to things. Pulling herself up on the edge of the table with her square hands.*

*She might have said a word*.

*And I wasn't there*.

---

*[Lex*,] said Ember.

He started walking again.

*[Lex, I'm sorry*.]

*It's not your fault*.

*[I know*.] A pause. *[I'm sorry anyway. I'm sorry I didn't know. I should have known. I'm a historian. I should have read it somewhere.]*

*Nobody knew*.

*[Seraphine would have*,] said Ember. *[If she'd had time. She'd have worked it out. From the frost. From Orrin. From everything.]* A pause. *[I think she probably has. Out there. Months ago.]*

*Then she'll have told them*.

*[Yes*,] said Ember. *[They'll know. They've known for months.]* Very quietly. *[They'll know we're not coming back till Lantern Night.]*

---

They saw the road on the fourth Pim.

Lex didn't call it a Pim anymore. There was no Pim. Pim was asleep against Hask's chest and hadn't woken since the thorns. They walked without stopping. They drank a cup when their chests went tight. They didn't sleep.

Seven cups.

Six.

But Lex counted. In his head. Without wanting to. *Four. That's four of their days. Forty out there.*

---

The white road ran across the plain ahead of them. East to west. Straight as a ruler.

And on it, on both sides, the kept.

Not as many as before. Not here. Not this close to the Veil. A thin line of them, on either side of the white stone. Standing. Still.

Facing west.

All of them. Turned. Toward the palaces. Toward the hall. Toward the ring of dead thorns far off on the horizon, where the rest of the household had gone, scraping, at Orrin's word.

These hadn't gone.

These were too far. Or too slow. Or too old. Standing at the side of the road with their gold faces turned west and their gold eyes moving.

And humming.

*...home...*

---

"*There*," said Antho.

He was pointing. East. Down the road. Toward the Veil. Toward a smudge on the horizon, grey, the edge of the world.

And on the road, a little way short of it, in the middle of the white stone—

A small gold shape.

Walking.

---

Pim woke up.

Lex didn't know how. He hadn't stirred in four of their days. He'd lain against Hask's chest white and limp and empty, breathing so slow that twice Lex had stopped Hask to put his hand on the boy's chest and feel it rise.

He woke now.

All at once. His head lifting. His eyes opening. Grey-violet. Looking past Hask's shoulder. Down the road.

At the small gold shape on the white stone.

"*Ilda*," said Pim.

---

She was further than she'd been.

That was the first thing Lex saw, as they came down onto the road and walked toward her. She was further *west* than she'd been. Further from the door. Further toward the palaces.

Following.

The line of gold scrapes behind her on the white stone went back and back toward the Veil. Toward the place where she'd stood for nine hundred years at the side of the road with a wooden spoon in her hand.

A finger's width every hour.

For weeks. For months. Out there. For however many Pims it had been in here.

Walking after a boy who'd looked back.

---

Pim got down.

He wriggled out of Hask's arms and stood on the white road. Unsteady. Thin. White. In his blue coat with the brass buttons.

And walked to her.

Lex didn't stop him. Antho didn't. Hask didn't. They stood on the road and watched the boy walk the last few yards to the small gold girl in her grey dress too big for her, with her wooden spoon, mid-step.

He stopped in front of her.

Her eyes were on his face.

Not darting. Not fast. Steady. On him.

"*You're still coming*," said Pim.

---

Her mouth didn't move. It couldn't. Not now. He'd poured himself into her once, on the road, and it had been enough for one word. He had nothing left to pour.

But her eyes didn't leave his face.

And a tear ran out of one of them.

Out of the corner. Out of the gold. Clear. Wet. Down her gold cheek. Onto the white stone.

Pim looked at it.

"*I have to go*," he said. "*Through the door. Home. There's a King in our world. And an old man who's dying.*" He swallowed. "*I'll come back. I promised. When the pool's big. When there's room.*"

The gold girl's eyes stayed on his face.

"*Don't follow*," said Pim. "*Not through the door. You'd come apart. Like the cousins.*" His voice was very small. "*Stay on the road. Stay where I can find you.*"

---

He put out his hand.

Not to touch her. He held it out. In the air. A foot from her gold hand. The one without the spoon. Open.

The way he'd drawn it. In pencil. On a page of Seraphine's notebook. A boy holding out his hand.

He held it there for a long time.

Then he took it back. And turned round. And came back up the road to the others.

He didn't look back.

Not once. Not twice.

But Lex did. And saw the gold girl's eyes follow him. All the way. As they walked east down the white road toward the edge of the world.

And behind them, very faint—

*Scrape*.

Not following.

*Staying*.

---

The door was ahead.

Tall. Dark. Wide. In the grey of the Veil. The edge of the world drawn back on either side like curtains. And beyond it, through it, a smudge of other light.

Pale. Thin. Grey.

Not the grey-gold of the Court. Not the grey of the Veil.

*Winter* grey.

Lex stopped on the white road and looked at it.

At the light coming through the door at the edge of the world. Cold. Thin. White at the edges. The light of a short day at the end of the year.

And at something drifting in through the door on a wind that had never once blown in the Court in nine hundred years.

Small. White. Turning over and over in the dim grey-gold air.

Landing on the white stone at his feet.

And melting.

---

"*Snow*," said Antho.

Very quietly. Beside him. Looking at the door.

"*It's snowing*," he said. "*Out there.*"

Lex didn't answer.

He looked at the snow melting on the white stone at his feet. At the door. At the winter light beyond it.

And beyond that—far beyond, two hundred and forty miles east, in a birch wood at the end of a valley, in a cottage with a blue door—

An old man in a bed. Breathing. In. Catch. Out.

Going thin.

He started to run.
