# Down

They lit the lanterns at Harrow before Lex left.

He hadn't meant to stay for it. He'd meant to go at first light. The pool was half a day on Vharuun. The Knot was further. Mal would carry them, but Mal was slow, and careful, and frightened of the hungry place, and Lantern Night was *tonight*.

But Wren had said, *wait*.

And he'd waited.

---

She'd lit the first one at dawn. In the east window of their cottage. Beside the gold hornlight on the sill, which would never glow again. An ordinary candle. In a holder Brannoc had made, with no glass.

Then she'd gone across the stream.

To the blue-door cottage. To the west window. Where the boards had come down at last, all of them, and the glass behind them was soaped white. She'd taken a cloth from her pocket and wiped one pane clear. Just one. And set a candle in front of it.

Facing west. Facing the road. Facing the gold path going up through the birch wood, and the grave by the stream, and the grey stone with two words on it.

"*For Arion*," she'd said. "*From Wren.*"

---

Then Seren.

His mother had come out of the blue door with something in her arms. Wrapped in a cloth. Square. Small.

Lex knew it before she unwrapped it. He'd made it. In the spring. Before midsummer. Before the crossing. At the little forge in the lean-to, late, when he couldn't sleep.

*In case I'm not back*, he'd said. To Brannoc. Handing it over. *For Lantern Night. Put it in the west window.*

He'd thought he'd be back. He'd thought it'd be a fortnight. He'd made it anyway.

*In case*.

---

She didn't unwrap it.

She stood on the oak ramp with it in her arms and looked at him.

"*He said look on Lantern Night*," she said. "*Not before. He said, I'd like to see your face when you read it.*" Her voice was very steady. "*He won't see it.*"

"*Mam*—"

"*So I'll wait*," said Seren Harrow. "*Till you come back up. Till tonight. Till you're standing here.*" She held the bundle against her chest. "*And I'll unwrap it then. And you can see my face instead.*"

He looked at her for a long time.

"*All right*," he said.

---

And then everyone.

He didn't know where they came from. They came up the track through the birch wood in the grey morning. Not many. A dozen. Twenty. People from Ashbourne. People who'd stayed, when the King came down into the Vale, when everyone else went east.

Hollis Penn the baker, who'd walked from Highcrest with a sack of bread on his back because he said nobody should go to the bottom of the world on an empty stomach.

Old Mag. On a cart. Pulled by Tilda, with her gold hand on the shaft and her other hand on the reins. Wrapped in three shawls with her cat in her lap and her one bright black eye on everything.

Mother Orla from the Crooked Kettle. Pell, in his ranger's green. The Dunstan twins, who were Brawlers now and going to be Wardens.

Each of them carrying a lantern.

---

They didn't say much.

They came into the clearing and set their lanterns down. In the snow. By the grave. By the stream. In a ring. Iron ones and tin ones and clay ones and one carved out of a turnip, because Pell's little sister had made it and wouldn't be left out.

And lit them.

Not waiting. Not for anyone. Given.

*For Arion*, said Hollis Penn. Setting his down. A plain tin one. *From the bakery. He liked the seed cake.*

*For Arion*, said Mother Orla. *From the Kettle. He never once paid his tab.*

*For Arion Vey*, said Old Mag. From the cart. In her cracked old voice. While Tilda set the lantern down for her, an old brass one, green with age. *From Mag. Who was ninety-six. And never greedy.*

---

Lex stood on the ramp and watched them.

He couldn't speak. He stood there with the basket on his back and the crown in the straw and Aurelian's hammer on his belt and watched twenty people from a village he'd been born near set lanterns down in the snow round his father's grave.

*[They liked him*,] he thought. In the dry voice. In the shape.

*I didn't know*.

*[Nobody ever knows*,] said the shape. *[Till Lantern Night.]*

---

Lark sat on the ramp beside him.

Wren had set her down there. On a sheepskin. Wrapped in so many blankets only her face showed. Round. Fierce. With her fluff of dark hair standing up like a bird's crest.

She looked at the lanterns.

At the ring of small gold flames in the snow round the grave. At the faces of the people who'd brought them. At the candle in the west window.

And laughed.

Not the wind laugh. Not the gold laugh. Something else. Small. Delighted. The laugh of someone who has never seen so many lights in one place and thinks they've all been lit for her.

"*It's her birthday*," said Wren. Very quietly. Standing behind him. "*She thinks they're for her.*"

"*Some of them are*," said Lex.

---

He knelt down.

On the ramp. In front of his daughter. On the sheepskin.

She looked at him.

"*I've got to go*," he said. "*Down. To the bottom of the world. To help a friend of your grandfather's.*" He swallowed. "*I'll be back tonight. I'll try.*"

She frowned at him.

"*It's your birthday*," said Lex Vey. "*You're one. A year ago tonight you were born on a heap of rubble with the whole world coming home round you.*" His voice cracked. "*And I was there. I was right there. You came out and you screamed once and stopped. Like you'd made your point.*"

Lark looked at him.

Then she reached out. One square fist. Out of the blankets. And put it flat on his chest. Over his shirt. Over the bell on its cord.

And pushed.

Hard. The way she pushed off his hand to stand.

"*No*," said Lark.

---

He didn't know what she meant.

He'd never know. She was one. She had six teeth and one word and a grip like a vice.

He thought she might mean *don't go*.

He thought she might mean *go on, then*.

He thought, kneeling on the ramp with his daughter's fist on his chest and twenty lanterns burning in the snow behind him, that she probably meant both. That she was a Harrow and a Vey and an Ashdown and a smith and a Mistwalker, and she could mean everything at once.

"*I know*," he said.

And kissed her on the forehead. Quickly. Fiercely. Like a seal stamped.

And stood up.

And went.

---

The pool lay in the green.

Under snow. The whole circle. A hundred miles of white with green underneath it, coming through in patches where the wind had blown the snow thin. And in the middle, a pool. Round. Dark. Grey-rimmed. Not frozen.

It never froze. Ivy said. It couldn't. There was too much room in it for ice.

Hask was standing on the rim.

With Kell's stick in his good hand and his gold arm hanging at his side. Coughing. He'd come back on Vharuun with them, the morning after the burial, and gone straight to the pool and taken the stick out of Kit's hands and stood in front of it.

He hadn't moved since.

---

Ivy Moss was sitting where she always sat.

On the far side of the rim. With her bad foot tucked under her and her good one dangling over the deep. With her crutch on the stone beside her.

And beside her, with his small thin legs dangling too—

Pim.

He'd come too. On Ashka. Behind Antho. He hadn't asked. He'd been in the clearing at Harrow at dawn, sitting on the mounting block, with his blue coat buttoned to the chin.

He'd slept for two days. He'd woken. He'd eaten four bowls of porridge and half a loaf of Hollis Penn's bread. He was still white. Still thin. Still nearly empty.

He'd come anyway.

"*I can't make room*," he'd said. To Antho. In the clearing. Three words. Then more. "*Not much. Not yet. I'm still empty.*" He'd looked at the dragons. "*But I can sit with Ivy. While you're down. So it's not frightened on its own.*"

---

Lex knelt on the rim.

*Mal*, he thought.

*[...smith...]*

It came up out of the pool. Out of the ground. Out of everywhere. Small. Soft. Young.

Frightened.

*[...smith, it's TONIGHT...]* The pull. The tilt. The whole snowy slope leaning toward him. *[...the iron one...I can feel him...he's at the edge...at the cold place...where I used to be...]*

*I know*.

*[...the grey one's so tired...]*

*I know*.

*[...you want to go DOWN...]*

*I want to go down*, thought Lex. *Tonight. Now. With my brother. With a crown.*

The pool was silent for a long time.

*[...the old man went...]* said Mal. Very small. *[...I felt him...on the river...in the boat...he went past me...]* A pause. *[...he said thank you...for the room...]*

Lex couldn't breathe.

*[...there was a light...]* said Mal. Wondering. *[...in the boat...gold...it said goodbye to me...it said, look after the smith...]*

---

Lex put his face in his hands.

On the rim of the pool. In the snow. With Antho kneeling beside him and Hask standing over them and Ivy and Pim on the far side, very still.

He didn't make a sound.

The pool leaned toward him. Softly. Like a dog leaning against your legs.

*[...smith...]*

*I'm here*.

*[...I'll take you...]* said Mal. *[...down...to the cold place...I'll be SO careful...]* A pause. *[...I'm frightened...]*

*I know*.

*[...I'll take you anyway...]* said the seventh Well. *[...the light said look after you...]*

---

They went down.

---

Lex didn't remember most of it. The dark closing over him, warm and close. The soft room. His brother beside him in it. The basket on his back, heavy with nothing.

Going down.

Mal going down faster than before. Not careless. Not the slow careful steps of carrying an old man in a chair. *Faster*. Because it was Lantern Night. Because something iron was at the edge of the cold place. Because a light in a boat had said *look after the smith*.

*[...all right...all right...I've got you...]*

He felt the threads. All round. Four million of them. Pulling in toward one point. Humming.

And one of them, among them, louder than all the rest—

*Clink*.

---

The room opened.

Grey light. Cold. The black glass.

He stepped out of the soft dark onto the lid of the world. Barefoot. Like his mother. Antho beside him.

And looked across it.

---

The Key. Standing up out of the glass in the middle of the lid like a sword out of a stone. Black iron. Bow, bit and blade.

The Lockward beside it. Grey. Notched like a mountain range. With its thin line of gold runes. *STAY*.

The grey thread wound round them both. Tight. Frayed. A grey cocoon. And above it, a smudge on the air. Bent. Still.

And a few paces off, standing on the black glass, a man.

---

Not big. Lex had expected big. He'd seen the King.

Ordinary. Middling height. Square. Heavy-shouldered. In a black coat rotten at the hem. With black hair cut short and a face like a closed door. Iron chains looped over both shoulders, a few coils left. And on his head a band of black iron, a hand wide, a finger thick, like the iron ring of a gate.

He'd turned round.

He was looking at them. At the two brothers standing barefoot at the edge of the glass. At the soft dark mouth of Mal's room behind them, open in the air.

He didn't move.

---

The smudge above the Key lifted its head.

"*Well*," said Elric Thorne. "*Took you long enough, lad.*"

---

Lex didn't answer.

He was looking at the man with the chains.

*Iron for the soldier who never once laughed*.

"*Caddoc*," he said.

The man looked at him. With black eyes under heavy brows.

"*Smith*," said Caddoc of the Iron Crown.

---

Nobody moved.

The threads hummed. The grey cocoon on the Key trembled. Very slightly. Behind Lex, he could feel Mal's room. Open. Shaking. Waiting.

"*You broke two of my rings*," said Caddoc.

"*Yes*."

"*One under the mountains*. *One at the start.*" The dark face didn't change. "*Nobody's ever broken one before. Not in nine hundred years.*"

"*They had flaws*."

"*Everything has flaws*," said Caddoc. "*I've been thinking about it. All the way down.*" He looked at Lex. At the hammer on his belt. "*Everything has a place where whoever made it stopped for a breath. You find it with a pick. And then you tap it with that.*"

"*Yes*."

"*Huh*," said Caddoc. He looked at Lex for a long moment. "*That's very good.*"

---

"*Go back*," said Lex.

The Crowned king didn't answer.

"*Go back*," said Lex again. "*Up the thread. To the Court. To your brother. Tell him no.*"

"*Everyone keeps telling me to tell him no*," said Caddoc.

"*Then tell him*."

"*I can't*," said the iron king. "*I gave my word. On the shingle. I said I'd go where he went and do what he asked.*" He shifted his weight. The chains clinked on his shoulders. "*He's not in the Court anymore. He's in your world. Walking west. Toward the edge.*" A pause. "*Waiting for me to bring him this.*"

He looked at the Key.

---

"*He was at our house*," said Lex.

Caddoc looked back at him.

"*Your brother*," said Lex. "*At Harrow. Two nights ago. At tea.*" His voice was very steady. He didn't know how. "*He sat on the floor of my mother's cottage with a cup she made him that went gold in his hand. And he cried.*"

The dark face didn't move.

"*My father told him about the door*," said Lex. "*Your father's door. The one he stood outside for a month. With his hand on the latch.*" He swallowed. "*My father told him what was on the other side. That it was just your father. Going. With you holding his hand.*"

Caddoc went very still.

"*He said*, *you should have gone in. He'd have liked you there.*" Lex looked at the iron king. "*And your brother put his face in his hands.*"

---

Caddoc didn't say anything for a long time.

The chains on his shoulders didn't clink. He didn't move at all. He stood on the black glass with his square hands at his sides and his heavy face like a closed door.

"*And then*?" he said at last.

"*And then he asked my father if he could keep him*," said Lex. "*And my father said no, thank you. I've had a very long life and a very good wife.*"

"*And then*?"

"*And then he asked my daughter*," said Lex Vey. "*She's one. Tonight. It's her birthday.*" His voice cracked. "*He held out his hand and asked if he could keep her. And she said no.*"

Caddoc looked at him.

"*Her first word*," said Lex. "*No.*"

---

The iron king didn't move.

And then, very slowly, the corner of his mouth moved.

Not a smile. Lex didn't think Caddoc had smiled in nine hundred years. Didn't think he knew how.

A *twitch*.

"*Hesper's*," said Caddoc of the Iron Crown.

"*Yes*."

"*Hes said no to all of us*," said Caddoc. "*In the garden. In the hall. On the shingle.*" The twitch at the corner of his mouth didn't go away. "*I nodded at her. On the shingle. I thought she'd won. I thought she was the only one of us who did.*"

"*She was*," said Lex.

"*Yes*," said Caddoc. "*I think so.*"

---

He turned round.

Away from them. Toward the Key.

"*It doesn't change anything*," he said. Not looking back. "*I'm sorry, smith. I am. I'm sorry about your father. I'm sorry about your daughter's birthday.*" He took a step toward the Lockward. "*I gave my word.*"

"*Caddoc*—"

"*I'll be quick*," said the iron king.

And put his hand on the Lockward.

---

The grey thread screamed.

---

Lex had never heard a thread scream.

He'd heard the Well scream, the spring before last, when Hask broke a core. He'd heard Antho scream with it. He'd heard a Moth Queen scream on the rubble of Duskhold.

He'd never heard this.

High. Thin. Fraying. Like a rope on a winch when the load's too heavy and the strands start to go, one by one, *ping*, *ping*, *ping*. The whole grey cocoon on the Key and the Lockward shuddering. Tightening. Pulling in round the black iron and the grey stone like a fist clenching.

Holding.

"*Let go, Thorne*," said Caddoc. His square hand on the Lockward's hilt. Pulling. "*It's over. Let go.*"

"*No*," said Elric.

---

Lex moved.

He didn't think. He hadn't thought at the Old Gallery, or the foot of the thread, or a field of gold at the edge of the world. His hand went to his belt and came up with Aurelian's hammer and he was across the black glass before Antho could shout.

*Find it*, he thought. *There's always one. Everything made has one.*

He didn't have Hask. He didn't have a pick that heard where things wanted to come apart.

He had something else.

---

He'd found it on the rubble of Duskhold. A year ago. On Lantern Night. With a Moth Queen standing over him, Sovereign, Tier V, beautiful and starving and hollow. He'd reached out with his tink and found her flaw.

*FLAW-SENSE*, the net had called it. *He hears where things are tired*.

He reached out with it now.

Not at the Lockward. Not at the Key.

At the chains.

---

Iron. Black. Looped over Caddoc's shoulders. A few coils left. Smooth. Perfect. No rust.

Made.

And everything made has a place where whoever made it stopped for a breath.

He found it.

The third link from the end. On the left shoulder. Where the chain had been wound on, nine hundred years ago, by a square dark man in a cocoon of silk, before he went to sleep. Where his hands had been cold. Where he'd paused, half asleep, and closed the link a hair's-breadth too loose.

*Tink*.

---

The link broke.

The chain fell off Caddoc's shoulder. Clattering on the black glass. Ringing. A loop of black iron, coiling, spilling.

Caddoc didn't flinch.

He didn't let go of the Lockward. He didn't turn round. He stood with his hand on the hilt and his face like a closed door and said, very calmly—

"*That one was old*."

And the chain on the glass moved.

---

It came up off the black lid like a snake.

Not slowly. Not like the iron at Coldwater, swelling, groaning. *Fast*. The loose end whipping up off the glass and round. Round Lex's ankle. Round his calf. Round his knee. Tightening. Cold. So cold. Colder than anything.

He went down.

On the black glass. On his back. The hammer flying out of his hand. Skittering. Spinning away across the lid.

The chain went on winding. Round his thighs. His waist. His chest. Binding his arms to his sides. Pulling tight.

*Iron for the soldier*.

*Binding*.

That was what Caddoc was. That was what he'd always been. Not keeping. Not looking. Not growing. *Binding*. Holding things where they were. Chaining them. Like a gaol. Like a gate.

Lex lay on the black glass bound from his ankles to his shoulders in nine-hundred-year-old iron and couldn't move.

---

"*I'm sorry*," said Caddoc. Not turning round. "*I'm not cruel.*"

"*LEX*," shouted Antho.

---

His brother came across the glass.

Lex saw him. From where he lay. Turning his head. Antho running. Barefoot. His black hair flying. His hands up. Dark coming out of them. Shade. Commanding. The dark that wasn't the Hollow's dark anymore, that was just the dark between stars.

He threw it.

Not at Caddoc. *Under* him.

A door.

---

Lex felt it open.

On the black glass. Under Caddoc's boots. A hole. Not in the glass. In the *space*. In the air just above the lid, where the iron king was standing. A Hollow Gate. The oldest trick Antho had. A short rift. A door from here to somewhere else.

Caddoc dropped.

Into it. All at once. Gone. Through the door. The Lockward still in his fist—

No. The Lockward stayed. Lex saw it. Caddoc's hand had been on the hilt and the door opened under him and he fell and his hand came off the hilt and the Lockward stayed in its slot in the glass beside the Key, grey, notched, *STAY*.

And on the far side of the black glass, a hundred paces off, another door opened. In the air. And Caddoc came out of it.

On his feet.

He'd landed on his feet.

---

He looked round.

At where he was. At the distance between him and the Key. At Antho, standing in the middle of the glass with his hands still dark.

"*Huh*," said Caddoc. "*A door.*"

"*I'm a Threshold Warden*," said Antho. Breathing hard. "*It's what I do.*"

"*You put me somewhere else*."

"*I can do it again*," said Antho Vey. "*Every time you get near it. As many times as it takes. All night. Till the lanterns go out.*"

Caddoc looked at him.

For a long time. With his black eyes under his heavy brows.

"*Can you*?" he said.

---

The chains came off his shoulders.

All of them. All at once. Not thrown. *Sent*. The last coils of black iron unwinding off the iron king's shoulders and going out across the black glass like a net cast over the side of a boat.

At Antho.

---

His brother opened a door.

In the air. In front of him. The chains went into it. Lex saw them go in. Saw the dark round the edges of the door, the Shade, the way between.

And come out.

*Behind* him.

Caddoc had sent them through. Lex didn't know how. He'd sent his chains into Antho's door and out of the other side of it, the way you'd thread a needle. Iron through dark.

They wrapped round Antho from behind.

His arms. His chest. His legs. Binding. Pulling him down. Onto the glass.

He fell.

---

The two of them lay on the black glass.

Bound. Both of them. In nine-hundred-year-old iron. Lex near the Key. Antho in the middle of the lid. Ten yards apart. Unable to move.

Caddoc walked back across the glass.

*Thud*. *Thud*. Not hurrying.

He passed Antho. He didn't look at him. He passed Lex. He didn't look at him either.

He stopped at the Lockward.

---

"*Thorne*," he said.

The smudge above the Key didn't answer.

"*Thorne*. *Let go.*"

"*No*."

"*You're going to tear*," said Caddoc of the Iron Crown. Very quietly. Almost gently. "*Your thread. If I pull and you hold. It'll come apart. Like a rope.*" He put his hand on the hilt of the Lockward. "*It'll hurt. And you'll go. And I'll have it anyway.*"

"*Then you'll have it anyway*," said Elric Thorne.

"*Why hold*?"

The smudge didn't answer for a moment.

"*Because I said I would*," said Elric. "*Three years ago. At the lid. I said I'd hold it.*" A dry rasp. "*I don't break my word either, Iron. Not anymore.*"

---

Caddoc looked at the grey smudge for a long time.

Then he nodded.

Once.

The way he'd nodded at Hesper on the shingle. The way he'd nodded at his sister when she was born.

"*All right*," he said.

And pulled.

---

The scream went on and on.

Lex lay on the black glass bound in iron with his face turned toward the Key and watched.

Watched the iron king brace his feet and pull on the Lockward's hilt with both square hands. Watched the grey thread round it tighten. Fray. Snap. *Ping*. A strand. *Ping*. Another. Unwinding from the cocoon in thin grey wisps that floated up into the dark and faded.

Watched the Lockward move.

A finger's width. Up. Out of the slot in the glass. A finger's width. Grinding. Grey stone on black glass.

*STAY*, said the last rune. Near the hilt. In Lex's hand.

It didn't stay.

---

"*ELRIC*," Lex shouted.

The scream didn't stop.

"*ELRIC*. *THE CROWN.*"

He didn't know if Elric could hear him. He didn't know if a thread being torn apart at the bottom of the world could hear anything at all.

He shouted anyway.

"*THE CROWN*. *IN THE BASKET. ON MY BACK.*"

He couldn't reach it. He was lying on it. On his back. Bound. With the basket crushed under him and the iron round his arms and the three last Hush-cups and a plain grey silver circle in the straw, pressed into the black glass under his spine.

He couldn't reach it.

---

The Lockward came out.

---

It came all at once. The last finger's width. A grinding. A crack. A sound like a bell struck once and broken.

And the grey stone came up out of the slot in the black glass in Caddoc's square hands.

And the thread round it—

Lex watched it go.

Not all. The thread was still round the Key. Wound round the black iron. Bow, bit and blade. Holding. Thinner now. So thin. Frayed and torn and shredded, like a rope that's been dragged across a rock.

But round the Lockward, nothing. The grey stone in Caddoc's hands was bare. The thread that had been wound round it for three years hung off it in shreds. Floating. Fading.

The scream stopped.

---

Silence.

The threads hummed. Four million of them. All round. In the dark.

The grey smudge above the Key hung very still. Very faint. Almost not there at all. Like the ghost of a figure in a misted mirror.

Caddoc stood on the black glass with the Lockward in his hands.

He looked at it.

At the grey stone. Notched like a mountain range. At the thin line of gold runes. At the last one, near the hilt.

*STAY*.

"*Good work*," he said. Very quietly. "*It nearly held.*"

---

He set it down.

On the glass. Gently. The way you'd set down something someone had made very carefully, that deserved to be set down gently.

And turned to the Key.

---

"*Thorne*," he said.

The smudge didn't answer.

"*Thorne*. *You're still on it.*"

The thread round the Key trembled. Thin. Shredded. Holding.

"*You've got no bar now*," said Caddoc. "*No ward. Nothing to hold it but you. And you're nearly gone.*" He put out his square hand. Toward the Key. Toward the black iron bow sticking up out of the lid. "*Let go. It's over. I'll take it to my brother. You can rest.*"

"*No*," said Elric.

Barely a sound. A rasp. A breath with no breath in it.

"*No*," said Elric Thorne again. "*Not you. Not ever.*"

---

Caddoc stopped.

His hand. An inch from the bow of the Key.

Stopped.

He looked at the smudge for a long time.

"*That's hers*," he said. "*Hes's. That's what she said. On the shingle.*"

"*I know*," said Elric. "*I heard it. Up the thread. From a hedge-witch. Through a rose.*" A dry rasp. Almost a laugh. "*I've been practising.*"

---

Antho moved.

Lex saw it. Out of the corner of his eye. Ten yards off. In the middle of the black glass. His brother. Bound in iron. Chains round his arms, his chest, his legs.

*Moving*.

Not struggling. Antho wasn't struggling. He was lying very still with his eyes shut and his face very calm, the face he got when he was holding a door.

And the chain round him—

*Opened*.

---

Not broke. Lex would have heard it break. It didn't break.

It *opened*. A link. One. In the middle of the chain round Antho's chest. Lex saw it. Saw the black iron loop, closed, perfect, smooth, made nine hundred years ago by a square dark man in a cocoon—

Open. Like a door.

And the chain fell off him.

---

"*Every closed thing is a door*," said Antho. Getting up. On his knees. On the glass. Breathing hard. His face white. "*A ring's a door. A link's a door. It's shut. That's all.*" He looked at Caddoc. "*I'm a Threshold Warden. I open doors.*"

Caddoc turned round.

He looked at Antho. At the chain on the glass round him. At the one open link.

"*Huh*," said the iron king.

---

Antho went for Lex.

Not for Caddoc. Not for the Key. For Lex. Across the glass. On his knees. Scrambling. His hands out.

He got there.

He got his hands under Lex. Under his back. Under the crushed basket.

"*Hold still*," said Antho.

"*I can't move*."

"*Then that's easy*," said his brother.

And reached into the basket.

---

Lex felt him find it.

Through the straw. Past the iron cups, cold. Past the iron lids. To the thing in the middle. Plain. Grey. Silver. A circle a little bigger than a man's hand could span.

With seven points.

Antho's hand closed round it.

And the cold came up through the basket and through Lex's back and through the iron round him like a hand laid flat on a door.

*No*.

---

Caddoc felt it.

Lex saw him feel it. Saw the dark head jerk round. Saw the black eyes go wide. Saw the square hands, which had been reaching for the Key, stop.

"*What*—" said the iron king.

Antho pulled it out.

Out of the basket. Out of the straw. Out from under Lex's back. A plain grey silver circle in a Threshold Warden's burned hands.

And the *no* came out with it.

---

It filled the Knot.

Lex felt it. All round. Pouring out of the crown in Antho's hands. Across the black glass. Into the dark. Into the threads. Cold. Quiet. Patient. Steady.

*Not you. Not ever.*

Twenty generations of it. Every Harrow woman who'd kept the box. Every one who'd moved on at the end of summer. Every one who'd broken the song on Rhosyn's name.

A girl of seventeen on a shingle. With her voice shaking.

A baby of one on a sheepskin. With her fist in her mouth.

*No*.

---

Caddoc stepped back.

One step. Away from the Key. Away from the crown. His square hands coming up. Not to fight. To shield his face. The way a man shields his face from a cold wind.

"*Hesper's*," he said.

Very quietly. Wonderingly.

"*That's Hesper's*."

---

"*Elric*," said Antho.

He was kneeling on the glass beside Lex. With the crown in both his hands. Looking at the Key. At the shredded grey thread still wound round it. At the smudge above it, so faint, almost gone.

"*Elric*," he said again. "*Catch.*"

And threw it.

---

Lex watched it go.

He couldn't move. He lay on his back on the black glass bound in iron with his head turned and watched a plain grey silver crown spin through the air across the lid of the world.

Turning. Over and over. The seven blunt points catching the cold grey light. Not shining. Silver gone soft and grey with nine hundred years of dark. Turning.

Toward the Key.

Toward the grey smudge above it.

Toward a thread that had been a man, that had been a herring-fisher's son with a stolen book, that had been a Stormcaller and a mentor and a traitor and the first friend a lonely Warden-Architect had in two hundred years.

That had always wanted to go first.

---

The thread reached up.

A strand of it. Grey. Thin. Shredded. Unwinding from the Key. Reaching up into the air. The way it had reached across a foot of glass in the spring to touch an old man's fingers.

The way it had reached across the gap to a young man in the bow of a boat.

And caught the crown.
