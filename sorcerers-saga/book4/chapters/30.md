# The Crack

Antho ran.

Down the white road. Toward the door. With his brother ahead of him and the basket bouncing on Lex's back and the snow coming in through the tall dark gap in the grey, drifting, turning, melting on the white stone.

Snow. In the Court. Where nothing had moved for nine hundred years.

He'd never seen anything so beautiful.

He'd never been so frightened.

---

*Months*.

He couldn't stop thinking it. Every step. Every breath. *Months. Months. It's been months.*

His father. In a bed under a boarded window. With the Lantern burning beside him. Three panes of glass and one of horn.

*I'll try*, he'd said. *I'll breathe in and out until you walk back through that door. That's all I'm going to do. That's my whole job now.*

For months.

---

Seraphine. At the Keep. At the top of the passage stair. Writing in her book. Counting the lanterns for him because he wasn't there to count them. One thousand, one hundred and thirteen. One of them gold.

For months.

*Reason six hundred and five. He checks.*

He hadn't checked. Not in months. Not once. He'd been walking down a white road in a country with no days, thinking it had been a fortnight.

---

His mother. Wren. The baby.

The King.

---

"*Antho*," said Lex. Ahead of him. Not turning round. "*Behind us.*"

He looked back.

---

The road behind them was moving.

Not the road. The *kept*. On either side of it. The thin line of gold servants who'd been standing at the edges of the white stone near the Veil, facing west. Too far, Antho had thought. Too slow. Too old.

They'd turned round.

All of them. Every one. Facing east now. Facing the door. Facing four people running down the middle of the road toward it.

And walking.

*Scrape*. *Scrape*. *Scrape*.

A finger's width. A finger's width. Not like Ilda, an hour between each step. *Fast*. A step every breath. Every heartbeat. The gold feet scraping on the white stone. The gold hands reaching. A washerwoman. A groom. A boy with a hoop. An old man with a stick.

Coming.

---

And over them, above them, standing on nothing—

A thin bent shape of grey-gold dust. With two dim sparks for eyes.

"*Forgive me*," said Orrin. "*Forgive me, masters. He asked.*"

---

"*Faster*," said Lex.

They ran faster.

The door was close now. Very close. A hundred yards. Tall. Dark. Wide. The grey of the Veil drawn back on either side of it like curtains. The thorns still hanging off the edges, snapped, dangling, the ones Rhosyn had sent into the crack before they came. Black. Dead.

And beyond it, through it, white.

Snow. Falling. A grey winter sky. Flat bare ground, white. The cold light of a short day at the end of the year.

The world.

---

"*Masters*," said Orrin. Behind them. Above them. Closer. "*Please. Stop. He doesn't want you hurt. He only wants the crown.*"

Nobody stopped.

"*He felt it go*," said the steward. His voice like sand poured out of a sack, following them on the dead still air. "*From the chair. From the tower. In your world, at your mother's door, sitting on her step. He felt it lift.*" A pause. "*He's very sad. He didn't know it was there. He didn't know Rosie had it. He'd have left it alone, he says, if he'd known. He says it was hers.*"

"*Then let us take it home*," shouted Lex. Not stopping. "*If it's hers.*"

"*He can't*," whispered Orrin. "*He knows what you want it for. The Key. The knot. The last turn.*" The dust was very close now. Antho could feel it. On the back of his neck. Like cold sand. "*He can't let you have the one thing that says no to him.*"

---

The dust came down.

Antho felt it before he saw it. A cold. A hiss. A soft dry falling, all round, like sleet. Grey-gold. Glittering. Out of the air. Out of the thin bent shape overhead.

Not the gold of the Court. Not the gold that slid off Hask and couldn't stick to anyone who drank.

*His*.

The King's gold. From the pouch. Silk, tied with a gold cord. A pinch of his. For anyone who wants it.

Orrin was scattering it.

Like seed.

---

"*COVER*," shouted Antho.

He didn't know what he meant. There was nothing to cover with. There was nothing on the white road but white stone and gold servants and the door, eighty yards off, sixty, with the snow coming through it.

He threw his arm up over his face.

He felt it land. On his sleeve. On his hand. Cold. Like sleet.

And then *go in*.

---

He'd seen it in Tilda. In Wren. In Bet Dale and the boy at the Veil and nine thousand people on a white road.

He'd never felt it.

It felt like nothing.

That was the worst thing. A patch on the back of his hand. The left. The one he'd cut at the edge of the world. Cold for a moment. And then not cold. Not warm. Not anything. Just *stopped*. Stopped being his.

He looked at it.

Gold.

A patch the size of a coin. On the back of his hand. Spreading.

---

He couldn't do what Lex did. He couldn't find the room in front of it. He wasn't the Hollow. He was a door.

He ran anyway.

With the gold spreading on the back of his hand. Toward the knuckles. Toward the wrist. With the dust falling round him like sleet. With the scraping behind him, faster, faster, a step every breath.

Forty yards.

---

"*PIM*," shouted Hask.

Antho looked round.

The big man was behind him. Running. Heavy. Steady. With the boy in his arms. And the dust falling on him. On his shaved head. On his shoulders. On the scarred leather of his jerkin.

Not sliding off.

*Sticking*.

---

Antho saw it. Saw Hask see it.

The dust landing on Hask's bare head. On his neck. On his arms. And *staying*. Not rolling off like rain off oilcloth. Not sliding. Settling. Spreading.

Going in.

Hask's face changed.

"*It's sticking*," he said. Running. Not stopping. "*It's sticking. It never*—"

He didn't finish.

He didn't need to. Antho knew. He'd known since the thorns. Since a big man knelt in the dust with a Crowned queen's hand in his and cried for the first time since he was nine.

*It thinks I'm one of theirs*, Hask had said. In the cellar at Harrow. With the gold sliding off him. *It won't keep what it thinks it's already got*.

He wasn't one of theirs anymore.

He'd said sorry. To a pool. With a Warden's stick in his hand. He'd whistled back to his dead sister in a glass palace and she'd smiled. He'd knelt in the dust and held Rhosyn's hand and wept.

He'd changed.

And the gold knew.

---

Hask curled.

Running. He curled himself round the boy. His enormous shoulders hunched. His head bent down over Pim's. His arm—his right arm, his pick-arm, the one with the black iron scar from Coldwater—came up over the boy like a roof.

The dust came down on it.

Antho watched it happen. Running. Thirty yards from the door.

The gold landed on Hask's right arm. On the back of the hand. On the wrist. On the forearm, where the iron scar was, black, a line from wrist to elbow.

And went in.

---

From the hand. Up. Fast. Faster than in Antho's own hand. As if the iron scar had left a road for it. The fingers. The palm. The wrist. Up the forearm. Over the black scar, which went gold, a gold seam in gold skin, like a vein of ore in a rock.

Up.

To the elbow.

And Hask kept running. With his gold arm curled over the boy. With his teeth set. With his face grey.

Not making a sound.

Coldwater hewers don't.

---

Twenty yards.

"*LEX*," shouted Antho. "*HASK'S ARM*—"

His brother turned. Running. Saw.

And stopped.

---

"*Don't stop*—"

Lex didn't listen. He never listened. He'd never listened to anyone in his life, Wren said, except when they were telling him something he already knew.

He stopped on the white road with the dust coming down round him and the kept scraping toward him, ten yards back now, eight, and turned, and caught Hask's gold arm in both his hands as the big man came past.

And shut his eyes.

---

Antho saw his face.

The listening face. The one he'd had kneeling by Tilda on the green at Ashbourne. Kneeling by Wren in the dark kitchen at Harrow.

Finding the room.

In front of the gold. Above the elbow. Where it was still creeping. Where it hadn't set.

Antho felt it open. A gap. A breath. A room with nothing in it.

And the gold came to it.

And stopped.

---

At the elbow.

Not past. Not up the arm to the shoulder. Not across the chest to the heart.

At the elbow.

And didn't go back.

Lex opened his eyes. Looked at the arm. Gold from the fingertips to the elbow. Set. Finished. The iron scar a gold seam down the middle of it.

"*It's set*," he said. Hoarsely. "*I'm sorry. It went too fast. It was already*—"

"*It's my pick-arm*," said Silas Hask.

Lex looked up at him.

"*I'll learn left*," said Hask. Very rough. "*Tilda did. Go.*"

---

The scraping was five yards back.

Four.

A gold washerwoman with a gold basket. Reaching. Her gold hand an arm's length from Lex's back. Her gold eyes moving. Slowly. To the basket on his back. To the crown in the straw.

And over her, above her, the dust. Thin. Bent. Two dim sparks.

"*Masters*," whispered Orrin. "*Please.*"

---

And the snow lit up.

---

Not gold. Not the deep heavy gold of the King. Not the grey-gold of the Court.

*White*. Clear. Cold. Glittering. Like frost. Like ice over deep water. Like a thousand panes of glass catching a winter sun.

From the door.

From the snow drifting in through it. From every flake. Every one. Catching the light. Shining.

And in every flake—

---

Antho saw it.

Not a face. Not a thousand tiny faces, the way there'd been in the gold dust at Harrow.

*Eyes*.

Pale. Grey. Ice over deep water. Open.

In every flake of snow drifting in through the door at the edge of the world.

Looking.

---

"*Orrin*," said Ysenne.

---

The dust stopped.

Above the white road. Above the washerwoman. Above the scraping kept. It stopped falling. It hung in the air. Grey-gold. Glittering. Still.

The thin bent shape of the steward stopped with it.

"*Majesty*," it whispered.

"*Stop*," said the Glass Queen.

---

Her voice came from the snow. From every flake. Small and far and singing, like a wet finger round the rim of a glass. Not a thousand voices. One voice. Coming from everywhere at once.

"*Majesty*—" said Orrin. "*Majesty, he asked. His Majesty. He felt the crown go. He said bring it back.*"

"*I know what he said*."

"*Then*—"

"*I've never told him no*," said Ysenne. "*Not once. In nine hundred years. Not when he asked me to look through the crack. Not when he asked me to take the blood. Not when he asked me to bring Rosie home.*" A pause. "*I put chains on my own sister because he asked.*"

The dust hung in the air.

"*I'm not telling him no now*," said the Glass Queen.

A long silence.

"*I'm telling you*," she said.

---

The kept stopped.

All of them. All at once. The scraping. The reaching. The gold hands an arm's length from Lex's back.

Stopped.

Antho felt it. Felt something pass over them. Over the whole thin line of gold servants on either side of the white road. Like a hand laid over their eyes.

They stood still. Mid-step. Mid-reach. Their gold eyes moving. Slowly. Up. To the snow.

To the eyes in the snow.

"*She can command them*," said Antho. Very quietly. "*Can't she. Not just Orrin. She's Crowned. She's his sister. They're the household.*"

"*She's stopping them*," said Lex.

---

"*Majesty*," whispered Orrin. "*He'll know. He'll know you stopped me.*"

"*Yes*."

"*He'll be*—"

"*He'll be sad*," said Ysenne. "*He's always sad. He's been sad for nine hundred years. Since a door. Since Father.*" A pause. "*He'll be sad, and he'll forgive me, because I'm his sister, and he keeps everything.*"

"*Majesty*, *why*—"

"*Because an old man broke my mirror*," said the Glass Queen. "*And said kept things can't see.*" Her voice was very quiet. "*And because a baby looked at me. And because a boy made room in me with nothing left.*" A pause. "*And because I've been sitting in a room with no glass in it for longer than I've ever sat anywhere, Orrin. Not looking at anything. Just sitting.*"

The dust didn't move.

"*Let them go*," said Ysenne.

---

Antho looked at the snow.

At the flakes drifting in through the door. Each one with a pale grey eye in it. Open. Looking.

Looking at Pim.

At the boy in Hask's arms. White. Limp. Breathing very slow. With one gold arm curled over him like a roof.

"*Room-maker*," said Ysenne. Very softly. From the snow.

Pim didn't wake.

"*Tell the old man*," said the Glass Queen. "*Arion. When you get home. If he's still there.*"

Antho went cold.

"*Tell him I thought about what he said*," said Ysenne. "*About the baby. About kept things.*" A pause. "*Tell him I think he was right. Tell him I'm sorry.*"

"*Tell him yourself*," said Antho.

The eyes in the snow turned. Toward him.

"*He's going*," said the Glass Queen. "*Isn't he. I can see it. In every window at Harrow. In the frost on his spectacles. In the glass of your Lantern by his bed.*" Her voice was very gentle. "*I can see him going, warden. Like a candle at the end of its wick.*"

Antho couldn't breathe.

"*Run*," said Ysenne. "*Run, and you might be in time.*"

---

They ran.

The last twenty yards. Down the white road. Past the gold servants standing still with their eyes on the snow. Under the dust hanging in the air, grey-gold, unmoving.

Through the door.

---

Cold.

That was the first thing. Cold. Real cold. The kind that bites. The kind that gets into your fingers and your ears and your nose and makes your eyes water. Winter cold. After nine hundred years of a country with no weather at all.

Then wind.

Off the Veil. From the west. Hard. Bitter. Full of snow. Hitting him in the back like a hand as he came out of the grey corridor of the Veil's thickness, out of the dim, out of the hum.

Into the world.

---

He fell over.

He didn't mean to. His legs went. He'd been running for—he didn't know. Days. Their days. And the room in his chest was thin, thinning, and the pressure had gone—the Court's pressure, the full heavy push—and his body didn't know what to do without it.

He fell on his knees in the snow.

And breathed.

All the way down.

No catch. No push. No full heavy air. Just cold. Just wind. Just air. Thin and sharp and empty and *his*.

Room.

All of it. Everywhere. As much as he wanted.

He knelt in the snow at the foot of the Veil and breathed and cried and couldn't stop.

---

Lex was on his knees beside him.

Breathing. The same. Great ragged gulps of cold air. His face wet. The basket still on his back. The crown in the straw. Nine hundred years of *no*, carried out of the Court on a smith's back.

Hask was standing.

He'd come through last. With Pim. And he was standing in the snow at the foot of the Veil with the boy in his good arm and his gold arm hanging at his side, and he wasn't breathing great gulps.

He was coughing.

---

Short. Dry. A rattle. Twice. Then a breath. Careful. Further than halfway.

Not all the way.

The grey. The catch. Back. The moment he stepped out of the Court. The moment the full heavy air that had filled his lungs all the way down for the first time since he was nine was gone, and there was only cold thin winter air, and room, and the grey.

He stood in the snow and coughed.

And smiled.

"*There it is*," said Silas Hask. Hoarsely. "*Hello again.*"

---

Antho looked up.

At the door. Behind them. In the Veil. Tall. Dark. Wide. The grey drawn back on either side.

At the snow drifting in through it. Into the Court. Onto the white road. Each flake with a pale grey eye in it.

Closing.

One by one. The eyes in the snow. As the flakes landed on the white stone and melted. As they drifted past the gold servants and settled on the dust.

Closing. Like someone going to sleep.

---

And beyond the door, on the white road, just inside, very faint—

A small shape. Gold. In a grey dress too big for her. With a wooden spoon.

Standing at the side of the road.

Not walking. Not following.

Staying.

Her eyes were on the door.

---

Greyfall was white.

Antho saw it when he could stand. A mile off. On its ridge. The walls. The towers. The gate. All of it under snow.

And the gold.

The road came out of the door at the foot of the Veil and ran east across the bare lands toward the fort. A furlong wide. Gold. Every stone. Every stump. A field with forty sheep in it, gold, under a white blanket of snow. A boy among them, gold, mid-step, with snow on his cap and his shoulders and his outstretched arm.

The snow stayed on the gold.

It settled on it and didn't melt. It lay on the gold sheep and the gold boy and the gold road like a cloth over furniture in a shut-up house.

Everything else, it melted on.

---

They walked to the fort.

Slowly. Lex with the basket. Antho with his gold-patched hand held against his chest. Hask with Pim, coughing.

Nobody spoke.

The gold road ran up to the east gate of Greyfall. Up the slope of the ridge. Under the walls.

And in the gate—

---

Antho had known.

Wren hadn't told him. Nobody had. He'd been beyond the edge of the world for months that felt like a fortnight.

He'd known anyway. Since the crack. Since a hard plain man with a hatchet face said *I'll get them out. I promise. And then I'll stand somewhere.*

He stood in the middle of the east gate.

Gold. All of him. From his cap to his boots. His Keepers' grey, gold. His stiff arm, gold. His hatchet face, gold. Every line in it. Every crease.

And in his hands, held crosswise in front of him, the way Fenn had held it all night at the south edge between the families and the Mothwing—

A staff. Plain ash. Gold.

Snow lay on his shoulders. On his cap. On the staff. A thin white line along the top of it.

His eyes moved.

Slowly. So slowly. To the four people coming up the gold road toward him through the snow.

---

Antho stopped in front of him.

He didn't know what to say. He stood in the snow at the foot of the east gate of Greyfall and looked at Daxian Roole.

*Get out*, he'd said. At the crack. *When he comes. Don't stand in front of him. You can't.*

*I'll get them out*, Roole had said. *I promise. I'll send the raven.*

*And then I'll stand somewhere*.

"*You got them out*," said Antho.

The gold eyes were on his face.

"*You sent the raven*," said Antho. "*It reached Harrow. Wren told the Keepers. They all went east.*" His voice was shaking. "*You kept your promise.*"

The eyes didn't move.

"*Fenn would be*—" He stopped. Couldn't. Tried again. "*Somebody has to stand somewhere. That's what he said. Isn't it.*"

Nothing.

"*You did*," said Antho Vey. "*You stood.*"

---

Lex came up beside him.

He looked at Roole for a long time. At the gold face. At the snow on the shoulders. At the staff held crosswise.

Then he reached up. Slowly. And brushed the snow off Roole's shoulders. Off his cap. Off the top of the staff.

Gently. The way you'd brush snow off a child coming in from the cold.

"*We'll come back*," said Lex. Quietly. "*There's a crack. Pim found one. In a girl on the road. Where her door was.*" He took his hand away. "*Everything's a crack first. We'll come back.*"

The gold eyes moved.

To Lex's face.

And stayed there.

---

"*Smith*," croaked a voice.

They all turned.

On the wall above the gate. On the parapet. In the snow. A raven. Old. Ragged. One-eyed. With a thin silver ring on its leg.

It looked down at them with its one good eye.

"*Smith*," it croaked again. And then, after a moment, as if remembering something it had been taught a long time ago by a man with a hatchet face who thought it was funny—

"*Come back*."

---

The dragons were in the high pasture behind the fort.

Curled in the snow. Vharuun round a boulder, enormous, bronze and copper and old gold with a white blanket on his back. Ashka beside him, smaller, blue-grey, with snow on her wings.

Asleep.

Vharuun opened one eye as they came over the rise.

Molten gold. Ancient. Patient.

He looked at them for a long time.

Then a picture. Clear. Dropped into Antho's head like a stone into a well. And into Lex's. Antho saw his brother's face change.

Not a sheep.

A clearing. A birch wood. Two cottages. A blue door.

And a light on the step in front of it. Gold. Huge. Waiting.

---

There was a letter tied to his horn.

Grey thread. Paper. Folded small. Lex untied it with shaking fingers and unfolded it and read it in the snow. Antho read it over his shoulder.

Seraphine's hand. Small. Neat.

*Ten to one*.

*Lantern Night is in seven days. He's at Harrow. Fly.*

---

"*Seven days*," said Lex.

He looked at the snow. At the dragons, with snow on their backs. At the drifts round the boulder, deep, unbroken.

"*When did she send it*?" he said. "*How long have they been here?*"

Antho looked at Vharuun.

The great bronze head lifted. The molten eye looked at him.

A picture. The sun. Going down. Coming up. Going down. Coming up. Going down.

Three times.

"*Three days*," said Antho. "*They've been here three days.*"

"*Four days*," said Lex. "*Four days to Lantern Night.*" He looked east. Toward the world. Toward two hundred and forty miles of gold road, wandering. "*It's a day's flight. Maybe less. If we don't stop.*"

"*We don't stop*," said Antho.

---

He looked at his hand.

He'd forgotten it. The gold. On the back of his left hand. The one he'd cut at the edge of the world.

It had stopped spreading.

He didn't know when. Somewhere in the Court. Somewhere on the white road, when Ysenne said *stop*. A patch. Gold. The size of a coin. On the back of his hand. Over the knuckle of the first finger.

Set. Finished. Full.

He flexed his hand. The fingers moved. The knuckle didn't quite. Stiff. Like a joint that's been broken and healed.

"*It's not much*," said Lex. Looking at it.

"*No*."

"*I can try*—"

"*It's set*," said Antho. "*Leave it.*" He looked at it. At the gold on the back of his hand. At the place where his father's knuckle would be, if his father's hand were his. "*It's the size of a coin. I'll keep it.*"

He looked at Hask's arm. Gold to the elbow. Hanging at his side. And Wren's fingers. And Brannoc's. And Tilda's hand.

"*We're all going to have a little*," he said. "*All of us. Everyone who was there.*" A pause. "*So we remember.*"

---

They flew.

East. Over the gold road. Over the bare lands. Over the Blightmarch, green, with snow on it, and a small dark glint in the middle like an eye, and a black house on the slope with a lantern in its window.

The gold road went round the Blightmarch. South. In a great wide curve.

It didn't touch the green.

Antho saw it. From the air. A gold line running across the white land toward the green circle. And stopping. At the edge. Sharp as a knife. Not a speck of gold on the green.

And then going round.

"*Lex*," he said. Into the wind. "*Look.*"

His brother looked.

"*It won't take*," said Antho. "*On Mal's ground. The gold won't take.*" He looked at the green circle, a hundred miles across, with a pool in the middle. "*He walked round.*"

Lex didn't say anything for a long time.

"*Write it down*," he said at last.

---

They flew all day.

Over the shires. Over the downs. Ashka beside Vharuun, beating hard. Pim asleep in front of Antho, against his chest, with Ivy's cat in the pocket over his heart. Hask on Vharuun's back behind Lex, with his eyes shut and his good hand white on the dragon's scales and his gold arm across his knees.

Coughing.

The snow stopped at noon. The sky cleared. Cold and hard and blue.

And then, in the afternoon, with the sun going down red behind them—

The Vale.

---

Antho saw it come up over the edge of the downs. Long. Green under the snow. A river. A village with a forge in it, smoke going up from one chimney, only one, the Forge's, and the long roof, and a black dot on the chimney-top that might have been a raven on a nest of spoons.

And beyond the village, at the far end of the Vale, a birch wood.

Gold.

A gold path going up through it. Up the hill. From the clearing at the bottom. Straight up to the top and over. North. Toward the mountains.

And in the clearing at the bottom—

---

Light.

Gold. Huge. Warm. Like metal in a crucible. Like the inside of a forge.

On the step.

On the oak ramp.

In front of a blue door.

---

"*He's there*," said Lex. Very quietly. "*Sitting on the step. Like Vharuun showed us.*"

Antho didn't answer.

He was looking at the cottage. At the boarded windows. At the chimney, with a thin thread of smoke going up from it into the cold red evening.

At the west window. Boarded.

At a gap in the boards. A small one. One plank taken down. A crack.

And light coming through it. Faint. Soft. From inside.

Three squares of light. And one soft blur.

---

"*The Lantern*," said Antho. Hoarsely. "*It's lit. My Lantern. By his bed.*"

"*Then he's*—"

"*It's lit*," said Antho Vey. "*He's keeping it lit. Or Mam is. Somebody's keeping it lit.*"

He held Pim tighter.

"*He's trying*," he said.

---

They came down in the high pasture above the birch wood.

Antho was off Ashka before she'd landed. With Pim in his arms. Stumbling in the snow. Running.

Down. Through the birches. The green ones, bare now, white trunks in the dusk. Not the gold path. He didn't go near the gold path.

Down the hill. Toward the stream. Toward the clearing. Toward two cottages and a gold birch and a gold woodpile and a gold cart.

Toward a blue door.

Toward the light on the step.

---

Lex was beside him.

Running. With the basket on his back. With nine hundred years of *no* in the straw between the last few cups.

Neither of them said anything.

They came out of the birch wood at the top of the clearing.

And stopped.

---

He was sitting on the step.

The King. Enormous. Gold. Glowing like metal in a crucible. With his great gold hands on his knees and his white and gold robe spread out on the snow round him, turning it gold. With his crown of gold leaves, crooked.

Sitting on the oak ramp in front of the blue door.

Not knocking. Not trying to come in.

Just sitting.

Waiting.

---

He turned his head.

Slowly. His great gold head. His beard. His crooked crown.

And looked up the clearing. At the top of it. At the edge of the birch wood. At two men standing in the snow. One with a basket on his back. One with a boy in his arms. And behind them, coming out of the trees, a big man with a gold arm, coughing.

The Gilded King smiled.

"*Cousins*," he said.

His voice was deep. Like a bell. Warm.

"*You're just in time*," said Oszric of the Crowned. "*I was about to put the kettle on.*"
