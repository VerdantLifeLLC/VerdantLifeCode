# Two Old Men

They went on the ninth day after the fall.

Ilsa said the eighth. Arion said the seventh. Seren said not until the hip was properly mended and she didn't care what anybody said. They settled on the ninth, which nobody liked, which Brannoc said was how you could tell it was the right answer.

Vharuun carried them.

He didn't complain. Lex had expected him to. He'd expected a picture of sheep, or a cave with nothing in it, or a dragon with a sore back. But when he led the great bronze head down to the clearing and showed him the old man in the wheeled chair on the oak ramp, wrapped in two blankets, with his white hair standing up in the morning wind, Vharuun only looked.

For a long time. With one molten eye.

And then lowered his wing to the grass. All the way. Flat. So the chair could be wheeled straight up onto it.

*[He's never done that*,] said Ember. Very quietly.

*No*.

*[Not for you. Not for anyone.]*

Lex didn't answer. He pushed his father's chair up the dragon's wing, slowly, carefully, onto the broad bronze back, and Brannoc lashed it there with ropes and leather and a great deal of swearing, and his mother climbed up behind with her bare feet on the scales and sat down beside the chair with her hand on its arm.

On *HOME*.

Vharuun lifted off so gently the chair didn't move at all.

---

Lex watched his father on the way.

He couldn't help it. He sat in front of the chair, on the dragon's neck, and kept turning round. He told himself he was checking the ropes.

His father wasn't looking at him. His father was looking down.

At the Vale, going away below them. At Ashbourne. At the downs. At the long green of the western shires in the spring sun, every field and hedge and copse sharp and bright and new. He sat in the wheeled chair lashed to a dragon's back with the wind in his white hair and his spotted hands on the arms and looked down at the world going by under him, and his face—

His face was the face Ember had described. At the top of the Ashbourne road. Forty years ago. Seeing the Vale for the first time.

*Oh*.

---

The pool lay in the green.

Hask was standing on the rim. With Kell's stick in his left hand and his right arm in a sling, bandaged from wrist to elbow, with a line of grey-black showing at the edges where the iron had gone in. He watched the dragon come down. He watched the chair come off the wing.

He didn't say anything when Lex wheeled it across the grass toward the pool. He just stepped aside. Out of the way. And then, after a moment, came round behind the chair and put his good hand on the back of it beside Lex's, and helped push it over the uneven ground.

Ivy Moss was sitting on the rim. Where she always sat. She looked up as they came.

At the chair. At the old man in it.

Her strange light eyes went very wide.

"*Oh*," she said. "*It knows you.*"

---

*[...the old man...]*

Lex felt it before he heard it. The pull. The tilt. The whole green slope leaning, very slightly, toward the chair.

*[...the old man who poured back...]*

His father went still.

*[...in the dark room...at the top of the tower...he drank from HER hands and he was young...and then he poured it all back...into me...]* The voice was very small. Very soft. Wondering. *[...I felt it...it was warm...]*

"*It remembers you*," said Ivy. Quietly. "*It says you're the one it made room for. After. When you were so old you nearly fell out.*" She tilted her head. Listening. "*It says you're smaller.*"

"*I am smaller*," said Arion Vey.

He said it out loud. To the pool. To the deep. As if it were a person.

*[...you got OLDER...]*

"*I did*."

*[...I gave you room...I gave you ROOM...]* A small anxious tilt. *[...wasn't it enough...]*

His father looked down into the pool. Into the dark. At the three small gold lights at the bottom of it, curled together like people round a fire.

"*It was plenty*," he said. Very gently. "*It was more than I'd any right to. A whole spring. A wife who brings me pies.*" He smiled. "*Thank you.*"

The pool was quiet for a long moment.

*[...pies...]* said Mal. Doubtfully.

"*I'll explain later*," said Arion.

---

*[...lamp-lady...]*

His mother was kneeling at the rim.

She'd gone down on her knees on the grey stone beside the chair as soon as they reached the pool, the way she always did, and put her bare hands flat on the rim. She'd walked under the world beside this thing for thirteen years. She'd been folded up inside it. She'd told it the one about the miller's cat four hundred and eleven times.

"*Hello, little one*," she said.

*[...lamp-lady you came...]* The pull went soft. Warm. Like a dog leaning against your legs. *[...you brought the old man...]*

"*I did*."

*[...why...]*

His mother looked at Lex.

Lex knelt down beside her. At the rim. With his hand on the stone.

*He wants to go down*, he told it. *To the Knot. To the grey one. To where you used to be.*

The pool went still.

---

*[...no...]*

Very small. Very quiet. Frightened.

*[...not the old man...he's too OLD...he's like glass...]*

*I know*.

*[...the hungry place...it's COLD there...it's where I was when I was the other thing...]* The voice trembled. *[...I'll break him...]*

*You won't*, thought Lex. *You carried me. You were so careful.*

*[...you're not glass...]*

"*Little one*," said his mother.

The pool went quiet.

"*He wants to say sorry*," said Seren Harrow. "*To someone. Before he goes.*" Her hands were flat on the rim. "*You know about sorry. You know how big it is. How long you can carry it. The big one said sorry to you. Here. At this edge. With the Warden's stick.*"

Hask, behind the chair, went very still.

"*And the grey one said sorry to you*," said Seren. "*When he first went in. You told Lex. Sorry I didn't know you were a person.*" She leaned forward over the deep. "*Now the old man wants to say it. To the grey one. And the grey one wants to say it back.*" She was quiet. "*They've been carrying it for three years. Both of them. Like a stone in a shoe.*"

*[...like the smith's question...]*

"*Like that*," said Seren. "*But heavier.*"

The pool was silent for a long time.

*[...I'll be careful...]* said Mal at last. Very small. *[...I'll be SO careful...I'll make it soft...]*

---

It made the room bigger this time.

Lex felt it. When he slid off the rim into the deep and it closed round him, warm and close and dark. It wasn't a small round space for a man sitting with his knees drawn up. It was larger. Wider. Soft at the edges, like a feather bed. Like a cupped hand big enough to hold three people and a wheeled chair.

His mother came down beside him. Barefoot. Into the dark. Into the soft.

And then the chair.

Hask lowered it. From the rim. Slowly. With his good arm and Ivy's directions and Kit holding the ropes. Lex reached up out of the dark and took the wheels and guided it down, and the deep took it, and held it, and his father came down into the room in his wheeled chair with his blankets round him and his hands on *STEADY* and *HOME*.

And the dark closed over all three of them.

---

"*Oh*," said Arion. Very quietly. In the dark.

"*Are you all right*?"

"*I'm*—" His father's voice stopped. Started again. "*It's warm.*"

"*It's always warm*."

"*I thought it would be cold*." The chair didn't move. Lex couldn't see his father's face. He could only hear him breathe. Slow. Careful. Further than halfway. "*I've spent two hundred years studying this. Did you know? The Hollow. The thing under the lid. What it was. What it wanted.*" A pause. "*I wrote eleven volumes. I thought it was cold. I thought it was hungry.*"

*[...I WAS...]* said Mal. Very softly. All round them. *[...I was cold...I was hungry...]*

"*And now*?"

*[...now I'm carrying you...]*

His father didn't say anything else for a long time.

Lex heard him breathe in the dark. And then he heard something else. Very faint. Very soft.

His father was crying.

---

They went down.

Slowly. Much more slowly than before. Lex felt it. Mal was being careful. Mal was being so careful. Going down through the dark the way you'd carry a full bowl down a flight of stairs. A step. Wait. A step.

*[...all right...]* it kept saying. *[...all right...all right...I've got you...]*

His mother was humming.

Not the Lantern Song. The other one. The one with the broken rhyme. *Gold for the eldest, who feared to grow old.* Very softly. In the dark. And when she got to the line that went wrong—*thorns for the gardener who listened, and drank*—she stopped, and waited, and then went on.

"*Why do you stop*?" said his father. In the dark.

"*Because it's where it went wrong*," said Seren. "*So you always remember.*"

---

He felt the threads.

The way he had before. All round. On every side. More of them than stars. Pulling in toward one point. Humming. Singing.

His father felt them too.

Lex knew because the breathing in the dark changed. Caught. Stopped. And then went on, very slow, very deep.

"*Lex*," said Arion.

"*Father*."

"*Can you see them*?"

"*No*. *I lost that.*"

"*So did I*," said his father. "*When I gave you the Root. When you gave it away.*" A long breath. "*But I can feel them. I'd forgotten. I'd forgotten what they felt like. All of them. All at once.*" His voice was very strange. "*I measured them with a pendulum for eleven years. I drew a map. I thought I understood.*"

"*And*?"

"*I understood nothing*," said Arion Vey. "*Nothing at all.*" A pause. "*It's beautiful, Lex. It's so beautiful. I never knew.*"

---

The room opened.

Light. Grey. Cold. The black glass.

Mal didn't let go. Lex felt it hold. The soft dark room stayed round them, open at the front, like the mouth of a cave, like a hand held cupped with the fingers parted. They sat in it at the edge of the black glass and looked out.

At the lid. A mile of black. Smooth. Cold.

At the Key in the middle of it. Standing up out of the glass like a sword out of a stone. The Lockward beside it.

At the grey thread wound round both of them. Tight. Frayed. A grey cocoon on black iron.

And at the shape above it. A smudge on the air. Bent. Still.

His father didn't move.

He sat in his wheeled chair in the mouth of Mal's room with his blankets round him and his spotted hands on the arms and looked across the black glass at the grey shape on the Key.

He didn't say anything.

He didn't need to.

---

The shape lifted its head.

Slowly. The way it had before. Like an old man looking up from a book. Coming back from a long way off.

And looked across the black glass.

At the cave of soft dark at the edge of the lid. At the smith sitting in it. At the barefoot woman.

At the old man in the chair.

Lex felt the threads go still.

All of them. All four million, one hundred and six thousand and some. For one breath. As if the whole net had stopped humming to listen.

---

"*Arion*," said Elric Thorne.

---

His father didn't answer.

Not straight away. He sat in his chair. Looking. And Lex, beside him, could see his face now. In the grey light off the glass.

It wasn't still.

That was the thing. It wasn't still like water in a bucket. It wasn't arranged. It wasn't closed like a book. It was open. All of it. Every fold and crease. As open as a door with the wind blowing through it.

"*Elric*," he said.

---

Nobody said anything for a long time.

The grey smudge on the Key didn't move. The old man in the chair didn't move. Lex didn't move. His mother sat on the soft dark floor of Mal's room with her knees drawn up and her arms round them and didn't move either.

The threads hummed.

Far off, very far, down a long silver line running out into the dark—

*Clink*.

---

"*You got old*," said Elric.

"*You got thin*," said Arion.

A sound. Dry. Rasping. From the grey smudge. Like wind through a gap in a wall.

It was a laugh.

"*I've been trying to think what to say*," said Elric. "*For three years. Ever since I came down here. Every day. I'd think, if he ever comes. If he ever stands there. What do I say.*" The smudge tilted. "*I had it all worked out. A speech. Very good one. Very moving.*"

"*So did I*," said Arion. "*I wrote it down. Last night. Three pages.*"

"*And*?"

"*I left it on the table*," said Arion Vey.

The dry sound came again. Louder.

"*Of course you did*," said Elric Thorne.

---

Lex's mother stood up.

She did it suddenly. All at once. The way she did things. She stood up in the mouth of Mal's room, barefoot, on the soft dark, and took two steps out onto the black glass, and stopped. In front of the chair. Between her husband and the grey thread on the Key.

And put her hands on her hips.

"*You*," said Seren Harrow.

The smudge looked at her.

"*You opened the wards*," said Seren. "*At Caldris. You let the Pale Warden in. You carried my husband out of a crystal in a sack.*" Her voice was very steady. "*I don't remember him. I don't remember any of it. I don't remember thirty years.*" She tilted her head. "*But I've read the letter. Page thirty. The only page where his hand shakes.*"

The smudge didn't move.

"*So before you say anything to him*," said Seren Vey, "*anything at all, you should know that I've brought a pan.*"

---

There was a long silence.

"*You haven't*," said Elric.

"*I have*." His mother reached into the bundle at her hip and brought it out. Small. Iron. A frying pan. The little one from the blue-door cottage, the one she did eggs in. She held it up. "*My mother threw one at him the first time he came to the house. She missed. I won't.*"

The smudge stared at her.

And then the grey thread round the Key shook. All of it. All at once. The whole cocoon of it trembling on the black iron like a struck harp.

Elric was laughing.

Properly. For the first time. Not a rasp. Not a dry sound like wind through a wall. A laugh. Thin and cracked and strange, coming out of a thread that hadn't laughed in three years, but a laugh.

"*Oh*," he said. "*Oh, Arion. She's exactly like you said.*"

His father was smiling.

"*She's worse*," he said.

---

His mother put the pan away.

She didn't sit down. She stood on the black glass in front of the chair a moment longer. Looking at the grey smudge. With her square face very straight.

"*One more thing*," she said.

The smudge waited.

"*At the end*," said Seren. "*At the lid. On the night you went in. You said something.*" She swallowed. "*Lex told me. You were holding the Lockward. Your hands were old. And you looked at him. And you said, tell him.*"

The smudge went very still.

"*You meant Arion*," said Seren. "*Tell Arion. You didn't say what.*" Her voice cracked. Just a little. "*I've been wondering what. For a year. I think he has too. For three.*" She looked at the grey shape on the Key. "*So I'm going to go and sit down now. And you're going to tell him yourself.*"

She went back into Mal's room. And sat down on the soft dark floor beside the chair. And put her hand on the arm of it. On *HOME*.

And didn't say anything else.

---

The smudge didn't speak for a long time.

The threads hummed. *Clink*, far off. Lex sat beside his father's chair in the mouth of Mal's room and didn't move.

"*Can you come closer*?" said Elric at last. Very quietly. "*I can't. I'd like to see your face.*"

His father looked at Lex.

Lex stood up. And took the handles of the chair. And looked back into the room, at the soft dark.

*Can I take him out*? he asked it. *Onto the glass. Just a little way.*

*[...it's COLD out there...]*

*I know*.

*[...I'll come too...]* said Mal. *[...I'll make it soft...]*

---

He pushed the chair out onto the black glass.

And the room came with them.

Not all of it. A little. A softness. Spreading out round the wheels of the chair as Lex pushed it, across the black, like a rug unrolling. Warm. Dark. A small moving circle of room, round an old man in a chair, crossing the lid of the world.

He stopped a few paces from the Key.

Close enough to see the grey thread clearly. Every turn of it. Every fray. Close enough to see how thin it was worn in places. Like an old rope.

Close enough to see the shape above it lean down.

---

"*There you are*," said Elric.

Arion didn't say anything.

"*You look terrible*," said Elric.

"*Thank you*."

"*You look about four hundred*."

"*I'm two hundred and thirty-four*."

"*You look about four hundred*," said Elric Thorne. "*You look like a walnut someone left in a drawer. You look like a heron in winter.*" The smudge leaned closer. "*I've never seen you look anything but forty-five. In forty years. Not once.*" A pause. "*It suits you. Being old. It suits you better than being forty-five ever did.*"

His father looked up at the grey shape.

"*Does it*?"

"*You look like someone*," said Elric. "*Finally. You always looked like a portrait of someone. Now you look like a person.*"

---

"*Elric*," said his father.

The smudge waited.

"*You were right*," said Arion.

Lex felt the threads go still again.

"*At the lid*," said his father. "*Lex told me. You said, he was always right. That was the worst thing about him.*" His spotted hands were very still on the arms of the chair. "*You were right. It was. It is.*"

The smudge didn't move.

"*I was right at Avaloria*," said Arion Vey. "*You weren't ready. You weren't. I was right to say so.*" He looked up. "*And I said it gently. And I meant it kindly. And it was the cruellest thing I've ever said to anyone in two hundred years.*"

---

"*No*," said Elric.

"*Yes*."

"*No*. *I opened the wards. I carried you out in a sack. I sold you to a man with black veins for a shard of your own Root.*" The smudge was shaking. "*That's cruel. That's what cruel is. You saying I wasn't ready isn't*—"

"*Forty years*," said Arion.

The smudge stopped.

"*Forty years*," said his father again. "*The first day. In the library. You were twenty-one. You had a stolen book under your arm and a raven on your shoulder. And you looked at me across the table and said, you're younger than I thought.*" His voice was very quiet. "*And I said, I'm older than you think. And you looked at me. And you sat down. And you said, tell me.*"

The grey thread round the Key was trembling.

"*Nobody had ever said that to me*," said Arion. "*Not in a hundred and eighty-eight years. Not Aurelian. Not anyone. Tell me. Not tell me what to do. Just tell me.*" He swallowed. "*So I did. For forty years. I told you everything. Almost everything.*"

"*Arion*—"

"*And I never once said it back*," said Arion Vey.

---

Silence.

Total silence. The threads. The glass. Even the iron, far off, down the long silver line. For one moment, nothing at all.

"*Not once*," said his father. "*In forty years. I never once said to you, tell me. I never asked about the village. About your mother. About why you stole the book. About what you wanted.*" His hands were shaking on the arms of the chair. "*I knew you wanted something. I could see it. Every day. Forty years. I could see it in you like a fire behind a door.*" A pause. "*And I never asked what it was. Because I was afraid if I asked, you'd tell me. And then I'd have to do something about it.*"

The smudge didn't speak.

"*So I said you weren't ready*," said Arion. "*Instead. Gently. Kindly. Because I was right, and it was easier to be right than to ask.*"

---

Lex couldn't breathe.

He stood behind his father's chair with his hands on the handles and couldn't breathe. Behind him, in the mouth of Mal's room, he heard his mother make a small sound. Very small.

"*So*," said his father. Very quietly. To the grey shape on the Key. "*I've come a long way. In a bubble. To the bottom of the world. On a dragon. With a broken hip.*" A ghost of a smile. "*To say it. Finally. Forty-three years late.*"

He looked up.

"*Tell me*," said Arion Vey.

---

The smudge didn't move for a long time.

And then it bent. Lower. Over the Key. Over the Lockward. Over its own grey thread, wound tight. The way a man bends over something when he's trying very hard not to let you see his face.

"*Tell you what*," said Elric. His voice was barely a voice at all.

"*Anything*. *Everything.*" His father leaned forward in the chair. As far as he could. "*The village. Your mother. The book. What you wanted.*" A pause. "*What you've been doing down here for three years. What it's like. Whether you're frightened.*"

"*I'm a thread*, *Arion.*"

"*Threads can be frightened*," said his father. "*I've got one in my son's head that's terrified.*"

---

Elric told him.

Lex didn't hear all of it. He didn't try to. He stood behind the chair with his hands on the handles and let it go past him. It wasn't his. It was theirs.

But he heard some.

A fishing village on the Saltreach coast. Herring boats. A mother who mended nets and sang to them while she did it, so the fish would come. A boy who wanted to be someone. Anyone. Anyone but a herring-fisher's son with salt-cracked hands and nothing to his name.

A book in a Warden's saddlebag. Stolen. The first book he ever read. About the Spire. About the Root. About a family called Vey who were born holding what everyone else had to earn.

*I wanted to be first*. Lex heard that. Heard it clearly. *Just once. Just once in my life, I wanted to be the one who went first. Not behind. Not second. Not the one who held the other man's coat.*

And Arion, very quietly: *You were first.*

The smudge stopped.

*You were the first*, said his father. *The first friend I had in two hundred years. The first person who ever sat down across a table from me and said tell me. Before Seren. Before anyone.*

A long silence.

*You were first, Elric. I just never told you.*

---

The grey thread round the Key was very still.

Lex looked up at it. At the smudge on the air. Bent. Shaking, a little.

He couldn't see a face. There was no face. Just grey. Just smoke.

But he thought, if there had been one, it would have been wet.

---

"*Tell him*," said Elric at last.

His voice was very rough.

"*That's what I meant*," he said. "*At the lid. On the night I went in. Tell him.*" The smudge lifted. A little. Toward the chair. "*Tell him I'm sorry. Tell him I'd do it differently. Tell him I knew he was right and I hated him for it and I loved him anyway, for forty years, every day.*" A pause. "*Tell him I was always his friend. Even in the sack. Even at Caldris.*"

"*Even then*?" said Arion. Very quietly.

"*Especially then*," said Elric Thorne. "*Why do you think it hurt so much?*"

---

His father put out his hand.

Slowly. Shaking. Across the last pace of black glass between the chair and the Key. Toward the grey thread wound round the black iron.

He couldn't reach it. His arm wasn't long enough. His hip wouldn't let him lean.

He held his hand out anyway. In the air. Open. A foot from the thread.

And the thread moved.

A strand of it. Just one. Unwinding from the cocoon on the Key. Slowly. Carefully. The way you'd let out a fishing line. Grey. Thin. Frayed at the end.

Reaching across the foot of air between them.

And touching his father's fingers.

---

Lex saw his father's face.

He'd seen it at the window at Harrow, looking west. He'd seen it on the dragon's back that morning, looking down at the world.

He'd never seen it like this.

Neither of them said anything. There was nothing to say. They'd said it.

An old man in a wheeled chair at the bottom of the world with his hand held out. And a thread. Grey. Frayed. Wound round his fingers. Like a hand round a hand.

Forty-three years.

---

*Clink*.

---

The thread jerked.

All of it. The whole grey cocoon on the Key. Tightening. Like a man flinching from a blow. The strand round his father's fingers pulled back. Fast. Winding itself back onto the Key and the Lockward, tight, tighter.

"*Damn*," said Elric.

"*What is it*?"

"*Him*." The smudge had lifted its head. Turned. Away from them. Toward the dark at the edge of the glass. Toward the one bright silver line running out into it. "*He's closer than he was. He's speeding up.*"

And down the silver line, from the dark, came a voice.

---

"*Thorne*," it said.

Low. Flat. Heavy. Rough as gravel. Coming up the last thread from very far away, through the black rings, through the rock under the world, through the dark.

"*I can feel you*," said Caddoc of the Iron Crown. "*You've let some of your thread go. I felt it slack.*" A pause. "*You're tired.*"

"*I'm busy*," said Elric. Sharply. "*I've got company. Go away.*"

"*I'll be there by the lanterns*," said the voice. As if he hadn't spoken. "*I told the smith. I'll tell you. I'm not going to hurry. I'm not going to be late.*" A pause. "*When I get there, I'll take the bar out. I'll be quick. You won't feel much.*" Another pause. "*I'm not cruel.*"

"*You keep saying that*," said Elric Thorne. "*Nobody who isn't cruel keeps saying it.*"

The silver line was silent for a moment.

"*Huh*," said Caddoc.

---

"*Caddoc of the Iron Crown*," said Arion.

The line went still.

His father had turned his chair. Not much. Lex had felt him do it, felt the wheels shift under his hands. He was facing the dark now. The edge of the glass. The silver line.

"*Who's that*?" said the voice.

"*Arion Vey*," said his father. "*I was Warden-Architect. I knew your sister. A little. At the end.*"

Silence.

"*The old man*," said Caddoc at last. "*Mae's letters. The clever one.*"

"*That's me*."

"*She said you were dying*."

"*I am*," said Arion Vey. "*That's why I came down. To say something to my friend before I can't.*"

---

The silver line was quiet for a long time.

*Clink* didn't come.

"*My father died slowly*," said Caddoc.

Lex went very still.

"*Before the pool*," said the voice. Low. Flat. As if it were reporting the weather. "*Before any of it. A wasting thing. In his chest. It took a year.*" A pause. "*Oz couldn't bear it. He couldn't go in the room. He'd stand outside the door. Shaking. Then go and find the scholars and shout at them. Find a cure. Find something.*"

Nobody spoke.

"*Mae sat with him sometimes*," said Caddoc. "*Rosie brought him flowers. Ysy looked at him and cried and went away. Hes was too little. She sat on the floor by the bed and played with a toy bear.*" Another pause. "*I sat with him. Every night. At the end. I held his hand. I didn't say anything. He didn't want anything said.*"

His father didn't speak.

"*And then he went*," said Caddoc of the Iron Crown. "*In the night. With his hand in mine.*" The voice didn't change at all. "*And Oz found the pool the next week.*"

---

"*You understand it*," said Arion. Very quietly. "*Don't you. An ending.*"

"*I sat with one*."

"*Then why*—"

"*Because I gave my word*," said Caddoc. "*On the shingle. To my brother. Who couldn't go in the room.*" A pause. "*He's frightened, old man. He's been frightened for nine hundred years. Since that door. Somebody has to go in the room for him.*"

The silver line went quiet.

"*I'm sorry you're dying*," said Caddoc. "*It's a hard thing. I hope someone holds your hand.*"

"*Someone will*," said Arion.

"*Good*," said Caddoc of the Iron Crown.

And then, far off, down the long silver line—

*Clink*.

---

Nobody said anything for a while.

The threads hummed. The grey cocoon on the Key loosened, a little. Breathed.

"*Well*," said Elric at last. "*That was strange.*"

"*Yes*."

"*I've been listening to him for six weeks*. *He's never said more than twenty words together.*" The smudge turned back toward the chair. "*He likes you.*"

"*He's frightened too*," said Arion. "*In a different way. He's frightened of breaking a promise.*"

"*That's not frightened*. *That's stubborn.*"

"*Sometimes there isn't much difference*," said his father. "*I should know. I've been both for two hundred years.*"

---

"*Show me*," said Arion.

The smudge tilted.

"*The knots*," said his father. "*Aurelian's notes. The ones you've been reading. Lex told me. Only a hand that's refused a crown.*" His grey eyes were very sharp. "*I'd like to see them. Before I go. I spent eleven years with a pendulum trying to read the net from the outside. I'd like to read it once from the inside.*"

Elric was quiet for a moment.

"*You can't see threads*," he said. "*Neither of you can. You gave it up.*"

"*No*," agreed Arion. "*But you can.*" He leaned forward in the chair. "*Tell me what they say. Read them to me. Like Seren reads me my letters.*"

---

So Elric read them.

Lex listened. He stood behind the chair and listened to a thread read a dead man's notes to a dying one, at the bottom of the world, in a voice like wind through a wall.

The Key unwrites. Whatever it is turned against.

Turned in the Knot, the net feels it. All of it. And the net, being a net, and having nobody's hand on it, goes where the hand goes.

Only a hand that has already refused a crown may turn it and remain itself.

*If it must be turned again, let the hand wear the tenth crown*.

"*There's more*," said Elric. And stopped.

His father went still.

"*Read it*," said Arion.

"*Arion*—"

"*Read it*, *Elric.*"

---

The smudge didn't speak for a moment.

Then, very quietly—

"*The Veil was tied last*," read Elric. "*It is the last knot. It holds the whole edge. If it is ever unwritten, the Key must be turned all the way. Past closed. Past open. Into the place where it was made.*" A pause. "*A key turned that far does not come back. And neither does the hand that turns it.*"

Lex went cold.

"*Whoever turns it last*," said Elric Thorne, "*goes with what it unwrites.*"

---

Nobody spoke.

Lex stood behind his father's chair and felt the cold go all the way through him. Down through his chest. Down through his arms. Into his hands on the handles of the chair.

*Whoever turns it last goes with what it unwrites*.

"*You didn't tell me*," he said. "*Last time. You told me the rest. You didn't tell me that.*"

The smudge turned toward him.

"*No*," said Elric.

"*Why not*?"

"*Because you've got a baby*," said Elric Thorne. "*And a wife who breaks noses. And a dragon who wants sheep.*" A pause. "*And you'd have started working out how it could be you.*"

---

His father was looking at the Key.

At the black iron. At the Lockward. At the grey thread wound round them both, frayed and tight.

"*It won't be him*," said Arion. Very quietly.

The smudge looked at him.

"*It won't be Lex*," said his father. "*Will it.*" He wasn't asking. "*You've worked it out. You've had three years. You've had nothing else to do.*" His spotted hands were very still on the arms of the chair. "*You're already in the Knot. You're already wound round the Key. You're already mostly thread.*"

Elric didn't answer.

"*You're already holding it*," said Arion Vey.

"*It's the only thing I'm good for now*," said Elric Thorne. Very gently. "*Arion. Let me be good for something.*"

---

His father didn't say anything for a long time.

He sat in the chair with Mal's soft dark spread out round its wheels and his blankets round his shoulders and looked at his friend. At the grey shape on the Key.

And then he nodded.

Once. Slowly. The way a man nods when he's been shown a thing he already knew and hoped wasn't true.

"*First*," he said.

The smudge went still.

"*You'd go first*," said Arion. "*Wouldn't you. Ahead. Before anyone. Into whatever's on the other side.*" A ghost of a smile. "*Before me. Before Caddoc. Before the Veil.*"

"*I hadn't thought of it like that*."

"*Yes you had*," said his father. "*You've had three years.*"

A dry sound from the smudge. A rasp.

"*All right*," said Elric. "*I had.*"

---

*[...smith...]*

Lex felt the room pull.

Behind them. Mal. The soft dark round the wheels of the chair shivering. Drawing in.

*[...smith...the old man...he's getting COLD...]*

He looked down.

His father's hands on the arms of the chair were white. Not pale. *White*. The knuckles blue. His breath, Lex realised, was coming short. Shallow. Not further than halfway. Not even halfway.

"*Father*—"

"*I know*," said Arion. "*I know. It's time.*"

He looked up at the grey shape on the Key.

"*I won't come back*," he said. "*This is the only time.*"

"*I know*."

"*I'd like to say something clever*."

"*Don't*," said Elric Thorne. "*You'll only get it wrong. You always get the clever ones wrong. You're much better at the plain ones.*"

His father smiled.

"*Goodbye, Elric*," he said.

"*Goodbye, old friend*," said Elric. "*Tell me. When you get there. Whatever it's like on the other side.*" A pause. "*I'd like to know, before I go.*"

"*I'll try*," said Arion Vey.

---

Lex turned the chair.

Back across the black glass. Toward the mouth of Mal's room, open at the edge of the lid, soft and dark. His mother was standing in it. Waiting. With her arms out.

Behind them, he heard Elric say one more thing.

Very quietly. So quietly Lex wasn't sure, afterward, if he'd heard it at all, or only felt it, the way you feel the threads.

*Thank you for coming down*.

His father didn't turn round.

But Lex, pushing the chair, saw his spotted hand lift. Just a little. From the arm of the chair. From *STEADY*. And open. And close.

As if he were letting go of a thread.

---

They came up into a grey afternoon.

Mal brought them up so slowly that Lex thought it would never end. A step. Wait. A step. *All right. All right. I've got you.* His mother had her arms round his father in the chair, holding him. Warming him. Her whole body curled round the old man in his blankets like a cat round a hearth.

They came up out of the pool onto the grass.

Hask was there. And Kit. And Ivy, on her crutch, white-faced. They took the chair. They lifted it out onto the green. Hask with his good arm. Kit with both. Lex climbing out after.

His father sat in the chair on the grass in the grey light with his eyes shut.

Breathing.

Short. Shallow. But breathing.

---

"*Is he*—" said Ivy.

"*He's all right*," said his mother. Kneeling on the grass by the chair. Rubbing his white hands between hers, hard, the way you'd rub warmth back into a child who'd been out in the snow. "*He's cold. He's tired. He's all right.*"

His father opened his eyes.

He looked at the sky. Grey. At the green slope. At the black house above it with the lantern in the window. At the pool.

"*Thank you*," he said. To the pool.

*[...was it...was it enough...]* said Mal. Very small.

"*It was everything*," said Arion Vey.

---

He looked at Lex.

"*That's done*," he said.

Lex knelt down by the chair. And took one of his father's hands from his mother. And held it.

It was so cold.

"*That was the last thing*," said his father. Very quietly. "*The last thing I needed to do. I wrote a list. In the winter. In the grey book. Things to do before.*" He smiled. Faintly. "*That was the last thing on it.*"

"*What else was on it*?"

His father looked at Seren. Kneeling on the other side of the chair, rubbing his other hand. Her face white. Her eyes wet.

"*Be kissed*," said Arion Vey. "*Properly. By someone who remembers it.*"

Seren made a sound.

"*Done*," said his father. "*Last week.*" He closed his eyes again. "*Everything after this is just—extra.*"

---

The raven came while they were still on the grass.

Lex heard it first. A harsh croak out of the grey sky. And then the beat of wings. Not Roole's. Smaller. Black. Glossy. With something glittering clamped in its beak.

It came down on the grey rim of the pool beside Kell's stone and dropped the glittering thing. It rang on the stone. A spoon. A silver one.

Quill.

He looked at Lex with one black bead of an eye.

"*Smith*," he croaked. Then, after a moment, as if remembering something else. "*Brannoc. Brannoc says.*"

"*Brannoc says what*?"

The raven cocked his head.

"*Dust*," he croaked. "*Dust. Over Ashbourne.*" He shook his feathers. "*A cloud. Gold. Coming.*"
