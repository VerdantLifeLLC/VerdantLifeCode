# The Tenth Tower

The stair was very narrow.

Lex went first. He had to turn sideways in places, where the dead trunk of the tree twisted and the hollow inside it pinched. The steps were grown, not cut. Shelves of grey wood curling out from the wall in a spiral, worn smooth in the middle as if feet had climbed them for a long time.

No feet had. Not for nine hundred years. Only the Ferryman's, once. Thirty-two years ago. Going up with a crown in his long grey hand. Coming down without it.

Lex climbed.

And the *no* came down the stair to meet him.

---

It didn't push.

He'd thought it would. He'd felt it from the clearing, from the path, from the edge of the thorns. A cold steady refusal pouring out of the tower like air down a chimney. He'd thought, climbing into it, it would be like climbing into a wind.

It wasn't.

It went *round* him. Down the stair. Past his shoulders. Past his face. Cold. Quiet. Patient. The way water goes round a stone in a stream. It knew he was there. It knew what he was. And it let him through, and went on down past him to the clearing, to the thorns, to the woman standing among her roses, saying the thing it had been saying for thirty-two years.

*Not you. Not ever. Hush.*

---

*[Lex*,] said Ember.

Very quiet. Very close.

*I know*.

*[It's like your mother*,] said Ember. *[At the blue door. When the dust came.]* A pause. *[It's like every Harrow woman who ever stood at a door with her hand flat on the wood.]*

*Twenty generations*, thought Lex.

*[Twenty generations*,] said Ember. *[All saying the same thing. In one crown.]*

---

Antho was behind him.

Lex could hear him breathing. Slow. Careful. Further than halfway. He'd drunk his last cup at the edge of the thorns. They'd counted, before they went in. Nine left. Nine cups in the basket on Lex's back. Thirteen days. Fewer.

Lex didn't do the sums.

He climbed.

---

The stair went round six times.

He counted. He couldn't help it. Six turns of the spiral, up through the hollow of the dead white tree, with the grey light coming dim through cracks in the bark. And then the hollow widened. And the stair stopped. At a floor.

Grey wood. Grown. Like the rest.

And a room.

---

It was a girl's room.

He'd known it would be. His mother had dreamed it. The Ferryman had described it. Rhosyn had grown it.

He hadn't known it would be like *this*.

Small. Round. The inside of the top of the tree. With walls that curved and a ceiling that came to a point overhead, like the inside of a bell, like the inside of a tent. Grey. Silver-grey. The wood smooth and dry and dead.

A bed. Against the wall. Small. Narrow. A girl's bed. Grown out of the floor in one piece, like the stair. With a coverlet on it. Not cloth. *Leaves*. Woven. Thousands of them. Overlapping. Like the scales of a fish. Grey now. Dead. Dry as paper.

They'd been green once. He could tell. The shapes. The veins. Rose leaves. Every one.

A shelf. On the wall. Grown out of it like a bracket fungus on a tree. With three things on it.

A window. Small. Round. Facing west. Looking out at nothing. At the grey-gold dim. At the edge of the plain.

And a chair.

---

He stopped in the doorway.

He couldn't help it. He stood at the top of the stair with his hand on the curved grey wall and his brother behind him and looked at the room.

And it was so *sad*.

---

Not frightening. Not grand. Not a throne room. Not a vault. Not a tomb. A room a girl of fifteen had grown, in a dead country, in the first year of the exile, for her little sister. With everything she could remember about her sister's room at home. In case.

In case she changed her mind.

In case she came.

In case she wanted somewhere to sleep.

---

He went to the shelf first.

He didn't know why. The chair was there. In the middle of the room. Facing the window. With something on it.

He couldn't look at it yet.

He went to the shelf.

---

A bowl. Grey wood. Grown. Shallow. With something in the bottom of it.

Dust. Fine. Red-brown. Dry as powder. A little heap of it in the bottom of the bowl. And in the dust, here and there, shapes. Curls. Tiny. Brittle. The ghosts of petals.

Roses.

A bowl of roses. Left on a shelf in a girl's room nine hundred years ago. Gone to dust.

Lex didn't touch it.

---

A bear.

Small. Carved. Wood. The same grey wood as the room. No bigger than his hand. Sitting up, with its front paws in its lap and its head a little on one side. Worn smooth. So smooth the face had almost gone. As if it had been held. A great deal. By a small hand. For a long time.

It hadn't, of course. Nobody had ever held it. Nobody had ever been here.

Rhosyn had grown it worn.

*She sat on the floor by the bed*, Caddoc had said. Up the long silver thread. To Arion. In the dark at the bottom of the world. *Hes was too little. She sat on the floor by the bed and played with a toy bear.*

*A girl who married a bear*, Maelis had told her. Rhosyn had said. Through the rose. *When Hes had the fever. Mae sat up all night and told her a story about a girl who married a bear.*

Lex looked at the worn grey bear on the shelf.

*She remembered*, he thought. *Rhosyn. Every detail. The bowl of roses. The bear. How worn it was. How small the hand that held it.*

*She grew it all from memory. In a dead country. For a sister who never came.*

---

And the third thing.

Not on the shelf. *In* it. Scratched. Into the grey wood. Small. Uneven. With something sharp. A thorn, maybe.

Letters. In a language he couldn't read. Old. Curling. Beautiful.

And under them, smaller, newer, in a different hand—no. The same hand. But later. Much later. Shakier. In letters he *could* read. Square. Plain. The world's letters. The letters Orrin used.

The same letters as a scrap of grey silk tied to a rose at the foot of the Veil.

*HES*

*I'M SORRY*

*R*

---

"*She came back*," said Antho.

He was beside Lex. Looking at the scratches on the shelf. His voice very quiet.

"*The old letters are from the first year*," he said. "*When she grew it. I can't read them. But they're old. They've gone grey with the wood.*" He touched the newer ones. Lightly. With one finger. "*These are new. These are thirty-two years. The night the Ferryman came.*"

"*She said she couldn't come up*."

"*She couldn't come up the stair*," said Antho. "*Not after the crown was here.*" He looked at the scratches. "*Before. In the first year. Before the crown. She could come up. She grew it. She sat in here.*" He looked round the small round room. "*She must have come up a thousand times. In the first year. Sat on that bed. Waited.*"

"*And then*?"

"*And then she went to sleep*," said Antho. "*In her silk. For four hundred years. And when she woke, the crown was here. And she couldn't come up anymore.*" He touched the newer letters again. "*So she scratched this on the way down. That night. With the Ferryman watching. Before the stair said no.*"

---

*HES. I'M SORRY. R.*

Lex looked at it for a long time.

*She sent a rose*, he thought. *Through the crack. With HES on it. Asking.*

*And this was the last thing she said to her sister before that. Thirty-two years ago. On a shelf in a room her sister never saw.*

*I'm sorry*.

---

He turned round.

And looked at the chair.

---

It was small.

A girl's chair. Grown out of the floor in one piece like everything else. Grey wood. A seat. A back. Four legs that weren't legs, that were roots, curling down into the floor. Plain. No carving. No arms.

Facing the window.

Facing west. Out at nothing. At the grey-gold dim at the edge of the plain. At the edge of everything.

And on the seat, in the middle, exactly in the middle, where the Ferryman had set it down thirty-two years ago, the way you'd set down a sleeping child—

---

He'd seen the hollow in the box. In the cellar at Harrow. A year and a half ago. With Wren at the top of the ladder and his brother beside him with a Lantern. A circle pressed into the oak. Seven points.

He'd imagined it a thousand times since.

He'd imagined it beautiful. Shining. A thing out of a song. A crown that could say no to a king.

It wasn't beautiful.

It was the plainest thing he'd ever seen.

---

A circle. Silver. A little bigger than a man's hand could span. Not thick. Not thin. A band of metal a finger wide, round. And rising from the top edge of it, at even spaces, seven points. Short. Plain. Blunt-tipped. Like the tines of a fork. Like the teeth of a comb.

No jewels. No carving. No engraving. No polish.

The silver had gone soft and grey. Not tarnished. Not black. *Grey*. The way silver goes when it's been in the dark a very long time and nobody has ever once rubbed it with a cloth. Dull. Quiet. Like a stone at the bottom of a river.

It sat on the seat of the chair in the dead grey room and it didn't shine.

And the whole room was full of *no*.

---

He felt it come off it.

From the circle. From the seven points. From the grey silver. Steady. Patient. Quiet. Not loud. Not a shout. Not a wall.

A *refusal*.

The way his mother said no. The way Old Mag said no to a dust-man through a cracked window. The way a girl of seventeen said no on a shingle, to her sister in chains, with her voice shaking and not letting it stop her.

*Not me. Not ever*.

Twenty generations of it. Every Harrow woman who'd ever kept the box. Every one who'd moved on at the end of summer. Every one who'd sung the song and broken it on Rhosyn's name. Every one who'd never let a moth in.

It was all in the crown.

It wasn't magic. Lex understood that, standing in the doorway of the tenth tower. It wasn't a spell. It wasn't a rune. It wasn't anything Aurelian had made or anything the net had given.

It was just a crown that had been put down.

And never picked up again.

And nine hundred years of not being picked up had *meant* something.

---

"*Lex*," said Antho.

"*I know*."

"*Are you going to*—"

"*I don't know*," said Lex.

---

He went to the chair.

Slowly. Across the small round floor. Past the bed with its coverlet of dead rose leaves. Past the shelf with its bowl of dust and its worn bear and its scratched *I'm sorry*.

He stopped in front of the chair.

He looked down at the crown.

And he couldn't.

---

His hand came up. On its own. The way it came up to the fire every morning. The way it came up to the Veil. Out. Toward the crown.

And stopped. A foot above it.

And stayed there.

*We break the ground*, he thought. *We never take the crown*.

*We never let a moth in*.

His mother's voice. In the cellar. Reading a letter from a dead woman. *We never take the crown*.

His mother's voice again. On a sheepskin by a hearth. In the spring. Telling a baby who'd just rolled over for the first time. *We break the ground. We never take the crown. We never let a moth in.*

He held his hand over the crown and couldn't bring it down.

---

*[Lex*,] said Ember. Very gently.

*It's the rule*.

*[I know*.]

*Mam's rule. Wenna's. Hesper's. Twenty generations. We never take the crown.* His hand was shaking. *And I've come all this way, through the edge of the world, past the King, through a glass palace, through a mile of thorns.* He swallowed. *To take the crown.*

Ember didn't say anything for a moment.

*[Malachi gave it to her*,] it said. *[On the shingle. He said, keep it. Hide it. Don't wear it. Pass it on.]*

Lex didn't move.

*[Keep it*,] said Ember. *[Hide it. Don't wear it. Pass it on.]* A pause. *[That's not taking, Lex. That's carrying. Wenna carried it three hundred miles in a box. Every Harrow woman carried it from cottage to cottage under a flagstone.]*

*That's different*.

*[Is it*?]

*They never wore it*.

*[And you're not going to*,] said Ember. *[Are you?]*

---

"*Lex*," said Antho.

He'd come up beside him. At the chair. Lex hadn't heard him move.

His brother was looking at the crown.

"*It's a door*," said Antho.

Lex looked at him.

"*I can feel it*," said Antho. Very quietly. Not looking away from the crown. "*It's a door. Shut. That's what it feels like. That's what it is.*" His voice was very strange. "*Every door I've ever felt wanted to be open. Or was waiting to be. Or had been shut by someone else, from outside. Bricked up. Kept.*" He swallowed. "*This one's shut on purpose. From inside. By the one who could have opened it.*"

"*Hesper*."

"*Hesper*," said Antho. "*She stood at the edge of the pool with it in her hands. And she could have put it on. And drunk. Like the rest of them.*" He looked at the grey silver. "*And she put it down instead. And shut the door. On purpose. On herself.*"

He was quiet for a long moment.

"*It's the first door I've ever felt*," said Antho Vey, "*that wants to stay shut.*"

---

He put out his hand.

Antho. Not Lex. His left. With the thin healing red line across the palm, where he'd cut it at the edge of the world.

Lex watched him.

He didn't stop him. He didn't say anything. He watched his brother's hand come down. Slowly. Over the crown. A Threshold Warden's hand. A hand that had stood in every kind of door.

"*We're not taking it*," said Antho. Quietly. To the crown. "*We're carrying it. Home. Where it's needed.*" His hand was an inch above the grey silver. "*Nobody's going to wear you. Nobody's going to put you on. I promise.*" A pause. "*You can stay shut. All the way.*"

He touched it.

---

Nothing happened.

That was the strangest thing. Lex had been braced for it. A flash. A cold. A *no*, slamming into his brother's hand like a door slamming on fingers.

Nothing.

Antho's fingers closed round the grey silver band. Gently. Carefully. The way you'd pick up a bird's egg.

And lifted.

---

The *no* went into it.

Lex felt it go. The whole room. The whole tower. The cold steady refusal that had been pouring out of the crown for thirty-two years, filling the room, filling the stair, filling the clearing and the thorns and spreading out across the grey-gold plain like a hand laid on a forehead—

It drew in.

All of it. Back. Into the crown. Into the grey silver. Into the seven blunt points. The way a breath draws in. The way a snail draws into its shell.

And the room was just a room.

Small. Round. Grey. Dead. With a bed and a shelf and a window and an empty chair.

---

And outside, far off, across the plain, Lex heard something.

Faint. So faint. At the very edge of hearing. Coming in through the small round window from the east. From the white road. From the palaces.

From the hall.

A sound like silk tearing.

---

Antho was holding the crown in both hands.

He was looking down at it. His face very still. His black hair falling over his forehead. The grey silver dull and quiet in his scarred fingers.

"*It's cold*," he said.

"*Is it saying anything*?"

"*No*." Antho turned it. Slowly. In his hands. "*Not out loud. Not anymore.*" He looked up at Lex. "*It's not saying no to anything now. It's not saying anything.*" A pause. "*It's just holding it. All of it. Inside. Waiting.*"

"*For what*?"

"*For someone who needs it*," said Antho Vey. "*For someone to put it where it's needed.*"

---

*[The Knot*,] said Ember. *[Lex. The Key. Only a hand that has refused a crown.]*

*I know*.

*[Elric*,] said Ember. Very quietly. *[It's for Elric. To wear. When he turns it. So the net offers him the crown and the crown says no.]*

Lex looked at the plain grey silver circle in his brother's hands.

*So Elric can go first*, he thought. *Into whatever's on the other side.*

*Wearing the one crown in the world that never wanted to be worn*.

---

He opened the basket.

On his back. He swung it round and set it on the floor of the tenth tower and lifted the lid. Nine Hush-cups. Each with an iron lid that sighed. Heavy with nothing.

There was room in the middle. Between them. In the straw.

Antho put the crown in.

Gently. Carefully. In the straw. Between the cups. Like a bird's egg in a nest.

And Lex put the lid back on the basket. And swung it onto his back again.

It wasn't heavier. A crown that size should have weighed something. Silver. A pound, maybe.

It weighed nothing. Like the cups. Heavy with nothing.

He'd carry it home.

---

He looked round the room one more time.

At the bed with its coverlet of dead rose leaves. At the shelf. At the bowl of dust that had been roses. At the worn grey bear with its head on one side. At the scratches. *HES. I'M SORRY. R.*

At the empty chair. Facing the window. Facing west.

"*Should we leave something*?" he said.

Antho looked at him.

"*She grew all this*," said Lex. "*Rhosyn. For a girl who never came.*" He looked at the chair. "*And now we're taking the only thing that ever came. And leaving it empty again.*"

His brother didn't say anything for a long moment.

Then he reached into his coat.

---

It was a stone.

Flat. Grey. River-smooth. Exactly the right size to fit between your finger and your thumb.

Lex stared at it.

"*Mam gave it to me*," said Antho. Quietly. "*Before we left. At the door. She said, Father's got the first one. From the Ferryman's shore. This is the second.*" He turned it in his fingers. "*She picked it up on the shingle. When the Ferryman told her about Wenna.*" A pause. "*From the place where Hesper sat.*"

"*Where Hesper*—"

"*The Ferryman pointed*," said Antho. "*To Wenna. Thirty-two years ago. There, he said. She sat there. With the crown in her lap like a bowl.*" He looked at the stone. "*Mam went and stood in the same place. And picked up a stone.*"

He put it on the chair.

In the middle. On the seat. Where the crown had been.

Flat. Grey. Plain.

"*From her shore*," said Antho Vey. To the empty room. To the chair. To nobody. "*From where she sat. When she decided.*"

---

They went down.

Six turns of the spiral. The grey wood steps worn smooth. The light coming dim through the cracks in the bark.

It was different, going down.

The *no* was gone. The stair was just a stair. The tower was just a tower. Dead. Grey. A white tree coaxed up and round on itself in a dead country. Nothing coming down it anymore. Nothing saying hush.

Lex felt the difference like a draught.

Like a door left open in a house where all the doors had been shut.

---

They came out into the clearing.

And the roses were dying.

---

All of them. All round. A thousand red flowers on the black thorns, open, velvet, dark as wine.

Curling. At the edges. Going brown. Going black. The petals dropping. One by one. Onto the grey dust. Like the roses on the shingle, nine hundred years ago, falling one by one onto the grey stones while the Ferryman waited.

And Rhosyn was on her knees.

---

At the edge of the clearing. Where she'd been standing. Where she'd said *this is as far as I can go*. On her knees in the dust. With her thin arms round herself. With her crown of thorns crooked. With petals falling round her like red snow.

Pim was beside her.

Sitting in the dust. With his eyes shut and his face white and his hands open on his knees. Trying. Lex could feel him trying. A little room. A breath of quiet. Round the woman on her knees.

Not enough.

Hask was standing over them both. Enormous. With his pick in his good hand. Looking east.

"*Smith*," he said. Not turning round. "*Something's coming.*"

---

Rhosyn looked up.

Her face. Grey-green. Wet. Her green eyes very wide. Looking past Lex. Past Antho. At the tower behind them.

"*It's gone*," she whispered. "*Isn't it. You've got it. I felt it go.*"

"*Yes*," said Antho.

"*The no*." She was shaking. "*It's gone. It's not saying it anymore. For the first time in thirty-two years.*" Her hands were pressed against her chest. "*I can hear him again. Oz. Like the sun. Like a fire on the other side of a wall.*" She swallowed. "*And I can hear them.*"

"*Who*?"

The Thorn-Crowned looked east.

"*The hall*," she said. "*The silk. They're waking. All of them. Everyone who was sleeping because of it.*" Her voice was barely there. "*Orrin. And the household. Everyone who was still asleep.*"

"*The household's kept*," said Lex. "*They're gold. They can't*—"

"*They can walk*," said Rhosyn. "*If someone tells them. If someone has his voice.*" She looked up at him. "*Orrin has his voice. Orrin's had it for nine hundred years. He's the steward.*"

---

Lex heard it then.

Through the thorns. From the east. From across the plain.

Not footsteps. Not a march. Something slower. Something heavier.

*Scrape*.

Like a stone dragged a finger's width across another stone.

*Scrape*.

And another. And another. Not an hour apart. Not like Ilda on the white road, one step every hour.

Faster. Many. Hundreds. Thousands.

*Scrape. Scrape. Scrape.*

---

"*Hask*," said Lex. "*Can you see*?"

The big man was looking up. At the top of the thorns. At the black wall rising round the clearing, taller than the hall doors, covered in dying roses.

"*No*," he said. "*Too high.*" He looked down at Lex. "*But I can hear them, smith. I can feel them. Through the ground.*" His face was grey. "*It's like the pit. When the roof's settling. When a whole seam's moving at once.*"

---

"*The roses*," said Antho.

They all looked at him.

He was standing in the middle of the clearing with his face turned up. To the thorns. To the dying flowers. A thousand of them. Going black.

"*They saw the roses*," said Antho. Very quietly. "*From the hall. From the white road. From anywhere on the plain.*" His voice was very strange. "*A mile-wide ring of black thorns in the middle of a grey-gold country. And a thousand red roses opening on it all at once.*"

Rhosyn made a sound.

"*You didn't tell him*," said Antho. To her. Looking at her on her knees in the dust. "*Did you. You didn't say a word. Nobody asked you anything.*"

"*No*," whispered the Thorn-Crowned.

"*You just cried*," said Antho Vey. "*When I told you what the hum was saying. You sat down in the dust and cried.*" He looked up at the roses. "*And a thousand roses came out. Red. On the black. You could have seen them from the Veil.*"

---

Rhosyn put her face in her hands.

"*I didn't mean*—" she said. Through her fingers. "*I didn't—I couldn't stop them—every time I think of her a rose comes, I told Seren, I can't make them stop*—"

"*I know*," said Antho.

"*Ysy said I'd betray you*," said the Thorn-Crowned. "*She said I'd tell him. She said I always tell him.*" Her shoulders were shaking. "*I didn't tell him. I didn't say anything. I just*—"

"*You just grieved*," said Antho. "*Out loud. In red. Where everyone could see.*"

He knelt down beside her. In the dust. Beside Pim.

"*That's not betrayal*," he said. "*That's just being sorry where people can see it.*"

---

*Scrape. Scrape. Scrape*.

Closer. All round. Lex could feel it now too. Through his boots. Through the dust. Through the grey dead roots of the tower behind him. The whole ground trembling, very faintly, the way a table trembles when someone heavy walks across the floor.

From every side.

Not just the east. North. South. West. All round the ring of thorns. Closing.

And over it, through the thorns, faint and dry and soft as sand poured slowly out of a sack—

A voice.

---

"*Majesty*," said Orrin.

It came through the thorns. Through the dying roses. From outside. From everywhere at once.

"*Majesty Rhosyn*," whispered the steward. "*Forgive me. Forgive me for disturbing you.*" A pause. Like dust settling. "*His Majesty sends his love.*"

Rhosyn didn't move.

"*He felt the crown go*," said Orrin. "*Even from there. Even in their world. He felt it lift off the chair.*" The dry soft voice was very gentle. "*He asks me to ask you. Very kindly.*"

Silence.

"*Where are his cousins*?" said Orrin. "*And what have they taken from your tower?*"

---

The Thorn-Crowned knelt in the dust with her hands over her face.

She didn't answer.

Lex watched her not answer. Watched her thin shoulders shake. Watched her hands press harder over her mouth. Watched the last of the roses fall round her, black, dead, onto the grey dust.

She didn't answer.

*Wait*, Antho had said. *Before you answer. As long as you can.*

*How long*?

*Long enough*.

*I'll try*.

She was trying.

---

"*Majesty*," whispered Orrin. Patiently. Kindly. Through the thorns. "*He's waiting.*"

*Scrape. Scrape. Scrape*.

All round. Closing. The ground trembling.

Rhosyn took her hands away from her face.

She looked up. At Lex. At Antho kneeling beside her. At Pim, white and empty, trying. At Hask, standing over them all with his pick.

At the basket on Lex's back.

Her green eyes were very wet.

"*Run*," said the Thorn-Crowned.
