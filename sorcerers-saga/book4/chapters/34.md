# Quiet

Lex woke at dawn and the world was quiet.

Not silent. The stream was running. A blackbird was arguing with another blackbird in the birch wood. Somewhere below him in the cottage, Lark was awake and talking to herself in her language of vowels and indignation. Wren was breathing beside him. Slow. Asleep.

Quiet.

Inside.

---

He lay on his back in the loft and looked at the slope of the roof and listened.

Nothing.

For twenty-two years there'd been something. Not always a voice. Not always words. But *something*. A presence. A warmth. In the back of his head, the way a fire is in the back of a room even when you're not looking at it. Watching. Noting. Footnoting.

*[You're up early*.]

*[You dreamed it again*.]

*[I don't like it. Footnote three. Underlined twice.]*

He lay in the loft and waited for it.

It didn't come.

---

*Ember*, he thought.

Nothing.

*Ember. Good morning.*

Nothing.

He lay there for a long time.

Then he got up. And went down the ladder. And knelt at the hearth.

---

The fire was banked. A heap of grey ash with a red eye in the middle. The way it was every morning.

He knelt and blew on it. Gently. The way you'd wake someone who'd been up all night.

It woke. Flickered. Caught the kindling. Climbed.

And there it was.

*Good morning*, said the fire.

Not in words. It never used words. A warmth. A lift. A small bright gladness. The way a dog is glad when you come down the stairs.

It didn't know. It didn't know anything had happened. It didn't know an old man had stood up out of a bed across the stream and gone out of a blue door with a tall grey figure and a lantern. It didn't know a gold coal had risen out of a hearth and floated across a room and gone into the lantern.

It was just glad.

Because it was the Flame. Because it had been burning since before there were people to burn for. Because a man had knelt down in front of it and blown on its coals.

---

"*Good morning*," said Lex.

His voice cracked.

The fire leaned toward him. Warm. Glad.

He knelt in front of it with his hands on his knees and his face wet and said it again.

"*Good morning*."

And waited.

For the dry voice in the back of his head. *[You do that every morning*.] *[It's a fire.]* *[Malachi said that. To Aurelian. In almost exactly those words.]*

It didn't come.

He'd known it wouldn't.

He'd said it anyway.

*It would be rude not to*.

---

Lark came across the floor on her hands and knees.

She'd been crawling for months, Wren said. She was fast. She came across the kitchen flags from wherever Wren had left her in a sort of furious scuttling lurch, like a crab with a grudge, and arrived at the hearth beside him, and sat back on her bottom.

And looked at the fire.

At the flames. Climbing. Gold. Glad.

And reached for them.

"*No*," said Lex. Catching her hand. Her square fist. Too big for the rest of her. "*No, love. It's hot.*"

She looked at him.

The deep fierce frown. His frown.

"*No*," said Lark.

---

He laughed.

He didn't mean to. It came out of him cracked and wet and sudden, kneeling on the hearth with tears on his face and his daughter's fist in his hand.

"*No*," he agreed. "*No. That's right. No.*"

"*No*," said Lark. With great satisfaction. And took her fist back. And put it in her mouth.

And looked at the fire.

And didn't reach for it again.

---

*[She learns fast*.]

He heard it.

He *heard* it. Clearly. Dry. Gentle. Precise. In the back of his head. *[She learns fast. She gets that from you. You learned not to touch a forge in one go. At two. On Brannoc's woodpile. You cried for an hour.]*

He went very still.

Then he understood.

It wasn't Ember.

It was *him*. It was the shape Ember had left. Twenty-two years of a voice saying exactly what it would say, in exactly that tone, at exactly that moment. Worn into him. Like a path worn across a field. Like a step worn hollow.

He knew what it would say.

He'd always know.

It just wasn't there to say it.

---

He sat on the hearth with his daughter and cried until Wren came down the ladder.

She didn't say anything. She came and sat down on the flags beside him. And put her arm round him. And Lark climbed into his lap and grabbed his nose.

---

They buried his father at noon.

By the stream. At the edge of the clearing. Under the birches. Not the gold ones. The others. White trunks. Bare now. With snow in the forks of the branches.

Brannoc dug the grave.

He wouldn't let anyone help. He came up from the village at first light with a spade over his shoulder and Hedda behind him, and stood at the edge of the clearing for a long time looking at the blue door. And then he went to the place by the stream that Seren showed him and started to dig.

The ground was frozen.

He dug anyway. With his gold fingertips. Which couldn't feel the cold. Which couldn't feel the spade. He dug for three hours in the frozen ground with his bald head steaming and his breath smoking and his face like a block of grey stone.

When it was deep enough he climbed out and stood at the edge and looked down.

"*I stood witness*," he said. To nobody. "*At the wedding. In the snow. I cried so much the priest had to stop.*"

"*Three times*," said Seren.

Brannoc looked at her.

"*I remember*," said Seren Harrow. "*All three.*"

---

They didn't have a priest.

Sister Agathe was in Highcrest with the Queen. There wasn't time to send. Lantern Night was tomorrow. And there were things to do before then that couldn't wait.

So they did it themselves.

Antho and Lex carried him out. From the bed. Wrapped in the blanket Seren had knitted, the blue one that didn't match the door. He weighed nothing. Less than nothing. A bundle of sticks. Lex had carried bags of charcoal heavier.

They laid him in the grave by the stream.

And stood round it. In the snow. All of them.

---

Seren. Barefoot. In a blue dress. Lex didn't know where she'd found it. It was too big and the hem was frayed and it had been folded in a chest for a very long time.

*Mam made it*, she said. When Wren asked. *For the wedding. All autumn. I found it in the chest at the cottage. I didn't know what it was.* She looked down at it. *I know now.*

Antho. With his black hair loose and his face white and the gold patch on the back of his left hand. With Seraphine beside him, who'd been at the Forge with Hedda since she sent the dragons west, and had walked up the river path in the dark when Brannoc came for her. With ink on her fingers. Holding his hand.

Wren. With the baby.

Pim. Small. In his blue coat. Standing very close to Antho. With his hand on the pocket over his heart.

Hask. At the edge of the trees. With his gold arm and his good hand on the haft of his pick. Coughing.

Brannoc and Hedda. Side by side. Brannoc's arm round Hedda's shoulders.

Kestrel wasn't there. She was still walking. Somewhere in the Greyspine. Following a fire in a doorway.

---

Nobody said anything for a long time.

Then Seren knelt down. At the edge of the grave. In the snow. In her blue dress.

And put something in.

A stone. Flat. Grey. River-smooth.

The first one. From the Ferryman's shore. That she'd brought back in the spring and put in his hand and closed his fingers round. *Bring me back a stone*, he'd said. *For skipping.*

She laid it on his chest. On the blue blanket. Over his heart.

"*You never skipped it*," she said. Very quietly. To the grave. "*You kept it on the table by the bed. Every day. You picked it up and turned it over and put it back down.*"

Nobody spoke.

"*I'll skip it for you*," said Seren. "*Someday. On the river. Below the mill. Where Antho skipped one five times.*" Her voice cracked. "*I'll skip it and count. And I won't lie about how many.*"

---

Antho made a sound.

Lex looked at him.

His brother was looking at the grave. At the stone on the blanket. With his face doing something Lex had seen it do once before. In the snow, at the edge of the world. Laughing about who was taller.

"*Five*," said Antho. Hoarsely. "*It was five.*"

"*I know*," said Lex.

"*I told you four*."

"*I know*," said Lex Vey. "*You told me the truth later. Under the stars.*" He put his hand on his brother's shoulder. "*Father knew. He always knew. He said so. On the way back from the Heart. He said, your brother skipped it five times and told you four, and I've never been prouder of anyone.*"

Antho turned and looked at him.

"*He said that*?"

"*He said that*," said Lex.

He hadn't. Not exactly. Lex didn't know if he had. Ember would have known. Ember would have had it in Volume Two.

He said it anyway.

Somebody should.

---

They filled it in.

Brannoc. And then Antho. And then Lex. Taking turns with the spade. The frozen earth going back in. Covering the blue blanket. Covering the stone.

When it was done, Brannoc went to the cart.

He came back with something. Wrapped in sacking. Square. Heavy. He set it down at the head of the grave and pulled the sacking off.

---

A stone.

Not Gloam-black, like Kell's. Grey. Vale stone. From the quarry above Ashbourne. Cut square. Smooth.

And on it, in his big careful hand, the way he'd cut the first rune he ever struck:

*ARION VEY*
*He tried*

---

Lex looked at it for a long time.

"*When did you*—"

"*In the summer*," said Brannoc. "*When he stopped getting up. I went to the quarry. I thought, it'll be winter. Ground'll be hard. Won't be time to cut it then.*" He brushed the snow off the top of the stone with his enormous gold-tipped hand. "*So I cut it in the summer. And put it in the shed. And didn't tell anyone.*"

"*He tried*," said Antho.

"*That's what he said*," said Brannoc. "*Every time anyone asked him anything. Will you be here, Arion? I'll try. Will you hold on, Arion? I'll try.*" He looked at the stone. "*He never once said yes. He never once made a promise he couldn't keep. He just said, I'll try.*"

He looked at the grave.

"*And he did*," said Brannoc Holt. "*Lad. He did.*"

---

Lex wrote it that afternoon.

In the grey book. Sitting at the table under the west window in the blue-door cottage, where the bed was empty now and the curtain drawn back and the Lantern on the table beside it burning. Three panes of glass and one of horn.

He opened the book to the back.

Past the eleven thousand Vhessans. Past Kell. Past the twenty-three from the rim.

To a page with three lines on it. In a hand that started small and neat and got worse and worse until it was barely writing at all.

He hadn't read them. Not before. They weren't his yet.

He read them now.

---

*Arion Vey. Of the Spire. And of Harrow.*

*He turned back twice. And then he didn't.*

*He stood up*.

---

He sat with it for a long time.

His father's hand. Shaking. Going off the line. The last two words so wavering he could barely make them out.

*He stood up*.

He took the pencil out of the binding.

---

His own hand was big and clumsy and careful. A smith's hand. It always had been. He'd learned his letters from his mother at a kitchen table at Harrow, and then from nobody for a long time, and he still made them the way you'd make a weld. Slowly. Pressing hard.

He wrote under his father's lines.

*He kept the ledgers for two hundred years. He drew the world from the inside with a pendulum.*

*He sealed his sons and went away. He was sorry every day.*

*He came home. He was old. His wife brought him pies.*

He stopped.

He looked at it.

And wrote one more line.

*He tried*.

---

He didn't write anything else.

There wasn't anything else. Brannoc was right. That was all of it. That was the whole of a man in two words.

He went to close the book.

And stopped.

---

On the inside of the back cover. On the leather. Where he'd laid it, at the foot of the Veil, on a white road in a country with no days.

A moth.

Small. Grey-gold. Dead. On its back. With its legs folded up and its shredded wings spread out on either side. The painted eyes on them faded almost to nothing.

He'd forgotten it.

He looked at it for a long time.

*It went past the lanterns*, he'd said. To Antho. On the road. *Every one. It wanted them so much. And it went past.*

*Somebody should write that down*.

---

He turned back a page. To the blank one before his father's. The page he'd left blank after Kell, in the first week of winter, a year ago, by the pool. *For later*.

And wrote.

*A moth. Maelis's. The last one.*

*It carried my mother's blood from the Spire to the edge of the world. Forty nights. It went past every lantern in every village between Highcrest and the Veil. It wanted every one of them.*

*It went past*.

*It had been told to take it home*.

---

He closed the book.

He put it back in its oilcloth. Back inside his shirt. Against the bell.

The bell didn't ring.

It hadn't rung since the summer. Since before the Court. He'd thought it would ring, here. Last night. This morning. At the grave.

It hadn't.

He didn't understand.

He'd have asked Ember.

---

*[Because nobody's sad*,] he thought. In the dry voice. In the shape it had left. *[Not the right kind. Not the kind that needs company. Everyone who's sad has someone holding their hand.]*

He didn't know if that was true.

It sounded like something Ember would say.

That would have to do.

---

He opened his panel that evening.

He didn't mean to. He was sitting on the oak ramp of the blue-door cottage in the dusk, in the snow, with his back against the door. Alone. Looking at the grave by the stream. At the grey stone with two words on it.

He thought, *I wonder*.

And the panel came up.

He looked at it.

At the attunements. He hadn't seen them since before the Court. Tide and Gale, Speaking. Shade and Radiance, Answering. He didn't know when that had happened. Somewhere. In the glass palace. At the foot of the last thread. On the white road.

He didn't care.

He was looking at the empty space at the bottom of the panel.

Where there'd always been something.

Small. Gold. Faint. In the corner. A line he'd stopped noticing years ago, the way you stop noticing the sound of a clock.

It was gone.

And as he looked at the place where it had been, a new panel came up. Small. In the corner. Grey.

---

GUARDIAN CONSTRUCT — OFFLINE
"It chose."
:::

---

He sat on the ramp in the snow and looked at it until it faded.

*GUARDIAN CONSTRUCT — ONLINE*.

He remembered it. The first day. At nineteen. In the Ashbourne forge. When his seal cracked and the world went gold and a voice in his head said *Good evening, Smith. Please do not die. I have only just arrived.*

He'd named it Ember. For the gold of its letters.

*It chose*.

---

Antho came and sat down beside him.

On the ramp. In the snow. With his back against the blue door. He didn't say anything for a long time.

"*I lost a voice once*," he said at last.

Lex looked at him.

"*The Murmur*," said Antho. "*When you wove the net. When the Hollow unfolded. When Malachi sat with it in the deep.*" He looked at the grave. "*It was in my head for eight years. It was the worst thing that ever happened to me. It wanted. It was hungry. It talked through me.*" A pause. "*And then it was gone. One night. All at once.*"

"*And*?"

"*And I missed it*," said Antho. Very quietly. "*Isn't that mad? For weeks. I'd wake up at three in the morning and lie very still and listen. And it wasn't there. And I'd feel—*" He stopped. "*Not glad. I was glad. But not only glad.*"

"*That's not the same*," said Lex.

"*No*," agreed his brother. "*Mine hated me. Yours*—" He swallowed. "*Yours loved you.*"

---

Lex didn't say anything.

He sat on the ramp in the snow and looked at the grave and felt the quiet in the back of his head like a missing tooth. Like a room in a house where someone used to sleep.

"*It said chapter forty*," he said.

Antho looked at him.

"*Mam told me*," said Lex. "*What it said. At the end. Before it went into the lantern.*" He swallowed. "*It said, tell him I'll be in the main text. Not a footnote. Chapter forty.*"

"*What does that mean*?"

"*It was writing a book*," said Lex. "*The Book of What Was. Volume Two. Mostly footnotes. About things it didn't like.*" His voice cracked. "*It said I kept doing things it didn't like. Going down holes. Talking to Crowned. Falling in love with Mistwalkers who break my nose.*"

Antho laughed. Very softly.

"*And at the end*," said Lex. "*It said, I'll be in the main text.*" He looked at the grave. "*I don't know what's in chapter forty. I never read it. It never let me.*"

"*Maybe you'll write it*," said Antho.

Lex looked at him.

"*Somebody should*," said Antho Vey. "*You wrote the moth.*"

---

They sat there a long time.

The dusk went to dark. The snow stopped. The stars came out. Hard and white and close.

Behind them, through the blue door, the Lantern burned. On the table. By the empty bed.

"*Tomorrow*," said Antho.

"*I know*."

"*Lantern Night*. *He'll be there. Caddoc. At the Knot.*"

"*I know*."

"*We've got the crown*."

"*We've got the crown*," said Lex. "*In the basket. In the straw.*" He looked at the basket. On the ramp beside him. Where it had been since they came home. "*Three cups left. We won't need them. Not down there. Mal makes the room.*"

"*Elric*," said Antho.

Lex didn't answer for a moment.

"*Father went to see him*," he said at last. "*Last night. With the Ferryman. He said it was on the way.*" He looked at the stars. "*He promised Elric he'd tell him what it was like. On the other side. Before he goes.*"

"*Before he goes*," said Antho. Very quietly.

"*He's going to turn it*," said Lex. "*Isn't he. Elric. Wearing her crown. He's going to turn it and go with the thread. First.*"

"*Father said he'd worked it out*. *Three years. Nothing else to do.*"

"*I don't want him to*."

"*I know*," said Antho. "*Nor do I.*"

"*He betrayed Father*," said Lex. "*He opened the wards at Caldris. He carried him out in a sack.*" His voice was very strange. "*And I don't want him to go.*"

"*Father forgave him*," said Antho.

"*I know*."

"*At the Knot*. *In the spring. Tell me, he said. And Elric told him.*" Antho looked at his scarred hands. "*And Father said, you were first. The first friend I had in two hundred years.*"

Lex didn't say anything for a long time.

"*Then I suppose he should go first*," he said at last.

---

Wren found them there at midnight.

She came across the stepping stones from the other cottage in the dark, with a blanket round her shoulders and a candle in a holder with no glass. She stood at the foot of the ramp and looked at them. Two brothers. Sitting in the snow with their backs against a blue door.

"*Bed*," she said.

"*In a minute*."

"*Now*," said Wren Ashdown. "*Both of you. You've got to go down to the bottom of the world tomorrow and fight a Crowned king made of iron. You can't do that on no sleep.*"

"*I've done it before*," said Lex.

"*You've done everything on no sleep*," said Wren. "*That's why your nose has been broken twice.*"

Antho laughed.

He got up. Slowly. Stiff from the cold. He put his hand on Lex's shoulder for a moment. Then he went down the ramp. Past Wren. Into the dark. Toward the Coldwater crew's tent at the edge of the clearing, where Seraphine was waiting with ink on her fingers.

Wren came up the ramp. And sat down beside Lex. Where Antho had been.

She didn't say anything.

She put her head on his shoulder.

---

"*She said no*," said Lex. After a while. "*This morning. To the fire.*"

"*She says no to everything now*," said Wren. "*It's her favourite word. It's her only word.*" She smiled. In the dark. He could feel it on his shoulder. "*She said it to porridge. She said it to Hask. She said it to the goat.*"

"*Correspondence*?"

"*Correspondence ate her sock*," said Wren. "*She said no very firmly. Correspondence didn't listen. Correspondence never listens.*"

Lex laughed. Short. Cracked.

"*She said it to a king*," he said.

"*She said it to a king*," said Wren. Very quietly. "*Her first word. To the biggest thing in the world. With a fist in her mouth.*" She was quiet for a moment. "*Your father heard it.*"

"*He said it was the best word anyone ever said*."

"*It was*," said Wren.

---

"*It's her birthday tomorrow*," said Lex.

Wren went still.

"*Lantern Night*," said Lex. "*A year. She was born on the rubble. On Lantern Night. While the Well came home.*" He looked at the dark. "*And I'll be at the bottom of the world.*"

"*I know*."

"*I wasn't there when she stood up*. *I wasn't there when she crawled. I wasn't there for her first tooth after the first one. I wasn't there for*—" He stopped. "*I'll be there for her first word. I was there for that. That's one.*"

"*That's one*," said Wren.

"*And tomorrow I won't be there for her birthday*."

Wren didn't say anything for a long time.

"*You'll be there for the next one*," she said at last.

"*Will I*?"

She lifted her head off his shoulder. And looked at him. In the dark. In the starlight. With her green eyes very steady.

"*You'll try*," said Wren Ashdown.

He looked at her.

"*That's all anyone's got*," she said. "*Your father said so. Every day. For a year.*" She put her head back on his shoulder. "*You'll try. And it'll have to be enough.*"

---

He didn't sleep.

He lay in the loft with Wren asleep beside him and the baby asleep in her cradle under the window, and listened to the quiet.

At three in the morning, Lark woke up.

She didn't cry. She lay in her cradle and looked at the window. Soaped. White. With the starlight coming through it.

And then she looked at him.

He was lying on his side. Facing her. With his eyes open.

She looked at him for a long time. In the dark. With her grey-green eyes.

"*No*," she said. Very softly. Conversationally. As if remarking on the weather.

"*No*," Lex agreed.

She seemed satisfied. She rolled over. And went back to sleep.

---

He lay there and listened to her breathe.

*[She's going to be impossible*,] he thought. In the dry voice. In the shape. *[She's going to say no to everyone. Teachers. Kings. Dragons. Her own grandchildren. Footnote nine hundred and four.]*

*Good*, he thought.

And for a moment—just a moment—lying in the dark, he could have sworn the shape in the back of his head laughed.

---

Dawn came grey and cold.

Lantern Night.

He went down the ladder. And knelt at the hearth. And blew on the coals.

They woke. Flickered. Caught. Climbed.

*Good morning*, said the fire.

"*Good morning*," said Lex Vey.

And got up. And picked up the basket. And went out to the dragons.
