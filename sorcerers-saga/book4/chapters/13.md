# Room to Breathe

Seraphine Ilvane had said good morning to the Glass Queen every day for a month, and on the thirty-first morning, the Glass Queen said it back.

"*Good morning*," said Seraphine. To her spectacles. Holding them up to the window of the library at the Lantern Keep in the grey dawn light, the way she did every morning before she put them on. Checking the lenses for smears. "*Your Majesty.*"

And from the left lens, very small, very far, like a fly at a window—

"*You read too fast*."

Seraphine nearly dropped them.

---

She didn't, though. She held them very still, at arm's length, and looked at the tiny pale face in the left lens. No bigger than her smallest fingernail. White hair. Grey eyes. Watching her.

"*I'm sorry*," said Seraphine.

"*You're always sorry*," said Ysenne. "*You say sorry to doors when you bump into them. You said sorry to a book yesterday when you turned two pages at once.*" The tiny face tilted. "*Why do you say good morning to me?*"

"*It seemed rude not to*."

"*Rude*."

"*You're in my spectacles*," said Seraphine. "*Every day. You sit in them while I read. You said so. In the Hall of Lanterns.*" She swallowed. "*If someone's going to sit in my spectacles every day I ought to at least say good morning. It's only manners.*"

The tiny face was quiet for a long moment.

"*Nobody's ever said good morning to me*," said the Glass Queen. "*Not in nine hundred years. Not once.*"

"*Well*," said Seraphine. "*Now someone has.*"

She put the spectacles on.

The world went sharp. The library. The long tables. The shelves. The window, soaped white. And at the very edge of her left eye, if she didn't look straight at it, a faint pale shimmer. Like a reflection in a pane.

"*I'm going to read now*," said Seraphine. "*I'll try to go slower.*"

---

She didn't tell Antho.

Not that morning. She'd tell him later. She'd tell him everything later. That was how they did it. But not that morning. That morning she had a map to find.

---

The Spire was soaped.

All four thousand panes. From the ropes. She came up the long stair from the Crown Terrace in the middle of the morning, two days after the gold came down on Ashbourne, and the great white tower rose above her blind and milky against the spring sky like a candle with a cloth over it.

Inside, it was dim. Lamplit. The lamps were all horn now. Lex had shown the Spire's smiths how, and they'd spent a week scraping cow-horn thin as paper and fitting it into every lantern in the building. The light was soft and gold and blurred. Like being inside a cloud at sunset.

She went up.

Six floors in the clanking brass lift. Then a corridor. Then a door. Plain. Oak. With a brass plate on it that still said, in small neat letters, *WARDEN-ARCHITECT*.

Nobody had taken it down.

She didn't think anybody ever would.

---

The rooms were cold.

Nobody had lit a fire in them since the autumn. Since an old man in a wheeled chair had come up here in the night on Lantern Night with a boy and a dragon and drunk twenty years from a Moth Queen's hands, and stood up, and walked across the floor, and poured it all back.

The desk was still there. Under the long window. With the pale ring in the wood where a teacup had stood for two hundred years. A grey book open on it, at a page about the price of bread.

The bookcase.

Tall. Dark. Full. She knew which book to pull. Lex had told her. Arion had told Lex. A thin red one, on the fourth shelf, between a history of the Saltreach fisheries and a treatise on drainage. *Notes on the Stability of Load-Bearing Arches*. Volume Three.

She pulled it.

And the bookcase swung open.

---

The secret room was small.

She'd expected more. She'd built it up in her head, over a year of hearing about it. A hidden library. A vault. A cabinet of wonders.

It was a cupboard.

A narrow room, no wider than a corridor, with a window at the end. Shelves on both walls. Papers. Rolled. Folded. Stacked. Hundreds of them. Tied with string. Labelled in a small neat hand.

And on the floor, rolled in oilcloth, leaning in the corner like a carpet nobody had got round to laying—

The map.

---

She unrolled it on the floor of the Warden-Architect's study.

It took up most of the room. She had to move the chair. She had to weigh the corners with books. It kept trying to roll itself back up, the way old maps do, as if it were tired of being looked at.

It was the world.

Not the world as it looked from above. Not hills and rivers and roads. The world as it looked from *inside*. From underneath. The great threads of the net, running under the land like veins under skin. Drawn in ink. Hundreds of them. Thousands. Fine lines and thick ones, crossing and recrossing, knotting and unknotting, all of them running in, in, in, toward one point. Near the middle. Under the Drowned Country.

*The First Knot*, it said there. In tiny letters. *(Approx.)*

She sat back on her heels.

"*Oh*," she said. Out loud. To nobody. "*Oh, Arion.*"

He'd done this with a pendulum. With a compass. Eleven years. Walking. Every thread. Following each one with a weight on a string, watching it swing, marking where it pulled. Eleven years of walking the world with his eyes on a pendulum. Drawing it. Line by line.

He'd said he understood nothing.

She looked at the map spread out across the floor of his cold study, every line of it drawn by hand, and thought he'd understood a great deal more than he knew.

---

The last thread was easy to find.

It was the thickest line on the map. Drawn darker than the rest. Running in from the very edge, from the left side of the paper, from a long grey band he'd shaded in pencil and labelled *THE VEIL (EDGE)*. Straight as a ruler. Under the Sundered Lands. Under the grey country west of the Teeth. Rising, where it came under the Greyspine. A little circle drawn there, and a note: *Coldwater. ~200 fath. Audible?*

And then on. Under the mountains. Under the Drowned Country. Down. Into the Knot.

Beside it, all the way along, in tiny letters, he'd written a single word. Over and over.

*Last. Last. Last. Last.*

---

And tucked into the oilcloth, at the very end of the roll, where it would have been the last thing you saw when you unrolled the map—

A paper.

Small. Old. Folded twice. Not his hand. Not small and neat. Large. Square. Slanting. Pressed so hard into the paper that the pen had nearly gone through.

She knew that hand.

She'd seen it on a vellum in a box carried by a goat. *The one they drank. The one we caged. Forgive us.* She'd seen it in the margins of the Listeners' Codex. She'd seen it carved into the walls of a tomb at the Heart.

Aurelian.

---

It wasn't his paper. Not the original. She could see that at once. It was a copy. Made by someone who'd been very careful to copy the hand exactly, the slant of it, the weight, even a blot. A young man, perhaps. A long time ago. Copying something he'd found in a vault and didn't understand, so he'd have it. So he could look at it later.

There was a note in the corner. In the small neat hand.

*Copied from the third vault, Founders' level. Year 1101. Original crumbling. I do not understand this. Keeping it in case I ever do. — A.V.*

A hundred and forty years.

He'd kept it a hundred and forty years. In case.

She unfolded it.

---

*I have woven the edge last*, it said. *The Veil. It is the final knot and it holds all the rest. It holds them out.*

*I do not want to hold them out forever.*

*They are not all crowned. There are nine thousand with them who did not choose. Cooks and grooms and nurses and children. There are the dead who will come. There will be more dead, every year, in a country where no one is allowed to die properly.*

*I cannot help them. There is no room. The room is what I have caged, and it is starving, and if I let it near them it will eat them.*

*So I have left the Veil one last turn.*

*When there is someone on this side who can make room for what is on the other, unwrite it.*

*Not before.*

---

Seraphine read it four times.

She sat on the cold floor of the Warden-Architect's study with the great map spread round her like a sea and read it four times. Her lips moving.

And then she took off her spectacles. And held them up. To the soaped window.

"*Did you read that*?" she said.

The tiny face in the left lens didn't answer for a long time.

"*Yes*," said Ysenne.

"*Did you know*?"

"*No*." The voice was very small. Smaller than usual. "*Nine thousand. He counted them. The little man with the hammer. He counted our household.*" A pause. "*Nobody ever counts the household.*"

"*He wanted to bring them home*," said Seraphine.

"*He wanted to bring them home*," said the Glass Queen. Slowly. As if trying the words to see if they fit. "*When there was room.*"

She didn't say anything else.

When Seraphine looked again, the lens was empty.

---

She rode Ashka to the Blightmarch the next morning.

Antho flew her. He didn't ask why. He'd taken one look at her face when she came back from the Spire with the paper in her hand and her spectacles in her pocket, and gone to get the dragon.

She read it to him on the way. Shouting into the wind. On Ashka's neck. With his arms round her and the green of the western shires going by underneath.

He didn't say anything.

When she'd finished, he tightened his arms round her. And put his chin on her shoulder. And they flew the rest of the way like that.

---

Ivy Moss was sitting on the rim.

Where she always sat. With her bad foot tucked under her and her good one dangling over the deep. And beside her, on the grey stone, his small thin legs dangling too, his blue coat with the brass buttons buttoned to the chin—

Pim.

He'd come with them. On Ashka. Behind Antho. He hadn't asked. He'd just been in the stable when they went out, sitting on the mounting block, waiting.

He'd got off the dragon at the pool and walked straight to the rim and sat down beside Ivy. And neither of them had said anything. And they'd been sitting there ever since. Two quiet children with their feet over the dark.

"*They're friends*," said Antho. Quietly. Watching them. "*Did you know?*"

"*Since when*?"

"*Since the autumn*. *Since he came here with the stick for Kell.*" Antho's mouth twisted. "*They write to each other. Letters. Through Quill.*" A pause. "*I've seen one. It was a drawing of a cat. With no words.*"

"*And her reply*?"

"*A drawing of a cat*," said Antho. "*With no words. Slightly bigger.*"

---

Seraphine knelt on the rim.

She took a Hush-cup out of her satchel. One of the old ones. From the spring. Horseshoe iron, shaped by Lex's thumbs into the space inside a pair of cupped hands. The broken circle of the Hollow cut on the inside of the curve. Seven Hush-runes round the rim.

Empty.

"*Ivy*," she said.

The girl looked up. With her strange light eyes.

"*I need to ask it something*," said Seraphine. "*Mal. And I need you to tell me if it hurts.*"

---

She told Ivy first. Out loud. On the rim. With Antho behind her and Hask leaning on Kell's stick a few paces off and Pim on the other side of Ivy, listening.

The grey country. The Court. The pushing.

*Anyone with room in them, it pushes*. *Slowly. Firmly. The way the edge pushed the four who came apart.*

The Ferryman's words. *Unless you carried room with you. Your own. Enough to stand in. Enough to breathe.*

*Room can be carried*. *In a cup.*

Ivy listened. Her colourless eyes on Seraphine's face. When it was finished, she was quiet for a long time.

Then she turned. And looked down into the pool. And Seraphine felt her listen.

Not with her ears. Seraphine had never heard anything with her ears, not the way the others did. She heard the gaps. The spaces between letters. The silence round a sound. She'd been the first living person in nine hundred years to hear the Hollow, and she'd heard it the way you hear a held breath.

She heard Ivy listen now. Felt it. A small quiet opening in the air beside her. A girl leaning into a room.

*[...room...]*

There it was. Soft. Young. All round. Not in the ears.

*[...they want to carry ROOM...]*

---

"*It wants to know why*," said Ivy.

"*Because the place they're going hasn't got any*," said Seraphine. "*None at all. Not a breath. Not a crack.*" She looked down into the pool. At the three small gold lights far down. "*They'll come apart there. Unless they bring some of yours.*"

*[...where...]*

"*Beyond the edge*," said Seraphine. "*Where the ones who drank you went.*"

The pool went very still.

*[...THEM...]*

---

Seraphine felt it flinch.

All of it. The whole pool. The whole green slope. Every blade of grass in a hundred miles. Flinching. The way a child flinches when you say the name of the man who used to beat it.

*[...the ones who DRANK me...]*

"*Yes*."

*[...they drank and drank...I cried and they drank...the tenth one heard...she was the only one who heard...]*

"*I know*."

*[...they're the reason I was HUNGRY...]*

"*I know*," said Seraphine. Very gently. "*I know, little one. I'm not asking you to go there. I'm not asking you to give them anything.*" She held up the Hush-cup. Empty. "*Just a little. For the smith. For his brother. For a boy who makes rooms like you do.*" She glanced at Pim. "*So they can go into the place with no room, and fetch something, and come back.*"

*[...fetch what...]*

"*A crown*," said Seraphine. "*The tenth one's crown. The one she put down on the stone. The one that never touched you.*"

---

The pool was silent for a long time.

Seraphine knelt on the rim with the cup in her hands and waited. She was good at waiting. She'd waited at the bottom of a library for twenty years for someone to tell her she was clever. She'd waited for Antho to come back from the Hollow. She'd waited for him to ask.

*[...hers...]* said Mal at last. Very small. *[...the one who heard...]*

"*Hers*."

*[...she poured back...]* The voice was very soft. Wondering. *[...a cupful...of what she was born with...she poured it into me when I was nearly empty...it was warm...]*

"*Yes*."

*[...it was the first thing anyone ever GAVE me...]*

---

"*It says yes*," said Ivy Moss.

Seraphine looked at her.

"*It says yes*," said Ivy again. Her strange eyes were wet. "*It says for her. For the tenth. It says it'll give room for her crown.*" She swallowed. "*It says it'll hurt a little. Not much. Like giving blood. It says it can't give much at once. It's still little. It's only just home.*"

"*How much*?"

Ivy tilted her head. Listening.

"*A cup*," she said. "*Every few days. That's all. Any more and it starts to feel thin. Like when you're hungry.*" She looked at Seraphine. "*It doesn't want to be hungry again. Not ever.*"

"*No*," said Seraphine. "*Of course not.*" She turned the cup in her hands. "*How long till midsummer?*"

"*Forty days*," said Antho. Behind her.

"*Twelve cups*," said Seraphine. "*Maybe thirteen.*"

---

Ivy filled the first one.

She took the Hush-cup from Seraphine's hands. Carefully. With both of hers. And leaned out over the rim. Over the deep. Over the dark that went down forever. And dipped it.

Not into water. There was no water. Into the *room*.

And lifted it out.

Seraphine had expected—she didn't know what she'd expected. Dark. Something dark in the cup, like ink. Like the pool itself.

It looked empty.

The iron cup in Ivy's hands, with the broken circle cut inside it and the seven runes round its rim. Empty. Nothing in it at all. You could see the bottom. You could see the glyph.

But it was *heavy*.

Ivy's arms dipped as she brought it back over the rim. As if it were full to the brim with something. As if she were carrying a bowl of soup up a flight of stairs.

"*Oh*," said Ivy. Startled. "*Oh, it's heavy.*"

"*What does it feel like*?"

The girl looked down at the empty cup in her hands.

"*Like holding your breath*," said Ivy Moss. "*Right before you laugh.*"

---

*[...there...]* said Mal. Faintly. *[...that's a bit of me...be careful with it...]*

"*I will*," said Seraphine.

*[...it's not a FILL...]* said Mal. Anxiously. *[...it's not something to fill UP with...it's room...it's for having room IN...]*

"*I understand*."

*[...if they drink it they'll have room inside...where the grey can't press...]* A pause. *[...for a while...]*

"*How long a while*?"

The pool was quiet.

*[...three days...]* said Mal. *[...a cup is three days...for one...]*

---

Seraphine did the sums on the rim.

She didn't need paper. She'd been doing sums in her head since she was four. She did them now, sitting on the grey stone with the Hush-cup in her lap, heavy with nothing.

Twelve cups. Thirteen if they were lucky. Three days each, for one person.

Lex needed room. Antho needed room. Pim—

She looked at Pim.

He was looking back at her. With his grey-violet eyes. Very steady.

Pim had room already. A little piece of the seventh. Made himself, out of being hurt, a long time ago. Moths couldn't drink from him. Maybe the grey couldn't press him.

Maybe it could. He was a boy. He was fourteen. Nobody knew.

Hask had drunk the Moth Queen's light once. Hask had worn wings. Maybe the grey would know him a little. Maybe it wouldn't push.

Maybe it would.

"*Thirty-six days*," she said. Quietly. "*Of room. If it's only Lex and Antho, that's eighteen each.*" She swallowed. "*If Pim needs it too, that's twelve. If Hask needs it, nine.*"

Nobody said anything.

"*Nine days*," said Antho. "*To walk into a country with no sun and find a tower in a forest of thorns and get a crown off a chair and walk out again.*"

"*If Hask needs it*."

"*And if he doesn't*?"

"*Twelve*," said Seraphine.

---

Hask came across the grass.

Slowly. With his bandaged arm in its sling and Kell's stick in his good hand. He stood over them at the rim and looked down at the empty iron cup in Seraphine's lap.

"*I won't need it*," he said.

They looked up at him.

"*She was in me*," said Silas Hask. "*Maelis. For a whole season. Her light. In my chest. In my eyes. I had her wings.*" He looked at the cup. "*I know what it feels like to have no room. I had no room for months. She filled it all up.*" His face didn't change. "*I don't think their country will push me much. I think it'll know me.*"

"*You don't know that*."

"*No*," agreed Hask. "*But I'm going. And I'm not drinking the little one's room. Not after what I did to it.*" He turned. And went back to his place by the black stone. And stood there. In front of the pool. With the stick.

Nobody argued.

You didn't argue with Silas Hask. Not when he'd decided.

---

"*Twelve days*," said Antho. Very quietly. "*If Pim needs it.*"

Pim, on the rim, said nothing.

He looked at the cup in Seraphine's lap for a long time. And then he looked at Ivy. And Ivy looked back.

And something passed between them. Something Seraphine couldn't hear. Not in words. In the gap. In the space where the words would have been.

Ivy nodded.

Pim turned to Seraphine.

"*Fourteen*," he said.

---

She stared at him.

"*Fourteen*," said Pim again. He hardly ever said anything. Two words in a row was a speech. "*Not twelve.*" He pointed. At the cup. Then at himself. Then at the pool. "*I can make room. In the grey. Like I do. For sleeping.*" He looked at Antho. "*Then you don't drink at night. Only in the day.*"

Antho's face went very still.

"*At night you sleep in my room*," said Pim. "*Not the cup's room.*" He spread his small thin hands. "*Saves two days.*"

"*Pim*—"

"*I can do it*," said Pim. "*Ivy says.*"

They looked at Ivy.

"*It says he can*," said Ivy Moss. "*Mal. It says the boy makes rooms like it does. It says he made one for the moths and they couldn't drink. It says he'll make one in the grey and it won't be able to push.*" She looked at Pim. "*For a while. Not forever. He'll get tired.*" A pause. "*It says, tell him to be careful. It says, he's only little too.*"

---

Antho didn't say anything for a long time.

He knelt on the rim beside Seraphine and looked at the boy. At Pim. Who had been silent for twelve years and talking for three. Who had come up a mountain once in the snow, beaten and thin and starved, and stood outside the door of the Lantern Keep and said nothing at all. And Antho had opened the door and said, *come in*.

"*You're not coming*," said Antho.

Pim looked at him.

"*It's too dangerous*," said Antho. "*It's beyond the edge of the world. It's the Crowned. It's the gold. It's*—"

"*I'm coming*," said Pim.

---

Seraphine had heard him say perhaps four hundred words in three years.

She'd never heard him say anything like that. Not in that voice. Not flat and quiet and absolutely certain, like a door closing.

"*You need me*," said Pim. "*Fourteen days.*" He looked at Antho. "*Not twelve.*"

Antho opened his mouth.

And Seraphine put her hand on his arm.

He looked at her.

She didn't say anything. She didn't need to. She just looked at him. And then at Pim. At the small thin boy in the blue coat with the brass buttons, sitting on the rim of the seventh Well with his feet over the dark, who had decided.

Antho looked at her for a long moment.

Then he closed his mouth.

---

Brannoc made the lids.

He came up to the pool three days later on a cart, with Hedda driving and a box of iron in the back and his fingertips shining gold. He set up his little travelling forge on the grass below the Coldwater crew's house and lit it and didn't ask anyone for help.

Seraphine watched him work.

He picked up a bar of iron out of the fire with his bare hand.

She made a sound. Everyone did. Hedda swore. Kit, at the black stone, dropped Kell's stick and had to pick it up again.

Brannoc stood at the anvil with a bar of white-hot iron in his gold-tipped fingers and grinned through his beard.

"*Told you*," he said. "*Every smith in Caldor's going to be jealous.*"

---

He made twelve lids. Round. Flat. Iron. Each one fitted to a Hush-cup so well it sighed when you set it on. And on the underside of each one, where it would face the room inside, he cut a single rune. In his big careful hand. The first rune he'd ever learned. The one Lex had shown him in the Ashbourne forge six years ago, the night before Thornhollow, the night Brannoc Unbound.

*KNIT*.

"*So it holds*," he said. When Seraphine asked. "*So nothing gets out that shouldn't. So nothing comes apart.*" He looked at the cups lined up on the grass. "*That's all a lid's for, lass. To keep a thing whole till you need it.*"

---

She told Antho that night.

Not the paper. He knew the paper. She'd shouted it into the wind on Ashka's neck. The rest. The part she'd been carrying since the Spire.

They were in the little black house on the slope above the pool. The Coldwater crew's house. Brigid had given them the back room. There was a window. Facing the pool. With the *HOME* lantern in it, glass, soaped.

He was sitting on the bed with his back to the wall. She was sitting beside him with her knees drawn up and her spectacles off, folded, face down on the sill.

"*He meant them*," she said.

Antho waited.

"*Aurelian*," she said. "*When he wrote it. When he said, unwrite it when there's someone who can make room for what's on the other side.*" She looked at her hands. "*I thought he meant the Crowned. At first. Bring the Crowned home.*" She shook her head. "*He didn't. He meant the household. The nine thousand. The ones who didn't choose.*"

"*The kept*."

"*The kept*. *And the dead.*" She swallowed. "*He wrote, there will be more dead, every year, in a country where no one is allowed to die properly. He knew, Antho. Nine hundred years ago. He knew what it would become. A country full of people who can't get to the door.*"

Antho didn't say anything.

He'd stood by a bed in the Lantern Keep and felt for a door that wasn't there anymore.

---

"*He left it for Mal*," said Seraphine. "*Do you see? The last turn. He left it for when the Hollow was healed. When it could make room for them. When it could let them come home and not eat them.*" Her voice cracked. "*He left it for a pool in a green field that's still so little it can only give one cup every three days.*"

"*It's not ready*."

"*It's not ready*." She looked at him. "*He said not before. He underlined it. Not before.*"

"*And we're going to do it anyway*."

"*We're going to do it anyway*," said Seraphine Ilvane. "*Because if we don't, Caddoc comes down the thread on Lantern Night and the Gilded King picks up the net. And then there's no Mal. No room. No door. Not for anyone.*" She put her face in her hands. "*We're not ready. We'll have to do it anyway. And the Veil comes down. And nine thousand gold people and four Crowned and every dead thing that's drifted in that country for nine hundred years comes walking across the Sundered Lands toward a pool that can only spare one cup at a time.*"

---

He put his arm round her.

She leaned into him. She didn't cry. She'd done her crying in libraries, a long time ago, alone. She just leaned. And breathed.

"*There's something else*," she said. Into his shoulder.

"*Tell me*."

"*The crack*," said Seraphine. "*To get through it. To cross. You'll need Harrow blood. Yours. And Lex's.*" She swallowed. "*On the Veil. Enough to open it wider. Wide enough for four people to go through.*"

"*I know*."

"*It'll stay open*," said Seraphine. "*Antho. Once it's wider, it'll stay wider. That's what blood does to the Veil. That's what the first finger's depth did. It doesn't close again.*" She lifted her head. Looked at him. "*To fetch the only thing in the world that can stop them, you have to open the door for them first.*"

He didn't say anything for a long time.

"*I know*," he said at last.

"*You've thought about it*."

"*I've thought about nothing else*," said Antho Vey. "*Since the Ferryman.*" He looked out of the window. At the soaped *HOME* lantern. At the pool below, dark, with three small gold lights at the bottom of it. "*Every way through it ends with us opening a door we don't want opened. Every single one.*" A pause. "*I'm a Threshold Warden, Seraphine. I'm the door. That's the whole of what I am.*"

---

She picked up her book.

She didn't know why. It was in her satchel, on the floor by the bed. She leaned down and got it out and opened it on her knees to the last page with writing on it. And took her pen out of the binding.

Antho watched her.

She wrote. In her small neat hand. By the light of the soaped lantern.

*Reason six hundred and four*, she wrote. *He was offered a love for a love, on a shingle, to buy back his mother's thirty years. He couldn't give me up. He was ashamed of it.*

She stopped.

*He shouldn't be*, she wrote.

---

He was reading over her shoulder.

He didn't say anything. He sat on the bed beside her with his arm round her and read it twice.

"*How did you know*?" he said at last. Very quietly. "*About the shingle. I never told you.*"

"*You came home*," said Seraphine. "*From the river. And you didn't say anything. You came into the library and sat down beside me and didn't say anything at all, for an hour. You just watched me write.*" She looked at him. "*You've never done that before. Not once. You always talk.*"

"*I was checking*," said Antho.

"*Checking what*?"

"*That I still knew why you were smiling*."

She looked at him for a long moment.

Then she wrote one more line.

*Reason six hundred and five. He checks.*

---

The frost came at dawn.

She woke to grey light through the soaped window and Antho asleep beside her with one arm flung over his face. She sat up. Reached for her spectacles. Folded on the sill. Face down. Where she'd left them.

She turned them over.

And there was frost on the left lens.

Not a face. Not the tiny pale shimmer. *Frost*. On the inside of the glass. Fine and white and feathery. Letters. In a narrow slanting hand.

She held them up to the grey light and read them.

---

*Scribe*.

*Good morning*.

She stopped. Read it again. Her heart going.

*Good morning*, the frost said. *I've never said it before. I'm told it's manners.*

*Something else, since you're always saying sorry to things.*

*Rosie's been sending more roses. Through the crack. Every night since the first one. Little ones. Red. With no notes.*

*Oz has noticed.*

---

The frost faded.

As she watched. The fine white letters going soft at the edges. Melting. Running. A thin wet line down the inside of the lens, like a tear.

And then nothing. Just glass.

Seraphine sat on the edge of the bed in the grey dawn with her spectacles in her hand and Antho asleep beside her and the pool below the window, dark, with three gold lights at the bottom.

*Oz has noticed*.

She put the spectacles on.

At the very edge of her left eye, if she didn't look straight at it, a faint pale shimmer. Watching.

"*Thank you*," said Seraphine. Out loud. Very quietly.

The shimmer didn't answer.

But it didn't go away, either.
