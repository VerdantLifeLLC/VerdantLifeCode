# Tea with the Gilded King

He minded his head on the lintel.

Lex watched him do it. From the top of the ramp. With Wren's hand in his and the baby on her chest and the basket on his back. The Gilded King rose up off the step like a mountain standing, and turned, and looked at the blue door, and at the woman in it with her hands on her hips.

And bent.

All the way. Folding himself down. His great gold head. His crooked crown. His shoulders, wider than the doorframe, turning sideways to fit. He went through the door of the blue cottage the way a bear goes into a cave. Carefully. Slowly. With his hand up to keep his crown from catching on the top.

"*Thank you*," he said. To Seren. In the doorway. Very gently. "*For asking.*"

"*I didn't ask*," said Seren. "*I told.*"

He looked down at her.

"*Yes*," said Oszric of the Crowned. "*You did.*"

---

Lex didn't move.

He stood at the top of the ramp in the snow and looked at his mother. At her hands on her hips. At the flour on her dress.

"*Mam*," he said. Very quietly. "*You let him in.*"

"*He was always going to come in*," said Seren. "*He told me so. Through the rose. In the spring.*" She looked at him. With her brown eyes very steady. "*I'll say no. And I'll come in anyway. And we'll have tea.*"

"*Then why*—"

"*Because this way it's my door*," said Seren Harrow. "*And my tea. And my house.*" She stepped back. Into the dim. "*And he's my guest. Not the other way round.*" She looked at all of them. "*Come in. All of you. Your father's waiting.*"

---

Lex went in.

---

The cottage was dim. Boards over every window but one. A fire in the hearth, banked. The table under the west window, with two cups on it and a tin.

And the bed.

Under the window. With the curtain drawn back. With three pillows. With the Lantern on the table beside it—his brother's Lantern, three panes of glass and one of horn—burning. Throwing three clear squares of light on the walls and one soft blur.

And in the bed—

---

He'd thought he was ready.

He'd thought about it. All the way across the Court. All the way back. All day on Vharuun's neck over the gold road. *Months. It's been months. He'll be older. He'll be thinner. He'll be*—

He wasn't ready.

---

His father was very small.

Smaller than a child. That was the first thing. He lay on the three pillows under the blanket and there was almost nothing of him under it. A shape. Narrow. Like a bundle of sticks.

His face was a walnut. Brown. Creased. Folded in on itself so far there was barely a face left. The bones of it sharp under the skin. His hair a white wisp, so thin you could see the pink of his scalp through it.

His hands on the blanket were bones. Bones in paper. Spotted. Twisted. Still.

His eyes were the same.

Grey. Clear. Sharp. Turning on the pillow. Slowly. To the door.

To him.

---

"*Good evening*," said Arion Vey.

---

Lex couldn't speak.

He stood in the middle of the room with the basket on his back and looked at his father and couldn't say anything at all.

*I'll try*, his father had said. *I'll breathe in and out until you walk back through that door. That's my whole job now.*

He'd done it.

For months. For a hundred and forty days. In this bed. Under this window. With the Lantern burning.

He'd tried.

---

Lex went to the bed.

He didn't remember crossing the room. He was standing in the middle and then he was on his knees by the bed with his father's hand in both of his, and it was so cold, so thin, like holding a bird's wing, and his father was looking at him.

"*You're late*," said Arion.

Lex made a sound. Not a word.

"*A hundred and forty days*," said his father. Faintly. A thread. "*I counted. Seraphine did the sums. Ten to one.*" The ghost of a smile. "*You said a fortnight.*"

"*I didn't know*," said Lex. Hoarsely. "*Nobody knew. Caddoc told me. At the foot of the thread. He said it like everyone knew.*"

"*Everyone in the Court knew*," said Arion. "*Nobody out here.*" He breathed. In. Catch. Catch. Out. "*It doesn't matter. You're here.*"

"*I'm here*."

"*Did you get it*?"

Lex looked at him.

His father's grey eyes went past him. Over his shoulder. To the basket on his back.

"*It's in there*," said Arion. Very quietly. "*Isn't it. I can feel it. Like a cold draught. Like your mother at the door.*" A faint smile. "*Like twenty generations of no.*"

"*Yes*," said Lex.

His father closed his eyes.

"*Good*," he said.

---

*[Lex*,] said Ember.

Very small. Very far. Like a voice at the bottom of a well.

*[He's so thin*.]

*I know*.

*[I can feel him. The thread. From me to him. It's—Lex, it's like a hair. Like a spider's thread. Like the last strand of a rope.]*

Lex held his father's hand.

*[I don't know how he's still here*,] said Ember. *[I don't know how he's done it. A hundred and forty days on a thread like that.]*

*He tried*, thought Lex.

*[Yes*,] said Ember. Very quietly. *[He tried.]*

---

The others came in behind him.

Antho, with Pim asleep in his arms. He laid the boy down on the sheepskin by the hearth, gently, and covered him with a blanket, and Pim didn't wake. Then he came and stood at the end of the bed. And looked at their father.

He didn't say anything.

He didn't need to. Lex saw his face.

Wren, with Lark. She came and stood beside the bed. Close. With the baby on her chest. And Lark looked down at her grandfather on his pillows. With her grey-green eyes. Frowning.

Arion opened his eyes.

He looked up at the baby. For a long time.

"*She's grown*," he said.

"*She stood up*," said Wren. "*In a bothy. On the Teeth. On my hand.*"

"*Did she*," said Arion. Wonderingly. "*Did she.*"

"*She bit a goat*," said Wren.

Arion laughed.

It wasn't much. It was barely a sound. A breath, a catch, a crack in it, something that went in and didn't come out for a long moment and then did. But it was a laugh.

"*Good*," he said. "*Good girl.*"

---

Hask didn't come in.

He stood outside. On the ramp. In the snow. With his gold arm hanging at his side and his good hand on the doorframe. Coughing.

"*I'll stand here*," he said. When Lex looked round. "*At the door. In front of it.*" He looked into the dim room. At the bed. At the old man. At the enormous gold shape at the hearth. "*Somebody should.*"

---

The King was sitting on the floor.

Lex hadn't seen him sit. He'd been looking at his father. When he looked round, the Gilded King was on the flags by the hearth. Cross-legged. Like a mountain folded. With his great gold hands on his knees and his white and gold robe spread round him.

The flags under him were gold.

Spreading. Slowly. Out from where he sat. A foot. Two. The grey stone going gold, every crack and seam, the way frost spreads across a pane.

He saw Lex look at it.

"*I'm sorry*," said Oszric. "*I can't help it. Everything I touch.*" He looked at the gold spreading on the floor. "*I'll try to keep still.*"

---

Seren poured the tea.

She did it at the table. Under the west window. With her back to the room. Lex watched her. The kettle. The tin. Hollis Penn's tin. The good kind. She spooned the leaves into the pot and poured the water and put the lid on and stood there with her hands flat on the table, waiting for it to steep.

Nobody spoke.

The fire ticked. The Lantern burned. Three clear squares and one soft blur on the walls.

His father breathed. In. Catch. Catch. Out.

---

She poured it into two cups.

The chipped blue one with the crack down the handle. And the other. White. Lopsided. Too thick. Brannoc's first thing that wasn't iron.

She took the blue one to the bed.

She sat down on the edge of it and lifted his father's head off the pillow, gently, with one arm, and held the cup to his lips. He drank. A sip. Less. Most of it ran down his chin. She wiped it with her sleeve.

"*Good*," said Arion. "*Good tea.*"

"*It's the good kind*," said Seren. "*In a tin.*"

"*I can tell*."

She laid his head back on the pillow.

Then she took the white cup. And crossed the room. And knelt down on the floor in front of the King. On the grey flags. Just short of where they turned gold.

And held it out.

---

The Gilded King looked at it.

At the white cup. Lopsided. Too thick. Full of tea. Steaming. In a hedge-witch's square freckled hands.

He didn't take it.

"*I can't*," he said. Very quietly.

"*It's tea*," said Seren. "*You came for tea. You said.*"

"*I know*." He looked at the cup. "*I can't drink it. Everything I touch.*" He lifted one great gold hand off his knee. Slowly. And held it out. Not to the cup. Beside it. A foot away. "*If I take it, it'll go gold. The cup. The tea. All of it. In my hand.*" His voice was very gentle. "*I haven't tasted anything in nine hundred years.*"

---

Seren didn't take the cup back.

She knelt on the floor in front of the King with it held out in both hands, steaming.

"*Take it anyway*," she said.

He looked at her.

"*Take it*," said Seren Harrow. "*It's yours. I made it for you. I bought the good kind. In a tin.*" She didn't lower the cup. "*If it goes gold, it goes gold. It's still yours. You can keep it.*"

The King didn't move for a long moment.

Then he reached out.

And took the white cup out of her hands.

---

It went gold.

From his fingers. Outward. The lopsided white clay. The thick rim. The handle that Brannoc had got wrong. Gold. And the tea inside it. The surface of it. Gold. A gold disc in a gold cup. Still. Not steaming anymore.

The King held it.

In his great gold hand. Very carefully. The way you'd hold a bird's egg. And looked down at it.

"*Thank you*," he said.

His voice was barely a voice.

"*Nobody's ever*—" He stopped. "*In nine hundred years. Nobody's ever made me anything.*" He turned the gold cup in his hand. Slowly. Looking at it. At the lopsided rim. At the thumb-print in the clay near the handle, where Brannoc had pressed too hard. "*Orrin brings me things. The household brought me things. Before. Everything I wanted.*" A pause. "*Nobody ever made me a cup of tea I couldn't drink and gave it to me anyway.*"

"*Well*," said Seren. "*Now someone has.*"

She stood up. And went back to the bed. And sat down on the edge of it. And took her husband's hand.

The King sat on the floor with the gold cup in his hands and didn't say anything for a long time.

---

"*Oszric*," said Arion.

The King looked up.

His father was looking at him. From the pillows. With his grey eyes. Very steady.

"*Mae said you were the eldest*," said Arion. Faintly. "*She said you always went first.*"

"*I did*."

"*She said you kept things*."

"*I do*," said the Gilded King.

"*Why*?"

---

The King didn't answer.

He sat on the gold floor by the hearth with the gold cup in his hands and looked at the old man in the bed.

"*You know why*," he said at last. "*Cad told you. Up the thread. At the bottom of the world. When you went down to see your friend.*" A pause. "*He told you about our father.*"

"*He told me you couldn't go in the room*."

The King flinched.

Lex saw it. A small movement. A tightening. In the great gold face. In the shoulders. The way a man flinches when you touch a bruise he didn't know you could see.

"*Yes*," said Oszric.

---

"*A year*," said the Gilded King.

He said it to the cup. To the gold tea. Not to anyone.

"*A wasting thing*. *In his chest. It took a year.*" His voice was very quiet. Very even. "*He was the King. He was the strongest man in the world. He lifted me onto my first horse with one hand. He laughed so loud the windows rattled.*" He turned the cup. "*And then he started to cough. And then he couldn't ride. And then he couldn't walk. And then he couldn't get out of bed.*"

Nobody spoke.

"*Mae sat with him*," said Oszric. "*Rosie brought him roses. Ysy looked at him and cried and went away.*" A pause. "*Hes sat on the floor by the bed with a toy bear. She was four. She didn't understand. She thought he was just tired.*"

"*And Caddoc held his hand*," said Arion.

"*Cad held his hand*," said the King. "*Every night. At the end. Not saying anything. He was eleven.*" He looked up. At the old man in the bed. "*I was fourteen. I was the eldest. I was the heir. I was going to be King.*"

"*And you stood outside the door*."

"*And I stood outside the door*," said Oszric of the Crowned.

---

The fire ticked.

"*Every night*," said the Gilded King. "*For a month. At the end. I'd go to the door of his room. I'd stand there. I'd put my hand on the latch.*" He looked at his hand. Gold. Holding a gold cup. "*And I couldn't. I couldn't open it. I could hear him breathing. On the other side. In. And a catch. And a catch. And out.*" His voice cracked. "*Like yours.*"

Lex's hand tightened on his father's.

"*And I'd stand there*," said Oszric. "*Shaking. With my hand on the latch. Listening to every breath. Waiting for the one that didn't come.*" He swallowed. "*And then I'd go away. And find the scholars. And shout at them. Find something. Find a cure. Find anything.*"

"*And they couldn't*."

"*And then he went*," said the Gilded King. "*In the night. With Cad holding his hand. And Mae asleep in the chair. And Hes asleep on the floor with her bear.*" He looked at the gold cup. "*And me outside the door. With my hand on the latch.*"

---

Nobody said anything for a long time.

Lex looked at him. At the biggest man he'd ever seen, sitting on the floor of his mother's cottage with a gold cup of tea in his hands, crying.

He didn't want to feel sorry for him.

He did.

---

"*And a week later you found the pool*," said Arion.

"*A week later I found the pool*," said Oszric. "*In the cellar. Under the palace. Hes had been sitting by it since she was four. Talking to it. Nobody listened.*" He wiped his face with the back of his free hand. It came away wet. "*The scholars said it was just a spring. Very old. Very good. And I drank from it. And I didn't get tired. And I didn't get old. And I thought*—"

He stopped.

"*You thought*, *nobody I love will ever have to go through that door*," said Arion.

The King looked at him.

"*Yes*," he said.

---

"*So you drank it dry*," said Arion. Very quietly. "*All of you. And it starved. And it screamed. And Hesper heard it. And you didn't.*"

"*I didn't listen*."

"*No*."

"*I couldn't*," said the Gilded King. "*If I listened, I'd have to stop. And if I stopped*—" He looked at the gold cup. "*Then everyone would go through the door. Mae. Rosie. Ysy. Cad. Hes. One by one. And I'd stand outside every one of them.*"

"*And now*?"

"*And now nobody goes through it*," said Oszric. "*Not in the Court. Not ever. Nine thousand of them. Kept. Safe.*" He looked up. At the old man. "*I walked out among them, in the first years, when they started to come apart. When there was nothing left to drink. I went out with my hands.*" His voice was very soft. "*And not one of them ever went through the door.*"

"*Because there isn't one*," said Antho.

---

Everyone looked at him.

He was standing at the end of the bed. With his hands at his sides. With a gold patch on the back of his left hand, the size of a coin.

"*I felt for it*," said Antho. "*On the white road. In every one of them. Nine thousand.*" His voice was very steady. "*The last door. The one everyone gets.*" He looked at the King. "*It isn't there. Not in one of them. You didn't stop them going through it. You bricked it up. With gold. From the outside.*"

The King looked at him.

"*They hum*," said Antho Vey. "*Did you know? All of them. One note. For nine hundred years.*" He swallowed. "*Pim made a girl say a word. On the road. Ilda. Your spit-girl. She said mam. And the hum remembered what it was saying.*"

"*What*—" The King's voice was barely there. "*What is it saying?*"

"*Home*," said Antho.

---

The Gilded King didn't move.

He sat on the gold floor with the gold cup in his hands and his furnace eyes on Antho's face. And for a moment—just a moment—Lex could look at them. The eyes. They'd dimmed. Like a fire when you throw water on it. Like a forge when the bellows stop.

"*Home*," said Oszric.

"*Nine thousand of them*," said Antho. "*Standing at the side of your road. With their doors bricked up. Saying home.*"

---

The King looked down at the cup.

For a long, long time.

"*They are home*," he said at last. Very quietly. "*They're with me. In the Court. Safe.*"

Nobody answered.

"*They're safe*," said the Gilded King again. As if he were saying it to himself. As if he'd said it to himself every day for nine hundred years. "*Nothing can hurt them. Nothing can take them. Nobody goes through the door.*"

"*Nobody comes out of it, either*," said Arion.

The King looked up.

---

"*You stood outside it*," said his father. From the pillows. Very faintly. Very clearly. "*Your father's door. For a month. With your hand on the latch.*" A breath. In. Catch. Catch. Out. "*You never went in.*"

"*No*."

"*So you never saw what was on the other side*."

The King didn't answer.

"*I'll tell you*," said Arion Vey. "*Since you came for tea. Since I'm the clever one. Since I'm nearly there.*" His grey eyes were very bright in the Lantern-light. "*On the other side of that door was your father. In a bed. Dying. With your brother holding his hand.*"

"*I know what was*—"

"*And he was all right*," said Arion.

---

The King went very still.

"*He was all right*," said Arion. "*Caddoc told me. Up the thread. In the dark.*" A faint smile. "*He didn't want anything said. So nobody said anything. Caddoc held his hand. Mae slept in the chair. Hesper slept on the floor with her bear.*" A breath. "*And he went. In the night. With his son holding his hand.*"

"*He went*," said Oszric. Hoarsely.

"*He went*," said Arion. "*That's all. That's all it was. He was tired, and he went, and someone he loved was holding his hand.*" He turned his head on the pillow. Slowly. To look at the Gilded King. "*You've been standing outside that door for nine hundred years, Oszric. Thinking there was something terrible on the other side.*"

The King didn't speak.

"*There wasn't*," said Arion Vey. "*There was just your father. Going. With his hand in your brother's.*" A pause. "*You should have gone in. He'd have liked you there.*"

---

The Gilded King put the cup down.

On the gold floor. Beside him. Very carefully. The way you'd put down something that might break, though it couldn't break now, nothing could, it was gold all the way through.

And put his face in his hands.

---

Lex watched him.

He'd seen a lot of people cry this year. His mother in the cellar. Wren in the snow. Hask in the thorns. Rhosyn in the dust. Antho on his knees in a glass palace.

He'd never seen anything like this.

The biggest man in the world. Gold. Crowned. Glowing like metal in a crucible. Sitting on the floor of a cottage with a blue door, with his face in his great gold hands, shaking.

Not making a sound.

The floor round him went on turning gold. A foot. Two. Three. Spreading. Toward the bed. Toward the hearth. Toward Pim, asleep on the sheepskin.

Wren moved. Quickly. Quietly. She picked the boy up, blanket and all, and carried him to the other side of the room.

The King didn't notice.

---

"*I can keep you*," he said.

Through his hands. Very muffled.

Arion didn't answer.

"*I can*," said the Gilded King. Lifting his face. Wet. Gold. "*Now. Tonight. One touch. You'd never have to go. You'd never have to be tired. You'd lie in that bed with your wife holding your hand for a thousand years.*" His voice was shaking. "*She'd never have to listen to you stop. Your sons would never have to stand outside the door.*"

"*They're not outside it*," said Arion. "*They're in the room.*"

The King stared at him.

"*Look*," said his father. Faintly. "*Look round. They're all in the room. My wife's holding my hand. My son's holding the other one. My other son's standing at the end of the bed. My son's wife is over there with my granddaughter.*" A breath. In. Catch. Catch. Catch.

Out.

"*Nobody's outside the door*," said Arion Vey. "*Not one. That's all I ever wanted.*"

---

"*No*," said the Gilded King. "*No. Please. Let me. Please.*" He was leaning forward. On his knees now. On the gold floor. His great gold hands out. Open. "*I've never asked anyone. Not in nine hundred years. They always asked me.*" His furnace eyes were burning, wet, enormous. "*I'm asking. Please. Let me keep you.*"

His father looked at him for a long time.

"*No, thank you*," said Arion.

The King's face broke.

"*I've had a very long life*," said Arion Vey. Very gently. "*And a very good wife.*"

He turned his head. On the pillow. To look at her. At Seren. Sitting on the edge of the bed with his hand in hers.

"*Twice*," he said.

---

His mother made a sound.

Lex didn't look at her. He couldn't. He knelt by the bed with his father's other hand in both of his and looked at the floor and heard his mother make a sound he'd never heard anyone make.

Not crying. Something under crying. Something that came up from very deep, from a warm empty hollow where thirty years used to be, and found something there.

---

The King sat back.

Slowly. On his heels. On the gold floor. With his great gold hands falling into his lap.

He looked at the old man in the bed for a long time.

"*Hes said that*," he said at last. "*Not those words. But that. On the shingle. To Mae. Not me. Not ever.*" He looked at his hands. "*I never understood it. Nine hundred years. I never understood why anyone would choose the door.*"

"*And now*?" said Arion.

The Gilded King didn't answer.

He looked at the gold cup on the floor beside him. At the lopsided rim. At the thumb-print near the handle.

"*I don't know*," he said.

---

And then he looked at the baby.

---

Lex saw it happen.

Saw the King's furnace eyes move. Slowly. Off the old man in the bed. Across the room. To the far wall. To where Wren was standing with Pim asleep at her feet and Lark on her chest.

And stop.

On the baby.

And change.

---

The hunger.

Lex had seen it once. From a hundred miles away. Through Wren's eyes, when she came home from the western shires white-faced and told him. *Like a man looking through a window into a warm room on a winter night.*

He saw it now.

In the great gold face. In the burning eyes. The grief going out of them. Not gone. Pushed aside. Covered. The way a fire is covered when you throw on new fuel. And under it, rising, the other thing.

*Something new. Something that's never been anyone's. Full of room.*

---

"*Oh*," said Oszric. Very softly. "*There she is.*"

Wren went very still.

"*Look at her*," said the Gilded King. Wonderingly. "*Look at her. She's grown. She was so small in the summer. In the wind. Laughing.*" He leaned forward. On his knees. "*And now she's—oh. Oh, look at her hands.*"

Lark was looking back at him.

Over Wren's shoulder. From the sling. With her grey-green eyes. Frowning.

"*She's new*," said the King. "*She's full of things that haven't happened yet. Do you know how long it's been since I saw anything new? Nine hundred years. Not a child. Not a rose. Not a thought I hadn't had before.*" His voice was very soft. Very gentle. Very hungry. "*And there she is.*"

"*No*," said Wren.

---

The King didn't move toward her.

He stayed on his knees on the gold floor by the hearth. With his hands in his lap. Not reaching.

"*I won't take her*," he said. "*I said. In the mountains. On the ridge. I don't take. I never have.*" He looked at Wren. "*I'll ask.*"

"*The answer's no*."

"*Not you*," said the Gilded King. Gently. "*I asked you. On the ridge. You said no. You held up your gold hand in the mouth of the cave so I'd see it.*" He smiled. Sadly. "*I saw it.*"

He looked at the baby.

"*Her*," said Oszric.

---

He held out his hand.

Not toward her. Not reaching. Just held it out. In the air. Over the gold floor. Palm up. Open. One great gold finger extended. The way he'd held it out to a shaking grandmother on the edge of a well in the western shires. The way you'd hold your hand out to a frightened animal, to let it come to you.

"*Little one*," he said.

The room was very quiet.

"*Little one*," said the Gilded King. Very softly. To the baby on Wren's chest. "*I'd like to keep you. Forever. Exactly as you are. So you're never sick, or sad, or frightened, or old.*" His furnace eyes were very bright. "*So you're always this new. Always this full of room.*"

Lark looked at the gold hand.

"*May I*?" said Oszric of the Crowned.

---

Nobody breathed.

Lex knelt by the bed with his father's hand in his and couldn't move. Antho at the end of the bed. Seren on the edge of it. Wren against the wall with her gold fingers on the baby's back and her other hand on her knife.

Hask in the doorway. Coughing. Stopped.

His father on the pillows. Watching. With his grey eyes.

And the baby looked at the King.

At his gold hand, held out. At his gold face. At his crooked crown of gold leaves. At his eyes, burning.

She frowned.

The deep, fierce, furious frown. Her father's frown. The frown he gave the bellows when they wouldn't catch.

And she said—

---

"*No*."

---

It was very small.

Barely a word. A sound. A breath with a shape to it. Round. Short. Certain. Coming out of a baby nearly a year old who had never said a word in her life. Who had vowels and a grip like a vice and six teeth and had stood up once, on her mother's gold fingers, in a bothy on the Teeth.

*No*.

Not loud. Not frightened. Not crying.

The way her grandmother said it at a blue door to a dust-man.

The way her great-great-great-grandmother, twenty times over, said it on a shingle in the grey light to a sister in chains.

*Not me. Not ever*.

---

The room didn't move.

Wren had her hand over her mouth. Seren had gone white. Antho had gone very still at the end of the bed, the way he went still at doors.

Lex couldn't breathe.

*Her first word*.

*Her first word, and it's no*.

---

The Gilded King didn't move either.

He knelt on the gold floor with his hand held out and his face—

His face did something Lex had never seen a face do.

It cracked.

Not broke. Not crumpled. *Cracked*. A line. Through the gold. Through the hunger. From the forehead down. Like a crack running through a block of ice in the first warm spring sun.

And light came through it.

Not his light. Not furnace-light. Not gold.

Something else.

---

He laughed.

Lex didn't expect it. Nobody did. The King knelt on the floor of the cottage with his hand held out to a baby who'd just said her first word, and it was *no*, and he threw back his great gold head and *laughed*.

Not the bell. Not the warm roar from the rose. Something else. Something young. Something that cracked in the middle. The laugh of a boy of fourteen at dinner when his little sister says something outrageous and he laughs so hard he falls off his chair.

"*Hes*," he said. Through it. Laughing. Crying. Both. "*Oh, Hes. Oh, it's you. It's you. It's you all over again.*"

He couldn't stop.

He knelt there and laughed with tears pouring down his gold face and his hand still held out, open, to a baby who had said no.

"*No*," he said. "*Of course. Of course it's no. It was always going to be no.*" He wiped his face with his free hand. "*Twenty generations. She'd have said no to me in her cradle. She'd have said no in her mother's womb.*"

He lowered his hand.

Slowly. Back into his lap.

"*Thank you*," said Oszric of the Crowned. To the baby. Very softly. "*Thank you for answering. Nobody's ever answered.*" He looked at her. At her frown. At her fist. "*Hes never did. She never said another word to me. Not one. Not ever.*" His voice cracked again. "*And you said one.*"

---

Lark frowned at him.

And then, very deliberately, she put her fist in her mouth.

And looked away.

---

Wren laughed.

She couldn't help it. It came out of her cracked and wet and sudden, standing against the wall with her hand over her mouth and tears on her face.

And then Seren.

And then, at the end of the bed, Antho.

And Lex, kneeling by the bed with his father's hand in his, felt his own chest crack open and something come up out of it that was half a laugh and half a sob and wholly, entirely, helplessly *proud*.

*Her first word*.

*No*.

---

His father was laughing too.

Lex felt it. Through his hand. A shiver. A tremor. A breath that went in and caught and caught and caught and turned into a cough and turned back into something like a laugh.

"*Good girl*," whispered Arion Vey. "*Oh, good girl.*"

His hand tightened in Lex's.

And then let go.

---

Not all the way.

Lex felt it. The grip going. Not slack. Just—*less*. The way a hand loosens when someone's falling asleep.

He looked down.

His father's eyes were closed. His face on the pillow was very still. Smiling. A little.

His chest under the blanket—

In.

Catch.

Catch.

Catch.

---

The Lantern flickered.

On the table. By the bed. His brother's Lantern. Three panes of glass and one of horn.

The flame inside it shrank.

Not out. *Down*. Small. Smaller. A bead of gold. A spark. The three clear squares of light on the walls going dim. The soft blur going dimmer.

And holding.

Barely.

---

"*Arion*," said Seren.

Catch.

Catch.

The room was silent.

Catch.

---

Out.

---

The breath came out.

Long. Slow. Ragged. And then in again. Shallow. Short. Barely there. But in.

The Lantern flame steadied. A little. Didn't grow. Didn't shrink.

Held.

---

Lex looked up.

At the King.

He'd stood up.

Lex hadn't heard him move. He was standing by the hearth. Enormous. Gold. His crown of gold leaves almost touching the beams. His great gold hands at his sides. The laughter gone out of his face.

Very still.

Looking at the door.

---

"*Someone's coming*," said Oszric.

His voice was very quiet. Very strange.

"*On the river*," said the Gilded King.

Nobody moved.

"*I can feel it*," he said. "*In the water. In the stream outside. In the snow melting off the roof.*" He wasn't looking at any of them. He was looking at the blue door. At the dark beyond it. At the night. "*All rivers are his water. A little. He used to say.*"

His hands were shaking.

Lex had never seen them shake.

"*I know that boat*," said Oszric of the Crowned. "*I've sat in it.*"

---

And somewhere outside, in the dark, in the snow, very faint—

The creak of a pole.

And the soft slap of water against a hull.

And a light.

Small. Grey. Swaying. Coming up through the birch wood.

Not gold.

The colour of a lantern seen through river-mist.

---

Hask stepped back from the doorway.

He didn't say anything. He just stepped back. Out of the door. Onto the ramp. Into the snow. Aside.

The way you step aside for something you know you're not meant to stand in front of.

---

And somebody knocked.

Not like Orrin. Not three soft taps, courteous, patient.

Once.

Very gently.

The way you knock on a door when you know the person inside has been waiting for you.
