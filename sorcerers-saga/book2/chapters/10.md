# Shadows Awakened

*Bring it up, child.*

The voice was so kind.

That was the worst of it. Lex had faced the Pale Warden, who'd spoken softly and reasonably about mercy while black mist poured out of his veins. He'd faced Elric in the black corridors of Duskhold, furious and bitter. He'd faced the Murmur in his brother's head, and the Barrow-King, and the Drowned Dead.

He'd never faced anything that sounded like it *loved* him.

*Bring it up. He's waiting. He has been waiting so long, poor man. And your friends are up there, and they're frightened, and there are so many shadows.* A pause, warm and gentle as a hand on his hair. *You don't want anyone to get hurt. I know you don't. You never have.*

Lex stood on the black glass column in the middle of the Sunwell with the Bit in his fist. The fire-bridge burned gold at his feet. Below him, a hundred feet of red-glowing pit breathed slow heat.

*[Lex.]*

Ember. Very faint. Very far away. As if it were calling to him from the bottom of a well.

*[Lex, don't listen. Don't listen to it. I'm—I'm going down. I'm going to find it. Hold on.]*

*Ember, no—*

*[It's in* our *house, Lex. It's in the Root. It's in the place I live.]* Ember's voice was shaking. *[I'm not letting it sit in there and look out of your eyes like it owns you.]*

And it was gone.

He felt it go. Down. Down into the deep gold, into the dark at the bottom of himself, toward the warm vast stirring thing that had just woken up there.

*Oh*, said the voice in the gold. Gently. Almost amused. *Oh, the little construct. Arion's clever little copy.* A pause. *He always did make things too brave for their own good.*

And then, from far down in the gold, Lex felt something that made his blood turn to ice.

Nothing.

Ember's warmth. Its humming, steady, hearth-like presence that had been with him every hour of every day for four years. Gone. Not dead. He'd have known if it were dead. Just *gone*. Swallowed. Like a coal dropped into deep water.

"*No*," said Lex aloud. His voice echoed off the walls of the Well. "*No. Ember. EMBER.*"

*Hush*, said the voice in the gold. *It's quite all right. I haven't hurt it. I would never hurt it.* A pause. *I've only put it somewhere quiet, so that we can talk.*

---

He ran.

Across the fire-bridge. Back over a hundred feet of red-glowing nothing, the gold bridge flexing and leaning under his boots like a living thing. He reached the edge. The stair. Up. Two steps at a time. Round and round, up through the red-black stone, into the black glass, the Bit clenched in his fist so hard the jagged teeth of it bit into his palm.

*That's it*, said the voice. *That's my good boy. Up you come.*

He didn't answer it. He didn't know how. He'd never had to fight anything that lived *inside* him. Antho had. For six years. Every hour of every day. And Lex had never, not once, really understood what that meant until now.

He came up out of the stair into the black tower. Into the doorway. Into the light.

And stopped.

---

The crater floor was full of shadows.

Umbral Wardens. Dozens of them. Thirty. Forty. Tall and featureless and dark, standing in a great ring around the foot of the black tower, their long fingers hanging at their sides. Not moving. Not yet. Waiting.

Inside the ring, the Unlit.

Dorran stood in front, with his great hammer raised in both hands and his feet planted on the black glass. Kestrel was beside him, both hands burning, her hair a crown of fire. The twins stood back to back behind them, knives out, slings loaded. Oriel Sant sat on her mule in the very center of the ring, with her black thorn stick across her knees and her white eyes turned toward the tower door.

And standing at the edge of the ring, just inside it, between the shadows, two people.

Elric Thorne, in his fine grey coat. The Bow hanging black at his throat. His face healed from Kestrel's burns, but pink and new on one side, like a scald. He was smiling.

And beside him, a woman Lex had seen only once, three years ago, across the floor of the High Council chamber on the morning Queen Lysandra stripped her of her office. She had walked out of the Spire before the guards could reach her, and nobody had seen her since.

High Warden Isaura Kell.

She'd changed. That morning she'd been a tall, cold, silver-haired woman in white and silver, the most powerful person in the Order, with a face like a winter morning. She was still tall. Still cold. But her silver hair had been cut short, ragged, as if with a knife. Her white robes were stained and travel-worn. And her face had gone gaunt and hard, all the polish worn off it, so that what was left was bone and will.

Her eyes were still the same. Pale grey. Calculating. Missing nothing.

They found Lex in the doorway of the tower. And held.

"*Lex Vey*," said Isaura Kell. "*You've grown.*"

"Everyone keeps saying that."

"*Everyone is correct.*" Her voice was flat. "*You were a boy the last time I saw you. A frightened boy in a borrowed coat, standing at the back of the Council chamber, watching me lose everything.*" A pause. "*Now you're the man who broke my Order.*"

"Your Order broke itself."

"*Did it?*" Kell's thin mouth moved. Not a smile. "*Three years, and your precious Unbound are being cut out of the world one by one, like fruit off a tree. Because you took the lids off.*" She inclined her head toward Elric. "*He's right, you know. He's been right all along. The Ceiling was a lid. It kept things in. And it kept things* out.*" Her pale eyes didn't waver. "*I would never have chosen him as an ally. I despise everything he's done. But he understands what you never did. That freedom is a door. And doors open both ways.*"

---

"*Bring it here, Lex*," said Elric.

He held out his gloved hand. The same gesture as in Ysmir's vault. Patient. Reasonable. A teacher's hand.

"*Bring me the bit, and we'll go. All of us. No one gets hurt.*"

"*He's lying*," said Kestrel. Her voice was ragged. "*Smith, he's lying, he always lies—*"

"*Kestrel*," said Elric, without looking at her. "*Do be quiet. You're not in this.*" He kept his eyes on Lex. "*You know I'm not lying. You know what I want. I've always told you exactly what I want.*" A thin smile. "*It's the Veys who lie. Your father lied to me for forty years. Your brother lies to himself. You—*" He tilted his head. "*You haven't learned yet. You're still very honest. It's one of the things I've always liked about you.*"

*He's right*, said the voice in the gold. Gently. *You know he is. Give it to him, child. Then he'll go. And your friends will be safe.*

Lex stood in the doorway of the black tower, with the Bit in his fist and a voice in his head and a ring of shadows around the people he'd brought here.

*Think*, he told himself. *Think. Like a smith. What's the metal? What's it want to do?*

Elric couldn't come in. That was the first thing. The tower was a Vey door. Elric had stood in front of Ysmir's vault for two years and never got through. He couldn't come into the tower. He couldn't get down to the Well.

But he didn't need to. He had the Unlit.

And Lex had—

*The Well*.

He felt it. Behind him. Below him. Down the long spiral stair, at the bottom of the pit. Awake now. Breathing. Gold and red and grieving. *Acquainted*. It had heard him, and he had heard it, and it had built him a bridge.

And it had seen something like these shadows before.

*Something coming up out of the ground. Something hungry. Something with no shape, that ate light.*

Lex looked at the Umbral Wardens. Tall and dark and faceless. Folded out of a dead man's shadow, nine hundred years ago, on an anvil of gold.

*Made of Aurelian's shadow*, he thought. *And Aurelian fell into the Hollow. Into the thing he sealed there. And something wearing him is talking to me right now.*

*The Well's seen that before. It knows what it tastes like.*

"All right," he said aloud. "I'll bring it."

Kestrel made a sound like a slap.

Elric smiled.

And Lex took one step out of the doorway of the tower, onto the black glass of the crater floor, and went down on one knee, and laid his bleeding palm flat on the glass.

*Remember*, he told the Well.

---

It remembered.

He felt it come. Up from the bottom of the pit. Up through the red-black stone, through the black glass, through the floor of the crater. Not fast. Not erupting. *Rising*. The way a man stands up out of a chair when he hears a sound he's heard once before, a long time ago, in the worst night of his life.

The glass under Lex's palm went warm. Then hot. Then it began to *glow*. Red, spreading out from his hand across the black crater floor like ink through water. Under the Unlit's feet. Under the ring of shadows. Under Elric and Kell.

"*What are you—*" said Elric sharply.

The Umbral Wardens moved.

All at once. Forty of them. Turning their faceless heads toward the red glow spreading under their feet. And Lex saw them *flinch*. Saw it run through all forty of them at once, a single shudder, like grass in a gust of wind.

They knew it too.

*Child*, said the voice in the gold. Not warm now. Sharp. *Child, stop that.*

"*No*," said Lex.

And the Sunwell came up.

---

It came up through the glass in a column.

Not under the Unlit. Lex had asked it. *Not them. Not my people. Never again.* It came up under the *shadows*. Forty columns of fire, gold-white and roaring, bursting up out of the black glass of the crater floor exactly where the Umbral Wardens stood, like forty suns rising at once from below.

And the shadows *burned*.

Not the way Lex's Ember Strike had burned them on the moor, passing through, leaving them rippling and whole. Not the way Kestrel's fire had gone through them in Ysmir's temple. They *burned*. Like paper. Like dry leaves. Like something that had been waiting nine hundred years to be burned and had finally found the right fire.

The Sunwell had burned something like them once before. It had burned the hungry thing back down into the dark under the world, and turned a hundred miles of grassland into glass doing it. It remembered the taste.

Forty Umbral Wardens went up in forty pillars of gold-white flame and were *gone*. Not unfolded. Not unmade. Burned. Ash. Less than ash. A faint dark smoke, rising into the white sky over the crater, and then nothing.

The light was enormous. Lex couldn't see. He knelt on the glowing glass with his hand flat on the floor and his eyes screwed shut and the light coming through his eyelids red and gold, and he heard the Well roaring, a sound like the whole world breathing out, and under it, the voice in the gold.

Screaming.

Not in pain. In *fury*. A long, high, terrible scream, coming up from the very bottom of him, from the deep gold where Ember had gone. A sound like a beautiful thing tearing.

*How DARE you—*

And then nothing. The voice cut off. Like a door slamming.

*Commanding*. The fourth stage. *Not yet*, thought Lex, dimly. *That's not mine yet. That's the Well's.* And he could feel it: the borrowed fire pouring through him, far too much, far too hot, burning along every channel of him like molten iron poured into a mould that wasn't ready for it.

*Enough*, he told the Well. *Enough. Thank you. Please. Enough.*

And slowly, grudgingly, the fire went down.

---

When he could see again, the crater was steaming.

The black glass floor was cracked in forty places, forty round black holes where the columns had come up, still glowing red at their edges. Smoke drifted. The air shimmered.

The Unlit were standing in the middle of it. Alive. All of them. Kestrel had her arms wrapped around her head, and her fire had gone out entirely, and she was staring at the forty smoking holes with her mouth open. Dorran had lowered his hammer. The twins were clutching each other. Oriel sat on her mule, entirely calm, her white eyes turned toward Lex.

"*Well*," she said. "*That was loud.*"

Lex tried to stand up. His legs didn't work. He'd burned something out of himself. He could feel it. The channels the borrowed fire had poured through were raw and blistered and aching, the way his hands had ached after the first time he'd forged a blade at twelve, before they'd learned to hold the hammer properly.

He got one foot under him. Then the other.

And then he looked for Elric.

---

Elric was standing at the edge of the crater, where the slope began.

He hadn't burned. Of course he hadn't. He wasn't a shadow. Neither was Kell. The Sunwell had burned only the hungry thing, the way Lex had asked, and the two people standing among the shadows had been left whole.

Elric's fine grey coat was singed again. His face was white. He was staring at the forty smoking holes in the crater floor. And then at Lex.

And Lex saw something on his face he'd never seen there before.

Fear.

Not of Lex. Of whatever had screamed. Elric had heard it. Lex was suddenly sure of it. The voice in the gold, the kind voice that had told him where to find the Bow, that had promised him everything, had screamed in fury through the Key at his throat, and Elric had heard it, and for one moment he'd heard what it really sounded like.

Then it was gone. Smoothed over. His face was calm again.

"*Very clever*," he said quietly. "*Very clever, Lex. You always were.*" He brushed ash off his sleeve. "*But you've forgotten something.*"

And he turned his head, and looked at Ilse Marrow.

---

He didn't need the shadows.

Lex understood that a moment too late. Elric had never needed the shadows. They were only to hold the Unlit still. To keep them in one place. The Key had always been enough.

But the twins were shaded. Their threads were hidden, the way Lex's was. The Lampshade. Elric couldn't see them. Couldn't hook them.

"*Isaura*," said Elric.

High Warden Kell lifted one hand.

Lex felt it. Even without the gold. Even shaded. A great cold silver *pressure*, coming down out of the air over the crater like a lid. Inspection. The Order's oldest power. The thing every Warden of the Kindling had done to every child in Caldor for nine hundred years. *Show yourself. Show your thread. Show what you are.* And she was Tier IV, the highest Sanction the Order had ever granted, and the Lattice still remembered whose voice it had obeyed for twenty years.

The Lampshades tore.

Lex felt his own rip. Like a cloak torn off in a high wind. His thread blazing out, gold and silver, naked in the net. And beside him, the twins: two bright loose Unbound threads, suddenly *visible*, shining like two stars.

"*No*—" said Ivo.

Elric turned the Bow.

---

He hooked Ilse.

Lex saw it. Saw the girl's head snap back. Saw her face go slack and white. Saw her knife fall from her fingers onto the glass with a small bright sound. And saw her start to fade. Not her body. *Her*. Going pale at the edges. The color draining out of her patched green coat.

"*ILSE—*"

Ivo caught her as she fell. He was screaming her name. *Ilse, Ilse, Ilse*, over and over, holding her against him, and Lex could see his hands going *through* her, a little. Just at the edges.

"*Bring it here*," said Elric quietly. "*Lex. Bring me the bit.*"

His gloved hand was on the Bow at his throat. Turning it. Slowly. Like a man turning a key in a lock he isn't quite sure of.

And Ilse Marrow was coming apart in her brother's arms.

---

Dorran moved first.

He didn't go for Elric. He went for Kell. Across the cracked glowing glass in four enormous strides, his great hammer coming round in a two-handed swing that would have stove in a castle gate.

Kell didn't move. She raised her hand, and said one word, and Dorran Vask *stopped*. Mid-stride. Frozen. His hammer hanging in the air at the top of its swing. His great slab of a face straining.

"*Vask*," said Isaura Kell. She was looking up at him with something almost like surprise. "*Dorran Vask. My best Warden of the Kindling. Twenty-two years without a single complaint.*" Her pale eyes moved over his face. "*I wondered where you'd gone.*"

"*Let*," said Dorran, through his teeth, "*her*. *Go*."

"*I'm not holding her. He is.*" Kell's voice was very calm. "*I'm only holding you.*" She tilted her head. "*Your brand still answers, Vask. The Sanction's in the thread, not the skin. You know that. I taught you that.*"

Dorran's whole body shook. The hammer trembled at the top of its arc. His face had gone dark red. A vein stood out on his shaved scalp like a rope.

And Lex saw his forearm. The old Kindling-brand. Glowing. Silver. Answering her.

---

Kestrel was looking at Elric.

Not burning. Not moving. Just standing there, on the cracked glass, her fire gone out, staring at the man in the grey coat with an expression Lex couldn't read.

Elric glanced at her. And smiled. Very faintly.

"*You see*," he said softly, to her. Only to her. "*This is what the Key does. It holds. It lets go. It can cut a thread.*" His eyes moved to Ilse, fading in her brother's arms. "*And it can write one back.*"

Kestrel didn't say anything.

"*When I have all three*," said Elric, "*I can write anyone back. Anyone at all. Every name in that book. Every one.*" He looked at her. "*Your brother, for instance.*"

Behind Kestrel, faint and pale, Tamsin Varn went very still.

---

*Bring it to him*, said the voice in the gold.

It was back. Quiet. Calm again. As if it had never screamed. Gentle as a hand on his hair.

*Bring it to him, child. And the thief girl lives. Do you want her to die? Is a piece of metal worth a girl's life?*

Lex stood on the cracked floor of the crater with the Bit in his fist.

*Think*. *Think*. *What does the metal want?*

But there was no metal. There was no trick. There was a girl fading in her brother's arms, and a man with a key, and a choice.

He'd made it before he knew he was making it.

"Let her go," he said. "Let her go, and it's yours."

"*Throw it.*"

"Let her go *first*."

Elric looked at him for a long moment. Then he turned the Bow. Slowly. The other way.

Ilse gasped. A great tearing breath, like a drowning girl coming up for air. Her eyes flew open. Color flooded back into her face. Not all of it. But some. She clutched at her brother's coat, and Ivo made a sound Lex never wanted to hear again, and pulled her against him.

Lex threw the Bit.

---

It turned over and over in the air above the crater, black and jagged, a row of teeth cut out of the world. Elric caught it in one gloved hand.

And held it up. Beside the Bow, at his throat.

And brought them together.

They *clicked*.

Lex heard it. Everyone heard it. A sound like a lock turning in a door the size of the world. Not loud. Small. Final. The black jagged teeth of the Bit sliding into the loop of the Bow, fitting, joining. Two pieces of the Key of Shadows, made one.

And the sky went dark.

Not all of it. Not night. A shadow. Passing over the sun like the shadow of a great bird. Just for a moment. The whole Glass Desert going dim, the blinding white light turning grey, the reflections in the glass going out like candles. A cold wind blowing across the crater from nowhere, smelling of wet ash and old stone.

And then the light came back.

---

They came out of the glass two days later, at noon, onto the red stony plain. And Queen Ishara of the Sunreach was waiting for them.

She was alone. Her riders were back at the edge of the scrub, a long white line under the hot sky. She sat on her golden horse with Zahir on her shoulder and watched them come. Six ragged, burned, glass-cut figures and a mule. And one faint shape that only Lex could see.

She looked at Lex for a long moment.

"*Zahir saw*," she said. "*From above. The fire. The shadows burning.*" Her hawk's face didn't change. "*And the old man. And the black thing you threw him.*"

"Yes."

"*You gave it to him.*"

"He was killing my friend."

Ishara's gaze moved to Ilse. Pale and faded, leaning on her brother's shoulder. Then back to Lex.

"*And now my people*," she said, "*are in more danger than before.*"

"Yes," said Lex. "I'm sorry."

The Queen of the Sunreach sat on her horse and looked at him for a long time. Then she looked north. Toward Saffaran. Toward the great old Stone on top of the mesa, and the thousands upon thousands of capped threads running out from it.

"*Your friend with the red braid*," she said, "*told me something else. Last spring.*"

He waited.

"*She told me that when you take the Ceiling off*," said Ishara, "*the threads are brighter. Easier to see. Easier to cut.*" Her voice was very calm. "*She told me that is why the old man takes the Unbound. Because they shine.*"

"Yes."

"*She told me so I would understand what I was asking for.*" Ishara turned back to him. "*I have thought about it every day since. Every day for a year.*" A pause. "*I am still asking.*"

Lex stared at her.

"*You have your gold back*," said the queen. "*I can see it in your eyes. They were brown and ordinary in the glass, and now they are gold again.*" She leaned forward in her saddle. "*Whatever was looking out of them has already seen everything. You told me so, at the edge of the glass. So there is nothing left to hide.*" Her hawk's eyes were very fierce. "*Free my people, smith. Now. Today. Before you go.*"

"If I do, they'll shine. He'll see them. He'll come for them."

"*Then they will see him coming*," said Queen Ishara of the Sunreach, "*with their eyes open. And they will fight him with the Ashari fire their grandmothers had, which your Order took from us two hundred years ago.*" Her voice rose, very slightly. "*Better to burn bright and be hunted, smith, than live forever under a lid. Ask your red-haired witch. Ask your thieves. Ask anyone in the Sunreach.*"

Behind Lex, very quietly, Ilse Marrow said: "*She's right.*"

Everyone looked at her. She was leaning on Ivo, pale as dust. But her grey eyes were very clear.

"*I'd do it again*," she said. "*Being Unbound. Even knowing. Even after today.*" She swallowed. "*Two years of being* bright. *I wouldn't give them back for anything.*"

Lex looked at her. Then at Ishara.

Then he climbed up behind the Queen of the Sunreach on her golden horse, and they rode north to Saffaran.

---

He did it at sunset, at the great old Stone at the top of the mesa.

All of Saffaran came out to watch. They crowded the terraces and rooftops and the hanging gardens, thousands of them, in white robes and bright head-cloths, silent, watching the big young man from Caldor kneel at the foot of their Stone and lay his scarred hands on it.

He didn't shade himself. There was no point. Whatever was at the bottom of the gold had already seen. Let it watch.

*Watch this*, he thought at the door. *Watch me do this.*

And reached into the Stone with the Root, and found the Treaty of Scald, two hundred years old and heavy as a millstone, pressed down flat on every thread in the Sunreach. And put his hands under it.

And *lifted*.

It was the hardest Unbinding he'd ever done. Harder than Highcrest. Harder than Kessick. The Treaty had been set by forty Wardens at once, two centuries ago, and every year since, the Order had renewed it, pressing down and down. It didn't want to come up. It fought him. It weighed on him like a mountain.

He didn't have Ember to help him. He was alone in the Root for the first time in four years. Just him and the gold and the weight.

But he'd learned something in the Glass Desert. From a grieving Well. *Not do this. Help me*.

So he didn't command the Stone. He asked it. *They've been under there so long. Let them up. Please. Let them breathe.*

And the old Stone of Saffaran, which had been forced to hold down its own people for two hundred years, and had hated every minute of it, sighed.

And let go.

---

The light came up off the mesa like a sunrise at sunset.

Gold and silver, pouring up out of the Stone and out through every thread in the city, out across the plains and the canyons and the red mountains, out into every village and herder's camp and riverside town in the whole of the Sunreach. Lex felt it go. Felt the threads come loose, all at once, hundreds of thousands of them, like a whole country taking its first deep breath after a lifetime of holding it.

And from the terraces and the rooftops and the hanging gardens of Saffaran, a sound. Not a cheer. Something older. A long, rising, ululating cry, thousands of voices at once, the old cry of the Sunreach, that Lex learned later had not been heard in the city for two hundred years.

And then fire.

All over the city. Small flames, appearing in people's hands. On their fingers. In their hair. Little gold and red flames, flickering into being on every terrace and rooftop, like the lanterns at the festival in Highcrest. The Ashari fire. Coming back.

Lex knelt at the foot of the Stone with his hands on it and watched the whole city of Saffaran catch fire, gently, joyfully, without burning, and felt the tears running down his face.
