# The Prophecy of Return

Harrowgate looked exactly the way Lex had pictured it, every night since the festival.

A village of grey stone under the Ironbacks. Forty houses. A church. A mill-race running loud with snowmelt. And on the green, a Lattice Stone, leaning a little, with frost on its north face even in spring.

He'd never been here. He'd only seen it in Roole's report and Corin's eyes and the Ledger's narrow elegant hand. *Mella Bell, of Harrowgate. Sixteen. Weatherwise. She sang to the goats.* But he knew it the moment Ashka came down over the ridge in the grey dawn. He knew the green. He knew the Stone. He knew the cottage with the blue shutters at the end of the lane, where a boy of twelve was standing in the doorway in his nightshirt, looking up at the dragon coming down out of the sky.

Corin Bell didn't wave. He didn't run. He stood very still in the doorway with his hands at his sides and his face white.

And when Lex slid down off Ashka's neck on the green and walked up the lane toward him, the boy said, in a voice that cracked:

"*You came*."

"I came."

"*She's asleep*," said Corin. "*She's always asleep in the day now. She sleeps all day and walks all night.*" He swallowed. "*Mam doesn't notice. Nobody notices. They just say, Mella's tired, let her rest.*" His eyes were very bright. "*I'm the only one who notices.*"

Lex knelt down in the lane. So their eyes were level.

"You noticed last time too," he said. "When she was taken. You were the only one who remembered her."

"*I know*." Corin's chin trembled. "*It's horrible. Being the only one.*"

"I know," said Lex. "But it's why she came back."

---

Mella Bell was asleep in the little room under the eaves. Lex stood in the doorway and looked at her.

Sixteen. Thin. Brown-haired, like her brother. Asleep on her side with her knees drawn up and one hand under her cheek. An ordinary girl. In an ordinary room, with a window looking out at the mountains and a shelf of wooden goats that someone had carved for her, badly.

He looked at her with True Sight.

*[Lex*,] said Ember quietly. *[Her tithe-thread. It's seven times thicker than it should be. It's not taking a tenth. It's taking*—] A pause. *[It's not taking. It's* listening. *It's a line. Running straight down. Like a string on a tin can.]*

*[To whatever's singing at the bottom of it.]*

Antho came up behind him on the narrow stair. He looked past Lex at the girl on the bed. He didn't use True Sight. He didn't need to. He stood in the doorway with his grey eyes very dark and *listened*.

"*She's humming*," he said quietly. "*In her sleep. You can't hear it with your ears.*" He tilted his head. "*It's the same tune. As Ilse. At the Stone at Hollow's Foot.*"

---

They waited for night.

There was nothing else to do. Malachi said so, sitting on the bench outside the Bells' cottage with his straw hat over his eyes and a goat chewing his sleeve. *Whatever it is, it comes up at midnight. When the fire goes out. When the pull's strongest. So we wait for midnight.*

Corin's mother made them supper. She was a thin tired kind woman who looked at the dragons sprawled across the village green and the giant storm-mother on the ridge above the church, and the small brown barefoot ancient man with the goats, and the red-haired archer and the two gold-eyed brothers, and said, very calmly, "*Well, I suppose you'll want stew*," and went inside and made some.

She didn't notice anything wrong with Mella. Lex watched her. When Mella came downstairs at dusk, yawning, in her nightdress, her mother kissed her on the forehead and said, "*There you are, love. You were tired.*" And that was all.

Mella sat at the table. She ate her stew. She smiled at Corin and ruffled his hair. She looked at Lex across the table, at his gold eyes, and went pink, the way girls in villages did when they met the Smith of Ashbourne from the terrible song.

"*You're him*," she said shyly. "*Aren't you. The one who Unbound me. At the Stone. In the spring.*"

"Yes."

"*I felt it*," said Mella. "*When you did it. Like a window opening.*" She looked down at her bowl. "*And then I don't remember anything for a while. Months. Corin says I was gone.*" She frowned. "*I don't remember being gone. I just remember—*"

She stopped.

"*What*?" said Lex gently.

"*Singing*," said Mella Bell. Her eyes had gone vague. Unfocused. "*Someone singing. Very far away. Very beautiful. To all of us.*" She smiled. A slow, dreamy, faraway smile. "*He said he'd come for us. He said he'd sing us home.*"

Across the table, Corin's spoon clattered into his bowl.

---

At midnight, Mella got up and walked to the Stone.

They were waiting for her. In the dark at the edge of the green. Lex and Antho and Wren and Malachi, and Seraphine with her crossbow, and Kestrel, who'd come on Vharuun's back with Tamsin and hadn't said a word all day. Corin, who'd refused to stay inside, holding Wren's hand so hard his knuckles were white.

The cottage door opened. Mella came out.

In her nightdress. Barefoot. With her eyes open and her face calm and her brown hair loose down her back. She walked down the lane in the moonlight, not hurrying, not stumbling, the way you walk somewhere you've been a hundred times.

She was humming.

Lex heard it. Not with his ears. With the Root. A thread of sound, running out of her, down through the thin grey tithe-line into the ground. And coming *back* up. Answering. From very far down. A voice. A man's voice. Singing.

He couldn't make out the words. He didn't need to. It was the most beautiful sound he had ever heard.

*[Lex*,] said Ember sharply. *[Lex, don't listen to it. Don't listen.]*

*I'm not*—

*[You are. You stopped walking. You've got your eyes closed.]*

He opened them. He hadn't known they were shut.

Beside him, Wren was swaying. Very slightly. Her bow hanging from her hand. Seraphine's crossbow was pointed at the ground. Corin had let go of Wren's hand and taken a step toward the Stone.

And Malachi was standing very still with his straw hat in his hands, and his shifting eyes gone wide and wet.

"*Oh*," the old man said softly. "*Oh, that's a voice. I've never heard a voice like that. Not in nine hundred years.*"

---

Antho lit the Lantern.

Lex heard him do it. A small sharp click of the shutter. And then the gold light, spilling out across the dark green. Not wide. A small circle. Around Antho. Around the people closest to him.

*Hush*. Twelve runes on the glass. Lex had cut them himself, four years ago, in a forge in the mountains. *A rune of silence. For bells that ring too loud, or dogs that bark too much, or voices that won't stop.*

The singing *stopped*.

Not everywhere. Not out in the dark, where Mella was walking. But inside the circle. Inside the gold light. Like a door slamming. Lex felt it cut off, and felt his head clear, and felt Wren jerk upright beside him with a gasp.

"*What*—" she said. "*What was*—"

"*Stay in the light*," said Antho. His voice was very calm. Very cold. "*All of you. Stay in the light. Corin*—"

The boy was still walking. Out of the circle. Toward the Stone. Toward his sister.

Antho caught him by the sleeve.

Not by the arm. By the sleeve. Between finger and thumb.

Corin stopped. He looked back. His eyes were vague. Unfocused.

"*Not you*," said Antho Vey. Very gently. "*You're the one who remembers. You stay here with me.*"

And something in the boy's face cleared. Like fog lifting. He looked down at Antho's hand on his sleeve. Then up at Antho's face. And came back into the light.

---

Mella reached the Stone.

She laid her hand on it. Flat. On the frosted north face. And the humming coming out of her rose. Higher. Clearer. Joining the voice from below. Harmonizing. A duet. A girl in a nightdress and something very far down, singing together in the dark.

And the Stone went black.

Lex saw it happen. Saw the faint silver glow of the old Lattice Stone go out. Not dim. *Out*. And then saw it go darker than dark. Black. A hole. A shape cut out of the moonlight, Stone-shaped, with nothing inside.

And from the hole, something came up.

---

Grey. Like mist. Like a person made of mist.

It rose out of the black Stone slowly, the way smoke rises out of a chimney on a still night. Unfolding. Taking shape. Tall. Thin. A man's shape, in a long robe that hung to the ground. But no face. Just a smooth grey oval where a face should be.

And a mouth.

Only a mouth. Wide. Open. In the middle of the smooth grey nothing. Singing.

It stood beside Mella Bell at the Stone. And Mella, with her hand still on the black stone, turned her head and looked up at it. And smiled. The dreamy, faraway smile.

And took its hand.

"*Mella*," sang the grey thing. In a voice like a choir. Like a hundred voices at once, all perfectly in tune. "*Mella Bell. Weatherwise. Who sings to the goats.*"

"*Yes*," whispered Mella.

"*We've been waiting for you*," sang the grey thing. "*All of us. All the ones who went down to the edge of the dark and heard the singing. We remember you. Even if no one up here does.*"

"*My brother remembers*," said Mella. Very faintly.

The grey thing's mouth paused.

"*Your brother*," it sang, "*is very small. And very far away. And the singing is so much louder.*"

---

Lex moved.

He walked out of the Lantern's light. Into the dark. Toward the Stone.

The singing hit him like a wave. He staggered. Felt it go through him. Into his chest. Into the Root. Down through the gold, toward the door. And at the door, something *answered*. Warm. Gold. Humming along.

*Oh*, said the warmth at the bottom of him. Fondly. *Oh, my Cantor. Isn't he lovely?*

He set his teeth.

*Not tonight*, he thought at it. *Not tonight. Not ever.*

He kept walking.

The grey thing turned its faceless head toward him. Its wide singing mouth turned toward him. And the song changed.

"*Heir*," it sang. "*Heir of the Root. Blood of the one who wove the net. You've come.*"

"Let her go."

"*She came to me*." The mouth smiled. Wide. Too wide. "*She's always come to me. Every night. Since the spring. She hears me in her sleep. They all do. All sixty.*" The song swelled. "*They were at the edge of the dark, heir. For weeks. For months. And I sang to them. And they* listened. *They'll always listen now. It's in their threads. A string, running down to me.*"

"Let her go."

"*I can't*," sang the grey thing. Almost sadly. "*I'm only the voice. The herald. The first note.*" It lifted Mella's hand in its own grey one. "*I've come to sing you a song, heir. From my master. A song for you. A prophecy.*"

And it sang.

---

It wasn't loud. That was the strange thing. Lex had expected it to be loud. It was soft. Gentle. A lullaby. And it went out from the black Stone in every direction, through the dark, through the ground, through the tithe-threads, the way the Lattice went out from a Stone. Through every thread.

He felt it go. Felt it go *everywhere*.

*When the Key is barred and the night is done,*
*the Root lies bound where the threads begun.*
*The heir must come to the Heart alone*
*and break the chain on the buried bone,*
*or every Stone in the world shall sing,*
*and every thread to the Hollow bring,*
*and the bright ones first, and the young ones last,*
*till the song is sung and the world is passed.*

---

Silence.

The grey thing stood beside the black Stone with Mella's hand in its own. Its singing mouth closed. Waiting.

And far away, through the Root, through the net, through every thread in Caldor, Lex felt it.

*Humming*.

Not just Mella. Not just Ilse at the Stone at Hollow's Foot. Everywhere. Sixty threads. All over the world. All the Unnamed who had come back. Humming the same song at the same moment, in their sleep, in their beds, walking out of their houses in the dark toward their village Stones.

Hollis Penn in the Lower Town of Highcrest. Bram Sowerby on the White Shore. Amira bint Hassa in Qarn, at the edge of the glass. Tarek al-Nur. Odo Ferris. Garret Shale, in Thornby, getting up from his mother's kitchen table and walking out the door.

All of them.

And around them, through the Stones, through the tithe-threads, very faint, very far, something else. Other threads. Ordinary threads. People who had never been unwritten. People who had never been anywhere near the edge of the dark. Hearing it. Just a little. Just a whisper. Stirring in their sleep.

*Every Stone in the world shall sing*.

*[Lex*,] said Ember. Its voice was shaking. *[Lex, it's spreading. Through the tithe-threads. Every Stone's got one. Every soul's connected. It's using the Tithe like a—like a—]*

*Like a choir*.

*[Like a choir*.]

---

"*Come to the Heart, heir*," sang the grey thing softly. "*Alone. And break the chain. And my master will be free, and the Root will be whole, and the singing will stop.*" Its mouth smiled. "*Or don't. And I'll keep singing. Every night. Louder. Through every Stone in the world.*" It looked down at Mella, smiling dreamily at its side. "*And they'll come to me. The bright ones first. The ones you Unbound. They shine so loudly.*"

"*And the young ones last*," said Lex.

"*And the young ones last*." The mouth widened. "*Children are so hard to sing to. They don't listen properly. They're always thinking about something else.*"

Behind Lex, in the circle of gold light, Corin Bell made a small sound.

"*Choose, heir*," sang the herald of the Cantor. "*The Heart. Or the world.*"

---

And Lex did the only thing he could think of.

He put his hand on the Stone. On the black hole where the Stone had been. Beside Mella's.

And reached for the Tithe-thread with the Root.

It was there. Running down. Thick. Grey. Humming. Seven times thicker than the others, where Mella's thread joined it. A string on a tin can, running down to something singing at the bottom of the world.

He took hold of it.

And *pulled*.

---

The whole world screamed.

He felt it. Through the Root. Through every thread in the net. A great tearing shudder, like a sheet being ripped in half. Not from Harrowgate. From *everywhere*. Every Stone in Caldor. Every thread. Every soul. Jerking. Like a fish on a line. Like a puppet with its string pulled.

And Mella Bell screamed. Her hand tore free of the grey thing's. She fell to her knees on the frosty grass, clutching her chest.

And in the cottages around the green, in the dark, Lex heard others. Corin's mother. The miller. The priest. Every soul in Harrowgate. Crying out in their sleep. Waking. Clutching their chests.

*[STOP*,] shouted Ember. *[LEX. STOP. You're pulling on* everyone. *The Tithe's in every thread. Every single one. You can't pull one without pulling all of them.]*

He let go.

The scream stopped. The shudder stopped. Mella lay on the frosty grass, gasping. The grey thing stood over her. Watching.

And laughing. Softly. A choir of a hundred voices, laughing.

"*You can't cut it, heir*," sang the herald. "*Not one. Not here. Not without cutting everything.*" Its mouth was very wide. "*The Tithe is in every thread in the world. It's woven in. It's part of the net. You'd have to unmake the whole net to cut it.*"

It leaned down. Close. Its smooth grey faceless head inches from Lex's.

"*And only one person ever knew how to make the net*," it sang softly. "*And he's buried at the Heart.*"

---

Antho came out of the dark.

He walked straight across the green, out of his own circle of gold light, with the Lantern held high in front of him. Its shutter wide open. Twelve Hush-runes blazing white on its glass.

He walked straight up to the grey thing.

And held the Lantern up in front of its singing mouth.

"*Be quiet*," said Antho Vey.

And the herald of the Cantor *stopped*.

Not silenced. *Stopped*. As if someone had put a hand over its mouth. Its wide grey lips froze, half-open. Its grey body went rigid. The singing that had been going out from the black Stone through every thread in the world simply—ceased. Like a bell with a cloth over it.

"*I had a voice in my head for eleven years*," said Antho quietly. "*Telling me things. Singing to me in my mother's voice.*" He held the Lantern steady. "*My brother made this. For me. So that voices couldn't act through me while it was lit.*" He tilted his head. "*It works on other people's voices too. It turns out.*"

The grey thing's frozen mouth trembled.

"*Go back down*," said Antho. "*Tell your master we heard his song.*" He leaned closer. "*Tell him he got the tune wrong.*"

And he closed the Lantern's shutter. Sharp. *Click*.

The herald of the Cantor went down. Into the black Stone. Fast. Like smoke sucked down a chimney. Gone.

And the Stone at Harrowgate went from black to grey, and from grey to silver. And was a Stone again.

---

Mella was crying.

Lex knelt beside her on the frosty grass. She was curled up with her arms around her knees, shaking, sobbing. Awake. Properly awake. For the first time since the spring.

"*I was there*," she was saying. "*I was there. At the edge. In the dark. There were so many of us. And he was singing.*" She clutched at Lex's sleeve. "*He sang every night. He said we were his. He said we'd always be his.*"

"*You're not his*," said a voice.

Corin Bell. Standing over his sister in his nightshirt. Shaking. Fierce.

"*You're not his*," said Corin again. "*You're mine. You're my sister. You sing to the goats, and you're terrible at it, and they don't even like it.*" His voice cracked. "*And you burned the porridge last week and blamed me. You're* mine.*"

Mella looked up at him.

And then she reached up and pulled her little brother down into her arms, and held on, and wept into his hair.

Lex looked at her with True Sight.

---

Lex stood in the yard of the Bells' cottage at dawn and looked at the Stone on the green.

Silver again. Faint. Ordinary. With frost on its north face. And running down from it, into the ground, a thin grey thread that he could feel now every time he looked. Thicker than it should be. Humming. Very faintly.

*Come to the Heart, heir. Alone*.

*[You can't*,] said Ember. *[Lex. You can't. It's a trap. It's at the bottom of the Root, where the door is. It wants you to walk right through it.]*

*I know*.

*[Then don't*.]

*And let every Stone in the world sing?*

Ember didn't answer.

*Make the lanterns*, he thought. *Buy time. As much as we can. And find another way. A way to cut the Tithe without cutting everyone.*

*A way to unmake the net*.

*[Only one person ever knew how to make the net*,] said Ember quietly. *[That's what it said.]*

*And he's buried at the Heart*.

He looked at Malachi. Sitting on the chopping-block with his hat in his hands. The youngest of seven. Who had stood beside his brother on the night of the Weaving and said *you can't cage a storm by becoming the bars*.

*Not the only one*, thought Lex.

"*Mal*," he said.

The old man looked up.

"*You were there*," said Lex. "*At the Weaving. You watched him make it.*" He swallowed. "*Do you know how he did it?*"

Malachi stared at him for a very long time.

"*Smith*," he said at last. Very softly. "*I've been waiting nine hundred years for someone to ask me that.*"
