# The Wellworld of Flame

The Ashbourne Forge had twenty anvils, and every one of them stopped ringing when the dragons came down on the green.

Lex saw the students pour out of the long hall. Leather aprons and scarred forearms and soot-black faces, forty of them, staring up at a storm-dragon the size of a cathedral settling onto the village green like a mountain lying down. Behind them, Mother Orla in the door of the Crooked Kettle with a ladle in her hand and her mouth open. Behind *her*, on the forge chimney, a raven, who looked at Vharuun, looked at Ashka, looked at Lex sliding down off Ashka's neck, and said in a voice like a rusty hinge:

"*Late*."

"Hello, Quill," said Lex.

"*Very late. Bacon gone. Girl burned the tongs.*"

"*I did NOT*," shouted a voice from the crowd of students, and Tilda Crane pushed her way to the front, red-faced and freckled, in her too-big apron. "*It was ONE pair. And they were already cracked. And he wasn't even there, he was on the chimney eating Mother Orla's—*" She stopped. She'd seen Lex's face. "*Master Lex*," she said, in a different voice. "*What's happened?*"

He didn't know how to answer. There was too much. The Glass Desert and the Sunwell and the masquerade and the Hollowmaw and his mother behind a cracked black door. He stood on the green of the village where he'd grown up, with his forty students staring at him, and didn't know where to start.

"I need the forge," he said at last. "The big hearth. Tonight."

Tilda looked at him. Then at the dragons. Then at the strange band of people climbing down off Vharuun's back: the giant, the twin thieves, the blind old woman, the red-haired witch, the Royal Archivist with her spectacles askew, the wet boy in the borrowed coat.

"*Right*," said Tilda Crane. "*I'll bank the others down.*"

---

The great hearth stood at the far end of the long hall. Brannoc had built it with Lex, three years ago, when they rebuilt the forge after the fire. A huge round fire-pit of black stone, waist-high and wide as a cartwheel, with a hood of hammered copper above it and a chimney going up through the roof. It was the heart of the Ashbourne Forge. The other nineteen anvils were lit from it every morning, with coals carried in iron pans down the long hall. It had never once gone out.

Lex stood in front of it at midnight.

The hall was empty except for the people who'd come with him. The students had been sent to bed, protesting. Tilda had stayed until the last moment, banking the side-fires, and then gone, looking back over her shoulder all the way to the door.

Antho stood at Lex's left hand. Wren at his right. Kestrel a pace behind Antho, where Antho could see her. The others were further back, by the anvils. Seraphine with a notebook open, pen poised. Teo, pale, dripping onto the stone floor. Dorran. The twins. Oriel on a stool with her stick across her knees.

"*How many can go through?*" said Wren.

"I don't know," said Lex. "Malachi said a Speaker opens the door. He didn't say how wide."

"*Three*," said Oriel Sant, from her stool. "*Besides the smith. I can see it. The door's the size of a hearth. Four can pass.*" She tilted her head. "*Choose well.*"

Lex looked at them.

"*Me*," said Antho. "*Obviously. And Kestrel, because I'm not letting go of her sleeve.*" He glanced at Kestrel. She didn't say anything. Her face was very still. "*And she hears Flame. Better than anyone here except you.*"

"*And me*," said Wren. Very firmly.

"Wren—"

"*Hammer. You promised. Properly. In a wrecked ballroom with my shoes off.*" Her green eyes were very bright. "*Never again, you said.*"

He looked at her. He remembered the masquerade. His own voice. *I promise properly. Never again.*

"Never again," he said.

---

He put his hands into the fire.

He'd done it before. At Malachi's hearth, on the shore of the Stillwater, the first time he ever heard Flame. In the coals of a hundred forges since. It didn't burn him anymore. It hadn't burned him in years. The fire leaned into his hands like a cat into a stroke, warm and glad and *alive*.

He closed his eyes.

And *Spoke*.

Not to this fire. Through it. Down. Down through the coals and the black stone of the hearth and the earth of the Vale beneath, down to the place below the Lattice where the river ran, where Flame came up from the deep like water from a spring.

*I need to come in*, he told it. *Please. I need to come all the way in.*

For a long moment, nothing.

Then the fire under his hands went *white*.

Not gold. Not orange. White, the white of iron at welding heat, the white of the center of the sun. It rose up out of the hearth in a column, silent, roaring without sound, so bright that he saw it through his closed eyelids. And it *opened*.

He felt it open. The way you feel a door open behind you in a cold room. A rush of heat. A smell like hot metal and summer and the inside of a bread oven. And a sound, very far off, like a thousand bells ringing at once.

He opened his eyes.

The column of white fire stood in the hearth, taller than a man, and in the middle of it, there was a door. Not a door. A *hole*. A round space in the white fire where the fire went *deeper*, gold and red and endless, like looking down a tunnel into the heart of a forge.

*[Oh*,] breathed Ember. *[Oh, Lex. Look at it.]*

"*Well*," said Wren, beside him. Her voice was not quite steady. "*Hammer. After you.*"

He stepped into the fire.

---

It was like being born.

He thought that afterward, trying to explain it to Seraphine, who wrote down every word. Like being pushed through something too small and too hot and too bright, with everything pressing on you, and then coming out the other side into air and light and noise, gasping.

He came out on his hands and knees on a floor of gold.

Not gold metal. Gold *light*. Solid. Warm. Like standing on the surface of a lamp-flame. It held him up. It was hot, but not burning. It hummed under his palms.

He lifted his head.

---

The Wellworld of Flame was the most beautiful thing he had ever seen.

There was no sky. Or rather, the sky was fire. A great dome of it, overhead, gold and red and orange, moving slowly, the way the surface of a forge-fire moves when you look down into it. Not burning. *Breathing*. Rising and falling, slow and steady, like the chest of something enormous and asleep.

And under the sky, a land.

Hills of embers. Red and black and glowing, rolling away to every horizon like a landscape made of coals. Rivers of liquid light, gold and white, running down the hills in long bright ribbons, pooling in valleys into lakes of fire so bright he couldn't look at them. Trees. *Trees*, made of flame. Tall and slender, with trunks of red fire and branches of gold and leaves of flickering white, swaying in a hot wind that smelled of cinnamon and iron.

And everywhere, *life*.

Things moving. In the rivers of light, long sinuous shapes swimming, like eels made of sparks. In the trees, small bright birds, flitting from branch to branch, trailing fire. On the hills, creatures like lizards, long and low and glowing red-gold, basking on the embers with their eyes half closed. Salamanders, Lex thought. The old stories called them salamanders. He'd never believed in them.

And far away, on the very edge of sight, so far that it was only a glow, a light. Brighter than everything else. White and steady and enormous. Like a star sitting on the horizon.

*[The heart*,] said Ember. *[That's the heart of it. Lex. That's where the fire comes from.]*

---

Wren came through behind him. Then Antho. Then Kestrel.

Wren landed on her feet, of course, light as a cat, and stood with her bow in her hand and her mouth open, staring up at the burning sky. Her red braid was lifting in the hot wind. Little sparks were catching in it.

"*Oh*," she said. "*Oh, Hammer. Oh.*"

Antho came through on his knees, the way Lex had. He knelt on the gold floor and lifted his head and looked around at the burning world. His face was white. His grey eyes were very wide.

"*It's loud*," he whispered.

Lex looked at him. "Loud?"

"*Not the sound. The—*" Antho pressed his hand flat against the gold floor. "*There's no dark here. None. No Shade. No silence.*" He swallowed. "*Everything's full. Every inch of it. Everything's burning.*" He looked up at Lex. "*I've never been anywhere without any dark at all.*"

And Kestrel came through last.

She didn't fall. She didn't kneel. She stepped out of the fire onto the gold floor as if she'd been walking through doors of flame all her life. And stopped.

And her hair *blazed*.

Not the low red glow it had been since the Hollowmaw. Not the furious roar of the temple at Ysmir. Something else. Bright, and gold, and steady, like a crown. Her whole body lit up from within. Fire in her hands, her hair, her eyes. Gold fire, pouring off her, and *leaning*, the way Lex's fire leaned, toward the burning sky and the burning hills and the burning trees.

She stared down at her own hands.

"*It knows me*," she whispered. "*It knows me. My grandmother's fire. The Ashari fire. It* knows *me.*"

And all across the hills of embers, the salamanders lifted their heads. And turned. And looked at her.

---

They walked toward the light.

There was no other direction. Every river of liquid fire ran toward it. Every tree leaned toward it, like flowers toward the sun. Every salamander on every hill had its head turned toward it. The heart. The white star on the horizon.

It was hot. Not burning. But hot the way a summer afternoon is hot, a heavy steady heat that pressed on you and made you slow. Wren's face was flushed and running with sweat. Antho had taken off his coat and slung it over his shoulder and was walking with his head down, as if against a wind.

Lex didn't feel it. Not the way they did. He walked through the Wellworld of Flame and felt it all around him like warm water, like a bath, like coming home.

*[Careful*,] said Ember. Quietly. *[Lex. Careful.]*

*Of what?*

*[Of how good it feels.]*

He didn't answer. But he noticed, after a while, that he'd stopped sweating. That the heat had stopped feeling like heat. That when he looked down at his hands, the gold glow that always came off them had got brighter. Much brighter. And spread. Up his wrists. Up his arms.

He made himself look away.

---

The salamanders followed Kestrel.

They came down off the hills in ones and twos. Then dozens. Long low glowing shapes, gliding over the embers on short legs, their eyes like drops of molten gold. They didn't come near Lex, or Antho, or Wren. They went to Kestrel. They walked beside her, and behind her, and in front of her, a great slow shining procession, like a cat's tail of fire trailing behind her across the hills.

She didn't seem to notice. She walked with her face turned toward the white light on the horizon and her hair blazing gold, and her lips moving.

"*She's talking to them*," said Wren quietly.

"*She's talking to it*," said Antho. He was walking close beside Kestrel, the way he'd promised. Not touching. But close. "*All of it. The whole world.*"

Lex dropped back to walk beside them. And he heard what Kestrel was saying. Very low. Over and over.

"*I'm sorry. I'm sorry. I'm sorry. I'm sorry.*"

---

They walked for a long time. Lex didn't know how long. There was no sun to measure by. Only the slow rise and fall of the burning sky, breathing overhead.

And the light grew.

It stopped being a star on the horizon. It became a glow. Then a blaze. Then a great white column, rising out of the land ahead of them, so tall it touched the burning sky and so bright it cast no shadows at all. Like the column of white fire that had opened in the Ashbourne hearth, but a thousand times bigger. A pillar of the purest fire. Silent. Roaring without sound.

And at its foot, a lake.

Not of liquid light. Of *fire*. White fire. Perfectly still. Perfectly flat. Like a mirror made of flame, spreading out from the foot of the pillar in a great circle, miles across.

They came down the last hill to its shore and stopped.

The salamanders didn't go any further. They stopped on the hills behind, in a great glowing crowd, hundreds of them, and lay down on the embers with their heads toward the lake. Waiting.

"*This is it*," said Kestrel. Her voice was strange. Far away. "*The first fire. The one all the others come from.*"

Lex looked at the white lake. At the pillar rising out of it.

"How do we get the coal?" said Wren. "Malachi said fire from the heart. A coal. How do we—"

*You ask*.

The voice came from everywhere.

From the lake. From the pillar. From the burning sky. From the embers under their feet and the fire in their own bodies. Not loud. Not soft. A voice like the crackle of a log on a winter hearth, and the roar of a forest fire, and the hiss of a struck match, all at once.

*You ask. And I ask you a question back. And if your answer is true, you may take what you need.*

The lake moved.

A shape rose out of the white fire. Slowly. Not a woman. Not a man. A *shape*, tall and slender and made of flame, white at its heart and gold at its edges, with no face but two burning points where eyes would be. It stood on the surface of the lake as if the lake were stone.

*I am the Hearth*, said the shape. *I am the first fire. I was burning when your world was dark, and I will be burning when it is dark again.* The burning eyes moved over them. Over Wren. Over Antho, who flinched. Over Kestrel. And stopped on Lex.

*Smith*, said the Hearth. *I have heard you. Every morning, for ten years. You say good morning to my children in your forge before you light them.*

Lex didn't know what to say.

*Nobody does that*, said the Hearth. *Not in nine hundred years. Not since the one who wove the net.* A pause. *He said good morning too. Before.*

---

*Ask*, said the Hearth.

Lex stepped forward. To the edge of the white lake.

"I need a coal," he said. "From your heart. To forge a bar. To stop a key from turning. If it turns, the lid comes off the world, and something comes out from under it that eats light." He swallowed. "It'll eat you too. Eventually. All the fire. All of it."

The Hearth was silent for a moment.

*I know what lies under the lid*, it said. *I burned it once. Long ago. In a land of grass and rivers. My child burned it, and burned everything with it, and has grieved ever since.*

*The Sunwell*, thought Lex.

*You woke my child*, said the Hearth. *In the glass. You went down and listened, and told it that it had done well.* The burning eyes were very bright. *It sent word to me. Through the deep. It said: a smith came. He listened.*

"It deserved to be listened to."

*Yes*. A long pause. *Ask your question, smith. Then I will ask mine.*

"I've asked it. Can I have the coal?"

*That is a request. Not a question*. The Hearth tilted its faceless head. *But very well. Here is mine.*

And the white fire of the lake rose up around Lex's feet.

*What do you burn for?*

---

He didn't answer at once.

He'd been asked a version of it before. By Malachi, on the shore of the Stillwater. *What's your fire for, smith?* And he'd said *to make things*, and Malachi had laughed and said *that's not an answer, that's a job*.

He thought about it now. Standing at the edge of the first fire in the world, with the white flame lapping at his boots.

*To make things*. That was true. He loved making things. He loved the moment when the iron gave, and bent, and became what it wanted to be. He loved the ring of the hammer and the hiss of the quench and the weight of a finished blade in his hand.

But that wasn't it. Not all of it.

*To protect people*. That was true too. Antho. Wren. His father. His students. The Unlit. Forty-eight names in a dead woman's book. He'd burned for them. He'd burned the Umbral Wardens in the glass and the shadows on the Hollowmaw slope.

But that wasn't it either.

He looked down at the white fire around his feet. And thought about the Sunwell. About the plain of red earth and green grass and singing people, and the sun rising from below to save them, and burning them.

*It saved them. And it killed everyone it was saving*.

*Fire doesn't choose who it burns. The only thing you can choose is what you burn it for.*

"I don't know," he said.

The Hearth went very still.

"I don't know," said Lex again. His voice was rough. "I used to think I did. To make things. To protect people. To fix what's broken." He looked up at the burning eyes. "But the Sunwell burned to protect people. And it killed them all. And the man I'm trying to stop thinks he's burning for something too. He thinks he's earned it. He thinks the world owes him." He swallowed. "And somewhere at the bottom of me there's a door, with something behind it that sounds kind and loves me and wants me to burn for *it*."

The white fire was very hot around his ankles now. He didn't move.

"I don't know what I burn for," said Lex. "I only know I have to keep asking. Every day. Every time I light the forge. What's this fire for? Is it the right fire? Am I burning the right thing?" He met the Hearth's eyes. "Because the day I stop asking, I'll be like the Sunwell. Or like Elric. Or like the thing behind the door. Burning, and sure, and wrong."

---

The Hearth didn't say anything for a long time.

The white lake was very still. The pillar of fire rose silent behind it. The salamanders on the hills lay with their golden eyes fixed on the shore.

*That*, said the Hearth at last, *is the first true answer I have heard in nine hundred years.*

The white fire around Lex's feet sank back into the lake.

*Every one who comes here tells me what they burn for*, said the Hearth. *To protect. To build. To destroy. To win. Kings and sorcerers and Founders. They are all very sure.* The burning eyes moved, slowly, over the lake. *The one who wove the net was very sure. He stood where you stand and told me he burned to keep the world safe. And I believed him, and gave him what he asked.*

A long pause.

*He was wrong*, said the Hearth. *He was so sure, and so wrong. And the net he wove became a cage. And the thing he caged got into him through the bars.*

Lex didn't move.

*You are not sure*, said the Hearth. *Good. Stay that way. Keep asking.* It lifted one long burning arm. *Take your coal.*

---

The lake parted.

Not like water. Like a curtain. The white fire drew back on either side, opening a path from the shore to the foot of the great pillar. A path of gold light, solid and warm, leading straight into the heart of the first fire in the world.

Lex looked at it.

*[Lex*,] said Ember. Very carefully. *[Lex, look at your hands.]*

He looked.

The gold glow had spread. All the way up his arms now. To his shoulders. He could feel it creeping up his neck. And his hands—

His hands were fire.

Not burning. *Being*. His fingers flickering at the edges, gold and white, like candle flames. He could see the gold floor through his palms.

*[It's Becoming*,] said Ember. *[The fifth stage. Malachi warned you. Commanding is when you tell the Well what to do. Becoming is when the Well tells you. When you go in so deep you* turn into it.*]* Its voice was urgent. *[Lex, it's been happening since we came through. Slowly. The longer you stay, the more you burn. If you walk into that pillar—]*

*I'll come out with the coal.*

*[You won't come out at all. You'll* be *the coal.]*

Lex looked at the path. At the pillar. At the white fire at its heart, roaring without sound.

It was so beautiful. It felt like coming home. Like every forge he'd ever stood at, every fire he'd ever lit, every morning he'd said *good morning* to the coals. All of them coming from here. All of them going back here.

*It would be so easy*, he thought. *Just walk in. Just keep walking.*

*No more keys. No more doors. No more kind voices at the bottom of me. Just fire.*

He took a step onto the path.

---

A hand closed on his sleeve.

Cold. The coldest thing in the Wellworld of Flame. Long-fingered and scarred and very, very cold, like a hand that had spent eight years holding a breach shut in the dark.

He looked down.

Antho. Holding his sleeve. Not his arm. His *sleeve*. Between finger and thumb. The way a ten-year-old boy had held a fifteen-year-old's sleeve, on a woodpile, in the dark, every night for a year.

His brother's face was grey and running with sweat. His grey eyes were very dark. In the Wellworld of Flame, with no Shade anywhere, he looked like a man standing in a gale. But his hand was steady on Lex's sleeve.

"*No*," said Antho.

Lex stared at him.

"*I don't remember the stone*," said Antho. "*I paid it away. I don't remember the millpond, or the heron, or you being ten.*" His voice was very quiet. "*But I remember the woodpile. Every night. For a year. You came out and sat beside me in the dark, and you didn't say anything, and you held my sleeve.*" His fingers tightened. "*You held on. Every time the voices called me into the trees, you held on.*"

"Antho—"

"*It's my turn*," said Antho. "*I'm holding on.*"

And Lex felt it. Through the Bloodbond. Not fire. Not gold. Something cold and quiet and deep. His brother's Shade, the absence, the silence between heartbeats, pouring down through the thread that bound them together and into Lex like cold water into a quenching trough.

And the fire in his hands *hissed*.

Steamed. Like hot iron in the slack tub. The gold glow on his arms flickered. Dimmed. Drew back. Down from his shoulders. Down his arms. To his wrists. His hands. His fingers solid again. Flesh. Scarred and callused and his own.

He stood on the path of gold light, one step from the shore, with his brother holding his sleeve, and breathed.

---

She was gone a long time.

They stood on the shore and waited. Lex. Antho. Wren, who had come up beside Lex without a word and put her hand in his and held on very tight. The salamanders on the hills behind them. The Hearth, standing on the white lake, still as a flame in a windless room.

The pillar roared without sound. They couldn't see into it. It was too bright. They could only see the path of gold light running into it, and nothing coming out.

*[What do you think it's showing her?]* said Ember quietly.

*Tamsin*, thought Lex. *The Hollowmaw. The rag. Forty-one graves. Ilse.*

*[All of it,]* said Ember.

*All of it.*

Wren's hand tightened on his.

"*If she doesn't come out*," she said quietly. "*If she Becomes. If she just—stays.*"

"She'll come out," said Antho.

"*How do you know?*"

He didn't answer for a long time. He stood on the shore of the white lake with his coat over his shoulder and his face grey and running with sweat, and looked at the pillar.

"*Because she's got something to carry*," he said at last. "*Something heavy. She's going to carry it for the rest of her life.*" He was quiet. "*People with something to carry always come back out. They can't put it down long enough to stay.*"

---

She came out.

Walking. Slowly. Down the path of gold light from the foot of the pillar. Not blazing anymore. Her hair was red again. Just red. Her face was white and wet and very calm.

And in her cupped hands, something burned.

Small. The size of a hen's egg. A coal. But not like any coal Lex had ever seen. It was white at the heart, and gold at the edges, and it didn't flicker. It burned *steady*, absolutely still, like the flame of the lamp in the niche at the Hollowmaw. And it *sang*. Very faintly. A sound like a far-off bell.

She came off the path onto the shore. She held it out to Lex.

He took it. It sat in his palms like a living thing. Warm. Humming. The heart of the first fire in the world.

Lex couldn't see it. Not with True Sight. Kestrel wasn't in the Lattice. But the panel came up anyway, in silver, hanging in the burning air of the Wellworld over her head. As if the fire itself had written it.

---

*Go now*, said the Hearth. *The door is closing. The world you came from is moving faster than this one. You have been here a day. There, it has been an hour.*

A day for an hour. Lex did the sum. *Forty days*. *Thirty-eight, now. Thirty-seven*.

*Take my coal to the other three*, said the Hearth. *To Stone. To Tide. To Gale. They will not be as kind as I am.* A pause. *They do not remember the one who wove the net as fondly as I do. They remember what he became.*

"Thank you," said Lex.

The burning shape inclined its faceless head.

*Keep asking, smith*, it said. *Every day. Every time you light the forge.*

And the white fire of the lake rose up around them like a wave, and lifted them, and they were falling, upward, through gold and red and white, through heat and light and the sound of a thousand bells—

---

—and out onto the black stone floor of the Ashbourne Forge, at a quarter past one in the morning, in front of a hearth burning perfectly ordinary orange.

Seraphine was there, with her notebook. She'd fallen asleep on it. She jerked awake when they came through, and her spectacles fell off, and she scrabbled for them on the floor.

"*An hour*," she said, peering at them. "*You've been gone an hour. What happened? Did you get it? What was it like? Did you—*"

Lex opened his hands.

The Heart-Coal sat in his palms, white and gold and humming, filling the long dark hall of the Ashbourne Forge with a light like a summer noon. Every one of the nineteen banked side-fires down the length of the hall flared up at once, gold, as if saying hello.

Seraphine stopped talking.

Behind her, Teo, who had been asleep on a bench, sat up and stared. Dorran stood up from the anvil he'd been leaning on. The twins woke, and Ilse made a small sound. Oriel Sant turned her white face toward the light.

"*Oh*," said Oriel softly. "*Oh, that's lovely. I can almost see that.*"

And on the forge chimney, outside in the dark, a raven ruffled his feathers, and looked down through the smoke-hole at the light pouring up out of the hall, and said, to no one in particular, in a voice like a rusty hinge:

"*About time*."
