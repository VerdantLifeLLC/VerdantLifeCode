# The Unnamed Warden

She came out of the dark singing.

Lex saw her. Across the empty void beyond the broken pillars of the forge, following the path the faceless woman had walked. Walking on nothing, the way everything walked here. Slowly. Like someone who has been walking for a very long time.

A woman. Small. Dark-haired. In a faded blue dress gone grey at the hem.

Faint. Not like the thinned. Not like the Unnamed. Faint the way a candle is faint at the end of a long night, when it's burned down almost to the dish and the flame is small and steady and very tired.

She was singing. Low and a little rough. Flat on the high note.

*If the dark comes calling, love, don't you fear.*
*There's a light in the window, and I'm right here.*

Lex couldn't breathe.

He'd forgotten. He'd forgotten so much. He'd been eleven. Eleven years. He'd forgotten the exact color of her eyes, and the shape of her hands, and the way her hair fell when she bent over the hearth. He'd seen her in the Glass Desert, in the reflections, and it had been her, and it hadn't, because he'd forgotten too much to know.

He knew now.

Small. Dark-haired. A narrow face, with high cheekbones and a mouth that didn't smile easily. Antho's face. Ysmir's face. And eyes—

She lifted her head. She looked up the white slope. At the forge. At the broken pillars. At the people standing in it.

At him.

Grey. Her eyes were grey. Like Antho's. Like the sky between the stars.

And she stopped singing.

---

"*Seren*," said the thing at the Heart.

It said it pleasantly. Lightly. The way you greet a neighbour who has turned up unexpectedly at your door.

"*Seren Vey*. *You left your door.*"

Seren came up the white slope. Slowly. She didn't look at the thing at the anvil. She came to the edge of the forge, between two broken pillars, a little way behind the faceless woman in torn white robes. And stopped. And stood there.

"*You left yours*," she said. Her voice was low and rough and very tired. "*Eight hundred years ago. When you let the hunger in.*"

The kind face didn't change.

"*I've been following your Warden*," said Seren. "*Since the longest night. Since you came to my door and showed me your Choir.*" She looked at the faceless woman standing in the forge. "*Every night she goes to my old door, and pushes. And every night she comes back down through the dark. Alone. With no one to call her anything.*" Her grey eyes were very steady. "*So I followed her. And sang to her. So she'd have something.*"

"*How kind*," said the thing at the Heart.

"*Not kind*." Seren's mouth twisted. "*Stubborn. I've always been stubborn.*" She looked at the anvil. At the thing standing at it, in its leather apron. "*Your Arion's worse. You told me so yourself.*"

---

"*Mam*," said Antho.

It came out of him like a breath.

Seren turned her head.

She looked at him. Across the round white forge. Standing beside his brother with the Lantern in his hand and his clear grey eyes wet.

And her whole face changed.

"*Oh*," she said. Very softly. "*Oh, Antho.*"

She didn't move. She stood at the edge of the forge with her hands at her sides. Faint. Tired. Looking at her eldest son.

"*You've got so big*," she whispered. "*I could feel it. Through the door. Every night. But I couldn't see.*" Her voice cracked. "*You've got your father's height. And my face. Poor love.*"

Antho made a sound. Something between a laugh and a sob.

"*And Lex*," said Seren.

Her eyes moved to him. And he felt them. Like warmth. Like a hand on his face.

"*My little one*," she said. "*My Lex. Who used to hide under the table when it thundered.*" She looked at him for a long moment. At his shoulders. His scarred hands. His gold eyes. "*Look at you. Oh, look at you. You're the size of a door.*"

"*Mam*," said Lex. He didn't know his voice could sound like that.

"*I know, love*." She smiled. Small. Tired. *Hers*. "*I know.*"

---

"*How touching*," said the thing at the Heart.

It said it gently. Fondly. But it had turned. Away from Lex. Toward Seren. And its hand, the one it had held out to Lex, was lowered.

"*Eleven years*," it said. "*In the dark. Holding a door. Following my Warden. Singing.*" It tilted its head. "*And now you're here. At the Heart. Where your sons are.*" Its gold eyes were very warm. "*Did you think you could stop me, Seren? You? A hedge-witch from the Greyspine with a clay lamp?*"

"*No*," said Seren.

She walked into the forge.

Past the faceless woman. Past the broken pillars. Across the white floor. Not toward the anvil. Not toward her sons. Toward the *Warden*. She came round in front of her. And stood there. Looking up into the place where a face should be.

"*I didn't come to stop you*," said Seren Vey. Without looking at the thing at the anvil. "*I came to give her back her name.*"

---

The faceless woman didn't move.

Seren reached up. Slowly. And put her faint hand on the Warden's cheek. On the unremembered place. The place Lex's eyes kept sliding off.

"*I don't know it*," said Seren. "*I've been singing to her for months. In the dark. Every lullaby I know. Every song.*" Her voice was very gentle. "*And I don't know her name. Nobody does.*" She looked over her shoulder. At her sons. At the band of people standing in the forge. "*He took everyone who might have remembered.*"

"*Except one*," said Lex.

He was already reaching into his coat.

The Ledger. Cold and heavy against his chest. Where it had been since Ysmir's vault. Since the White Shore. Since the Rift. He took it out. He opened it. To the last page.

Two names.

He read the first one out loud.

"*Isaura Kell*," said Lex. "*Of Highcrest. Fifty-eight. High Warden.*" His voice was steady. "*She was carried once.*"

---

The faceless woman *flinched*.

Hard. Like a blow. Her whole body jerking. Her hands, at her sides, clenching so tight that Lex could see the knuckles stand out, grey and faint.

"*Don't*," she said. Thin. Dry. Desperate. "*Don't. He said you'd say things. He said it would hurt.*"

"*Does it*?" said Seren. Very softly. Her hand still on the woman's cheek.

The Warden didn't answer.

"*Isaura*," said Seren. Trying it. The way you try a key in a lock. "*Isaura. Is that you? Is that your name?*"

The faceless woman was shaking.

"*I don't*—" she said. "*I don't know. I don't know. It sounds*—" She stopped. "*It sounds like something I used to wear. A long time ago. Like a coat. That doesn't fit anymore.*"

"*It fits*," said a voice.

Ilse Marrow.

She'd come forward. Out of the group. Across the white floor. In her patched green coat, with her mouse-brown hair and her grey eyes. Solid. Whole. Her brother a step behind her.

She stopped in front of the Warden. Beside Seren.

"*Isaura Kell*," said Ilse. "*You were the High Warden. You put the Ceiling on everyone in Caldor. You put it on me.*" Her voice was very steady. "*And at the bottom of the Rift, when the dark came for me, you shoved me out of the way. And it took you instead.*"

The Warden's head jerked. Toward Ilse. The unremembered place where a face should be.

"*I don't*—"

"*You did*," said Ilse. "*I didn't see it. I was asleep. But Lex saw. And Ivo.*" She looked at her brother. "*And I've been thinking about it every day since. Every day. Why would she do that? Why would the woman who capped me throw herself in front of me?*"

Silence.

"*And Lex told me*," said Ilse Marrow. "*He said: because someone carried her.*"

---

Lex said it.

He'd known he would. Since the Rift. Since the Ledger. Since he'd read *she was carried once* in a dead woman's narrow elegant hand.

"*Dorran Vask*," he said.

The faceless woman went absolutely still.

"*He was your Warden of the Kindling*," said Lex. "*Twenty-two years. He never complained.*" He swallowed. "*And at the bottom of the Rift, when Elric had taken forty-one percent of you and left you on the stair to die, Dorran knelt down in front of you. And said: I'm not going to forgive you, Commander. But I'm not going to leave you either.*"

The Warden's hands had come up. To her face. To the unremembered place.

"*And he picked you up*," said Lex. "*And carried you. Down the stair. And when the Rift fell, he set you down and held up the mountain so you could get away.*"

"*Vask*," whispered the faceless woman.

"*Dorran Vask*."

"*He had very gentle hands*." It came out of her like something breaking. "*For such a big man.*"

And her face came back.

---

Not all at once. Slowly. The way a face comes clear through a frosted window when someone breathes on the glass. The unremembered place, *remembering*. Lex saw it. Saw a jaw come back. A thin mouth. High cheekbones. Hollow cheeks. Sunken eyes, pale grey, wide and wet and staring.

Isaura Kell.

Gaunt. Grey. Faint. Standing in the round white forge at the Heart of the world, in her torn white robes, with Seren Vey's hand on one cheek and Ilse Marrow standing in front of her.

She looked at Ilse.

"*You*," she said. Barely a whisper. "*The thief. From the Rift. The faded one.*"

"*Not faded anymore*," said Ilse. "*Elric wrote me back. At the end.*" She held up her hand. Solid. "*Because you pushed me out of the way first.*"

Kell stared at her hand.

And then she put her own hands over her face. Her own face. And bent forward. And her shoulders shook.

Seren put her arms around her.

Just like that. A hedge-witch from the Greyspine, holding the former High Warden of the Order in the middle of the Heart of the world. Holding her the way she'd held her boys when they were small and frightened of the noises in the walls.

"*There*," said Seren Vey. Very softly. "*There. There you are. Isaura.*"

The panel came up in gold, over Kell's bowed head, hanging in the air of the forge. Lex didn't know how. There was no net here. No Lattice. But it came anyway. As if the Heart itself had written it.

*[Lex*—]

He nearly fell over.

*Ember*.

*[I'm here*,] said Ember. Faint. So faint. Like a voice from the bottom of a well. *[I'm at the door. I've been at the door the whole time. Since the waterfall. Since the net went out.]* A pause. *[You're standing on it, Lex. The Heart. It's the bottom of the Root. I could feel you get closer and closer and I couldn't reach you and then you said her name, and the Heart wrote it down, and I*—]

*You can hear me*?

*[I can hear you. A little. Through the Heart. Through the door.]* Ember's voice was shaking. *[Lex. The thing. On the other side of the door. It's* angry. *I've never felt it angry before.]*

---

The thing at the Heart had not moved.

It stood at the gold anvil with its hammer in its hand and its kind face turned toward the edge of the forge, where Seren Vey was holding Isaura Kell. And it didn't say anything.

But the kind face was *slipping*.

Lex saw it. Not a flicker now. A slide. Slow. Like wax melting. The fair hair, the gold eyes, the tired beautiful features of the smith who had sung at his anvil, sliding down off something that wasn't there. And under it, for longer and longer each time, the hole. The hunger. A shape cut out of the dark, Aurelian-sized, with nothing inside.

"*Warden*," it said.

The voice wasn't kind. It wasn't warm. It was *enormous*. It came from the anvil and the pillar of gold and every grey tithe-thread falling into the Heart, all at once.

"*WARDEN. YOU ARE MINE. I GAVE YOU A NAME. I GAVE YOU A DOOR.*"

Isaura Kell lifted her head from Seren's shoulder.

She looked at the thing at the anvil. Her pale grey eyes very red. Her gaunt face very still.

"*You did*," she said. Her voice was thin and dry and precise. A voice that had given orders for twenty years. "*You gave me a door. The one at the Hollowmaw. You said it was mine now.*"

"*IT IS MINE. YOU ARE MINE.*"

"*No*," said Isaura Kell. "*You said the* door *was mine.*"

She stepped back from Seren. Gently. She turned to face the anvil. She stood very straight in her torn white robes, in the middle of the forge at the Heart of the world, the way she must have stood at the head of the High Council for twenty years.

"*I was High Warden of the Order*," she said. "*I know about doors. I know about Wardens. I know what it means to be given one to keep.*" She lifted her chin. "*A Warden decides who comes through. That's what the word means.*"

And she *reached*.

---

Lex felt it. Through the Heart. Through the Root. Through every thread that ran down from the world into the Forbidden Lands.

A pipe. One pipe. The last one. The widest. Running down from a cracked black door at the bottom of a mountain hung with a thousand lanterns. Held open, every night, for months, by a faceless woman pushing from the inside.

Kell reached down it. With the authority she'd been given. The Warden of the door.

And *closed it*.

Not slammed. Not sealed. *Closed*. The way a careful person closes a door behind them when they leave a room. Firmly. Finally. And turned the key.

Far away, through the Root, Lex felt the black glass door at the bottom of the Hollowmaw *settle*. Felt the crack across its face, that had been there since the night of the Blade, draw together. Felt it seal. Not by anyone pushing. By someone on the inside, deciding.

And felt, faint and far, a man with his hands on the glass sit back. And look at the two lamps in the niche. And put his face in his hands.

"*It's closed*," said Isaura Kell. Very quietly. "*I'm the Warden. I've decided. Nothing comes through.*"

The thing at the anvil *screamed*.

Not a voice now. The kind face was gone. All of it. Slid off. And what stood at the anvil was a *hole*. A hunger. A shape cut out of the white forge, tall and narrow, with nothing inside it but dark. The smith's apron hung on it like a coat on a hook. The scarred hands holding the hammer were the only solid things.

And the scream went out through the Heart. Up the pillar of gold. Up every tithe-thread.

Into the world.

Lex felt it hit. Every Stone. Every thread. Every soul.

*Pulling*.

Not a quarter. Not a third. *Half*. More. The tithe-threads going taut as bowstrings, all over the world, and *yanking*. Down. Hard. Every soul under the net losing more than half of what they were in a single terrible heartbeat.

And all over the world, people *fell*.

He saw it. Through the Heart. Highcrest. People collapsing in the streets, in their beds, at their tables. Clutching their chests. Going grey. Going faint. Saffaran. The Ashari fire guttering out on every terrace. The Wickhollow. Every candle going out at once. Ashbourne. Tilda Crane on her knees by the great hearth, gasping, her freckled face going pale.

Brannoc. Hedda. Mother Orla. Quill on the chimney, toppling.

His father, at the Hollowmaw. Sliding sideways onto the black floor beside the sealed door.

*Everyone*.

---

"*STOP*—"

He didn't know he'd shouted it. He was on his knees on the white floor. He felt it in himself too. The pull. Going down through his own thread. Taking. A tenth. A quarter. Half. His strength going out of him like water out of a cracked jug.

Beside him, Wren had fallen. Her bow clattering on the white stone. Antho was on one knee, gripping the Lantern, his face grey.

Behind him, the others. Going down. Kestrel. Tamsin. Seraphine. Teo. The twins. One by one. Like wheat in a gale.

Only three stood.

Oriel Sant. Sitting on the white floor at the edge of the forge, with her black stick across her knees. Untouched. She'd never had a thread. Never been in the net. Nothing to pull.

Malachi. Small and brown and barefoot. The Last Listener. Unlatticed. Standing at the edge of the forge with the silver bell ringing in his hand. Ringing so loud now that it was almost a scream.

And Seren. Faint and grey and very tired, holding Isaura Kell. Neither of them had threads anymore. They were beyond the net. In the dark. Where the Tithe couldn't reach.

---

The hole at the anvil turned toward Lex.

It didn't have a face. It didn't have eyes. But he felt it look at him. The way he'd felt it look at him through his own eyes in the Glass Desert. Through the door at the bottom of his Root.

"*OPEN*," it said. "*THE*. *DOOR*."

The pull went up. Further. Sixty percent. Seventy.

All over the world, people stopped moving.

"*OPEN IT, OR I TAKE THEM ALL. EVERY SOUL. EVERY THREAD. DOWN. INTO ME. NOW.*"

Lex knelt on the white floor of the forge with his hands flat on the stone and felt the world going out of everyone he'd ever loved.

*[Lex*,] said Ember. Faint. Desperate. *[Lex, don't. Don't. It's what it wants. It's what it's always wanted*—]

*I know*.

*[Then*—]

*Ember*, thought Lex. *Listen to me. Are you at the door?*

A pause.

*[Yes*.]

*Right at it?*

*[Right at it. I'm sitting on the doorstep. I haven't moved since the waterfall.]*

*Is anyone else there?*

Another pause. Longer.

*[Yes*,] said Ember. Slowly. *[Yes. Further down. Below the door. Very far down. Something small. Tired.]* A pause. *[It's been trying to talk to you. The whole time we've been here. It keeps saying the same thing.]*

*What?*

*[Look for the flaw*,] said Ember. *[Over and over. Look for the flaw.]*

---

Lex turned his head.

His brother was beside him. On one knee. Grey-faced. The Lantern in his hand, guttering. His clear grey eyes on Lex's face.

"*Antho*," said Lex.

"*Don't*." It came out of his brother like a groan. "*Don't you dare. Don't you*—"

"*Hold my sleeve*."

Antho stared at him.

"*Hold my sleeve*," said Lex again. "*Like you promised. Between finger and thumb.*" His voice was very steady. He didn't know how. "*And when it goes through, don't let go.*"

"*When what goes*—"

"*When I open the door*," said Lex.

He turned back to the anvil.

To the hole. The hunger. The thing standing in a smith's apron with a hammer in its hands, pulling the world down into the dark.

"*All right*," said Lex Vey. "*I'll open it.*"

---

The pull stopped.

All at once. Like a held breath let out. The tithe-threads going slack. All over the world. People gasping. Lying where they'd fallen. Breathing.

The hole at the anvil was very still.

And slowly, slowly, the kind face came back. Slid back up over the dark, like a mask being put on. Fair hair. Gold eyes. The tired beautiful features of the smith who had sung at his anvil. Smiling.

"*My boy*," it said. Warm again. Kind again. Loving. "*Oh, my boy. I knew you would.*"

"*Lex*," said Wren. From the floor. Her voice cracked. "*Lex, no*—"

"*I know*," he said. Without looking at her. "*I know, Wren. Trust me.*"

He felt her go still.

*Trust that if he goes into the dark, he will come back out*, the First Breath had said. In the sky. *Not because you held on. Because he will.*

"*I'll come back*," said Lex. "*I always come back. Because you're there.*"

He heard her breathe. Once. Shaking.

"*You'd better*," said Wren Ashdown. Very quietly. "*Hammer.*"

---

He felt Antho's fingers close on his sleeve.

He stood up.

He walked across the white floor of the forge. Toward the gold anvil. Toward the pillar of light. Toward the thing in his ancestor's face, standing with its scarred hand held out, waiting.

He stopped in front of it.

"*Not like that*," he said.

The kind face tilted.

"*Not with your hand*," said Lex. "*Not out here. If you want in, come through the door. The way you came into him.*" He nodded at the face it was wearing. "*The way you came into my father's dreams, and Antho's head, and Elric's ear. Come through the bottom of the gold. Where you've been waiting.*" He swallowed. "*I'll open it from my side.*"

The thing looked at him for a long, long moment.

Its gold eyes were very bright.

"*You're afraid*," it said softly. "*I can feel it. All the way through you. You're so afraid, Lex.*"

"*Yes*."

"*Good*." It smiled. "*That's how I get in.*"

And it put its hand on the anvil.

And went *down*.

---

Lex felt it go.

Down. Into the anvil. Into the gold. Into the Heart. Into the root of every Root. The way Aurelian had gone down, nine hundred years ago, with his little brother screaming on a bucket. The smith's apron and the scarred hands and the kind face, sinking into the gold anvil like a man stepping into deep water.

Gone.

And at the bottom of his own Root, at the door—

*Pressure*.

Warm. Gold. Enormous. Leaning. On the other side of the door that had been open a crack since the Glass Desert. Leaning *hard*. The whole weight of it. Everything it had eaten in nine hundred years. Pressing.

*Open it, child*, said the voice. From right there. Right on the other side. *Open it. You promised.*

He closed his eyes.

He went down. Into himself. Into the gold. Down and down, past his own thread, past his brother's twined around it, past his father's and his mother's pale silver line. Down to the bottom. To the door.

Ember was sitting on the doorstep.

He couldn't see it. There was nothing to see. But he felt it. A small warm gold presence, humming, sitting right in front of the door with its back against it. The way his mother had sat against the door at the Hollowmaw. The way Antho had sat against the breach.

*[Lex*,] it said. Very quietly. *[Are you sure?]*

*No*.

*[All right*.] A pause. *[I'll be right here.]*

And far, far down, below the door, below everything, in the very deepest gold where the first thread went down into the dark, something small and tired and sad that couldn't sing said:

*Oh, child. Oh, no. Not you. Not one of mine.*

*It's all right*, thought Lex. *I'm looking for the flaw.*

And he put his hand on the door.

And opened it.
