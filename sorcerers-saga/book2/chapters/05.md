# The Cursed Prophecy

The verse hung in the air above the frozen book like breath on a winter morning. Letters of light, pale blue-white, written in Wellscript so old that even Ember had to read it slowly, word by word, the way Lex had once read the hymn-board in the Ashbourne chapel when he was six.

*[Give me a moment,]* it said. *[This is—old. Older than the Book of Making. Older than anything I've seen.]* A pause. *[All right. All right. Here.]*

And it read it to him, in the quiet of his head, while the blue light of the vault moved across his face.

> *Three the pieces: bow and bit and blade.*
> *One in the sundered dark, where the old roads fade;*
> *One in the glass, where the sun was unmade;*
> *One under lanterns, where the door is laid.*
>
> *When three are one, and the turning's paid,*
> *The lid will lift, and the night will stay,*
> *And what was hungry will have its day.*
>
> *Only a made hand can unmake the made.*
> *Only the blood that forged it can bar the way.*
> *And the blood that bars it, the blood will pay.*

Lex read it twice. The second time, the last line seemed to settle into his chest like a stone dropped into a well, falling and falling and never hitting bottom.

*And the blood that bars it, the blood will pay.*

"*Well*," said Kestrel Varn, behind him. "*That's cheerful.*"

He turned. She was standing in the narrow arch of the ice door, peering past his shoulder, her amber eyes narrowed. She couldn't read Wellscript. But the letters of light were reflected in her eyes, and she was frowning at them as if they'd insulted her mother.

"You can't read it," he said.

"*I don't need to read it. I can feel it.*" She rubbed her arms. "*It's like walking past a gallows. Every hair on my body's standing up.*" She glanced at him. "*What does it say?*"

He told her.

She was quiet for a while after.

"*Bow, bit and blade*," she said at last. "*That's a key. The parts of a key. The bow's the handle. The bit's the teeth. The blade's the shaft between them.*" She looked at him. "*He's got the bow. I've seen it. Round his neck on a chain. Black as a hole in the world.*"

"Yes."

"*So there's two more.*"

"Yes."

"*In the glass where the sun was unmade, and under lanterns where the door is laid.*" She chewed her lip. "*I don't know what either of those means.*"

Lex did.

Not the first one. Not yet. But the second—

*Under lanterns. Where the door is laid.*

*[Lex,]* said Ember, very quietly. *[The Hollowmaw.]*

He'd thought it before Ember said it. Hundreds of lanterns, hanging in the dark of a mountain cave. All of them lit now. A door between the world and the Hollow, sealed by two brothers with a lamp and a song. And his brother, living in the middle of it. With students. With a dragon. Watching the door.

*It's under the Hollowmaw. The third piece. That's why there was a breach there in the first place. That's why it opened there and nowhere else.*

*[And Elric knows it.]* Ember's voice was cold. *[He has to. He's been looking for nine hundred years' worth of hiding places with Aurelian whispering in his ear. If the voice told him the bit was in the glass, it's told him where the blade is too.]*

*Then why hasn't he gone for it?*

*[Because the Hollowmaw is the most guarded place in Caldor. Because your brother is Tier III and doesn't sleep much, and there's a storm-drake the size of a barn sitting on the doorstep.]* A pause. *[Because he wants all three, and he's taking them in order. The easy ones first.]*

Lex stood very still in the blue light.

*I sent Ashka home with Antho*, he thought. *I told her, watch the door.*

*[You did right.]*

*Did I? Or did I just put every one of the people I love in one place, right on top of the thing he wants most?*

Ember didn't answer.

---

"*Blood of my brother.*"

The voice came from everywhere. From the walls. From the floor. From every frozen memory in the glacier, all at once, so that the whole vault seemed to speak with one calm sad voice.

And then it came from somewhere particular.

From the ice behind the plinth. Lex saw it move. Not crack. *Move*, the way still water moves when something rises up through it from underneath. A shape, coming up through the clear blue ice from far down, from the deep places where the oldest memories were frozen. Taller than a man. Slender. Pale. Drawing nearer and clearer, the way a face comes clear through a frosted window when someone breathes on the glass.

A woman.

She stepped out of the ice.

She wasn't solid. She wasn't a ghost either, not the way the Drowned Dead had been ghosts, or the Barrow-King. She was ice. Made of it. Carved from it, as if a sculptor had worked the glacier for a hundred years to make a single statue and then the statue had decided to walk. Clear and blue and faintly glowing, with long hair that fell to her ankles in frozen ripples and a long plain gown with frost-flowers at its hem. Her face was calm and narrow and severe, with high cheekbones and a long straight nose.

It was Antho's face.

Not exactly. Older, and a woman's, and carved from ice. But the bones of it. The set of the eyes. The mouth that didn't smile easily. Lex had seen that face across a campfire for three years, and he'd have known it anywhere.

"*Ysmir*," he whispered.

"*What is left of her.*" The ice woman inclined her head. Her voice was the same voice that had come from the walls, but closer now, smaller, and very tired. "*An echo. A memory of a woman, kept in the place she made for keeping memories. I was the Keeper of What Was. When I died, I kept myself.*" Something moved at the corner of her frozen mouth. Not quite a smile. "*It seemed wasteful not to.*"

*[That's a Vey,]* breathed Ember. *[That is very definitely a Vey.]*

"Aurelian's sister," said Lex.

"*His elder sister.*" Ysmir's ice-blue eyes moved over him. Slowly. Taking him in, the way Hedda took in a new apprentice: hands first, then shoulders, then face. "*And you are his blood. Many times removed. I can see him in you. In the eyes, mostly. And in the hands.*" Her gaze lingered on his scarred knuckles. "*He had a smith's hands too. Before he had a king's.*"

"I have his hammer," said Lex. He didn't know why he said it. He took it out of his belt and held it up. Aurelian's little hand-hammer, worn smooth, with the faint gold tracery of runes on its cheeks.

Ysmir looked at it for a long moment.

"*Yes*," she said quietly. "*He made that the year he made the Lattice. He was so happy that year. He used to sing at the anvil.*" She was silent. "*I remember everything. That is the curse of my gift. I remember him singing. And I remember what he became.*"

"What did he become?"

She looked at him.

"*Afraid*," she said.

---

She showed him.

She didn't ask. She simply raised one hand of clear blue ice, and touched the air between them, and the air turned to ice, a pane of it, thin and clear as a window. And behind the window, something moved.

A forge. Not like Ashbourne. Huge, round, open to the sky, on top of a white mountain under stars so bright and close that Lex could almost hear them. And in the center of it, an anvil of gold, and a man standing at the anvil.

Tall. Fair-haired. Beautiful, in a tired way, like a fine blade that's been used too long. He wore a smith's leather apron over a robe of white and gold, and his sleeves were rolled up, and his forearms were corded and scarred exactly like Lex's.

He held a knife in one hand. A silver knife, long and thin, its blade shining like moonlight. And with it, slowly, carefully, the way a tanner takes the hide from a deer, he was cutting his own shadow off the floor.

Lex watched him do it. Watched the silver knife slide along the edge of the black shape on the stone where the man's shadow fell. Watched the shadow come away. Peeling up off the floor like a sheet of black silk. Lifting. Hanging from the man's hand, limp and dark and somehow *alive*, twitching faintly, the way a lizard's tail twitches after the lizard has dropped it.

And the man laid it on the gold anvil. And folded it. Once, and again, and again. And picked up a hammer. Not the little hand-hammer. A great one. And began to strike.

Every blow rang like a bell at the bottom of the sea.

There was a woman in the window too. At the edge of the forge. Tall and dark-haired, with Antho's face, flesh and blood and alive. Watching.

"*Brother*," she said. "*What are you making?*"

The man didn't stop. "*A key.*"

"*A key to what?*"

"*To the lid. The binding. Everything we've made.*" Strike. Strike. "*If something goes wrong, Ysmir. If we're wrong. If the Lattice turns into a cage instead of a shelter, or if what's under it gets loose. Someone has to be able to open it. To start again.*" Strike. "*So I'm making a way.*"

"*Out of your own shadow.*"

"*It's the only thing strong enough. The only thing that belongs to both sides.*" He looked up at her then. And his eyes, in the starlight, were gold. Root gold. Lex's gold. "*Don't look at me like that. I know what I'm doing.*"

"*Do you?*" The woman in the window came closer. "*You haven't slept in a month, Aurelian. You've stopped eating. You talk to the dark at night. I hear you, through the wall.*"

"*I talk to everyone at night. I'm the Root of the world. Everyone talks to me.*"

"*That's not what I mean, and you know it.*"

He struck again. The shadow on the anvil was taking shape now. Long and black and narrow. A key. A great black key, as long as a man's arm, with a looped bow and a shaft and a bit of jagged teeth like a mountain range.

"*It's done*," he said.

And he picked it up, and looked at it, and the woman who was Ysmir watched his face. And Lex, watching through the window of ice nine hundred years later, saw what she saw.

Love. Love like a man looking at his child.

And under it, so faint that he almost missed it, the thing Ysmir had named.

*Afraid*.

---

The window melted.

The vault came back. Blue light. Ice. The plinth with the frozen book. Kestrel, standing in the arch with her arms folded and her eyes narrowed. She hadn't seen any of it.

"*He broke it the next spring*," said Ysmir. "*The Key. In three. He came to me with the pieces in a bag and his hands shaking and asked me to help him hide them.*" She was quiet. "*He said he had made a mistake. That the Key was too dangerous to exist whole. That something in the dark had begun to ask him for it.*" She looked at Lex. "*I did not ask what. I was afraid to know. I took the bit to the glass. Our brother Tarn took the bow beyond the Veil. Aurelian kept the blade himself, and buried it under the mountain where the door was thinnest, so he could guard it.*"

"The Hollowmaw."

"*It was not called that then. But yes.*" She inclined her frozen head. "*And then I wrote the verse. And I built this place. And I came here to keep what was, because I could see what was coming, and I could not stop it.*" Her voice didn't change. "*He fell, a hundred years later. Into the Hollow. Into the thing he had sealed there. You know this.*"

"Yes."

"*And now someone is turning his key.*"

"An old man," said Lex. "Elric Thorne. He was my father's friend. He found the bow in the Sundered Lands three years ago. He's been using it to cut people out of the Lattice." His jaw tightened. "And there's a voice in his head. A kind voice. It told him where to look."

Ysmir closed her eyes.

For a long moment, the vault was silent. The frozen memories in the walls glimmered faintly blue. Somewhere far above, in the great hall, something creaked. Ice settling.

"*Then my brother is awake*," said Ysmir. "*Or something wearing him is.*"

---

"*The book*," she said. "*Take it.*"

Lex looked at the plinth. At the open book, frozen in its block of clear ice, its pages blank.

"What is it?"

"*The Ledger of What Was.*" Ysmir moved to stand beside it, her long frozen hair trailing across the floor without a sound. "*I made it when I made the verse. Because I knew that if the Key was ever turned again, it would be turned against people. Cutting them out of the Lattice. Out of memory. Out of the world.*" She looked at Lex. "*The Key unwrites. But I am the Keeper of What Was. And what has been written cannot be entirely unwritten. Not while someone keeps it.*"

She laid her ice hand on the frozen block.

It cracked. Clean, like the shell of an egg. The ice fell away in two halves, and the book lay on the plinth, open, its pages soft and old and smelling faintly of cold.

"*Your blood*," said Ysmir. "*On the page.*"

Lex took his knife from his belt. He looked at it. Then at his palm, already crossed with old scars from a dozen blood-wards and blood-doors. Avaloria. The Frostvault. The waystation. The Root.

*[Every Founder's door in the world wants a little piece of you,]* said Ember. *[It's like being a very popular cake.]*

He almost laughed. He cut his palm. Not deep. He pressed it to the open page.

And the book drank.

He felt it. A pull, gentle, the way the Hollow had pulled at Antho, the way the Umbral Wardens had pulled at his thread. But warm. Asking, not taking. The blood soaked into the pale old paper and spread through it in thin red lines. And where the lines went, the red turned to black. And the black turned into letters.

Names.

They wrote themselves. One by one. In a hand that wasn't his, a narrow precise elegant hand, flowing across the page as if an invisible pen were moving very fast.

*Mella Bell, of Harrowgate. Sixteen. Weatherwise. She sang to the goats.*

*Tamsin Varn, of Coldbrook. Twenty-one. Farmer. He brought the cows in every evening and talked to each one by name.*

*Odo Ferris, of Stellan's Ford. Forty. Cooper. His barrels never leaked.*

*Hollis Penn, of Highcrest. Thirty-four. Baker. He gave the burnt loaves to the children by the fountain and said they were the best ones.*

Lex made a sound.

*Hollis Penn.*

It came back to him. Like a door opening. The baker in the square. The flour on his arms. His *name*. Lex had held his thread and lost it and been unable to remember his name for nearly a month, and here it was, written in a dead woman's handwriting on a page he'd bled on, and the moment he read it he *knew* it. As if it had been there the whole time, just behind a curtain.

*Hollis Penn.* He'd had three children. He'd sung in the Lower Town choir, badly. His wife's name was Ada.

*[I've got it too,]* said Ember. It sounded shaken. *[Lex. I remember him. I couldn't, before. And now I can.]*

The names kept coming. Line after line. Page after page. The book turned its own pages, fluttering in a wind that wasn't there.

Twenty names. Thirty. Forty.

*Eda Moss. Fennick Hale. Rue Talbot. Corwin Ash.*

Each one with a line. Never more than a line. *She grew the best leeks in the valley.* *He could whistle like any bird you named.* *She was learning to read.* *He was going to be married in the spring.*

Forty-seven names.

And then the pen stopped.

The book lay still. Forty-seven names, in black ink, on pages that had been blank a moment ago.

Lex stared at them.

*Forty-seven people*, he thought. *He's taken forty-seven people. And every one of them was somebody.*

---

"*It cannot bring them back*," said Ysmir quietly. "*I want you to understand that. The Ledger remembers. That is all it does. While it is kept, they are not entirely gone. Their threads are not entirely cut. They linger at the edge of the world, like the man who walks beside you.*" Her ice-blue eyes went past Lex, to the doorway, where Tamsin Varn stood faint and pale beside his sister. "*If the Ledger is destroyed, they will be gone. Truly gone. Every thread cut through. And all that is left of them will go to the one who holds the Key.*"

"That's why he's been trying to get in here."

"*Yes. He has been at my door for two years. Breaking my house around me, piece by piece, trying to reach it.*" Her voice was very calm. "*Every memory he cracks in my walls is a memory lost. He does not care. He wants the Ledger burned. Then the forty-seven are entirely his, and he is that much stronger.*"

Lex looked at the Ledger. At the names.

"How do I bring them back?"

Ysmir was silent.

"*What the Key unwrites*," she said at last, "*only the Key can write again.*"

Kestrel moved. Fast. Out of the doorway, across the vault, to stand at Lex's shoulder and stare down at the Ledger, at the names she couldn't read except one. She had found it with her finger, as if she knew by instinct which line it was. *Tamsin Varn, of Coldbrook.* Her finger rested on it.

"*The key*," she said. "*His key. It can put them back?*"

"*It can.*" Ysmir looked at her. A long look. Sad. "*Whole, it can do almost anything. That is what makes it terrible.*"

Kestrel didn't say anything.

But Lex saw her face. And he saw her hand, on the page, close very slowly into a fist over her brother's name.

---

Something boomed, far above.

The whole glacier shook. Ice dust sifted down from the vault ceiling in a fine blue glittering rain. The frozen memories in the walls trembled.

Ysmir's head came up.

"*He is here*," she said.

Lex felt it a moment later. Through the Root. Through the net. A *cut*. A clean dark slice through the Lattice, like a knife going through silk. Somewhere above them, in the great hall of the temple. And through the cut, something coming.

*[Elric,]* said Ember. *[The vault opening. The blood. It rang through the whole net like a bell. He felt it.]*

*He's been waiting for someone to open it for him.*

*[Of course he has. He can't get in. You can. So he waited.]* Ember's voice was hard. *["My blood, the smith, will find it for you." That's what the voice told him. It's what the voice always tells him. It's using you to fetch things.]*

The Ledger. Lex snatched it up off the plinth. It was heavy and cold and fit in the crook of his arm like a sleeping cat. He shoved it inside his coat, against his chest, and felt it there, cold through his shirt, forty-seven names pressed against his heart.

Footsteps. Coming down the long passage from above.

Unhurried.

"*Go*," said Ysmir. "*Blood of my brother. Take the Ledger and go.*"

"There's only one way out."

"*Then you will have to go past him.*" She turned her ice face toward the passage. "*I will hold my house up for as long as I can. He will try to bring it down on you.*"

Kestrel's hands were already burning.

The footsteps came around the last bend.

---

He didn't look seventy anymore.

That was the first thing Lex noticed. Elric Thorne had been an old man when he walked into the Ashbourne forge four years ago. Thin and grey and stooped, with a tired, clever face and a tattered cloak. He'd been an old man at Duskhold, standing in a black corridor with his staff in his hand and fury in his eyes.

The man who came around the bend into Ysmir's vault was perhaps fifty. Tall and straight, his hair iron-grey instead of white, his face lined but firm. He wore a long coat of dark grey wool, very well cut, and good boots, and gloves. And around his neck, on a black chain, the Bow.

Lex had seen it in a hundred nightmares since the festival. Seeing it now was different. It was the size of his palm, a looped handle of black that wasn't metal and wasn't stone. It was *nothing*. A hole in the world, shaped like the handle of a key. When he looked at it, his eyes slid off it. When he looked at it with True Sight, it hurt.

"Hello, Lex," said Elric.

He sounded the same. That was the worst of it. The same dry, warm, faintly amused voice that had explained the Lattice to a terrified smith's apprentice over a campfire in the Greyspine. The same voice that had taught him mana-shaping on the Old Founders' Road.

"Elric."

"You've grown." Elric's eyes moved over him. Brown eyes. Not gold. They'd never been gold. "Three years. You look like your father did at thirty, if he'd ever had to work for a living." His gaze dropped to Lex's coat, where the Ledger sat against his chest. "And you've done my errand for me. Thank you."

"It's not yours."

"No. It's Ysmir's." Elric looked past him, at the tall shape of blue ice standing by the plinth. And something moved in his face. Not fear. Something like courtesy. He inclined his head. "Lady Ysmir. I've been knocking for two years. You might have answered."

"*You knock with a crowbar, Elric Thorne*," said the ice woman. "*I do not answer to crowbars.*"

"No," Elric agreed. "Nor did Arion. In forty years." He smiled. A thin, tired smile. "The Veys and their doors. Always shut. Always someone else outside them."

"You took forty-seven people," said Lex.

"Forty-eight now. There was a shepherd on the White Shore last night." Elric's voice didn't change. "And I took them because they were owed to me. I gave forty years of my life to your father's work. To the Lattice. I mended it with my own hands, Stone by Stone, while Arion sat in Avaloria and made speeches about responsibility. And when I asked, *once*, for what I'd earned, he said no." He touched the black thing at his throat. "So I've stopped asking."

"Those people never did anything to you."

"No. They were bright. That's all. Bright and loose and ripe, because you went through Caldor with your hammer knocking the Ceilings off every Stone you could find, and you never once thought about what that would *mean*. A Ceiling is a lid, Lex. It holds things in." His brown eyes were steady. "You took the lids off forty thousand people and called it freedom, and you never asked what else could get at them once the lid was gone."

Lex felt Ember go very still in his head.

"You don't get to make that my fault."

"I'm not. I'm telling you what's true. It's a different thing." Elric held out his gloved hand. "Give me the book."

"No."

"Give me the book, and I'll stop taking the young ones. The bright ones. I'll take the old and the dying. People who were going to go soon anyway." His voice was reasonable. Patient. A teacher's voice. "You can't stop me, Lex. But you can choose who pays."

"*He's lying*," said Kestrel.

Elric looked at her. For the first time. A long, curious, faintly puzzled look.

"I don't believe we've met," he said.

"*You took my brother.*"

"I've taken a lot of people's brothers." Elric's gaze moved over her. Slowly. And Lex saw him frown. Saw him try to look at her with whatever sight the Key gave him. And find nothing. No thread. No silver. Nothing at all. "Oh," he said softly. "Oh, how *interesting*. You're not in it at all, are you?"

"*Come closer*," said Kestrel, "*and find out.*"

Elric smiled.

And moved.

---

He didn't come at Kestrel. He came at Lex. One step, and his gloved hand closed around the black Bow at his throat, and he *turned* it.

Lex felt it.

Like a hook going into his chest. Not into his body. Into his thread. The bright gold-and-silver cord that ran from the center of him out into the great net of the world. The Bow found it the way a fishhook finds a fish's lip, and caught, and *pulled*.

He went to his knees.

It didn't hurt. That was the terrible thing. It didn't hurt at all. It was like falling asleep. Like being very, very tired and lying down in warm snow. He could feel himself coming loose. Thread by thread. Strand by strand. His name going soft at the edges.

And inside him, in the Root, in the gold place where Ember lived, someone screamed.

*[LEX—]*

Ember. Ember was *in* the thread. Ember was *part* of it, a construct cut from Arion's mind and woven into the Root, and the Key was cutting the Root, and Ember was coming apart with it.

*[Lex, hold on, hold ON, you have to—]*

He couldn't. He couldn't. He was so tired.

He rooted.

Not in Flame. In Stone. The way he'd done on the moor, against the Umbral Wardens. Down through the ice, down through the glacier, down and down into the bedrock of the world under it, old and cold and patient. *Stand*. *Hold*. *Stay*.

And held.

The hook in his thread caught. Stopped. Pulled harder.

He held.

"*Stubborn*," said Elric, through his teeth. "*You were always stubborn. You and your brother both.*" He twisted the Bow. "*But I've got forty-eight people's worth of*—"

Fire hit him in the face.

Kestrel Varn came across the vault like a falling star. Both hands burning. Hair burning. Coat burning at the hem. She didn't throw her fire. She *ran into* Elric with it, both burning fists, straight into his chest, and knocked him backwards across the vault into the wall of blue ice with a crash that shook frozen memories loose from the ceiling.

The hook in Lex's chest tore free.

He gasped. Came up off his knees. Felt his thread snap back into place like a bowstring, humming.

*[I'm here,]* whispered Ember. *[I'm here, I'm here, I'm—Lex. I'm here.]* It was shaking. *[That was—]*

*I know.*

Elric was on the floor against the wall. His fine grey coat was burning. He beat at it with his gloved hands, swearing softly. His face was red and raw on one side, the iron-grey hair singed. He looked up at Kestrel, standing over him with fire roaring off her hands.

And he did something Lex didn't expect.

He tried to use the Key on her.

Lex saw him do it. Saw his hand close on the black Bow, saw him turn it, aim it at Kestrel's chest. Where her thread should have been.

Nothing happened.

There was nothing there. There was nothing to hook. Kestrel Varn had never been Kindled, never been in the net, never had a thread at all. The Key reached for her and found the same nothing the Umbral Wardens had found. And she stood there, burning, completely untouched.

"*Can't*," she said, savagely. "*Can you, old man. Can't cut what was never tied.*"

And she hit him again.

---

He went through the wall.

Not really. He went into the keyhole. A black slit opened in the ice behind him as she struck, and he went backwards into it the way a fish goes back into dark water, and her burning fist hit the blue ice where his chest had been a moment before, and the ice *steamed*.

And then his voice came through the keyhole. From very far away. Calm again.

"*Keep the book, Lex. For now.*" A pause. "*But you'll bring me the bit. You know you will. The voice says so, and the voice has never once been wrong.*"

And then, colder:

"*And in the meantime, I'm afraid I've been inside this temple for a very long time, and I know exactly where it's weakest.*"

The keyhole closed.

And the whole glacier screamed.

---

It was the cracks.

Two years of cracks. Every crack Elric had made in Ysmir's house. In the walls, the floors, the columns, the frozen memories. And now, all at once, every one of them was widening. Lex could *hear* them. A sound like a thousand bones breaking at once, running up through the whole temple from the bottom to the top.

The vault floor split. A black crack opened across it from wall to wall, and the plinth tipped and fell into it, and was gone.

"*Run*," said Ysmir.

She was standing very straight in the middle of the vault, her ice hands raised. And the ice around her was *moving*. Not cracking. Flowing. Rising up the walls in great blue ridges, pouring itself into the cracks like mortar into stone, holding. Holding the ceiling up. Holding the walls together.

And she was getting smaller.

Lex could see it. She was pouring herself into the ice. Every crack she filled took a piece of her. Her long frozen hair was already gone, flowed away into the walls. Her gown. Her hands were thinning.

"*Ysmir—*"

"*Go, child.*" She didn't look at him. "*I cannot hold it for long. I am only an echo. Echoes fade.*"

"Come with us. You can—"

"*I am the Keeper of What Was. This is what was.*" Her voice was very calm. "*Every memory in these walls is going to be lost tonight. A thousand years of lost things. I will not leave them to break alone.*" She turned her head at last, and looked at him with her ice-blue eyes. Antho's eyes. "*Keep the Ledger. Remember them. Someone must.*"

Kestrel had his arm. Pulling.

"*Come on, smith. COME ON.*"

He went.

---

They ran up through a temple that was dying around them.

The passages shook. Ice fell in sheets. The frozen memories in the walls were breaking, one after another, with tiny sounds like bells. A child's toy boat. A wedding ring. A drawing of a house with a lantern in the window. Cracking. Shattering. Falling away into nothing.

Lex ran with the Ledger against his chest and Kestrel's burning hand on his arm and Tamsin Varn's faint shape beside them, and every time a memory broke he flinched.

They came out into the great hall.

It was falling. The blue dimness overhead was full of falling ice, great slabs of it the size of houses, crashing down onto the clear floor and shattering into a thousand glittering shards. The columns were swaying. The frozen memories under the floor were cracking, a great spiderweb of black lines spreading through the deep blue ice beneath their feet.

And far ahead, the great arched doorway. Three hundred feet tall. Open to the grey morning. With a black crack running up through it from threshold to keystone.

Kestrel let go of his arm. "*I can't hold it up*," she gasped. "*Fire's no good for this. It just melts and comes down faster.*"

"I know." Lex stopped in the middle of the falling hall. He went down on one knee. He put his free hand flat on the cracking ice of the floor.

And *spoke* to Stone.

Not Flame. Not this time. Stone. The bedrock under the glacier. The deep old patient bones of the world. He didn't command it. He wasn't strong enough to command it, not yet, not at Answering. But he *asked*. The way he'd asked it on the moor. The way Malachi had taught him: not *do this*, but *help me*.

*Hold. Just for a little. Just long enough. Please.*

And far down below the glacier, the bedrock of the world stirred.

He felt it come up. Slowly. Grudgingly. Like an old man getting up from a chair. Up through the ice, up through the cracks, a great slow grinding push from underneath. The floor stopped cracking. The columns stopped swaying. The falling slabs of ice, halfway down, seemed to hang for a moment in the air, as if the whole hall had taken a breath.

"*NOW*," he shouted.

They ran.

Down the length of the great hall, under the hanging ice. Past the swaying columns. Over the cracked floor where a thousand lost things were breaking under their feet. Toward the great doorway and the grey light beyond.

Behind them, Lex felt Ysmir. Felt her the way he'd felt his mother's thread once, in the Root. Faint and cold and very calm. Pouring the last of herself into the walls of her house. Holding them up. Holding them up.

They burst out through the doorway into the grey morning. Down the long carved stair. Out onto the frozen shore.

And behind them, the Temple of Ysmir came down.

---

It took a long time. Glaciers don't fall fast.

The great carved face of the ice cracked from top to bottom, along the line of Elric's crack, and slid. Slowly. Majestically. Like a curtain coming down at the end of a play. A thousand feet of blue-white ice, columns and arches and stairs and great carved Wellscript, sliding down into the frozen sea with a roar that went on and on and on, and threw up a cloud of glittering ice-dust that hung in the grey air like smoke after a fire.

When it was over, there was no temple. Just a raw blue scar on the face of the glacier, and a mountain of broken ice on the shore, and a silence so deep it rang.

Lex stood in it.

He had the Ledger against his chest. He could feel it there, cold and heavy, forty-seven names pressed against his heart.

*Forty-eight*, he thought. *A shepherd on the White Shore last night.*

He opened the book.

There, at the bottom of the last page, in the same narrow elegant hand, very faint, as if the pen had been running dry:

*Bram Sowerby, of the White Shore. Sixty. Shepherd. He named every one of his lambs after a star.*

And under it, fainter still, so faint he could barely read it, a new line. Not a name. Written in the same hand, but shakier.

*Keep them, child. I have kept them long enough.*

And then nothing. The pen had stopped.

Lex closed the book. He held it against his chest for a long time.

"*So*," said Kestrel.
