# The Key of Shadows

The bottom of the world was gold.

Lex hadn't expected that. Eight days down the stair in the dark, with the Lattice thinning to nothing and Ember gone silent and the cold getting into his bones. He'd expected the bottom of the Umbral Rift to be the darkest place there was.

It was the brightest.

They came round the last turn of the stair on the evening of the longest night, and the dark simply *ended*. Like stepping out of a cave into noon. Gold light, warm and steady, pouring up from below. Lex threw up his arm against it. Behind him, he heard Wren gasp, and Seraphine say something that wasn't a word.

He lowered his arm.

---

It was a cavern. So big that *cavern* was the wrong word. A hollow in the bottom of the world, round and vast, a mile across, a mile high. Its walls were black rock, smooth as glass, curving up into darkness overhead.

And from every part of those walls, threads.

Silver. Thousands of them. Millions. Coming out of the black rock on every side, from the floor to the unseen roof, thin as spider-silk and bright as moonlight. All of them running inward. Toward the center of the cavern. Converging. Thickening. Twisting around each other, braiding, plaiting, the way the strands of a rope twist together. Getting brighter as they came. Silver, then silver-gold, then gold.

And at the center, hanging in the air a hundred feet above the cavern floor, they met.

A knot.

Lex had never seen anything like it. Nobody had. It was the size of a house. A great tangled ball of light, silver and gold, every thread in the world tied together in one vast, intricate, impossible knot. It pulsed. Slowly. Like a heart. And with every pulse, light ran out along the threads, out to the walls, out through the rock, out into the net that covered the whole world.

*The first knot*, he thought. *Where Aurelian tied off the Lattice. Nine hundred years ago. With his own hands.*

He felt it in the Root. All at once. Like a door bursting open. The net, which had been fading for eight days, down to four percent, two, one, nothing, came roaring back. Not thin. *Full*. Every thread in the world, running through the knot over his head. He felt them all. Millions. The whole Lattice. Humming.

And in the gold, at the bottom of him, a familiar warmth.

*[—ex? Lex! Lex, I'm back, I'm here, oh, that was horrible, I was shouting the whole way down and you couldn't—]* Ember stopped. *[Oh.]* A long silence. *[Oh, Lex. Look up.]*

He was looking up.

*[That's it*,] breathed Ember. *[That's the beginning. That's where it all starts. Every thread. Every soul. Every class and level and Ceiling in the world. All tied off right there.]* Its voice was shaking. *[Your ancestor did that. With his hands. Lex, I don't think anyone's ever seen it.]*

*Someone has*, thought Lex.

And looked down.

---

Below the knot, the cavern floor was black glass.

A disc of it. Perfectly round. Perfectly smooth. A mile across, filling the whole floor of the cavern from wall to wall. Like the door at the Hollowmaw. Like the sealed breach. But a thousand times bigger.

*The lid*.

He knew it the way he'd known the knot. It was the other half. The knot was where the net was tied off. And the lid was what the net was *holding down*. A great black disc, pinned in place by every thread in the world, sealing the bottom of the Rift.

And under it, light.

Gold light. Coming up through the black glass from below. Faint, at the edges. Brighter toward the center. So bright, at the very center of the disc, that the glass there glowed like a window with a fire behind it.

And at the center of the glow, a keyhole.

Cut into the black glass. Small. The size of a man's hand. And in it, black, long, jagged, with its looped bow sticking up out of the lid like the handle of a door—

The Key of Shadows.

And kneeling beside it, alone in the middle of a mile of black glass, with his hands resting on its bow, Elric Thorne.

---

He didn't look up when they came down the last of the stair.

He knelt in the gold light with his head bowed and his hands on the Key, and didn't move. Lex walked toward him across the black glass. It was warm under his boots. Very warm. Like the hearthstone of a forge. He could feel the light underneath, pressing up.

He stopped twenty paces away.

"Elric."

Elric lifted his head.

He looked younger. Again. Thirty, now. Maybe less. Dark-haired, without a thread of grey. His face smooth and unlined. He might have been Arion's younger brother. He might have been the young man who'd walked into the Spire forty years ago and met a lean grey-eyed Warden who never seemed to get older.

But his eyes were black.

Not dark brown. Not nearly black. *Black*. All the way through. Like the Key in his hands. Like holes cut in a painted face.

"*Lex*," he said. "*You came.*"

His voice was the same. That was the worst of it. Dry, warm, faintly amused. The voice that had explained the Lattice to a terrified smith's apprentice by a campfire in the Greyspine.

"*I knew you would*," said Elric. "*It told me. It said, he'll come. He always comes. And he'll bring a bar.*" His black eyes moved to the leather case on Lex's back. "*Is that it? The ward? The thing you made to stop me?*"

"Yes."

"*May I see it?*"

Lex didn't move.

Elric smiled. "*No. Of course not.*" He looked back down at the Key. "*It doesn't matter. You can't use it. Not now. The Key's in the Lock. It'll only take a ward when it's turned closed. And it's not going to be turned closed.*" He lifted his head again. "*It's going to be turned open. At midnight. In about an hour.*"

---

They spread out behind Lex. He felt them come. Wren on his right, with her bow in her hand and an arrow on the string. Antho on his left, with the Lantern and his staff. Kestrel beside Antho, burning low. Tamsin behind her. Seraphine with her crossbow. The twins. Oriel on her stick.

And Kell. At the back. Faint and grey.

Elric looked at all of them. One by one. His black eyes passing over each face.

They stopped on Kell.

"*Isaura*," he said. Mildly surprised. "*I left you on the stair.*"

Kell didn't answer.

"*Someone carried you*," said Elric. He tilted his head. "*Who carried you, Isaura? Nobody remembers you. I took the people who might have. I made sure.*"

"*Dorran Vask*," said Kell. Her voice was barely a breath. "*He carried me. And then he held up the Rift so they could get down. And it fell on him.*"

Elric looked at her for a long moment.

"*Vask*," he said. "*Your Kindling-Warden. The big one.*" He was quiet. "*I'm sorry. I didn't mean the Rift to fall on anyone. It's only that when I unlock things, they come apart.*" He shrugged, very slightly. "*It's the nature of keys.*"

"*It's the nature of you*," said Antho.

---

Elric's black eyes moved to Antho.

"*Antho*," he said softly. "*Still angry. I always liked that about you. Your father never let himself be angry. Not once in forty years. You've got enough for both of you.*"

"*What are you doing?*"

"*You know what I'm doing*."

"*Say it*."

Elric was quiet for a moment. Then he looked down at the Key. At the gold light pouring up around it.

"*Under this lid*," he said, "*is everything. All the power the Lattice was built to hold down. Nine hundred years of it. The wild magic of the old world, before the net. The sorcerer-kings. The Founders' power. All of it.*" His hands tightened on the Key. "*Aurelian didn't destroy it when he wove the net. He couldn't. He just put a lid on it. And then he put a Ceiling on everyone, so that no one would ever be strong enough to lift the lid.*"

"*And you're going to lift it*."

"*A crack*." Elric's voice was very calm. "*Only a crack. Enough to reach in. Enough to take what's there.*" He looked up. "*And then I'll remake the net. Not with Ceilings. Not with Veys at the top and everyone else beneath. With* everyone *the same. Everyone free. No Root. No Authority. No blood-right. No family that gets to decide who's allowed to grow and who isn't.*"

"*Except you*," said Wren.

Elric looked at her.

"*Except you*," said Wren again. "*Someone has to hold the new net. Someone has to be at the center of it. Like Aurelian was. Like Arion is.*" Her green eyes were very hard. "*Someone has to be the Root. And that's going to be you.*"

Elric didn't answer for a long moment.

"*Someone has to*," he said at last. Quietly. "*Why not me? I've earned it. I've given forty years. I've walked every road and mended every Stone. I've been second for my whole life.*" His black eyes went to Lex. "*Why shouldn't I go first? Just once?*"

---

"*Because it's lying to you*," said Lex.

Elric went still.

"The voice," said Lex. "In the Key. The one that told you where to dig. The one that told you you'd always be second." He took a step forward. "It's lying to you, Elric. It's been lying to you since the Sundered Lands."

"*You don't know what it is*."

"I do. It's in me too." Lex touched his own chest. "At the bottom of the Root. There's a door. And it's on the other side. And it calls me *good boy*, and *child*, and it hums my mother's lullaby in a voice that isn't hers." He looked at Elric's black eyes. "My mother told you. Through the door at the Hollowmaw. She said, it's not Aurelian. Not anymore. Not for eight hundred years. It's wearing his face."

"*Your mother*," said Elric, "*has been in the dark for eleven years.*"

"And you've been listening to the dark for three."

Something moved in Elric's face. Just for a moment. Like the first fine crack in a sheet of ice.

And then the voice came.

Not from the Key. Not from Elric. From *below*. From under the lid. From the gold light pouring up through the black glass, all around them. Warm and kind and gentle as a hand on your hair.

*Oh, children*.

---

Lex felt it go through all of them.

Felt Wren flinch beside him. Felt Antho go rigid. Felt Kestrel's fire gutter and Seraphine's breath catch and Oriel lift her white face. Felt Kell, at the back, make a sound like a whimper.

He'd heard it before. In the Glass Desert. At the bottom of himself. But never like this. Never *out loud*. Never coming up out of the floor under his feet, filling a cavern a mile wide, warm and gold and loving.

*Oh, children. You came all this way.*

*So many of you. So brave. So tired. I can feel how tired you are.*

*The big one on the stair. I felt him fall. I'm so sorry. He was a good man. He carried so much.*

The voice was full of grief. Real grief. Lex could hear it. It *mourned* Dorran.

*Elric*, said the voice. *Don't listen to them. They're frightened. They've been frightened all their lives. They don't understand.*

*They've never had to stand behind anyone.*

Elric's hands were shaking on the Key.

*Midnight, Elric*, said the voice. Gently. *It's nearly midnight. Turn it. Just a little. Just a crack. And then you'll see. And then they'll all see.*

*You'll never be second again.*

---

Lex ran.

He didn't think. He went across the black glass toward the Key with the Lockward's leather case in his hands, tearing it open as he ran. Not to set it. He couldn't set it. Not with the Key turned open. But to *jam* it. To get it in the Lock somehow, beside the Key, in the way, before—

Elric turned the Key.

---

It was very quiet.

That was what Lex remembered afterward. Not a roar. Not a crash. A *click*. Small. Final. Like a lock turning in a door the size of the world.

And the knot over their heads *loosened*.

He felt it. Through the Root. Through every thread. The great tangled ball of silver and gold, a hundred feet above the cavern floor, every thread in the world tied together. And one strand of it, deep in the center, the first one, the one all the others had been tied to, *slipping*. Just a little. Just an inch.

And the whole net *shuddered*.

He felt it go out. Through the walls of the Rift. Through the Sundered Lands. Through Caldor and the Sunreach and the Greyspine and the White Shore, through every Stone and every thread and every soul under the Lattice. A single great shudder. Like the shiver that runs through a horse's flank. Like the moment before a cliff falls.

And the lid *lifted*.

---

Not much. Not all of it. The black glass disc under their feet, a mile across, *tilted*. Just at the center. Just around the Key. Lifting, like the lid of a pot when the water underneath begins to boil. A crack opening. A thin line of gold light around the Lock. Then wider. A hand's width. An arm's length.

And the light came up.

Gold. Blinding. Pouring up through the crack in the lid like the sun rising from below. Like the Sunwell. Like the eye of a forge. It hit the knot overhead and the knot *blazed*, every thread in it lighting up gold, and the gold ran out along the threads, out through the walls, out into the world.

And everywhere it went, the light went *out*.

---

In Highcrest, on the longest night of the year, the Queen of Caldor was standing at the window of her tower, looking at the lanterns. Every window in the city had one. She'd ordered it. *Light a lamp for the ones who've gone to the Rift.*

She saw every lantern in the city go out. At once. All of them. And the stars go out above them. And the moon.

And then there was no light at all. Anywhere. Just black.

She stood in the dark with her hand on the cold glass of the window and felt, for the first time since she was crowned, entirely alone.

And then, somewhere below in the black city, a single small flame. In a window in the Lower Town. A candle. Lit by hand. With flint and steel.

And another. And another.

The Queen of Caldor stood at her window and watched her city light candles in the dark, one by one, by hand, and wept.

---

In Saffaran, on the mesa, Queen Ishara of the Sunreach was asleep. She woke when Zahir screamed. She sat up in the dark. Absolute dark. No moon. No stars.

She held out her hand.

Fire came. Small and gold. The Ashari fire, in her palm, the way it had come every day since the smith from Caldor knelt at the foot of her Stone.

And all over the city, on every terrace and rooftop, her people woke in the dark, and held out their hands, and the fire came.

Saffaran burned in the eternal night like a beacon on top of the world.

---

In the Wickhollow, under the Bitter Hills, a little girl with no thread was asleep in a gallery full of candles. She woke when her mother shook her. She sat up. All around her, the Unlit were standing, looking up, at the rock ceiling, as if they could see through it.

"*What is it?*" she said.

"*The sky*," said her mother. "*It's gone out.*"

The little girl looked around the gallery. At the candles. Hundreds of them. Thousands. Beeswax and tallow, in jars and tin cups and on bare rock. All still burning. Warm and yellow and ordinary.

She picked up her candle-holder. Iron, with a twist in the stem like a vine, and a little drip-dish shaped like a leaf. She lit a candle in it, from the one beside her bed.

"*Then we'll take ours up*," she said. "*So the sky can see.*"

---

In Ashbourne, Tilda Crane was in the forge when the light went out.

She'd been banking the fires for the night. She'd been the last one there. She was always the last one there; she liked the forge at night, when the hall was empty and the side-fires were glowing red and she could hear the coals tick.

Every fire in the hall went out. All twenty. At once. Even the great hearth.

She stood in the absolute dark with the tongs in her hand and her heart hammering.

*Master Lex says good morning to them*, she thought. *Every morning. Before he lights them.*

She didn't know why she thought it. She just did.

She felt her way down the long hall to the great hearth. She found it with her hands. Cold. Dead. She knelt in front of it. She put her hands on the black stone.

"*Good morning*," she said. Her voice was shaking. "*I know it's night. I know it's dark. I'm sorry. Please. Please come back.*"

And under her hands, something *stirred*.

A spark. Deep in the dead coals. Gold.

Tilda Crane had heard Flame since she was fourteen. She'd never once heard it answer.

It answered now.

---

At the Hollowmaw, Arion Vey was sitting in front of the door when the lid lifted.

He felt it. Through the Root. Every thread in the world shuddering. The knot slipping. And then the light going out, and the dark coming down, and every lantern in the Hollowmaw, one thousand one hundred and eleven, going black.

Except one.

The little clay lamp in the niche. With a chip in its spout and a thumbprint in its base. Burning. Steady. Gold.

And the door *bulged*.

Toward him. Outward. The black glass straining, cracking, the long jagged line across its face spreading, branching. And behind it, the Hollow, pressing. Not the shallows. The *deep*. Everything that had been held under the lid for nine hundred years, pushing up through every crack in the world. And this was the biggest crack. The oldest. The one his wife had been holding shut for eleven years.

*Arion*. Her voice. Through the glass. Strained. Breaking. *Arion, I can't—*

He put his hands on the door.

"*I'm here*," he said. "*Seren. I'm here.*"

*It's so heavy—*

"*I know*." He pressed his palms flat against the bulging glass. He felt the weight on the other side. The whole Hollow. Leaning. "*I know. I've got it. I've got this side.*"

*Arion*—

"*Eleven years*," said Arion Vey. "*Eleven years you've held it alone. Not tonight.*" He put his forehead against the glass. "*Not tonight, love. Tonight I'm here.*"

And behind him, in the dark of the bell-shaped cavern, a small cold hand closed on his sleeve.

Pim.

And another. Odile. And Teo, dripping. And all the students of the Lantern Keep. Coming down the long dark passage with candles in their hands. Kneeling down beside the old man in front of the door. Putting their hands on the glass.

Holding.

---

At the bottom of the world, Lex was on his knees on the tilting lid.

The light from the crack was blinding. Gold. Pouring up all around him. He couldn't see. He couldn't see anything but gold. He felt the black glass under his hands, warm, tilting, lifting. He felt the Lockward in his fist. He felt the Root in him, every thread in the world, shuddering.

And he felt the door at the bottom of the gold.

*Open*.

Not a crack now. *Open*. Wide. Like a door flung back on its hinges. And through it, the warmth. The breathing. The voice. Coming up through the Root, through Ember, through the gold at the very bottom of him.

*There you are*, said the voice. *There you are. My boy. My good boy.*

*Come home*.

---

Something came up out of the crack in the lid.

Lex saw it through the gold. A shape. Rising. Slowly. Out of the light. Tall. Taller than a man. Slender. Wrapped in light the way a man is wrapped in a cloak.

A man.

Fair-haired. Beautiful, in a tired way, like a fine blade that has been used too long. In a robe of white and gold, with a smith's leather apron over it and his sleeves rolled up and his forearms corded and scarred. With a face Lex had seen once before, through a window of ice nine hundred years deep, bending over a gold anvil with a silver knife in his hand.

With eyes of gold. Root gold. Lex's gold. Antho's. Arion's.

He rose up out of the crack in the lid until he was standing on the light, a few feet above the black glass, with the Key of Shadows at his feet and Elric Thorne kneeling beside it. And he smiled.

It was the kindest smile Lex had ever seen.

"*Hello, children*," said Aurelian Vey.

---

Nobody moved.

Nobody *could*. Lex felt it. A pressure. Not like Kell's Inspection. Not like the Key. Warmer. Softer. Like a great gentle hand pressing down on all of them. Not hurting. Just *holding*. The way you'd hold a bird you didn't want to fly away.

Wren was frozen beside him, an arrow on her string, her green eyes wide. Antho was on one knee, the Lantern in his hand, its gold light pushed down to a tiny circle around him, guttering. Kestrel's fire was out. Seraphine had dropped her crossbow. The twins had their arms around each other.

And Elric.

Elric was looking up at the shape standing on the light. His black eyes wide. His young face *open*. Like a child's. Like a man seeing the sun for the first time.

"*It's you*," he whispered. "*It's really you.*"

"*It's really me*," said Aurelian Vey gently. He looked down at Elric. "*You did so well, Elric. So very well. Three years. Three pieces. All the way down to the bottom of the world.*" He reached down and touched Elric's dark hair. Very lightly. "*Thank you.*"

Elric made a sound. Like something breaking.

"*I promised you*," said Aurelian, "*that you'd never be second again.*"

"*Yes*." Elric's voice was barely a whisper. "*Yes. You promised.*"

"*And you won't be*." Aurelian smiled. And lifted his hand from Elric's hair.

And turned. Away from Elric. Toward the others. Toward Lex, on his knees on the lid with the Lockward in his fist.

"*You'll be nothing at all*," said Aurelian Vey, kindly, over his shoulder.

---

Lex saw Elric's face.

He would see it for the rest of his life. In dreams. In the quiet moments at the forge, between strikes. The face of a man who had given everything, forty years and three years and fifty-five souls and his own name, for a voice that had promised him he'd go first.

And had just been told, gently, kindly, over its shoulder, what he really was.

*A hand*. *A key-turner*. *Second*.

"*Lex*," said Aurelian Vey.

He was standing in front of him now. On the light. Close enough to touch. Tall and fair and beautiful. Smelling of hot iron and summer and the inside of a bread oven, like the Wellworld of Flame. His gold eyes looking down at Lex with so much love that Lex felt it like a blow.

"*My boy*," said Aurelian. "*My blood. My smith.*" He knelt. On the light, in front of Lex. So their eyes were level. "*Do you know how long I've been waiting for you?*"

Lex couldn't speak.

"*Nine hundred years*," said Aurelian softly. "*Nine hundred years, at the bottom of the world. Under the lid I made. Waiting for one of my blood to be strong enough.*" His gold eyes moved over Lex's face. "*Your father almost was. Two hundred years ago. I asked him. Through the Root. Every night. Come down, Arion. Come home. Let me in.*" A small sad smile. "*He said no. He's very stubborn, your father.*"

*Two hundred years*. Lex heard it. Filed it. Somewhere very far away.

"*But you*," said Aurelian. "*You opened the door yourself. In the glass. When you touched my shadow.*" He reached out and touched Lex's chest. Over his heart. Over the Root. "*You've been carrying me inside you ever since. All the way here.*"

His fingers were warm. So warm.

"*Let me in*," said Aurelian Vey. "*All the way. And I'll take this weight off you. All of it. The keys and the doors and the promises. The names in your book. The Ceilings. The world.*" His voice was so gentle. "*I'll carry it for you. The way I carried it for nine hundred years. You can rest, Lex. You can finally rest.*"

---

The door at the bottom of the Root swung wide.

Lex felt it. Felt the warmth come up through him. Through the gold. Through Ember, who was screaming somewhere very far away, *[LEX, NO, LEX]*—

Felt it reach his chest. His heart. His eyes.

Felt himself start to *go*. Not unwritten. Not fading. *Replaced*. Like iron in the coals, going soft, going gold, losing its shape, becoming something else.

*Rest*, said the voice. *Rest, my boy. Let me carry it.*

He was so tired.

He'd been tired for months. Since the festival. Since the baker. Since he'd walked north alone and found a faded man on a moor and a dead woman in a glacier and a grieving Well in the glass. Since Dorran.

*Let me carry it*.

It would be so easy. Like walking into the pillar in the Wellworld of Flame. Like Becoming. Just let go. Just let the gold have him.

He felt his hands go slack on the Lockward.

---

And a hand closed on his sleeve.

Cold. Long-fingered. Scarred. The coldest thing at the bottom of the world.

And another hand. On his other arm. Small and slim and calloused from a bowstring. Holding. Not tight. Not afraid. Just *there*.

And through the Bloodbond, cold and quiet and deep, his brother's voice.

"*Not him*," said Antho Vey.

Aurelian turned his head.

Antho was beside Lex. On his knees on the tilting lid. Gripping Lex's sleeve with one hand and the Keeper's Lantern with the other. The Lantern's light was almost gone. A tiny gold circle, barely the size of a coin, guttering. But it was lit. And in its light, Antho's face was grey and running with sweat and absolutely, coldly furious.

"*You don't get him*," said Antho. "*I had a voice in my head for eleven years. Telling me to come into the trees. Wearing my mother's face.*" His grey eyes were locked on Aurelian's gold ones. "*It was* you. *Wasn't it. All along. On the woodpile. In the breach. It was always you.*"

Aurelian looked at him.

And for one moment, just one, the kind face *slipped*.

Lex saw it. Through the gold. Through the warmth pouring into him. Saw the beautiful tired face of the smith who had sung at his anvil flicker, like a reflection in disturbed water. And underneath it—

Nothing.

Not darkness. Not a monster. *Nothing*. An absence. A hole. A hunger with no shape, that ate light. The thing that had come up out of the ground under a plain of red earth and green grass and singing people. The thing the Sunwell had burned back down into the dark. Wearing a dead man's face like a coat.

Then the face was back. Kind. Sad. Beautiful.

"*Antho*," said Aurelian gently. "*My poor broken boy.*"

"*I'm not broken*," said Antho Vey. "*And I'm not yours.*"

And he pulled.

---

Not with his hands. Through the Bloodbond.

Lex felt it. Cold. Quiet. Deep. Shade. The absence. The silence between heartbeats. His brother's whole Well, everything Antho had, everything he'd learned holding a breach in the world for eight years and a door for three, pouring down through the thread that bound them together.

Into the gold.

Into the place where the door was open.

The way he'd quenched Lex's fire in the Wellworld of Flame. The way you put hot iron in the slack tub. Cold water, pouring into gold.

The warmth in Lex's chest *hissed*.

Steamed. Recoiled. The gold drawing back, down, away from his heart, away from his eyes. Back down through the Root. Back toward the door.

And Wren's hand on his other arm. Not pulling. Not holding him back. Just *there*. The way she'd been there in the Wellworld of Gale, when she'd let go of his hand and the sky had caught him.

*I'll come back*, he'd said. *I always come back. Because you're there.*

She was there.

He came back.

---

Lex took a breath. A great shuddering gasp, like a drowning man coming up. The gold was gone from his eyes. Down. Back behind the door. Not shut. He could feel it. Still open. Still warm. But *below*. Where it belonged.

He looked up at Aurelian Vey.

The thing in his ancestor's face was looking down at him. Still kind. Still sad.

But its gold eyes had gone very, very still.

"*Not tonight, then*," it said softly. "*That's all right. I've waited nine hundred years. I can wait a little longer.*" It smiled. "*But the lid is open, my boy. The Key is turned. And I don't need you to come home anymore.*"

It stood up. On the light. And spread its arms.

"*I can come to you*," said Aurelian Vey.

And the crack in the lid *widened*.

And from below, from the gold, from the hungry nothing under the bottom of the world, something began to rise.
