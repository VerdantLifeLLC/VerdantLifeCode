# Reborn in Light II

Wren Ashdown watched Lex go grey.

She was on her knees on the white floor of the forge at the Heart, where she'd fallen when the hunger pulled. She'd got up as far as her knees. She couldn't get any further. She didn't want to. She knelt three paces from him and watched.

He was standing at the gold anvil. Very still. With his eyes closed and his scarred hands flat on the anvil's battered face. The way she'd seen him stand a hundred times, at a hundred forges, waiting for the iron to tell him it was ready.

And he was *fading*.

Not like the Unnamed. Not like Ilse at the bottom of the Rift, going faint at the edges. From the *inside*. She could see it. A greyness, spreading out from somewhere deep in his chest. Into his face. His hands. The gold glow that had always come off his skin, ever since the night in the Ashbourne forge four years ago when the seal cracked, going dim. Like a forge-fire left too long without bellows.

Antho knelt beside him. With his fingers on Lex's sleeve, between finger and thumb. His eyes closed. His face absolutely calm. Like a man asleep. Like a man standing in a doorway.

He wasn't grey. He was holding.

And on Lex's other side, a small faint woman in a faded blue dress was standing with both her hands pressed flat against his back. Over his heart. Over the Root. Singing. Very softly. Flat on the high note.

*If the dark comes calling, love, don't you mind*.

It wasn't helping. Wren could see that. Seren could see it too. Her face was wet and set and desperate, and she kept singing anyway.

---

"*It's eating him*," said Oriel Sant.

The old blind woman was sitting on the white floor at the edge of the forge, where she'd been all along. Her black stick across her knees. Her white eyes turned toward Lex.

"*I can see it*," she said. Very quietly. "*Inside him. Everything he is. Hanging up like tools in a forge. And the gold coming up the walls, taking them. One at a time.*" Her thin dry voice cracked. "*It's taken a lot already.*"

Wren made a sound she didn't recognize.

"*Can you see what*?" said Seraphine. She was sitting with Kestrel, both of them grey-faced, holding each other up. "*Which ones?*"

Oriel tilted her head. Listening.

"*An old bald man swinging a boy onto an anvil*," she said. "*A fire in a hut leaning toward his hands. A girl in the dust of a road, saying you can hit me now.*"

Wren put her hands over her face.

"*It's going for the deep ones now*," said Oriel. "*The old ones. The brightest.*" She was quiet. "*His mother's hands. With flour on them.*"

Seren's singing broke. Just for a moment. Then went on.

---

Malachi was ringing the bell.

He'd been ringing it since Lex opened the door. Since the thing in Aurelian's face put its hand on the anvil and went down. He stood at the edge of the forge, small and brown and barefoot, with his straw hat fallen off and the little silver bell held up in front of him in his gnarled hand.

He wasn't shaking it. He didn't have to. It rang on its own. *Ting. Ting. Ting.* Louder than Wren had ever heard it. So loud it filled the whole round white forge, bounced off the broken pillars, rang out into the empty dark of the Forbidden Lands.

Flat. The clapper was crooked. Every ring was a little off.

"*He can hear it*," said Malachi.

He said it to no one. To himself. To the bell. His shifting eyes were fixed on Lex, standing grey at the anvil.

"*He can hear it*," he said again. "*I can feel him. Through the bell. Through the Heart. Down there. At the bottom of it. With the boy.*" His voice was shaking. "*He's awake. For the first time in eight hundred years, he's* awake.*"

"*Who*?" said Wren.

The old man turned his head and looked at her.

"*My brother*," said Malachi.

---

He walked to the anvil.

Wren watched him go. Across the white floor. Past the broken pillars. Past Oriel and Seraphine and Kestrel and Tamsin and the twins and Teo, all on the floor, all grey and tired and watching. Past Isaura Kell, standing very straight at the edge of the forge with her hands clenched.

He stopped beside Lex. On the far side from Antho. Beside Seren.

He looked up at Lex's face. Grey. Fading. Eyes closed.

Then he looked down at the anvil.

Gold. Battered. Scarred by a thousand thousand blows. The anvil where his brother had forged the net. Nine hundred years ago. While a little boy sat on a bucket by the bellows with a bowl of soup he wasn't eating.

"*He went down*," said Malachi. Quietly. "*Right here. He put his hand on this anvil and went down into the gold, to hold the hunger.*" His gnarled fingers touched the edge of it. "*And I sat on a bucket. And screamed his name. And didn't follow.*"

"*You were twelve*," said Seren. Very gently. Still singing, under her breath. Between the words.

"*I was twelve*." Malachi looked at her. "*And then I was twenty. And fifty. And a hundred. And nine hundred and forty-one.*" His shifting eyes were wet. "*And I never followed. Not once. I sat by a fire in the Wastes and watched the road. For eight hundred years. And never once went down it.*"

He looked at Antho. Kneeling. Eyes closed. Fingers on Lex's sleeve.

"*Deep one*," said Malachi.

Antho's eyes didn't open. But his face changed. Just slightly. As if he'd heard.

"*You're on the door*," said Malachi. "*I can feel you. Standing on the threshold. Holding it shut.*" He swallowed. "*Will you let me through?*"

---

Wren didn't understand.

Nobody did. Not at first. Seren stopped singing. Kell turned her head. Seraphine sat up.

"*Mal*," said Seren. "*What are you*—"

"*He's my blood*," said Malachi. Simply. "*Lex. Aurelian's blood. Which is mine. The seventh child of seven.*" He looked down at his own gnarled brown hands. "*I never had the Root. I was born before the net. I never Kindled. I never had a thread.*" He laid his palm flat on the gold anvil. "*But the Heart doesn't care about threads. It cares about blood. Every Vey root starts here. Every one. Including the ones that never grew.*"

He looked at Antho.

"*Let me through, deep one*," said Malachi, the Last Listener. "*Let me go down. The way he did. I've been sitting on the bucket for nine hundred years.*"

---

Antho's eyes opened.

Wren saw it. Saw the clear grey of them, very far away, as if he were looking at Malachi from the other side of a door. Which he was.

He looked at the old man for a long time.

"*If I open it*," said Antho. His voice was strange. Distant. Like a voice from the bottom of a well. "*Even a crack. It'll try to come out.*"

"*I know*."

"*I'll have to hold it*."

"*I know*." Malachi smiled. Gap-toothed. Tired. "*You're very good at holding things, deep one. You've had more practice than anyone alive.*"

Antho was silent.

"*He's my brother*," said Malachi. Very quietly. "*Please.*"

And Antho Vey, Threshold Warden, who decided who came through, closed his eyes again.

And nodded.

---

Malachi went down.

Wren watched him do it. The way Lex had told her Aurelian did it, in the vision, nine hundred years ago. The old man stood at the gold anvil with his palm flat on its face and the silver bell ringing in his other hand. And he closed his eyes. And *sank*.

Not his body. His body stayed. Small and brown and barefoot, standing at the anvil. But something went out of it. Down. Into the gold. The way a man steps into deep water. Wren felt it go. Saw the anvil's battered face *ripple*, like a pond when a stone drops in.

And then the old man's body folded up. Slowly. Like a puppet with its strings cut. And sat down on the white floor at the foot of the anvil. With its back against it. With its head tipped forward and its eyes closed.

With the silver bell in its hand. Ringing.

---

Lex was on his knees in the dark at the bottom of the hunger when his ancestor's little brother arrived.

He didn't know it was Malachi at first. He'd lost so much. The gold was eating the deep ones now. He'd lost the Stillwater. He'd lost the eternal fire, and the apples, and *Listening is hearing the Well; Answering is the Well hearing you*. He knew there'd been an old man. Small. Brown. Laughing. He could feel the shape of him in the warm hollows where the memories had been.

And then the old man was *there*.

Standing in the dark beside him. At the bottom of the gold. Small and brown and barefoot. With a silver bell in his hand, ringing.

Looking at the tired thin grey-haired man curled on the floor in the remains of a smith's apron.

"*Aurelian*," said Malachi.

---

Aurelian Vey looked up.

His dim gold eyes found the old man standing over him. And went wide. And went bright.

"*Mal*," he whispered.

"*Hello, Aurelian*."

"*Mal*. *You*—" The tired man's voice broke. "*You're old.*"

"*I'm nine hundred and forty-one*," said Malachi. "*You'd be old too.*" He looked down at his brother. Thin and grey and curled in the dark. "*You* are *old. You look terrible.*"

Aurelian laughed. Cracked. Broken. And then, without any warning at all, he was crying. Great dry gasping sobs, like a man who has forgotten how and is remembering all at once.

Malachi knelt down. On the dark floor at the bottom of the hunger. In front of his brother.

He held out the bell.

"*You never fixed the clapper*," he said.

Aurelian looked at it. Small. Silver. Ringing. Flat.

"*I was never very careful with my folding*," he whispered.

"*No*," Malachi agreed. "*You weren't.*" His shifting eyes were running with tears. "*It's been ringing for nine hundred years, Aurelian. At the bottom of the sea. Every day. I was sad every day.*"

"*I know*." Aurelian reached out a shaking hand. And touched the bell. Very lightly. With one finger. "*I heard it. I think. Sometimes. Very far away. I didn't know what it was. I thought I was imagining it.*" He looked up at his little brother. "*I'm sorry, Mal. I'm so sorry. I should have come.*"

"*You couldn't*."

"*I should have*."

---

Lex knelt beside them in the dark and couldn't speak.

Above them, the gold was still eating. He felt it. Another chain. Another. *A woman in a faded blue dress, singing, flat on the high note*. He reached for it. Felt it going. Felt it start to—

"*Smith*," said Malachi.

He looked up.

The old man was looking at him. Kneeling beside his brother. With the bell in his hand.

"*He told you*," said Malachi. "*Didn't he. That he's the flaw.*"

"*Yes*."

"*And you couldn't strike him*."

"*No*."

"*Good*." The old man smiled. "*You shouldn't. That's not what a smith does. A smith doesn't break the thing he loves to get at the flaw.*" He looked at his brother. "*He finds another way.*"

"*There isn't another way*," said Aurelian. Very quietly. "*It's folded round me, Mal. I'm the seam. If he strikes it, I come apart too.*"

"*Only because you're holding on to it*."

Aurelian stared at him.

"*You've been holding on to it for nine hundred years*," said Malachi. "*With your arms round it. In the dark. Because you went down to hold it, and you're the most stubborn man who ever lived, and you never once let go.*" His voice was very gentle. "*Even when it got into you. Even when it ate you. You held on.*" He reached out and took his brother's thin grey hands in his own gnarled brown ones. "*And it's folded itself round your arms. Round your holding. That's the seam, Aurelian. Not you. Your* holding.*"

---

Aurelian Vey looked at his brother's hands on his.

"*If I let go*," he said slowly, "*it'll have nothing to hold it. It'll be free. In the dark. Loose.*"

"*No*," said Malachi. "*It'll have me.*"

The tired man went absolutely still.

"*I'm the Last Listener*," said Malachi. "*I've got all six Wells in me. I've had them for nine hundred years. Flame and Stone and Tide and Gale and Radiance and Shade.*" He smiled. "*I'm not a cage, Aurelian. I never was. I told you that on the night of the Weaving. You can't cage a storm by becoming the bars.*"

"*Then what*?"

"*You sit with it*," said Malachi. "*In the dark. Like a fire. Like a friend. You don't hold it down.*" His shifting eyes were very soft. "*You just stay with it, so it isn't alone. It's been alone longer than anything, Aurelian. Longer than the world. That's why it's so hungry.*"

Aurelian stared at him.

"*That's what I came down to do*," said Malachi. "*I've been sitting on a bucket for nine hundred years. I've finally worked out what I'm for.*" He tightened his hands on his brother's. "*Let go. I'll hold you. You let go of it. And the smith can strike the seam.*"

"*And you*?"

The old man smiled. Gap-toothed. Ancient. Delighted.

"*I'll be with you*," said Malachi. "*Wherever you go. That's all I ever wanted, you stupid clever man. That's why you made me a bell.*"

---

Aurelian Vey let go.

Lex felt it.

Not in the dark at the bottom of the hunger. *Everywhere*. Through every part of himself. Through the forge, up above, with the gold rising up its walls. Through the closed door, where his brother stood on the threshold. Through the Heart, and the anvil, and every thread that had ever run down into it.

Nine hundred years of holding. Letting go.

Like a breath let out. Like a fist uncurling. Like a man who has carried something so long he's forgotten it's not part of him, setting it down.

And in the dark, all around them, the hunger *shuddered*.

Lex saw it. Saw the seam. For the first time. Saw where the gold had been folded round Aurelian's arms, round his holding, ring after ring, for nine hundred years. And where the arms had let go, a gap. A line. A flaw. Running down through the whole of the hunger like the crack in a dropped plate.

The place where it wasn't folded properly.

The place where a man had been holding on, and wasn't anymore.

"*Now*," said Aurelian Vey.

He was holding out the little hammer. Worn smooth. With faint gold runes on its cheeks. The first thing he'd ever made for himself.

"*Strike there*," he said. "*What was folded, unfolds.*"

---

Lex took the hammer.

It was warm. It was always warm. It had been warm in his hand at the Umbral Wardens on the moor, and at the Severance chain in Sorn's ballroom, and at the anvil at the Hollowmaw the night he forged the Lockward.

It was *singing*.

Very faintly. In his palm. A working song. The kind smiths sing at the anvil. Losing the tune.

He looked at the seam. Running through the dark.

He looked at the two old men kneeling beside it. Holding each other's hands. One small and brown and barefoot. One thin and grey and tired.

"*Go on, child*," said Aurelian. Gently. "*I'll be all right. I've got Mal.*"

"*He's got me*," said Malachi. Grinning through his tears. "*God help him.*"

Lex lifted the hammer.

And struck.

*Tink*.

---

It came apart.

He'd unmade things before. He'd watched Umbral Wardens unfold like flowers. He'd watched a Severance chain slide off his father's shoulders like a dead snake.

This was different.

The seam *opened*. All the way. Top to bottom. And the hunger, that had been folded round Aurelian Vey for nine hundred years, folded into a face and a voice and a pair of scarred hands and a kind loving smile, *unfolded*.

Lex felt it go. Ring by ring. Every fold. Every crimp. Every piece of Aurelian it had taken and shaped into itself. Coming loose. Uncurling. Like a knot pulled out of a rope. Like a fist opening.

The face went first. The fair hair and the gold eyes and the tired beautiful features of the smith who had sung at his anvil. Unfolding. Gone.

The voice. *My boy. My good boy. Come home*. Unfolding. Gone.

The hands. The apron. The kindness. The patience. The waiting.

Gone.

And what was left—

---

What was left was very small.

Lex hadn't expected that. He'd thought it would be enormous. The hunger that had eaten the Sunwell's people and the Drowned Country and every drifting piece of every world in the Forbidden Lands.

It wasn't.

It was a *dark*. A small dark. The size of a man's two hands held together. Shapeless. Faceless. Mouthless. Floating in the dark at the bottom of everything.

*Hungry*. He could feel that. Still. A hunger with no shape. No face. Nothing to hold. Nothing to wear.

*Alone*.

He could feel that too. More than the hunger. Underneath it. A loneliness so old and so deep that it had been there before the world. Before the river. Before the Wells. A dark that had always been there, at the bottom of everything, with nothing in it but itself.

*It's been alone longer than anything*, Malachi had said. *That's why it's so hungry*.

The small dark drifted. Shapeless. Searching. Reaching out, blindly, for something to hold. Something to wear. Something to fill it.

And Malachi held out his hands.

---

"*Here*," said the Last Listener.

He was kneeling in the dark. His gnarled brown hands cupped in front of him. The way you'd hold a bird. The way Seren had cupped her hands round her lamp at the door.

"*Here*," he said again. Very gently. "*Come here. It's all right. You're not alone. Not anymore.*"

The small dark hesitated.

It had no eyes. No face. But Lex felt it *turn*. Toward the old man. Toward the cupped hands. Toward the voice.

"*I'm not going to cage you*," said Malachi. "*I'm not going to hold you down. I'm just going to sit with you. In the dark. For as long as it takes.*" His shifting eyes went black. The soft black of the sky between the stars. "*I've got Shade in me, little one. The real dark. Not the thing you pretended to be. The quiet. The rest. The space between heartbeats.*" He smiled. "*You're not the dark. You just got lost in it. A long, long time ago.*"

The small dark drifted closer.

"*Come on*," said Malachi. "*I've got a fire. And my brother. And a bell.*"

And the hunger at the bottom of the world, that had eaten worlds and worn a dead man's face and sung through a choir-master and rotted through a midwife, drifted into the cupped hands of a nine-hundred-and-forty-one-year-old man who had been sad for nine hundred years.

And Malachi closed his hands around it.

Gently.

---

Aurelian put his arms around his brother.

From behind. Around his shoulders. The way you hold someone you love who's holding something precious. And laid his grey head against Malachi's.

"*Mal*," he said.

"*Mm*."

"*Where are we going*?"

"*Down*," said Malachi. "*Into the deep. The real deep. Under the Hollow. Where the Wells come from.*" He looked down at the small dark in his cupped hands. Quiet now. Still. "*It's very peaceful down there. I've heard it. Through the Wells. All my life.*" He closed his eyes. "*We'll find somewhere to sit. And I'll build a fire. And we'll keep it company.*"

"*Forever*?"

"*For a while*," said Malachi. "*Nothing's forever. You taught me that.*" He opened one eye. "*And then maybe it won't be hungry anymore. And we can stop.*"

Aurelian Vey laughed. Softly. Into his little brother's hair.

And then he looked up. At Lex. Kneeling in the dark with the little hammer in his hand.

"*Child*," he said.

His gold eyes were bright. Clear. Not dim. Not tired. *Alive*. For the first time in eight hundred years. The eyes Lex had seen through a window of ice nine hundred years deep, on a young man at a gold anvil under stars too close and too bright.

"*The net*," said Aurelian. "*It's still there. Up above. Every ring. With the crimp in it. The Ceiling. The holes where the Tithe ran through.*" He smiled. "*Nobody's pulling on them anymore. But they're still crimped.*"

"*I know*."

"*Fix it*," said Aurelian Vey. "*Please. I made it so badly. I was so afraid.*" His voice was very gentle. "*You're not afraid. Not the way I was. You're afraid for* them. *That's the right kind.*" He looked at the hammer in Lex's hand. "*Take that up to the anvil. My anvil. And strike every ring. Uncrimp them. Close the holes. Make it what I should have made.*"

"*I don't know how*."

"*Yes, you do*," said Aurelian. "*You've known since you were eleven. Since an old bald man put you on an anvil and said, this is yours now.*" He smiled. "*You just have to ask it nicely.*"

---

They went.

Lex watched them. Kneeling at the bottom of the dark with the hammer in his hand. Two old men. One small and brown and barefoot, with his hands cupped around something quiet. One tall and thin and grey, with his arms around his brother's shoulders.

Sinking. Slowly. Down. Into the deep. Into the dark below the dark. Getting smaller. Fainter. Like the Knights of the Ford walking away across the black ink.

And as they went, Lex heard Malachi start to sing.

Badly. Loudly. Something about a miller's daughter and a very large cheese.

And after a moment, Aurelian joined in.

Worse.

Losing the tune.

And the little silver bell, wherever it was, in Malachi's hand, or on the floor of the forge at the Heart, or somewhere in between, rang once more.

*Ting*.

And stopped.

---

Lex came up.

Through the dark. Through the place where the hunger had been. Up into his own forge.

And the gold was *going back*.

He saw it. Standing in the middle of the long dark hall. The gold that had flooded his forge, risen up its walls, eaten his memories chain by chain. Draining away. Back down. Through the floor. Like water through sand. And as it went—

The chains came back.

Not all at once. One at a time. Up in the rafters, where they'd been. Iron and copper and bronze and silver and gold. Reforming. Link by link. Glowing.

*Brannoc swinging him onto the anvil at eleven. This is yours now, lad*.

Back.

*Fire in a hut on the shore of the Stillwater, leaning toward his hands*.

Back.

*A girl in the dust of a road. You can hit me now*.

Back.

*A woman bending over a hearth. Flour on her hands. Singing. Flat on the high note*.

Back. All of them. Every one. Everything the hunger had taken, it had folded into itself. And when it unfolded, it let them go.

He stood in his own forge with the chains of his whole life glowing overhead, and wept.

*[Lex*.]

The coal in the hearth. Gold. Bright. Humming. Not flickering anymore.

*[Lex. You're back. You're all back. I can feel them. Every one.]* Ember's voice was shaking. *[I thought I was going to go out. I thought*—]

*I know*.

*[What happened? Down there? I couldn't see. It was all dark and then it was all* light *and then*—]

*Malachi*, thought Lex. *Malachi happened.*

He looked at the great double doors of the forge. Closed. Locked. With his brother standing on the other side of them. Holding.

*Antho*, he thought. Through the Bloodbond. *Antho. It's gone. You can open it.*

A long pause.

And then, very slowly, the great double doors of the Ashbourne forge swung open. And outside them, instead of the yard and the well and the village green, there was light. Gold and white and silver. And his brother standing in it, with a lantern in his hand and his clear grey eyes wet.

"*Come on*," said Antho. "*There's a world to fix.*"

---

Wren saw him open his eyes.

She was still on her knees on the white floor. Three paces away. She'd been there the whole time. Watching him go grey. And then watching the grey stop. And go back. Slowly. From his hands. From his face. The gold coming back into his skin. Like a forge-fire when someone pumps the bellows.

He opened his eyes.

Brown-gold. Clear. *His*.

He looked down. At the white floor at the foot of the gold anvil.

Malachi was sitting there. With his back against the anvil. His head tipped forward. His eyes closed. Small and brown and barefoot, in his old robe, with his straw hat lying on the floor beside him.

With the little silver bell in his hand.

Silent.

Wren had never heard it silent. Not once since Teo brought it up from the sea.

Seren was kneeling beside the old man. With her hand on his shoulder. Her faded face wet.

"*He's gone*," she said quietly. "*Just now. When the bell stopped.*" She looked up at Lex. "*He was smiling.*"

He was. Wren could see it. The gap-toothed grin. Faint. Peaceful. The way you smile in your sleep when you're dreaming about something good.

Lex knelt down on the white floor.

He reached out. And very gently took the silver bell out of the old man's hand.

It didn't ring.

He held it for a long time. Small and silver. With its crooked clapper.

"*It's not ringing*," said Wren. Barely a whisper. "*Why isn't it ringing?*"

Lex looked at it.

"*Because he's not sad anymore*," said Lex Vey.
