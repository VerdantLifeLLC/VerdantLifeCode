# Reborn in Light I

Inside, it was the forge.

Lex had never known that. He'd been in the Root a thousand times. Reached into it, drawn on it, gone down into it once at the bottom of the Rift and looked at the threads. But he'd never been *inside* it. Never stood in it. Never seen what it looked like from the inside of himself.

It looked like the Ashbourne forge at night.

The long low hall. The smell of coal-smoke and hot iron and leather. The great hearth at the far end, banked down to a red glow. The anvil in front of it, black and battered, with a horn worn smooth by fifty years of Brannoc's hammer. The racks of tools on the walls. Tongs and hammers and punches and swages, every one of them in its place.

And hanging from the rafters, *chains*.

Hundreds of them. Thousands. Hanging down from the dark beams overhead like the lanterns in the Hollowmaw. Each one a different metal. Iron and copper and bronze and silver and gold. Each one glowing, faintly, from within.

He reached up and touched one. Iron. Warm.

*Brannoc swinging him up onto the anvil at eleven. This is yours now, lad.*

Another. Copper.

*Wren on the roof of the Spire. The wind in her hair. Laughing.*

Another. Silver. Very old. Very thin.

*A woman bending over a hearth. Flour on her hands. Singing. Flat on the high note.*

*[They're you*,] said Ember. *[All of them. Everything you are. Every memory. Every thread. Hanging up in the forge like tools waiting to be used.]*

He turned.

Ember was sitting in the hearth.

---

It was a coal. A single coal, in the middle of the great hearth's red glow. Bigger than the others. Brighter. Gold. Humming. He'd never seen it before. Not once in four years. Only heard it.

*[Hello, Lex*,] said Ember. *[Welcome home.]*

He couldn't speak.

*[I know*,] said the coal in the hearth. *[I know. I'm smaller than you thought. Your father made me out of a piece of himself the size of an ember. I suppose I should have expected it.]* A pause. *[I've always lived here. In your hearth. Keeping the fire in.]*

"*The door*," said Lex. His voice echoed in the long dark hall. "*Where's the door?*"

The coal pulsed. Toward the end of the hall. Not the end with the hearth. The other end.

The great double doors of the forge. The ones that opened onto the yard. The ones Lex had walked out of every morning of his life since he was eleven, to fetch water from the well.

They were open.

Not wide. A crack. And through the crack, light. Gold. Warm. *Pouring*.

---

It came in like a flood.

Lex watched it. Standing in the middle of his own forge, with the chains of his whole life hanging overhead. Watched the gold come through the crack in the great double doors. Not like light. Like *honey*. Thick. Warm. Slow. Pouring across the stone floor of the forge in a spreading tide. Lapping at the legs of the benches. At the base of the anvil.

And where it touched, things went *soft*.

He saw it reach the first rack of tools by the door. Tongs. Old ones. His first pair. The gold flowed up over them, gently, the way water flows over a stone. And the tongs *melted*. Not into the gold. Into *nothing*. Gone. And with them—

He reached for it. In himself. The memory of his first tongs. Brannoc handing them to him at eleven. *Mind your fingers, lad*.

Gone.

He couldn't remember. He knew there'd been something. He could feel the shape of it. A warm smooth hollow, the size of a pair of tongs. But nothing in it.

*[It's eating*,] said Ember. Very quietly. *[It's eating you, Lex. Starting at the door.]*

"*I know*."

*[Then shut it*.]

"*Not yet*."

---

The gold came in. And in. Spreading across the floor of the forge. Climbing the walls. Reaching for the chains hanging from the rafters.

And in the middle of it, rising up out of the flood the way the Hearth had risen out of the lake of white fire, a shape.

Tall. Fair-haired. In a smith's leather apron over a robe of white and gold. With scarred forearms and gold eyes and a kind, tired, beautiful face.

It stood in the middle of the forge, ankle-deep in its own gold, and looked around. At the hearth. At the anvil. At the chains of Lex's life, glowing in the rafters.

And smiled.

"*Oh*," said the thing in Aurelian's face. "*Oh, Lex. It's lovely. It's just like his.*"

It walked to the anvil. Brannoc's anvil. Black and battered. And put its scarred hand on it.

"*He had a forge like this*," it said softly. "*Inside. When I first came in. Smaller. Older. A forge on a white mountain under stars.*" It ran its hand along the horn of the anvil. "*I lived in it for eight hundred years. I ate it one tool at a time.*" It looked up at the chains. "*Yours is bigger. Fuller. So many people. So many memories.*" Its gold eyes were very bright. "*It'll last me a long time.*"

---

"*Now*," said Lex.

He didn't say it to the thing at the anvil. He said it to the door. To the great double doors of the forge, standing open, with the gold pouring through.

To his brother.

He'd felt Antho all along. Through the Bloodbond. Through the thread that ran from him to his brother, twined around his own like a vine, dark and cold and steady. Felt his brother's fingers on his sleeve, out there, in the round white forge at the Heart. Holding. Between finger and thumb.

*When it goes through, don't let go*.

He felt Antho come *in*.

---

Not into the forge. Into the *doorway*.

Lex saw it. Saw a shape appear in the crack between the great double doors, where the gold was pouring through. Tall. Lean. Dark-haired. In a dark blue coat. With a lantern in his hand.

Antho.

Standing in the doorway of Lex's Root. Not inside. Not outside. *In* the door. On the threshold. With the gold pouring past him on either side, and his clear grey eyes very calm.

He'd had a door in him for eleven years. The Hollow, pressing through, every hour of every day. He'd held a breach for eight years. He'd counted lanterns in front of a black glass door for three. He'd stood beside his mother's door, and his father's, and watched Isaura Kell close the last one from the inside.

He knew doors.

Better than anyone alive.

"*Hello, Lex*," said Antho. In the doorway. "*Nice forge.*"

The thing at the anvil turned its head.

And for the first time, Lex saw it understand.

---

"*No*," it said.

It said it softly. Almost wonderingly. It stood at Brannoc's anvil, ankle-deep in its own gold, and looked at the man standing in the doorway.

"*No*," it said again. "*You can't. You've got no door. I left. I felt you empty. Clarity a hundred. Hollow-link severed.*"

"*I've got no door in me*," Antho agreed. "*That's right. You left. It's an empty clean house with nothing in it but me.*" He lifted the Lantern. "*Which means I can stand in a doorway without anything pulling me through.*"

The gold was still pouring past him. Through the crack. Into the forge. But slower now. Thinning.

"*You came off the anvil*," said Antho. "*The Heart. Where every thread starts. Where the Tithe runs in.*" His voice was very calm. "*You came off it and went down into my brother's door. All of you. Everything you are. Because he said he'd open it.*" He looked at the thing at the anvil. "*And you're* in *now. All the way. Off the Heart. Off the Tithe. In here.*"

The gold stopped pouring.

The last of it trickled through the crack, past Antho's boots, onto the floor of the forge.

"*And I'm closing the door*," said Antho Vey.

---

He put his hand on the great double doors.

Not pushing. Not slamming. The way Isaura Kell had closed the Hollowmaw door. The way a careful person closes a door behind them when they leave a room.

He drew them shut.

And turned the key.

---

Lex felt it through everything.

Through the Root. Through the Bloodbond. Through the Heart, outside, where his body stood at the gold anvil with his brother's fingers on his sleeve. A *click*. Small. Final. Like a lock turning in a door the size of a man.

And then *silence*.

Not inside. Inside, the thing at the anvil was screaming. Lex could hear it. Feel it. But *outside*. In the world. Through the Heart. Through every Stone and every thread.

The tithe-threads went slack.

All of them. All over the world. Every grey thread running down from every Stone into the Forbidden Lands, into the pillar of gold at the Heart. *Slack*. Like the strings of a harp when the harper takes his hands away.

Nothing was pulling.

Because the thing that had pulled was in Lex's Root. Behind a closed door. With his brother standing on the threshold.

And outside—

Lex didn't see it. Not then. He heard about it after. From Wren, and Seraphine, and Oriel, and Seren, all of them telling it a little differently, the way people tell things that are too big for one telling.

He heard that his body stood at the gold anvil in the round white forge with its eyes closed and its hands flat on the anvil, very still.

He heard that his brother knelt beside him, with his fingers on his sleeve and his own eyes closed, and his face gone absolutely calm. Like a man asleep. Like a man standing in a doorway.

He heard that a panel came up over Antho's head. In silver and gold and violet, all at once. In the middle of the Forbidden Lands, where there was no net.

He heard that Seraphine read it, sitting on the white floor where she'd fallen, with Kestrel's arm around her. And started to cry. And couldn't stop.

And he heard that all over the world, at that moment, people who had fallen sat up. And breathed. And felt something come *back*. Not all of it. Not the half the hunger had pulled. But the pulling had stopped. And the tenth that had always run down, every day, for eight hundred years, had stopped running.

For the first time since the Weaving, nobody in the world was paying the Tithe.

---

Inside, the thing at the anvil turned around.

Slowly. In Lex's forge. Ankle-deep in its own gold. And looked at him.

The kind face was gone.

Not slid off. *Dropped*. Like a mask thrown on the floor. And what stood at Brannoc's anvil in the long dark hall was the hole. The hunger. A shape cut out of the inside of Lex's own Root. Tall and narrow. With nothing inside it. With a smith's leather apron hanging on it and two scarred hands at the ends of its arms.

"*You tricked me*," it said.

Not a voice. A *pressure*. Coming from everywhere in the forge at once. From the gold on the floor. From the dark between the rafters. From inside Lex's own chest.

"*You opened the door to shut it behind me*."

"*Yes*," said Lex.

"*You've locked me in with you*."

"*Yes*."

"*Do you understand what that means*?" The pressure was enormous. Lex felt it in his bones. In his teeth. "*I'm off the Heart. I'm off the Tithe. I've got nothing to eat but what's in here.*" The hole turned. Toward the chains. Hanging from the rafters. Glowing. "*And there's so much in here, Lex.*"

And the gold on the floor *rose*.

---

It came up the walls like a tide. Fast now. Not slow and warm and patient. *Hungry*. Climbing the racks of tools. Pouring over the benches. Rising toward the rafters. Toward the chains.

Lex saw it reach the first one.

Iron. Low-hanging. Near the door.

*Brannoc swinging him up onto the anvil at eleven*.

The gold flowed over it. And it went soft. And then it was gone.

He reached for it. In himself. And there was nothing. A warm smooth hollow. The shape of a big bald bearded man, and an anvil, and the word *lad*.

*Brannoc*, he thought. *Brannoc. I know that name. Who*—

He knew. He knew Brannoc. Brannoc Holt, the smith of Ashbourne. Sixty-one. Bald. Who'd raised him. He knew all of that. Facts.

But the moment on the anvil was gone. The warmth of it. The weight of Brannoc's hands under his arms. *This is yours now, lad*.

Gone.

The gold rose. Another chain. Copper.

*The first time he heard Flame. In Malachi's hearth. Fire leaning toward his hands*.

Gone.

---

He fought.

He didn't know how. Not in here. Not inside himself. But he tried. He lit his hands. Flame. Commanding. Gold-white fire roaring up off his fingers in the long dark hall of his own Root. And threw it at the rising gold.

And the gold *ate* it.

Of course it did. It was the hunger. It had eaten the Sunwell's people and the Drowned Country and every eaten world drifting in the Forbidden Lands. It ate fire. It ate light. It ate everything.

He tried Stone. He put his hands on the floor of the forge and asked the bedrock under it to rise. And it rose, a little, slow and grudging. And the gold flowed over it and it went soft.

*[Lex*,] said Ember. From the hearth. Its voice was shaking. *[Lex, it's no good. You can't fight it in here. It's* in *you. Everything you throw at it is you. It just eats it.]*

*Then what*?

*[I don't know. I don't know.]* The coal in the hearth was flickering. *[Lex, it's getting closer to the hearth. It's going to eat me too. And then the fire goes out. And then*—]

*And then it has me*.

*[And then it has you*.]

---

The gold rose. Chain after chain.

*Wren in the dust of the south road below Highcrest. You can hit me now*.

Gone.

*The Hearth, in the Wellworld of Flame. Keep asking, smith*.

Gone.

*Dorran at the plowshare. Huh*.

Gone.

He stood in the middle of his own forge with the gold rising around him and his memories going out one by one like lanterns in a passage, and felt himself getting *smaller*. Fainter. Less. Like the thinned in the grey valley. Like the man with the rubbed-out face.

*Lex Vey*, he thought. *Lex Vey. Twenty-two. Forgeheart Sorcerer. I*—

He couldn't remember what came next.

*I make things*, he thought. *I'm a smith. I*—

The gold was at his knees.

And the hole stood at the anvil, in its apron, watching him. Patient again. Calm again. It had all the time in the world. It had waited nine hundred years.

"*It doesn't hurt*," it said. Softly. "*Ask anyone.*"

---

*Look for the flaw*.

It came from very far down.

Not from the hearth. Not from Ember. From *below*. Below the floor of the forge. Below the gold. Below everything. From the very deepest part of Lex's Root, where his own thread ran down into the gold and joined his father's, and his father's joined his grandfather's, and on and on, down and down, to the first thread of all.

The thread that ran down from this forge, through every Vey who had ever lived, to a gold anvil on a white mountain. To the Heart.

And down that thread, from the Heart, from the very bottom, something had come in with the hunger. Through the door. Riding it. The way a splinter rides into a hand on the end of a stick.

*Look for the flaw, child*.

Lex looked down.

At the gold. Rising round his knees. Thick. Warm. Endless.

And in the gold, deep, very small, *something*.

---

He went down.

He didn't know how. He just did. The way he'd gone down into the Root at the Rift, looking for Ember. He let himself sink. Into the gold. Into the hunger. Into the thing that was eating him.

It was warm. So warm. It closed over his head like the not-water of the Drowned Country. Like the grey river. Like honey. He felt it going into him. Taking. Chain after chain.

He kept going down.

Through the gold. Through the hunger. Deeper. To the bottom of it. To the place where it had come from. The place it had been folded.

And there, at the very bottom, curled up in the dark like a child asleep—

A man.

---

Small. Smaller than Lex had ever imagined. Curled on his side in the deepest dark at the bottom of the gold, with his knees drawn up and his scarred arms wrapped around them. Fair-haired. Grey now, mostly. Thin. So thin.

In the remains of a smith's leather apron. Torn. Scorched.

Holding a hammer.

Not the great one. A little one. A hand-hammer. Worn smooth. With faint gold runes on its cheeks.

Lex knew that hammer. It was hanging on his own belt. Out there. In the world. On his body, at the anvil, at the Heart.

The man lifted his head.

His eyes were gold. Root gold. But dim. So dim. Red-rimmed. Tired beyond anything Lex had ever seen. The eyes of a man who has been holding something for nine hundred years.

"*You came*," said Aurelian Vey.

His voice was rough. Cracked. It lost the tune even when it wasn't singing.

"*Oh, child. Oh, I'm sorry. I'm so sorry. I told you not to.*"

---

"*You're the flaw*," said Lex.

He didn't know how he knew. He just did. The way he knew when iron was ready. The way he'd known in the Glass Desert that the Sunwell was grieving.

Aurelian looked at him for a long time.

"*Yes*," he said at last. "*I'm the flaw.*"

He uncurled. Slowly. Painfully. Like an old man getting up from a chair. He sat up in the dark at the bottom of the hunger, with the little hammer in his lap.

"*It made itself out of me*," he said. "*Over a hundred years. Ring by ring. It took my rings, the ones I'd forged for the net, and folded them into itself. Into a shape. A face. A voice.*" He looked down at his scarred hands. "*It needed a shape. It never had one before. It was just hunger. Just mouth.*" His mouth twisted. "*So it took mine.*"

"*And you were never very careful with your folding*."

Aurelian laughed. Cracked. Broken. Startled.

"*Who told you that*?"

"*You did*." Lex knelt in the dark across from him. "*In a dream. At the Hollowmaw. And Malachi. He said you used to say it to him. When something you'd made went wrong.*"

"*Mal*." The old man's face changed. Lit up. Faintly. Like a candle in a far window. "*Mal said that? He remembers?*"

"*He's here*," said Lex. "*At the Heart. Outside. He brought his bell.*"

Aurelian Vey went absolutely still.

"*His bell*," he whispered. "*The little one. Silver. With the crooked clapper.*" His voice broke. "*I made that. When he was six. So I'd know when he was sad.*"

"*It's been ringing for nine hundred years*," said Lex. "*At the bottom of the sea. Teo got it back. It's ringing now. Out there. As loud as it can.*"

The old man put his face in his hands.

---

"*I can hear it*," said Aurelian. Through his fingers. "*I've been hearing it. Since you came to the Heart. Very faint. Through all of this.*" He gestured, weakly, at the gold above them. The hunger. Pressing down. "*I thought I was imagining it. I've imagined so many things.*"

"*You're not*."

"*No*." He lifted his face. Wet. Tired. "*No. I know that ring. It's flat. The clapper's crooked. I never fixed it.*" A sound that might have been a laugh. "*I was never very careful with my folding.*"

Above them, the gold pressed down. Lex felt it. Felt it eating. Another chain. Another. *Seraphine at the bottom of the sea. I'm not afraid anymore*. Gone. *The Undertow. I promise*. Gone.

"*It's eating you*," said Aurelian. Quietly. "*Isn't it. Up there. In your forge.*"

"*Yes*."

"*It ate me too*. *A tool at a time. For eight hundred years.*" He looked up. "*There's only me left now. This. Curled at the bottom. Holding on.*" He held up the little hammer. "*And this. The first thing I ever made for myself. Before the net. Before anything.*"

"*I've got it*," said Lex. "*Out there. On my belt. Malachi gave it to me.*"

"*I know*." Aurelian's dim gold eyes were very soft. "*It's happy with you. I can feel it. Through the hunger. Through everything. It sings when you use it.*"

---

"*How do I unmake it*?" said Lex.

The old man looked at him for a long time.

"*You strike the flaw*," he said. "*That's how you unmake anything. You know that. You've done it a dozen times. The Umbral Wardens. The Severance chain.*" He held up his own hand. Thin. Shaking. "*Find the seam where the maker was careless. Strike there. What was folded, unfolds.*"

"*And the seam is you*."

"*The seam is me*." Aurelian nodded slowly. "*It's made of my rings. Folded round me. I'm the place where it's joined. If you strike me*—"

"*It comes apart*."

"*It comes apart*." He was very calm. "*It unfolds. All of it. Everything it's eaten. Everything it's built. The Ceiling. The Tithe. The face. The voice. It all comes undone. Like a knot pulled loose.*" He looked at Lex. "*And there's nothing left of it. Just hunger. No shape. No face. No hands. Just mouth. In the dark. Where it was before I came down.*"

"*And you*?"

The old man smiled. Tired. Gentle. *Real*.

"*Child*," said Aurelian Vey. "*I've been dead for eight hundred years. I've just been too stubborn to lie down.*"

---

Lex couldn't do it.

He knelt in the dark at the bottom of the hunger, with the gold eating his memories above him, chain by chain, and looked at the tired old man curled on the floor. And couldn't do it.

*He's the flaw. Strike the flaw*.

*He's a man. He's my blood. He sang at his anvil. He made his little brother a bell.*

"*I can't*," he said.

"*I know*." Aurelian didn't seem surprised. "*I couldn't either. When I was your age. I couldn't strike anything that was mine.*" He looked down at the hammer in his lap. "*That's why I built a cage instead. Because I couldn't bear to break the thing I'd caught.*"

Above them, another chain went. Lex felt it. *His mother's floury hands*. He reached for it. Nothing. A warm hollow. The shape of a woman bending over a hearth.

*Mam*, he thought. *Mam. I can't remember your*—

"*It's getting to the deep ones now*," said Aurelian. Very quietly. "*The oldest. The brightest. It always saves those for last.*" He looked up. "*You haven't got long, child. A little while. And then it'll have all of you, and it'll walk out of here in your face. Through your brother's door. Into the world.*"

"*Antho won't let it through*."

"*Your brother will hold that door until he dies*." Aurelian's voice was very gentle. "*And then it'll walk through anyway.*"

Lex knelt in the dark and shook.

*Strike the flaw*.

*I can't*.

*Strike the flaw*.

*I can't. I can't. He's*—

And from very far away, from outside, from beyond the hunger and the gold and the forge and the closed door with his brother standing on its threshold, he heard it.

*Ting*.

*Ting*.

*Ting*.

A little silver bell. With a crooked clapper. Ringing flat.

Ringing louder than it had ever rung in nine hundred years.

And Aurelian Vey lifted his head.

And his dim gold eyes, for the first time in eight hundred years, went *bright*.
