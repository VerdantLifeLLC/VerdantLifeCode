# The Wellworld of Tide

Teo Brask had not stopped talking since they left Ashbourne.

Antho had known him for a year. In that year, Teo had said perhaps a thousand words, most of them *sorry* or *yes, Master Antho* or *I think there's a leak in the cold-room*. He was a quiet boy. A homesick boy. A boy who sat at the back of the hall and dripped.

On Vharuun's back, flying east toward the coast with the wind in his face and the sea somewhere ahead of him, Teo Brask turned into someone else.

"*—and the marshes go on for miles*," he was saying, "*miles and miles, all reeds and water and little channels, and the tide comes in twice a day and fills them all up, and then goes out again, and you can hear it, Master Antho, you can* hear *it, it sounds like breathing, like the whole marsh is breathing in and out—*"

"*Teo*," said Seraphine faintly. She was sitting behind him on the storm-mother's back, clutching a scale the size of a shield with both hands and her eyes screwed shut. "*Please. I'm begging you. Stop talking about water.*"

"*Sorry, Mistress Seraphine.*" A pause of perhaps three heartbeats. "*It's only that there's a place near my village where the channels all meet, and when the tide turns, they make a whirlpool, a little one, and if you drop a leaf in it goes round and round and round—*"

Seraphine made a small despairing noise.

Antho, sitting behind her, put his hand on her shoulder. She leaned back against him without opening her eyes.

"*I hate the sea*," she said, very quietly, so only he could hear. "*I've always hated the sea. My father drowned in it. When I was six. In a storm off the Tidewater, on a merchant ship. They never found him.*" She swallowed. "*I've never told anyone that. Not even you.*"

He didn't say anything. He tightened his hand on her shoulder.

"*And now I'm going into it*," said Seraphine. "*Into the heart of it. The heart of all the water in the world.*" She laughed, a little wildly. "*Because I'm the only one who's read about the Wellworlds. Three paragraphs and a very bad poem.*"

"You don't have to come."

"*Yes, I do*." She opened her eyes and looked up at him. Behind her spectacles, they were wet. "*Because you're going. And I'm not letting you go into the sea without me. Not after my father.*"

---

They came down on the Tidewater coast at dusk.

Antho had never seen the sea. Not properly. He'd seen it from a distance once, from the top of a hill in the east, a grey line on the horizon. That had been in the bad years, when he'd been running from the Wardens with the Murmur screaming in his head. He hadn't looked at it long.

He looked now.

It was enormous. That was the first thing. Bigger than the sky. Grey and green and silver, stretching away to the edge of the world, heaving slowly under a darkening sky, with long white lines of foam rolling in toward the shore like the creases in a blanket. And it was *loud*. A deep, slow, endless roar, rising and falling, rising and falling.

*Breathing*, he thought. *Teo was right. It sounds like breathing.*

And under the roar, with Shade, he could hear something else. The silence in the deep. The great cold dark places under the water, where no light had ever reached. Miles of it. Endless. More dark than he had ever felt in one place, more even than the Hollow.

But not hungry. That was the strange thing. The Hollow's dark was hungry. This dark was just—deep. Patient. Old. Like the dark under a sleeping person's eyelids.

He stood on the shingle at the edge of the sea with the wind in his face, and for the first time since the Wellworld of Flame, he felt the knot in his chest let go.

---

Teo walked straight into the water.

He didn't take off his boots. He didn't roll up his trousers. He walked down the shingle and into the surf, up to his knees, his waist, his chest, and stood there with the waves breaking around him and his arms spread wide and his face turned up to the darkening sky. And he laughed. A great, wild, delighted laugh that Antho had never heard from him in a year.

And the sea *answered*.

Antho felt it. The whole vast grey-green body of it, shifting. Turning. The way a sleeping dog lifts its head when it hears its master's step. The waves around Teo changed. They stopped breaking. They came in and *curled* around him, gently, like arms. Like an embrace.

"*It missed me*," Teo shouted back to them, over the roar. His round face was shining. "*Master Antho, it missed me. It says I've been gone too long. It says I've been in the mountains in a cold-room talking to fish in a bucket and it's very offended.*"

Behind Antho, on the shingle, Lex started to laugh.

"*It says that?*" said Wren.

"*Not in words*." Teo was wiping his eyes. Or the sea was; it was hard to tell. "*But that's what it means.*"

---

They chose who would go through.

Teo, because the door was his. Lex, because he carried the Heart-Coal, and because the Lockward was his to forge. Antho, because Tide was his third Well, and because he'd been to the edge of the deep before, in the Drowned Country, and come back.

And Seraphine.

"*No*," said Antho.

"*Yes*."

"*Seraphine. Your father—*"

"*I know about my father, Antho. I was there when they came to tell my mother.*" Her face was very white. Her hands were shaking. But her voice was steady. "*I've read everything there is to read about the Wellworlds. Every scrap. Every mention. Every footnote in every Founder's codex in the Spire's sealed vaults.*" She pushed her spectacles up her nose. "*Three paragraphs. And a very bad poem. About Tide.*"

"*What does it say?*"

"*The poem?*" Seraphine closed her eyes. And recited, flatly, in the voice of someone who has read something many times and disliked it more each time:

*"The sea remembers every stone*
*that ever sank beneath her foam.*
*She keeps them all, and keeps them well,*
*and if you ask, she'll let you tell*
*the story of the one you lost,*
*but you must pay her what it cost."*

She opened her eyes.

"*It rhymes 'foam' with 'stone*,'" she said. "*It's terrible.*"

"*What does it mean?*" said Lex.

"*I don't know*." Seraphine looked at Antho. "*But I've read it a hundred times. And I think it's the most important thing anyone's ever written about Tide. And I'm the only one who knows it.*" She swallowed. "*So I'm coming.*"

Antho looked at her. Shaking. White. Terrified. Standing on the shingle with the sea that had taken her father roaring in front of her.

*She's braver than me*, he thought. *She always has been*.

"*Hold my hand*," he said. "*The whole time. Don't let go.*"

"*I won't*."

---

Teo opened the door at midnight, under a moon like a silver coin.

He stood chest-deep in the sea, with the waves curling around him, and put his hands flat on the water. Not in it. *On* it. As if it were a table. And the water held them up.

He closed his eyes.

And Antho heard him *Speak*.

Not words. Not with his mouth. With his whole body. A sound like the tide coming in over the marshes, slow and steady and endless. A sound like a whirlpool where the channels meet, going round and round. A sound like a homesick boy in a cold-room in the mountains, talking to fish in a bucket because there was nothing else that understood him.

*Let us in*, said Teo Brask to the sea. *Please. I've brought friends. They need to see your heart.*

And the sea *opened*.

Not parting. Not like a curtain. *Down*. A great slow whirlpool, forming in the water in front of Teo, turning, turning, wider and wider. Silver in the moonlight. A funnel, going down into the dark. Into the deep. Into somewhere that wasn't the sea anymore.

"*There*," said Teo. He was crying again. "*There it is. Come on.*"

He stepped into the whirlpool.

And went down.

---

Antho went after him, with Seraphine's hand crushing his fingers and the Keeper's Lantern held high in the other.

The water closed over his head.

He didn't drown. He'd known he wouldn't. He'd walked under the not-water of the Drowned Country with the Lantern's gold light around him like a bubble of air. This was different. This was *water*. Real, cold, salt, heavy. It pressed on his ears and his eyes and his chest. It filled his mouth.

And he could breathe it.

Not like air. Like something thicker. Something richer. Every breath of it went into him cold and came out warm, and as it went in, he *felt* things. Not saw. Felt. Faint and far and fleeting, like the edge of a dream.

A fisherman, hauling in a net, singing.

A child, dropping a ring off a pier, crying.

A ship, going down in a storm. Men shouting. A merchant, clinging to a spar, thinking of a little girl with spectacles at home in—

Seraphine screamed.

He felt it through her hand. Through the water. A sound that went through the deep like a crack through ice. He pulled her close. Wrapped his arm around her. Held her against his chest in the cold rushing dark.

"*I felt him*," she was saying. Sobbing. Into his coat. "*I felt him, Antho, I felt him, he was thinking about me, at the end, he was thinking about—*"

*The sea remembers*, thought Antho. *Every stone that ever sank beneath her foam*.

He held her. And they went down.

---

The Wellworld of Tide was dark.

Not the dark of the Hollow. Not hungry dark. The dark of the deep sea, miles down, where the light of the sun had never reached. Cold and still and heavy, pressing on every side. Endless. In every direction. No floor. No surface. No up or down. Just water, and dark, and silence.

And in the dark, lights.

Thousands of them. Millions. Small and faint and drifting. Blue and green and silver and pale gold, like stars in a night sky. But moving. Slowly. Turning and drifting and rising and falling on currents Antho couldn't feel. Some were tiny, pinpricks. Some were the size of a fist. A few, far off, were enormous, great slow-turning globes of pale light the size of houses.

"*Memories*," said Teo.

He was floating beside them in the dark, his round face lit blue by the drifting lights. He looked entirely at home. More at home than Antho had ever seen him, anywhere.

"*They're memories*," he said. "*Everything that's ever been dropped in water. Every stone. Every ring. Every tear. Every—*" He stopped. Looked at Seraphine. His face changed. "*Every person*."

Seraphine was very still in Antho's arms.

"*The sea keeps them*," said Teo quietly. "*That's what it does. That's what Tide* is. *It's change, and it's memory. It washes everything away. And it keeps everything it washes.*" He looked out at the drifting lights. "*All of them. Forever.*"

---

Lex came through behind them. Antho felt him: a sudden blaze of gold in the dark, as if someone had opened a forge door at the bottom of the sea. The Heart-Coal, burning white in Lex's hands. Its light pushed out into the deep in a great warm circle, and the drifting memories near it *flinched*. Scattered. Like fish from a shadow.

*Put it away*, said a voice.

It came from everywhere. From the water. From the dark. From the drifting lights. Cold and slow and heavy, like the weight of a mile of sea pressing down. Not angry. Worse than angry. *Tired*.

*Put away your fire, Root-heir. This is not a place for burning.*

Lex closed his hands over the Heart-Coal. Its light went dim. Not out. Just covered, glowing faintly gold between his fingers.

And the dark *moved*.

Something came up out of it. Below them, or ahead of them, or all around them; there was no direction here. Something vast. A shape in the deep, darker than the dark, rising slowly toward the little cluster of four people floating in the cold. Antho felt it with Shade. He felt the silence around it, the enormous shape of the absence where it was.

It was the size of a mountain.

---

It didn't have a face. It didn't have a shape. It was a *current*. A great slow-turning body of colder, darker, heavier water, moving through the deep like a whale moves through the sea. And all through it, like stars in a cloud, memories. Millions of them. Drifting through its body. Glowing faint blue and green and gold.

*I am the Undertow*, said the voice. *I am the pull beneath the waves. I am what takes the drowned down, and what keeps them.*

It turned around them. Slowly. They were tiny, beside it. Like four grains of sand beside a tide.

*I know you, Root-heir*, said the Undertow. *I knew the one who made you. The one who wove the net.*

Lex didn't speak.

*He came here*, said the Undertow. *Long ago. He asked for my water, to quench the steel of his great net. And I gave it to him. And he took it, and quenched his net, and laid it over the world.* The cold voice didn't change. *And the net held everything still.*

"*Still*?" said Antho.

*Still*. The Undertow turned toward him. He felt its attention like a weight of water pressing on his chest. *I am Tide. I am change. I am the coming in and the going out. Nothing stays. Everything moves. Everything that is washed away is kept, and everything that is kept is washed into something new.* A long pause. *That is how the world was meant to be. Moving. Changing. Growing.*

*And then the one who wove the net put a lid on it. A Ceiling. On every thread. On every soul. Thus far, and no further. Grow this much, and stop. Be this, forever.* The cold voice was very heavy. *He dammed the deep rivers of the world. He made every soul in it a pond, when it was meant to be a sea.*

*And I have hated him for nine hundred years.*

---

Seraphine's hand was crushing Antho's fingers.

Lex floated in the dark with the Heart-Coal glowing between his closed hands. His face was very still.

"I broke the Ceiling," he said. "Some of it. Forty thousand people in Caldor. Four hundred thousand in the Sunreach."

*You did*, said the Undertow. *I felt it. Every one. Like ice cracking in spring.* A pause. *Four hundred and forty thousand. Out of how many? There are millions under the net, Root-heir. Millions. Still capped. Still ponds. Still still.*

"I know."

*And now you come here, as he came, to ask for my water. To quench another piece of steel. Another lock. Another bar.* The great slow body of the Undertow turned around them. Colder. Heavier. *To hold something shut. To keep something still.*

"To stop a key from turning," said Lex. "If it turns, the lid comes off. Everything under it comes out. The thing that ate the drowned city at the edge of the Hollow. The thing that's wearing my ancestor's face."

*I know what lies under the lid. I have drowned its children*. The Undertow's voice didn't change. *And I know what the lid is made of. Locks and bars and Ceilings. Stillness. The same stillness that holds back the hungry thing holds down every soul in your world.* A pause. *You cannot have one without the other, Root-heir. That is the bargain the one who wove the net made. A world held still, to keep it safe.*

*Why should I help you make it stiller?*

---

Nobody answered.

Antho felt the question settle on all of them. Like a weight of water. Cold and true.

*Why should I help you make it stiller?*

He looked at his brother. Lex had his eyes closed. His jaw was tight. Antho could feel him through the Bloodbond, turning it over and over. Looking for the answer, the way he looked for the flaw in a piece of iron. And not finding it.

Because there wasn't one. The Undertow was right. The Lockward was a bar. A lock. A thing to hold the lid shut. To keep the world still.

*Then we fail*, thought Antho. *Here, in the dark, at the bottom of the sea. Because it's right.*

And then Seraphine spoke.

---

"*The sea remembers every stone*," she said.

Her voice was shaking. She was white as paper, floating in the dark in Antho's arms, with the drowned memories drifting around her and her father somewhere among them. But she lifted her chin. And said it again. Louder.

"*The sea remembers every stone that ever sank beneath her foam.*"

The Undertow went still.

"*She keeps them all, and keeps them well*," said Seraphine. "*And if you ask, she'll let you tell the story of the one you lost. But you must pay her what it cost.*" She stopped. Swallowed. "*It's a terrible poem. It rhymes 'foam' with 'stone.' I've hated it for eight years.*"

The Undertow didn't speak.

"*It's in a codex in the Spire*," said Seraphine. "*A Founder's codex. Unsigned. In a margin, in a hand nobody's ever been able to identify. Next to a page about the forging of the Lattice.*" Her voice was steadier now. "*I always wondered who wrote it. I thought it was a scribe. Bored. Doodling.*" She looked out into the dark, at the vast slow shape turning around them. "*But it wasn't, was it. It was him. Aurelian. After he came here.*"

Silence. Cold and enormous.

"*He wrote it after you gave him your water*," said Seraphine. "*I think he was trying to say thank you. Badly.*" She took a breath. "*I think he was trying to say: I know what I took from you. I know what it cost. And I'm sorry.*"

The Undertow turned around them. Once. Slowly.

*He never said sorry*, it said.

"*No*," said Seraphine. "*He wrote a bad poem in a margin instead. And then he hid it in a codex where nobody would read it for nine hundred years.*" Her mouth twisted. "*Men are like that. Even Founders. Especially Founders.*"

---

The Undertow was silent for a very long time.

Around them, the drowned memories drifted. Blue and green and gold. A fisherman singing. A child crying over a lost ring. A merchant clinging to a spar, thinking of his daughter.

*The one you lost*, said the Undertow at last. Quietly. To Seraphine. *Your father.*

Seraphine went rigid in Antho's arms.

*He is here*, said the Undertow. *I keep him. I keep them all.* A pause. *Would you like to see him?*

Seraphine didn't answer. She couldn't. She was shaking so hard Antho could feel it through his whole body.

*You must pay what it cost*, said the Undertow. *That is the rule. That is the poem. The sea remembers. And if you ask, I will let you tell the story of the one you lost.* The cold voice was very heavy. *But you must pay me what it cost you to lose him.*

"*What did it cost?*" whispered Seraphine.

*Your fear*, said the Undertow. *Of me. Of the sea. You have carried it since you were six years old. It has kept you on the shore your whole life.* A long pause. *Give it to me. And I will show you your father.*

---

Seraphine didn't move for a long moment.

Then she let go of Antho's hand.

He almost grabbed it back. He almost said *no*. But he looked at her face. White and wet and terrified. And underneath the terror, something else. Something he'd seen in her face once before, in the Spire, years ago, when she'd walked out of a week in Kell's cells with her mind entirely her own.

*Choosing*.

He let her go.

She floated out into the dark. Away from him. Away from the gold glow of the Lantern, into the deep, where the memories drifted. Alone. Very small. A girl with spectacles and ink-stained fingers, at the bottom of the sea that had taken her father.

"*Take it*," she said.

And Antho felt the Undertow take it. Felt a great cold current pass through her, gently, the way the tide goes through a rock pool. Felt something leave her. Something old and heavy and tight, like a knot that had been tied in a little girl's chest twenty years ago and never untied.

She took a breath. A deep one. The cold water of the deep going into her, and coming out warm.

And her face changed. Opened. Like a window being thrown wide.

And out of the dark, slowly, drifting toward her on a current only the Undertow could feel, came a light.

---

Antho didn't watch.

He turned away. He pulled Teo round with him, and Lex. And floated in the dark with his back to her, and let her have it. Whatever it was. Whatever the sea showed her.

He heard her, though. He couldn't help it. Shade heard everything.

He heard her say *Papa*. Very small. Like a six-year-old.

He heard her laugh. Once. Startled.

He heard her cry.

And he heard her say, after a long time, in her own voice, her grown-up voice, the Royal Archivist of Caldor: *Thank you. Thank you. I'll remember. I promise.*

When he turned back, she was floating in the dark with her spectacles in her hand and her face wet and the light gone. And she was smiling.

"*He was singing*," she said. "*At the end. On the spar. In the storm. He was singing a song he used to sing me at bedtime.*" She laughed, wet and shaky. "*He couldn't sing either. He was terrible. He was so terrible, Antho.*"

She came back to him through the dark. Not clutching. Not shaking. Swimming. Easily. As if she'd been swimming in the deep all her life.

She took his hand.

"*I'm not afraid anymore*," she said wonderingly. "*Of the sea. I'm not afraid of it at all.*"

---

*Now*, said the Undertow. *You, Root-heir.*

Lex lifted his head.

*She paid what the poem asks*, said the Undertow. *And I have shown her what she lost. That is between her and me.* The vast slow shape turned toward him. *But you still ask for my water. To quench a bar. To keep the world still.*

"Yes."

*Then you must pay too.* A long pause. *Not a fear. Not a memory. A promise.*

Lex waited.

*When this is done*, said the Undertow, *when your bar is set and the key is stopped and the hungry thing is held, you will not stop.* Its voice was very heavy. *You will break the net. Not mend it. Not patch it. Break it. And weave a new one. One that holds back the hunger beneath without holding down the souls above.*

Lex stared into the dark.

"*I don't know how*," he said.

*Neither did the one who wove the first*, said the Undertow. *He found out.* A pause. *Promise me, Root-heir. That you will not leave the deep rivers dammed. That you will let the world move again. Every soul in it. Every one. Not forty thousand. Not four hundred thousand.* The cold voice rose, very slightly. *All of them.*

The dark was very quiet.

Antho felt it through the Bloodbond. Felt his brother turn it over. Felt how big it was. The whole net. The whole world. Millions of Ceilings. The thing Aurelian had made, nine hundred years ago, with all the Founders and all their power, to be unmade and remade by a smith from Ashbourne.

And he felt his brother decide.

"*I promise*," said Lex.

---

*Then take it*, said the Undertow.

And from somewhere in its vast slow body, a light came drifting toward them. Not a memory. Something else. A drop. A single drop of water, the size of a plum, clear as glass, glowing faintly silver. It floated through the dark toward Lex and stopped in front of him, turning slowly.

"*I don't have anything to carry it in*," said Lex.

Seraphine let go of Antho's hand. She reached into her coat. And took out a small glass bottle with a cork stopper, half full of black ink.

She looked at it. Then she pulled out the cork, and poured the ink out into the deep sea, where it bloomed in a dark cloud and drifted away.

"*I'll buy more*," she said. And held out the empty bottle.

The drop of silver water floated into it. Lex pushed the cork in.

*Go now*, said the Undertow. *The boy will take you up. He knows the way. He has always known the way.*

Its great slow body began to sink. Away from them. Down. Into the deep.

"*Wait*," said Antho.

He didn't know he was going to say it until he did. The Undertow stopped. Its attention came back to him. A weight of water on his chest.

"*The poem*," said Antho. "*The sea remembers every stone.*" He swallowed. "*Is there—a stone. In a millpond. Below the Ashbourne mill. Skipped by a boy of ten. Five times.*" His voice cracked. "*Is it here?*"

Silence.

Beside him, he felt Lex go very still.

*There are a great many stones*, said the Undertow. *In a great many millponds.*

"*I know*."

*You paid away your own memory of it. To the Ferryman. At the grey river.* It wasn't a question. *I felt it. Your memory fell into his water, and his water runs into mine, eventually. All water does.*

"*Is it here?*"

The Undertow was quiet for a long time.

*Not your memory*, it said at last. *That is his now. He keeps it. But the millpond remembers. The water remembers. The stone touched it five times, and it kept each touch.* A pause. *I cannot give you back what you paid. But I can show you what the water saw.*

Antho couldn't speak.

*It will not be yours*, said the Undertow. *It will be the millpond's. Seen from below. From the water's side.* The cold voice was almost gentle. *Do you still want it?*

"*Yes*," said Antho.

---

The light came out of the dark.

Small. Pale gold. The size of a pebble. Drifting toward him on a slow current. He held out his hand, and it came and sat in his palm, and he looked into it.

And saw.

From below. From under the water. A summer sky, gold and blue, wavering through the surface. Willows, trailing green fingers. And a shape on the bank. Two shapes. A tall thin boy, kneeling. A small one, standing, with a fist drawn back.

*Plunk*. A stone coming down into the water. Sinking past. Settling into the mud.

*Plunk*. Another.

And then. A shadow, skimming across the surface. Touching. A ring, spreading out. Lifting. Gone. Touching again. A ring. Lifting.

One. Two. Three. Four.

Five.

And sinking. Slowly. Down through the gold-green water. Turning over and over. A flat grey stone, settling into the mud of the millpond bottom beside a hundred others. And above, wavering through the surface, the two shapes on the bank. The tall one picking up the small one. Swinging him round.

The water couldn't hear. The water didn't know what they were saying. But it saw the small boy's face, wavering through the surface, lit up like the sun.

The light in Antho's palm went out.

He floated in the dark at the bottom of the sea with his hand open and empty, and his eyes stinging with salt that wasn't the sea's.

It wasn't his. The Undertow was right. It was the millpond's. Seen from below. He didn't remember being the tall boy on the bank. He didn't remember the weight of the small one in his arms.

But he'd seen it. The stone, skipping. Five times. The rings spreading. The small face, lit up.

*It happened*, he thought. *It really happened. It's not just a story Lex tells me.*

A hand closed on his shoulder. Warm. Big. Scarred with forge-burns.

"*Five*," said Lex. Hoarse. "*You see? Five.*"

"*Five*," said Antho.

And then he laughed. At the bottom of the sea, in the dark, with the drowned memories of the world drifting all around him. He laughed until he couldn't breathe, and his brother laughed with him, and Seraphine held his hand and laughed too without knowing why, and Teo floated beside them looking bewildered and delighted.

*The sea remembers*, said the Undertow, very softly, from far below. Sinking. Going. *Every stone.*

---

Teo took them up.

It was faster than coming down. A great rushing upward current, cold and dark and then less dark, the drifting memories streaming past them like sparks from a fire, and then blue, and then green, and then silver—

—and they came out of the sea on the Tidewater coast at dawn, gasping, in a whirlpool that spun them out onto the shingle and then smoothed itself flat, as if it had never been.

The others were waiting on the beach. Wren with her bow. Dorran with a fire going. Kestrel sitting on a rock beside Tamsin, watching the sea. Oriel on her mule. The twins, asleep against each other under a blanket.

Wren reached Lex first. She didn't say anything. She just put her arms around him, soaking wet as he was, and held on.

Over her shoulder, Lex held up the little glass bottle. The drop of silver water inside it caught the dawn light and glowed.

"*Two*," said Wren into his chest.

"Two."

"*Two more*."

"Two more." He looked down at her. "Stone. And Gale."

"*Gale's mine*," said Wren. She leaned back and looked up at him, and grinned, wild and fierce. "*And I'm not afraid of anything.*"

Behind them, on the shingle, Teo Brask had sat down at the edge of the surf with his knees drawn up and the waves curling around his bare feet. He was talking to the sea. Very quietly. Antho couldn't hear the words. But he could hear the shape of the silence around them, and it was a happy shape.

Seraphine came and stood beside Antho at the water's edge. She'd taken her boots off. She was standing in the surf. Up to her ankles. Letting the cold grey water wash over her feet.

"*It's lovely*," she said, sounding surprised. "*It's actually lovely. Why did nobody tell me?*"

"*Everyone told you*," said Antho.

"*Well, I didn't believe them*." She took his hand. "*I believe them now.*"

They stood together in the surf as the sun came up over the sea, and Antho thought, very clearly, the way he hadn't let himself think in years:

*I want to marry her*.

He didn't say it. Not yet. But he held her hand, and watched the sun come up, and thought it. And the thought sat in the warm empty hollow in the dark, where something else had been, and fit there. Not the same shape. But warm.

*That's not nothing*, he thought.
