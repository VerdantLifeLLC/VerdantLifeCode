# The Forgotten Chronicles

Malachi came up the mountain on foot, with five goats.

Odile saw him first. She'd been on watch at the mouth of the Hollowmaw, sitting on Hedda's old barricade with her knees drawn up and her eyes half closed, listening to the valley. And she'd come down the long passage at a run, which Odile never did, and burst into the library where Lex and Antho and Seraphine were arguing over a map, and said:

"*There's an old man coming up the path. With goats. And he's* singing. *And Master Antho, he hasn't got a thread. He hasn't got anything. When I listen to him, there's just*—" She stopped. Frowned. "*There's just this enormous quiet. Like the bottom of a well.*"

Antho was already on his feet.

---

He was halfway up the switchbacks when they came out of the cave mouth. A tiny brown figure, small and bent and wrinkled as a walnut, barefoot on the cold stone of the path, with a staff of twisted driftwood in one hand and a battered straw hat on his head. Five goats trailed behind him in a ragged line. One of them was Correspondence. The others were strangers, and one of them was eating the hem of his robe as he walked.

He was singing. Loudly. Badly. Something about a miller's daughter and a very large cheese.

He looked up when they came out onto the slope. And his face, brown and creased and ancient, split into a gap-toothed grin.

"*Deep one*!" shouted Malachi, the Last Listener. "*Smith! And the little archivist with the spectacles! And*—" He peered past them. "*Is that a* storm-mother *on your mountain? Bless me. I thought they were all dead. Oh, she's lovely. Look at her. Look at her wings.*"

Vharuun, sprawled across the slope below the cave in the spring sunshine, lifted her vast head and looked down at the tiny old man on the path. And made a sound. Low and rumbling and deep. Like recognition.

*LISTENER*, she said. Lex felt it through the rock. *I REMEMBER YOU. YOU WERE SMALL. YOU HAD A BELL.*

Malachi stopped on the path.

The grin went out of his face. Just for a moment. Like a lamp blown out.

Then it came back. Smaller.

"*I did*," he said. "*I did have a bell.*" He looked up at the storm-mother. "*You've got a long memory, old girl.*"

*I AM A THOUSAND YEARS OLD*, said Vharuun. *I HAVE A LONG EVERYTHING.*

Malachi laughed. And then he came up the rest of the path, with his goats, and walked straight past Lex, and straight past Seraphine, and stopped in front of Antho.

And put his gnarled brown hand flat on Antho's chest. Over his heart.

He stood there for a long moment. His shifting eyes, which were every color and no color, going still. Going black. The soft black of the sky between the stars.

"*Ninety-nine*," he said.

Antho stared at him.

"*Clarity*," said Malachi. "*Ninety-nine. I can hear it. There's barely a whisper left in there.*" His eyes went to Antho's face. "*The thief in the empty house. It's nearly moved out.*"

"*It went quiet*," said Antho. His voice was rough. "*At the Rift. When I pulled Lex back. It went quiet and it hasn't really come back.*"

"*Because you told it no*." Malachi's hand stayed on his chest. "*At the bottom of the world. To its face. With your brother's sleeve in your fist.*" He smiled. The gap-toothed grin, but softer. "*That's all it ever needed, deep one. Somebody to say no to its face.*"

And then the old man did something Lex had never seen him do.

He put his arms around Antho. And held on.

---

"*Why*?" said Lex, later. In the library. With Malachi sitting cross-legged on the table, peeling an apple with a little bone knife, and the goats being chased round the slope outside by Ashka. "*You haven't left the Stillwater in four hundred years. You said so.*"

"*Four hundred and twelve*," said Malachi. "*Not that anyone counts.*" He held up a long curl of apple peel and examined it. "*Because the fire went out, smith.*"

Lex waited.

"*My fire*," said Malachi. "*The eternal one. In my hearth. It's been burning nine hundred years. It went out on the longest night. When the lid lifted.*" He bit into the apple. "*I relit it. I told you that, in my letter. It was very upsetting for the goats.*"

"*And*?"

"*And three nights later, it went out again*." Malachi chewed. Swallowed. "*And I relit it. And the next night. And the next.*" His shifting eyes were very still. "*Every night since the longest night. It goes out at midnight. Every night. Like something's blowing on it.*"

Nobody said anything.

"*It's a watch-fire*," said Malachi. "*Did you know that? That's what it's for. That's why it's eternal. My sister lit it. Ysmir. The year after the Weaving. She put me in the Stillwater with a fire and said, Malachi, watch the road.*" He set down the apple. "*The road to the Heart. Through the Wastes. Through the white. To the place where the first thread goes down.*"

"*The Heart*," said Lex. Very quietly.

Malachi looked at him.

"*You've heard that word*," he said. "*Haven't you. Recently.*"

"*In a dream*." Lex swallowed. "*From someone who said he was what's left of Aurelian.*"

The old man went very still.

---

"*Tell me*," said Malachi. "*Exactly. Every word.*"

Lex told him. The forge on the white mountain. The stars. The man at the gold anvil, singing badly. *Oh, no. Not you. Not one of mine*. The tomb. The Heart. *It's eating me, a little at a time*. *Look for the flaw. Everything that's made has one. Even it. Even me. I was never very careful with my folding.*

Malachi listened. He didn't interrupt. He sat on the library table with his gnarled hands folded on his knees and his shifting eyes fixed on Lex's face, and didn't move at all.

When Lex finished, the old man was quiet for a long time.

"*He said that*," said Malachi at last. "*I was never very careful with my folding.*"

"*Yes*."

Malachi closed his eyes.

"*He used to say that*," he said. Barely audible. "*When I was small. When something he'd made went wrong. A blade that cracked. A pot that leaked. He'd hold it up and look at it and laugh and say, well, Mal, I was never very careful with my folding.*" His voice cracked. "*Nobody else ever heard him say it. Nobody. It was a thing he said to me.*"

The library was very quiet.

"*Could the other one*—" said Antho. "*The thing that wears him. Could it know that? Could it have taken it from him?*"

"*Yes*," said Malachi. Without opening his eyes. "*Of course. It's had eight hundred years. It's had every memory he ever had.*" He was silent. "*It could have taken it. It could be using it. To make you trust it.*"

"*Then*—"

"*But it wouldn't*," said Malachi. He opened his eyes. They were wet. "*Because it doesn't understand why it matters. It's a hunger. It doesn't care about bells and folding and little brothers. It's never once in eight hundred years used anything like that.*" He looked at Lex. "*It uses the big things. Mothers. Fear. Pride. Being second. It doesn't know the small things are the ones that count.*"

---

"*I have to tell you something*," said Malachi. "*All of you. I should have told you in the Stillwater. I didn't. I was a coward.*"

He'd climbed down off the table. He was standing at the window of the library, looking out at the slope where Ashka was still chasing goats, with his back to them. Small and bent and brown in the spring light.

Lex looked at Antho. Antho looked at Seraphine. Seraphine had her notebook open on her knee and her pen poised and had stopped writing.

"*The Tithe*," said Malachi.

Nobody moved.

"*You found it*," he said, without turning. "*At the Stone in that little village. Fen Hollow. I felt you find it. Through the river.*" A pause. "*A grey thread. Running down. A tenth of every soul.*"

"*Yes*," said Lex.

"*I knew*," said Malachi. "*I've always known.*"

---

He told it standing at the window. Not looking at them.

"*There were seven of us*," he said. "*You know that. Aurelian, the eldest. Ysmir. Tarn. Oriane. Haldis. Brannagh. And me, the baby. Mal.*" He was quiet. "*We wove the net together. The Weaving. Nine hundred years ago. To cage the hunger. To stop the sorcerer-kings. To make the world safe.*"

"*I know*," said Lex. "*You told us. In the Stillwater.*"

"*I told you what I wanted you to know*." Malachi's voice was flat. "*I told you I begged him not to put himself into the net. That I stood beside him on the night of the Weaving and said you can't cage a storm by becoming the bars. That was true.*" He was silent. "*I didn't tell you the rest.*"

"*The Tithe was his idea*. *The Ceiling was his idea. He brought them to us after the Weaving. A year after. When he'd been down at the root of the net, holding the hunger, for a whole year.*" Malachi's gnarled hands were clenched on the windowsill. "*He came up, and he was different. Tired. Thin. His eyes were wrong. Too bright. And he said: the net isn't strong enough. The hunger's too big. We need two more things. A Ceiling, on every thread, so nobody ever grows strong enough to lift the lid. And a tithe. A tenth of every soul's mana, running down through every Stone, to feed the binding. To keep it strong.*"

"*And you agreed*," said Antho quietly.

"*We agreed*." Malachi's voice cracked. "*He was Aurelian. He was the eldest, the cleverest, the one who was always right. And we were frightened. The Sundering had nearly ended the world. We'd buried half our friends. We'd do anything.*" He was quiet for a long moment. "*So we agreed. All six of us. We put the Ceiling on the world. And we cut the Stones from the bones of the mountains, and ran the Tithe-threads down through every one of them. To the bottom. To him.*"

---

"*Ysmir found out*," said Malachi. "*A hundred years later. She was the Keeper of What Was. She remembered everything. And she noticed that the binding wasn't getting stronger. Not with all that mana running down into it. Not with a tenth of every soul in the world.*" His voice was very low. "*She went down and looked. As far as she could go. And she came back up and found me, and her face was white, and she said: Mal. It's not the binding. It's not going into the binding.*"

"*It's going into him*."

The library was absolutely silent.

"*The hunger had him by then*," said Malachi. "*All the way. A hundred years at the root of the net, holding it, and it had got into him through the bars the way I'd always said it would. And it was using him. His voice. His face. His place at the root of the net.*" He bowed his head. "*The Tithe was never for the binding. It was for the hunger. It had told him to ask for it. And he'd asked. And we'd said yes.*"

"*Then why didn't you stop it*?" said Seraphine. Her voice was shaking. "*If you knew. A hundred years after. Why didn't you cut the threads? Why didn't you*—"

Malachi turned around.

His face was wet. Ancient and brown and creased, and running with tears.

"*Because he was still in there*," said Malachi. "*My brother. Somewhere. Under it. Holding on.*" His voice broke. "*Ysmir felt him. When she went down. Very faint. Very deep. Still Aurelian. Still holding the hunger, even with it inside him. Still trying.*" He drew a long shaking breath. "*And the Tithe was the only thing keeping him alive. If we cut it, the hunger would have nothing to eat. Nothing but him. It would have eaten him all the way down, in a year, in a month. And then it would have been loose. With nothing holding it. Not even him.*"

"*So you let it feed*," said Antho.

"*So we let it feed*." Malachi looked at him. "*A tenth of every soul in the world. For eight hundred years. To keep my brother alive at the bottom of the dark, a little bit, enough to hold on.*" He was silent. "*Ysmir went to her temple and froze herself in the ice to remember everything, because she couldn't bear to live with it. Tarn went beyond the Veil and never came back. Oriane and Haldis and Brannagh grew old and died and took it with them. And I went to the Stillwater, and lit a fire, and watched the road. For eight hundred years.*"

He looked at all of them. One by one.

"*That's my confession*," said Malachi, the Last Listener. "*I let the world pay for my brother. Every soul. Every day. For eight hundred years. And I'd have kept letting it, forever, if you hadn't found the thread.*"

---

Nobody said anything for a very long time.

Lex sat in the library of the Lantern Keep and looked at the old man by the window. Small. Bent. Brown. Crying.

*A tenth of every soul*. *For eight hundred years.*

*To keep his brother alive*.

He thought about Kestrel, giving a bloody rag to a man with a key, for Tamsin. He thought about Ivo, sitting up every night to watch his sister fade. He thought about his mother, turning back across a grey river for a boy at a breach.

He thought about himself. In the Glass Desert. Throwing the Bit across a crater, for Ilse.

*You don't trade people for metal*, Ember had said. *That's the first thing a smith ever learns.*

*But what about people for people*?

*[I don't know*,] said Ember, very quietly. *[I don't think anyone knows. I think that's the question nobody can answer.]*

"*Mal*," said Antho.

The old man looked at him.

"*I'm not going to forgive you*," said Antho. Very gently. "*That's not mine to do. It's theirs. Everyone who paid.*" He stood up. Crossed the library. Stood in front of the old man by the window. "*But I understand you. I'd hate you less if I didn't.*"

Malachi stared up at him.

"*That's what Ilse said*," said Antho. "*About Kestrel. On the night of the Blade.*" He put his scarred hand on the old man's shoulder. "*It's the best any of us can do.*"

---

"*There's more*," said Malachi.

He'd sat down again, on the library table, cross-legged. Seraphine had made tea. He held the cup in both hands and looked into it.

"*The fire going out*," he said. "*Every night at midnight.*" He looked up. "*It's not something blowing on it. It's something drawing on it. Drawing the heat out. Down. Through the Wastes. Down the road to the Heart.*"

"*The Tithe*," said Lex.

"*The Tithe runs both ways now*." Malachi's shifting eyes were very dark. "*Since the longest night. Since the lid was barred. He can't come up through the middle anymore. So he's pulling.* Harder. *Through every thread. Every Stone. Drawing everything he can down to him.*" He set the cup down. "*And when he's pulled enough. When the pipes are wide enough.*"

"*He'll come up through them*."

"*Yes*." Malachi looked at Lex. "*Not him. Not yet. He's too big. Too hurt, from the lid. But things. Pieces. The things he's been keeping at the bottom of the world for eight hundred years.*" He was quiet. "*The ones nobody remembers.*"

*Kell*, thought Lex.

He didn't know why. It just came. A faceless woman in torn white robes, wrapped in a tendril of dark, going down through a crack in the lid.

*She's mine*, the voice had said. *Nobody remembers her*.

---

The raven came at dusk.

Not Quill. Not a Keeper's raven. A small black ragged thing, wet and exhausted, that came down out of the darkening sky and crashed into the window of the library and fell, stunned, onto the sill outside.

Seraphine got to it first. She opened the window and lifted it in, very gently, and it lay in her hands panting, with its eyes half closed.

There was a message tied to its leg. A scrap of paper, folded small. Written in pencil, in a child's careful hand, pressed so hard the lead had torn the paper in places.

Seraphine read it. Her face went white.

She handed it to Lex.

*Master Lex—*

*It's Corin Bell. From Harrowgate. You remember. The boy with the sister.*

*Mella's back. You brought her back. At the end of the winter. She just came home one night and sat down at the table like she'd never been gone and nobody remembered she'd been gone except me. Everyone just said oh, Mella, there you are. Like she'd been at the market.*

*But something's wrong with her.*

*She gets up at night. Every night. And walks out to the Stone. In her nightdress. With her eyes open. And stands there. With her hand on it.*

*And last night the Stone went black.*

*Not dark. Black. Like a hole. And something came up out of it. Out of the Stone. Grey. Like mist. Like a person made of mist. With no face.*

*It stood next to Mella. And she wasn't afraid. She took its hand.*

*And it said her name. In a voice like singing.*

*I'm frightened, Master Lex. Please come.*

*—Corin Bell (12)*

---

Lex read it twice.

Then he handed it to Antho. And Antho read it. And handed it to Malachi.

The old man read it slowly. His lips moving. When he got to the end, he folded it very carefully, along its creases, and set it down on the table.

"*The ones nobody remembers*," he said. Very quietly. "*Except the ones who were unwritten. And came back.*" He looked up. "*They were down there. Smith. All sixty of them. For weeks. For months. Cut out of the net. At the edge of the dark. Where he was.*"

"*Elric wrote them back*," said Lex. "*At the Rift. All of them.*"

"*He wrote their threads back*." Malachi's eyes were very dark. "*He couldn't write back where they'd been. What they'd heard. Who'd been singing to them, at the edge of the dark, all that time.*"

Lex thought about Ilse. Thirty-six percent gone. Pulled. *Like someone very far away, slowly reeling in a line.*

He thought about Hollis Penn. Sitting at his kitchen table in the Lower Town. Eating porridge.

He thought about Mella Bell. Sixteen. Weatherwise. Who sang to the goats. Walking out to the Stone at Harrowgate every night in her nightdress with her eyes open.

*A voice like singing*.

"*The Cantor*," said Malachi.

Everyone looked at him.

"*I don't know*," said the old man. "*It's a word. It came to me. Out of the river.*" He looked at the folded paper. "*Something that sings, and that people follow. Something very old. That he's had at the bottom of the world for a very long time.*"

---

Antho was already standing up.

"*Harrowgate*," he said. "*Tonight. Ashka can be there by morning.*"

"*Both of us*," said Lex.

"*All of us*," said Wren, from the doorway. Nobody had heard her come. She had her bow on her back and her boots on, and her face was very set. "*I'm not getting left in a library while you two fly off to fight a singing ghost in the middle of the night.*"

"*Not all of us*," said Malachi.

They looked at him. The old man was still sitting on the table. Cross-legged. With his cup of tea going cold in his hands.

"*Go to the girl*," he said. "*To Harrowgate. All of you. Go and see what's come up out of the Stone.*" He looked at the window. At the darkening sky over the Greyspine. "*But that's one Stone. One girl. One raven, that found its way here.*"

"*What do you mean*?"

Malachi looked at Lex.

"*Four thousand Stones in Caldor*," he said. "*Thousands more in the Sunreach. In the north. In the east.*" His voice was very quiet. "*And sixty people who came back from the edge of the dark.*" He set down his cup. "*How many ravens do you think didn't find their way?*"

Lex didn't answer.

*[Lex*,] said Ember. *[Lex. Sixty. Hollis Penn. Bram Sowerby. Amira bint Hassa. Tarek al-Nur. Odo Ferris. Garret Shale.]* A pause. *[Ilse.]*

He turned.

Ivo was standing in the passage behind Wren. Lex hadn't seen him either. His face was perfectly white.

"*Ilse*," said Ivo Marrow. "*She's been getting up at night. For a week. I thought she was just going to the privy.*"

He swallowed.

"*She was gone for an hour last night*," he said. "*I followed her. She was at the Stone at Hollow's Foot. Down in the valley. In the dark. With her hand on it.*" His voice cracked. "*Humming.*"
