# Allies and Adversaries

The faceless woman pushed, and the door groaned.

Lex felt it. Through the Root. Through the black glass. A pressure. Not like the night of the Blade, when the whole Hollow had leaned on it. Smaller. Narrower. One hand. One will. But *cold*. Precise. A pressure that knew exactly where the door was weakest, the way a High Warden had once known exactly where every Ceiling in Caldor was set.

The crack across the glass widened. A hair. A hair more.

And Arion didn't move.

He was still on his knees in front of the door with his hands flat on the glass and his face pressed against it. Palm to palm with the grey hand on the other side. His lips moving.

"*Seren*," he was whispering. "*Seren*."

"*Father*." Antho was beside him. The Lantern in his hand. "*Father, get up. It's not her. It's not her. Look at me.*"

Arion didn't look.

And the faceless woman on the other side of the glass tilted her head. Very slightly. As if listening to something far away. And her thin dry voice came through the crack.

"*He's been saying that name for three days*," she said. "*Every night. To the glass. I could hear him. Through the dark.*" A pause. "*I don't know who it belongs to. But he says it so beautifully.*"

The grey hand pressed harder.

"*I'd like to have a name someone said like that*," said the Unnamed Warden.

---

Antho put the Lantern against the glass.

Not held up. *Against*. Pressed flat to the black surface, right beside their father's hands, with its shutter wide and its twelve Hush-runes blazing white. The gold light poured into the crack.

And on the other side, the faceless woman *flinched*.

Not much. A little. Her grey hand jerked back from the glass an inch. Then pressed again.

"*That won't stop me*," she said. "*I'm not a voice, Anthoren Vey. I'm a hand.*"

"*I know*," said Antho. "*But it'll stop him.*"

He meant their father. Lex understood a heartbeat later. The gold light had poured over Arion too, kneeling against the glass. Over his face. Over his eyes. *Voices can't act through you while it's lit*. Whatever had been pouring through that crack into their father for three days, whatever had made him hear his wife in a stranger's hand, cut off.

Arion blinked.

He looked at the hand on the other side of the glass. Grey. Blurred at the edges. Long-fingered.

Not Seren's.

He made a sound Lex would never forget. Like something tearing. And took his hands off the door.

---

Lex took his place.

He didn't think about it. He went down on his knees in front of the black glass where his father had been, and put his hands flat on it, and *held*. With Stone. The way he'd held the Rift wall with Dorran. The way he'd held the floor of Ysmir's temple while the ice came down. *Please. Hold. Just a little longer*.

The mountain answered. Slow. Grudging. Old. But it answered. The crack stopped widening.

On the other side, the faceless woman pressed. He felt her. Cold. Narrow. Precise. Searching for the weak place.

"*Kell*," he said.

The pressure flickered.

Just for a moment. Like a candle in a draft. The grey hand on the other side of the glass went still.

"*What*," said the Unnamed Warden. Very quietly. "*What did you call me?*"

Lex opened his mouth.

And the pressure came back. Harder. Colder. The grey hand slamming against the glass.

"*Don't*," she said. "*Don't do that. Don't say things to me. He told me you would. He told me you'd say things.*" Her voice was very thin. "*He said it would hurt.*"

"*Does it*?"

The faceless woman didn't answer.

But after a long moment, the grey hand came off the glass. Slowly. And the shape behind the door drew back. Into the dark. Fading.

"*I'll be here*," said the Unnamed Warden. From very far away. "*He says the door is mine now. I'll be here every night.*"

And she was gone.

---

They sat on the black floor of the cavern for a long time after. Arion with his back against the wall, his face grey, his hands shaking in his lap. Antho beside him with the Lantern. Lex in front of the door. Pim, who'd come down the passage at some point without anyone noticing, sitting beside Arion with his small cold hand on the old man's sleeve.

"*I thought it was her*," said Arion at last. Barely a whisper. "*For three days. I thought she'd come back.*"

"*I know*," said Antho.

"*I sat here and talked to it*." His voice cracked. "*I told it things. Things I've never told anyone. About the Spire. About the night we met. About*—" He stopped. "*And it listened. And it said nothing. And I thought she was just tired.*"

Nobody said anything.

"*It said*," said Arion, "*it said it would like to have a name someone said like that.*" He put his face in his hands. "*I was saying her name to someone who doesn't have one.*"

Lex looked at the door. At the crack across its face. At the little clay lamp in its niche, burning straight up again. Not leaning.

*Kell*, he thought. *Isaura Kell. Fifty-eight. High Warden. She was carried once.*

*She flinched*.

---

Ilse came up from the valley at dawn.

Wren brought her. Half carrying her. Up the long switchbacks from the Stone at Hollow's Foot, with Ilse's arm over her shoulders and Ilse's feet stumbling on the stones. Ilse wasn't crying. She hadn't cried since the Stone. She walked with her eyes open and her face white and blank, like a sleepwalker, like Mella Bell on her way to the green.

She stopped at the mouth of the cave. She looked at Lex.

"*He made it look like nothing*," she said.

Lex didn't understand.

"*His wrist*," said Ilse Marrow. "*Where I was holding on. He made it look like nothing. The Lampshade. Our trick. The first one we ever learned.*" Her voice was very flat. "*And my hands believed him. And let go.*"

"*Ilse*—"

"*He said one of us has to stay*." She looked down at her hands. Open. Empty. "*He always says things like that. He's always the one who says the sensible thing.*" Her voice cracked, just once. "*And I always ignore him. And this time I didn't get to.*"

She looked up.

"*Where did it take him*?" she said. "*The Knight. Down the Stone. Where does it go?*"

Lex looked at Malachi. The old man had come up the mountain in the night on Vharuun's back, with Seraphine and Kestrel and Tamsin. He was standing in the mouth of the cave with his straw hat in his hands.

"*Down*," said Malachi quietly. "*Down the tithe. To the bottom.*" He looked at Ilse. "*To the Heart.*"

"*Then I'm going to the Heart*," said Ilse Marrow.

---

The Queen of Caldor came at noon.

On the storm-mother's back. Vharuun had gone in the night: Antho had asked her, and she'd gone without a word, a vast storm-blue shadow lifting off the slope and turning west toward Highcrest. And she came back at noon with three people clinging to her scales. Elowen Dunmore, in black armor, with Oathkeeper on her back. Daxian Roole, grey-faced, with his hatchet face set like stone.

And Lysandra.

In a plain grey riding cloak, with her dark hair coming down and her face white with exhaustion. She slid down off the storm-mother's shoulder onto the slope below the Hollowmaw and nearly fell, and Elowen caught her.

"*They're singing*," said the Queen of Caldor. Before anyone could speak. "*In Highcrest. Half the city. They're in the square around the Great Stone. Thousands of them. With their hands on it. Singing.*"

She looked up at Lex. Her dark eyes were very bright.

"*And there's a man*," she said. "*Standing on top of the Stone. A tall thin man in a long dark robe. With a wide mouth. Conducting them.*" She swallowed. "*I watched him from the castle wall. All night. He doesn't stop. He doesn't sleep. He just stands there with his hands up and sings, and they sing back.*"

"*The Cantor*," said Malachi.

"*Is that what he is*?" The Queen's mouth twisted. "*I thought he was the end of the world.*"

---

They met in the library.

All of them. It was the only room in the Lantern Keep big enough, and even then they were packed in shoulder to shoulder, standing between the shelves, sitting on the table, on the floor, on the windowsill. Lex looked round at them and thought: *this is everyone. Everyone left*.

The Queen, in the best chair, with Elowen standing behind her. Roole by the door with his arms folded. Arion in the corner, grey and silent, with Pim beside him. Antho and Seraphine at the table, with maps. Wren on the windowsill with her bow across her knees. Kestrel and Tamsin, standing together. Oriel on a stool, with her white eyes turned toward nothing. Malachi on the table, cross-legged, with an apple he wasn't eating.

And Ilse. Sitting on the floor in the corner with her knees drawn up and her arms around them. Alone. For the first time since Lex had known her.

"*Four*," said Seraphine.

She had her notebook open. She'd been writing since dawn. Her spectacles were askew and there was ink on her nose.

"*Four of them*," she said. "*Malachi felt them. Lex felt where they came up.*" She turned a page. "*I've been through every record I brought from the Spire. Every chronicle. Every ballad.*" She looked up. "*I think I know who they were.*"

Silence.

"*The Cantor*," said Seraphine. "*Brother Aldous of Hollin's Rest. A choir-master. In a village in the north, three hundred years ago. The records say he had the most beautiful voice in Caldor. People came from a hundred miles to hear him sing.*" She swallowed. "*And one midwinter night, he walked out of his church singing, and the whole village followed him. Every man, woman, and child. Into the hills. In the snow.*"

"*And*?" said Roole.

"*And they never came back*." Seraphine turned the page. "*Hollin's Rest was struck from the Order's maps in the next year. As if it had never been. The records say the Stone there went black.*"

"*He's been doing it for three hundred years*," said Malachi quietly. "*At the bottom of the world. Singing. That's all he knows how to do.*"

---

"*Mother Sallow*," said Seraphine.

She turned another page.

"*Agathe Sallow. A midwife. Of Millbridge, in the Vale.*" She looked at Lex. "*Two hundred years ago, there was a plague in the Vale. The grey cough. It killed half of Millbridge in a month. Everyone who could fled. The priest. The Warden. The lord.*" She was quiet. "*She stayed. The midwife. She nursed the sick until there were no sick left. Only dead.*" Seraphine took off her spectacles and rubbed her eyes. "*And then she nursed the dead. The records say when they came back to Millbridge in the spring, they found her in the church. With all of them. Laid out in rows. Washed and dressed and tucked in.*" A pause. "*And she was singing to them. Lullabies.*"

"*Gods*," said Wren softly.

"*And she rots things*," said Lex. "*Malachi said a rot.*"

"*She tends things*," said Malachi. "*That's what she knows. She tends them until there's nothing left to tend. And then she keeps tending.*" He looked at the apple in his hands. "*Everything she touches, she tries to keep. Forever. And nothing living can be kept forever.*"

---

"*The Twinned Knight*," said Seraphine.

Across the room, Ilse lifted her head.

"*Ser Corran and Ser Cael*," said Seraphine. "*The Knights of the Ford. Brothers. Twins.*" She looked at Ilse. "*There's a ballad. Everyone knows it. Brannoc used to sing it in the forge, badly. They held the ford at the Silverrun against an army out of the east. Back to back. For a day and a night. And the river rose, and they wouldn't give ground, and they drowned.*" Her voice was gentle. "*Holding each other. When they found them, after, they couldn't separate them. They had to bury them together.*"

"*Two who won't let go*," said Ilse. Very quietly. From the corner.

"*Two who won't let go*."

Ilse put her face down on her knees.

---

"*And the fourth*," said Seraphine.

She closed her notebook. She didn't open it again.

"*We know who she is*," she said. "*Lex saw her taken. At the Rift.*" She looked at the door of the library. As if she could see down the passage, down to the bell-shaped cavern, down to the black glass door and whatever waited behind it. "*Isaura Kell. High Warden of the Order.*"

"*Who put my Ceiling on me*," said the Queen of Caldor. Very quietly. "*At sixteen. At the Great Stone in the Spire. With the flag that weighted it.*" She looked at her hands. "*Who sent Seraphine to the cells for a week. Who tried to take my crown at the masquerade.*"

Nobody said anything.

"*And who threw herself in front of Ilse*," said Lex. "*At the Rift. When the dark came for her.*"

Everyone looked at him.

"*She did*," said Lex. "*I saw it. Ilse was thirty-six percent gone. The easiest thread in the cavern. The dark went for her. And Kell shoved her out of the way and took it instead.*" He looked at Ilse. "*You don't remember. You were half asleep. Ivo was carrying you.*"

Ilse had lifted her head again. She was staring at him.

"*Why*?" she whispered.

"*Because Dorran carried her*," said Lex. "*Down the stair. And told her he wasn't going to forgive her. And held up the Rift.*" He swallowed. "*And when the dark came for you, she said: tell his twelve villages the High Warden who gave the order was sorry too.*"

The library was very quiet.

"*And now she's at the door*," said Antho. "*Without a face. Without a name. Holding it for him.*" He was looking at the Lantern on the table in front of him. "*And when Lex said her name, she flinched.*"

---

"*They're pipes*," said Malachi.

Everyone turned to look at him. The old man was sitting cross-legged on the table, turning his apple over and over in his gnarled hands.

"*Each of them*," he said. "*They came up the tithe-threads. Four of them, at four Stones. And they're* holding *the threads open. Wide. Like a man standing in a doorway so it can't close.*" He set the apple down. "*Every night they hold them open, the pipes get wider. And the song gets louder. And more people walk out to their Stones.*"

"*And when the pipes are wide enough*—" said Lex.

"*He comes up*." Malachi's shifting eyes were very dark. "*Himself. Not a herald. Not a Choir. Him. Through the four widest pipes. Into Highcrest. Into the Vale. Into the valley below this mountain. And through your mother's door.*"

"*How long*?"

The old man was quiet.

"*Oriel*?" he said.

The blind woman on her stool turned her white face toward him.

"*Twenty days*," said Oriel Sant. "*Maybe less. I see four fires. Burning down. And at the end of them, something gold coming up out of the ground.*" Her thin dry voice didn't change. "*I see the Heart. I see a smith with a hammer. A brother with a lamp. A girl with empty hands.*" She tilted her head. "*And an old man with a bell.*"

Malachi went very still.

"*A bell*," he said.

"*A little one*," said Oriel. "*Silver. No bigger than a thumb.*" She smiled faintly. "*Ringing.*"

---

They made the plan in an hour.

Lex had thought it would take longer. Councils always took longer, in the stories. But there wasn't time to argue. And everyone in the room already knew, he thought, what they were going to say. They'd known since the night of the Blade. Since the Rift. Since the song.

"*We split*," said the Queen. "*There's no other way. Four of them, four places, twenty days.*"

"*Highcrest*," said Antho. "*The Cantor.*" He put his hand on the Lantern. "*He's a voice. Nothing stops voices like this.*"

"*One lantern against a whole city*?" said Roole.

"*Not one*." Antho looked across the room. At Pim. Sitting beside Arion, with his knees drawn up. "*The Lantern stops voices. But there's something better than a lantern.*" He held out his hand. "*Pim. Will you come?*"

The boy looked at him. A long, grave, careful look.

Then he got up, and crossed the room, and took Antho's hand.

"*I'll come too*," said Seraphine. "*I've read every record of Hollin's Rest. Every one. If there's anything in there about how to stop him*—"

"*And the Guard*," said Elowen Dunmore. "*It's my city. My Queen.*" She looked at Lysandra. "*Majesty, you'll stay here.*"

"*I will not*." The Queen was already standing. "*He's singing to my people. On my Stone. In my square.*" Her dark eyes were very hard. "*I'm a Sovereign, Elowen. I was Kindled to command. If anyone in Caldor can out-shout a choir-master, it's me.*"

---

"*The Vale*," said Lex. "*Mother Sallow. Millbridge.*"

He looked at Kestrel.

She looked back at him. Her amber eyes were very steady. She hadn't said a word all morning. She'd stood by the shelves with her brother beside her and listened.

"*Rot*," said Kestrel. "*She rots things.*"

"*Yes*."

"*Fire burns rot*." Kestrel's mouth twisted. "*My grandmother used to say. When the sheep got foot-rot, she'd burn it out. With the Ashari fire.*" She looked at her hands. "*I'll come.*"

"*And me*," said Tamsin.

Everyone looked at him. He hadn't spoken in council before. He stood beside his sister, thin and dark-haired, in a borrowed coat, and his face was very pale.

"*I was at the edge of the dark*," said Tamsin Varn. "*For months. Like Mella. Like Ilse. I heard him singing.*" He swallowed. "*I can still hear him. At night. Very faint. If I listen.*" He looked at Lex. "*If anything comes up out of the Stone at Millbridge, I'll hear it first.*"

Kestrel looked at her brother for a long moment.

Then she reached out and took his hand.

"*The Vale is mine*," said Lex. "*It's home. Brannoc's there. Hedda. Tilda. The forge.*"

"*And Teodra*," said Roole.

Lex looked at him.

"*Teodra Hale*," said Roole. "*The free company. She's been in the Vale since the winter, guarding the carts from Ashbourne. Two hundred swords.*" His hatchet face was very grim. "*I sent her word last night. She'll be at Millbridge by now.*"

---

"*And the Knight*," said Ilse.

She'd got up. She was standing in the middle of the library floor, in her patched green coat, with her hands clenched at her sides.

"*He took Ivo down the Stone*," she said. "*To the Heart. To the bottom.*" She looked at Malachi. "*How do I get there?*"

The old man looked at her for a long time.

"*The long way*," he said at last. "*There's no other. Down the Stone, you can't follow. Not without the song in you.*" He looked at the window. West. Toward the Teeth. "*Across the grey river. Through the Drowned Country. Into the deep. The Forbidden Lands.*" He was quiet. "*It's the way to the Heart, girl. It's the only way. It's the way all of us will have to go, in the end.*"

"*Then I'm going now*."

"*Not alone*," said Antho.

Ilse turned on him.

"*Not alone*," said Antho again. Very gently. "*I've been across the grey river. I know the Ferryman. I know the Drowned Country.*" He looked at the Lantern on the table. "*And I promised someone there I'd come back. With a light.*"

"*You're going to Highcrest*. *The Cantor*."

"*I'm going to Highcrest first*." Antho looked at her. "*Twenty days, Oriel says. The Cantor first. Then the river.*" His grey eyes were very steady. "*Wait for me. Ten days. Ten days, and I'll take you across myself. And we'll go down together and find your brother.*"

Ilse stared at him.

"*He sat up with you every night for six weeks*," said Antho. "*He held on when you were thirty-six percent gone.*" His voice was very quiet. "*You can wait ten days for him. He'd want you to come with a lamp.*"

Ilse Marrow stood in the middle of the library floor and shook.

Then she nodded. Once.

And sat down on the floor where she was, and put her face in her hands, and cried for the first time since the Stone.

Wren slid down off the windowsill and went and sat beside her. And put an arm around her. And didn't say anything.

---

"*And the door*," said Arion.

He was still in the corner. Grey. Quiet. But he'd lifted his head.

"*Someone has to keep the door*," he said. "*She'll be back. Every night. Pushing.*" His voice was very steady now. "*I'll keep it.*"

"*Father*—" said Antho.

"*I know what you're going to say*." Arion looked at him. "*That I thought she was Seren. That I sat there for three days and talked to her. That I'll do it again.*" His lean face didn't change. "*I won't. I know now.*" He looked at the Lantern on the table. "*And I'll have the students. Odile. Teo. And a lantern of my own.*"

"*I'll make you one*," said Lex. "*Tonight. Before I go.*"

His father looked at him.

"*Twenty runes*," said Lex. "*Not twelve. The heaviest I've ever made.*"

Something moved at the corner of his father's mouth.

"*Thank you*," said Arion Vey.

---

They were standing up. Moving. Talking in low voices. Splitting into groups. The Queen with Elowen and Roole, bent over Seraphine's map of Highcrest. Kestrel and Tamsin by the door, waiting for Lex. Malachi still on the table.

And Lex turned to go, and found Malachi watching him.

"*A bell*," said the old man quietly. "*She said a bell.*"

Lex came and stood beside the table.

"*You threw it in the sea*," he said. "*You told us. In the Stillwater. The night after the Weaving.*"

"*It rang and rang*." Malachi's voice was barely audible. "*All the way down.*" He looked at his gnarled hands. "*It was the only thing he ever made just for me. Not for the world. Not for the net. For me. So he'd know when I was sad.*"

*The sea remembers every stone*, thought Lex. *That ever sank beneath her foam*.

He turned his head.

Teo Brask was standing in the doorway of the library. Dripping. As usual. He'd been listening, as usual. He went red when he saw Lex looking at him.

"*Um*," said Teo.

"*Teo*," said Lex. "*How do you feel about going home for a bit*?"

The boy blinked.

"*To the coast*," said Lex. "*To the sea.*" He looked at Malachi. "*There's something at the bottom of it. Something very small. Silver. That rang all the way down.*" He looked back at Teo. "*Do you think the Undertow would give it back? If you asked nicely?*"

Teo's round face went very still.

Then it lit up. Like a window being thrown open.

"*I can ask*," he said. "*I can always ask. It likes it when I ask.*"

---

Lex made his father a lantern that night.

In the forge Brannoc had built at the bottom of the bell-shaped cavern, in front of the cracked door. With the Heart-Coal's ordinary cousin in the hearth, plain charcoal, and Aurelian's little hammer in his hand. Iron. Heavy. Squat and round, like a clay lamp, with a ring to carry it by. And on its glass, twenty runes. *Hush*. Twenty times.

And on the inside of the ring, where only the person carrying it would see, one more.

*STAY*.

He gave it to his father at dawn. Arion took it in both hands and looked at it for a long time. Turned it over. Found the rune on the inside of the ring.

He didn't say anything. He didn't need to.

He hung it in the niche beside Seren's clay lamp. Two lights, side by side, in front of the cracked door. And sat down on the black floor beside them, with the blanket over his knees and Odile cross-legged at his side.

"*Go*," he said. "*All of you. I've got the door.*"

---

They left at sunrise.

Antho and Pim and Seraphine and the Queen and Elowen and Roole on Vharuun, west, toward Highcrest and the singing city.

Teo on Ashka, east, toward the Tidewater coast and the sea. Alone. Clutching the dragon's crest with white knuckles and a grin so wide it nearly split his face.

And Lex and Wren and Kestrel and Tamsin and Malachi on horses Roole had brought up the valley, south, toward the Vale. Toward Millbridge. Toward home.

Ilse stood at the mouth of the Hollowmaw and watched them all go. Alone. With her hands open at her sides.

*Ten days*, she thought.

And sat down on the rock where Pim always sat, and pulled her knees up, and waited.
