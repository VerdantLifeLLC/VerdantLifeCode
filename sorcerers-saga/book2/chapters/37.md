# Reborn in Light III

He stood up at the anvil with the bell in one hand and the hammer in the other, and looked at the net.

He could see all of it. From here. From the Heart. From the gold anvil where every thread in the world had been struck the first time, nine hundred years ago, by a frightened young man with his little brother watching from a bucket. He'd felt it before, when he first stepped onto the white floor of the forge: every thread *starting* here. He saw it now. Not with True Sight. Not with the Root. With his eyes. As if the empty dark above the round white forge had turned to glass, and through it, spread out below him like a map, the whole of the world.

And over the world, the net.

Silver. Every thread. Every soul. Millions. Ring linked through ring linked through ring, the way Malachi had said. Like mail. Like a great shirt of silver mail thrown over the whole of the world, every link shining.

And in every link, a crimp.

He saw them. Tiny. Every one. A little pinch in each ring, where the thing that had worn Aurelian's face had gone back to the anvil a year after the Weaving and struck every ring a second time. Tighter. The Ceiling. *Thus far, and no further*. And through every crimp, a hole. A tiny dark hole, where the Tithe had run down.

Nothing was running down them now. The holes were empty. The tithe-threads hung slack, all over the world, grey and limp, like the strings of a harp with no harper.

But the crimps were still there. Every one. Millions of them. Holding every soul in the world to a Ceiling. Holding every soul's breath.

*A soul with a Ceiling is a soul holding its breath*, the First Breath had said. *Forever*.

---

*[Lex*,] said Ember. Very quietly. In the hearth. *[There are millions of them.]*

*I know*.

*[You can't strike them one at a time. You'd be here until the end of the world.]*

*I know*.

*[Then how*—]

He didn't answer.

He looked down at the little hammer in his hand. Worn smooth. With faint gold runes on its cheeks. The first thing Aurelian ever made for himself. Singing, very faintly, in his palm.

*You just have to ask it nicely*.

He thought about Brannoc. In the Ashbourne forge. Twelve years old, so desperate to be good at everything that he'd broken three hammers in a month. *You can't hurry iron, lad. It knows when you're hurrying. It goes stiff on you.*

He thought about the old Stone at Fen Hollow, sighing when he asked it. About the Stone of Saffaran, which had hated holding its people down for two hundred years and had let go the moment he said *please*.

About the Sunwell. The Hearth. The Undertow. The First Breath. The mountain.

*Not do this. Help me.*

*[Lex*,] said Ember slowly. *[You're not going to strike them. Are you.]*

*No*, thought Lex. *I'm going to ask.*

---

He turned around.

They were all there. On the white floor of the round forge at the Heart. Where they'd fallen when the hunger pulled. Getting up now. Slowly. Grey-faced and shaking and alive.

Wren. Three paces away. On her knees. With her green eyes on his face, wide and wet.

Antho. Standing. With the Lantern in his hand. Clear-eyed. Very tired.

His mother. Kneeling beside Malachi's body at the foot of the anvil. Faint. With her hand still on the old man's shoulder.

Kestrel and Tamsin. Seraphine. Ilse and Ivo. Teo. Oriel. And Isaura Kell, standing at the edge of the forge with her hands at her sides and her gaunt face very still.

"*I need help*," said Lex.

---

"*It's a forging*," he said. "*The biggest one there's ever been. Bigger than the Weaving. Bigger than anything.*" He held up the hammer. "*And you can't forge alone. Not something this big. You need a striker. And a bellows. And a fire. And a quench.*" He looked at them. "*And you need all six Wells. Because the net was forged from the river, and the river runs through all six.*"

Nobody spoke.

"*Flame*," said Lex. "*That's me. And Kestrel. Commanding. Both of us.*"

Kestrel looked at him. Then at her hands. And very slowly, she stood up. Her hair catching. Gold. The Ashari fire.

"*Gale*," said Lex. "*Wren. Commanding. You've got the breath.*"

Wren was already on her feet.

"*Tide*," said Lex. He looked at the round dripping boy at the back of the group. "*Teo. You Speak to the sea. I need a quench. The biggest quench there's ever been.*"

Teo Brask went white. Then red. Then stood up very straight, dripping, and said, "*Yes, Master Lex*."

"*Stone*," said Lex. "*That's me. Speaking.*" He looked down at the white floor of the forge. At the white mountain under it. At the bones of the world. "*And it's here. Under us. The anvil.*"

"*Shade*," said Antho. Quietly. "*Me. Commanding.*" He looked at the Lantern in his hand. "*Every forging needs a dark. For the iron to cool in. For the quiet after.*"

"*And Radiance*," said Lex.

He turned.

Toward the small faint woman kneeling beside Malachi's body at the foot of the anvil.

---

Seren Vey looked up at him.

"*You're a Listener*," said Lex. "*A hedge-witch. Unlatticed. Father told me, at Avaloria, when I was nineteen. He said you heard things nobody else could hear.*"

His mother looked at him for a long moment.

"*I hear light*," she said. Very softly. "*I always have. Since I was a girl. I hear it the way you hear fire. Lamps. Candles. Stars.*" She looked down at her faint hands. "*That's why I could carry the lamp into the dark. That's why it never went out.*"

"*Radiance*," said Lex.

"*I never knew what to call it*."

"*Will you*?" His voice cracked. "*Mam. Will you help?*"

Seren Vey stood up. Slowly. Faint. Tired. Eleven years of holding a door, and months of walking in the dark after a woman with no face.

She came around the anvil. She stood beside him.

And she reached up and put her hand on his face. The way she used to, in the cottage at Harrow, when he was small and frightened of the thunder.

"*Oh, my love*," she said. "*Of course I will.*"

---

They gathered round the anvil.

Lex at the anvil itself, with the little hammer in his hand. His mother at his right shoulder. Antho at his left, with the Lantern. Wren behind him, at the place where the bellows would be, if there were bellows. Kestrel and Teo on the far side of the anvil, facing him. Fire and water.

And the others in a ring around them. Seraphine with her notebook, not writing. The twins, holding hands. Tamsin. Oriel, on her stool. And Isaura Kell, standing a little apart, with her hands clenched.

"*What do we do*?" said Wren.

"*I don't know*," said Lex honestly. "*I've never done this.*" He looked down at the anvil. Gold. Battered. Scarred. "*Nobody has. Except him.*" He swallowed. "*I think you just—give it. What you have. Your Well. Into the fire. And I'll ask.*"

"*Into what fire*?" said Kestrel. "*There's no fire.*"

Lex looked up.

At the pillar. Gold. Rising from the anvil into the empty dark. The pillar every tithe-thread in the world had fallen into, for eight hundred years. Still standing. Still burning. Empty now. Nothing falling into it.

"*That one*," he said.

---

Kestrel gave first.

She put her hands into the pillar of gold. Both of them. To the wrist. And her fire went *in*. Red and gold. The Ashari fire. The oldest fire in the world, that the Hearth had looked at and said *it knows me*. Pouring off her hands into the pillar. And the pillar *roared*.

Not gold anymore. Gold and *red*. Flame. Real flame. Leaping.

Lex put his hands in beside hers.

His own fire. Commanding. Gold-white. The fire he'd said good morning to every day for ten years. *What's this fire for?* He gave it. All of it. Into the pillar.

And it became a *forge*.

He felt it. The pillar of gold that had been the Tithe's drain for eight hundred years, roaring up into the empty dark above the white mountain. Not falling. *Rising*. A column of fire, red and gold and white, so hot that the white stone of the forge glowed, so bright that the too-close stars overhead went pale.

*[Lex*,] said Ember. In the hearth. *[Lex, the Hearth. I can feel it. In the Wellworld. It's leaning. Toward us. All of it.]*

*Hello*, thought Lex. At the first fire in the world. *Hello. It's me. I'm asking.*

And far away, in a world made of fire, a burning shape standing on a lake of white flame turned its faceless head toward the Heart. And *leaned*.

---

Wren breathed.

She stood behind him, where the bellows should be, and opened her mouth, and breathed *out*. Into the fire. Not the First Breath. That was gone, into the Lockward. But the sky remembered it. And she was Commanding now. And she breathed out the way she'd breathed out in the Wellworld of Gale, when she let go of his hand and the sky caught him.

The wind came.

From everywhere. From nowhere. Pouring past her into the pillar of fire, the way air pours through a bellows. And the fire went *white*. Welding heat. The color of iron at the moment it's ready.

*Up, up, up, lightfoot*, said a voice in the wind. Lex heard it. Light and quick and laughing, like a girl running through long grass. *Higher. Come on.*

---

Antho lifted the Lantern.

Not into the fire. *Beside* it. At Lex's left shoulder. With its shutter wide and its twelve Hush-runes blazing. And its gold light, small and steady, falling on the anvil.

And around the anvil, where the gold light fell, a *quiet*.

Lex felt it. Shade. Not the hunger pretending to be the dark. The real dark. The quiet. The rest. The space between heartbeats. Antho was making a place for the iron to cool. A place for the quiet after.

*The dark isn't your enemy*, Malachi had said, four years ago, by the eternal fire. *It never was.*

His brother's face, in the Lantern's light, was very calm.

---

And his mother sang.

Not the lantern song. Something else. Something Lex had never heard. Low and rough and very soft. No words. Just a sound. Like light. Like the sound a candle makes, if a candle could make a sound. Like stars.

*Radiance*.

He'd never heard it before. Nobody had. Not in nine hundred years. Not since the Listeners. The sixth Well. The one that was only light.

It went into the fire. And the fire went *gold* again. Not white. Not red. *Gold*. The gold of the lanterns at the Hollowmaw. The gold of the little clay lamp with the chip in its spout. Warm and steady and kind.

And somewhere very far away, in a cavern at the bottom of a mountain, two lamps in a niche in front of a sealed black door *flared*. All at once. Bright as noon.

And a man sitting on the black floor in front of them looked up.

---

"*Teo*," said Lex.

The boy was on the far side of the anvil. Shaking. White. Dripping. Looking at the pillar of fire roaring up into the dark.

"*When I strike*," said Lex, "*I need you to quench. All of it. Everything. The whole net.*"

"*The whole*—" Teo's voice cracked. "*Master Lex, I can't. I'm fifteen. I talk to fish in a bucket.*"

"*You talk to the sea*," said Lex. "*And the sea talks back.*" He looked at the boy. "*It said it missed you, Teo. On the beach. It said you'd been gone too long.*"

Teo stared at him.

"*Ask it*," said Lex. "*Ask the Undertow. Tell it I'm keeping my promise.*" He swallowed. "*Tell it the deep rivers won't stay dammed. Tell it I said all of them.*"

Teo Brask closed his eyes.

And Lex heard him. Not with his ears. The way he'd heard him on the Tidewater coast. A sound like the tide coming in over the marshes. Slow. Steady. Endless. Like a homesick boy talking to the sea.

*Please*, said Teo. To the deep. To the dark under all the water in the world. *Please. He's keeping his promise. He needs you. All of you.*

And far away, under all the oceans of the world, something vast and slow and cold turned in the deep.

And *came*.

---

Lex lifted the hammer.

It was singing. Loud now. In his palm. A working song. The kind smiths sing at the anvil to keep the rhythm. Losing the tune. He knew that tune. Brannoc sang it. Badly. He'd heard it every day of his life since he was eleven.

He looked down at the anvil.

At the net. Spread out below him, through the glass of the empty dark. Every ring. Every soul. Every crimp.

*I'm not going to strike you*, he thought. At all of them. At every ring. Every thread. Every soul in the world. *I'm going to strike the anvil. The one you were all made on. And it's going to ring. And when you hear it*—

He took a breath.

*When you hear it, you can let go. If you want to. You don't have to hold your breath anymore.*

*Please*.

And he struck.

---

*Tink*.

---

It wasn't loud.

He'd thought it would be. The biggest forging there had ever been. He'd thought it would shake the world.

It was a *tink*. Small. Bright. Clear. Aurelian's little hammer on Aurelian's gold anvil. The sound a smith makes a thousand times a day. The sound Lex had made every morning of his life since he was eleven.

And it rang.

Out from the anvil. Up the pillar of fire. Through the six Wells pouring into it. Flame and Gale and Shade and Radiance and Stone. Out into the empty dark. Down the slack grey tithe-threads. Into every Stone. Every thread. Every ring.

Everywhere.

---

In Highcrest, the Queen of Caldor was standing on the steps of the Spire, at dawn, looking at the silver Great Stone in the square. She'd been there all night. She hadn't slept since the hunger pulled.

She heard it. A tiny bright sound. Like a hammer on an anvil, very far away.

And felt something in her chest *let go*.

She didn't know what it was. Not at first. A loosening. A breath. As if she'd been holding it her whole life without knowing, and someone had just said *you can stop now*.

She looked down at her hands.

They were glowing. Faintly. Silver and gold.

All around her, in the square, in the streets, in the windows of the golden city, people were stopping. Standing still. Looking at their hands.

Glowing.

---

In Saffaran, Queen Ishara of the Sunreach was on the highest tower, with Zahir on her shoulder, watching the sun come up over the glass.

She heard it.

And the Ashari fire in her palms, which had come back when the smith from Caldor knelt at the foot of her Stone, went *gold*. Brighter than it had ever been. Brighter than her grandmother's stories.

And all over the city, on every terrace, every rooftop, her people's fire went gold with it.

---

In the Wickhollow, under the Bitter Hills, a little girl with no thread was asleep with an iron candle-holder in her arms.

She woke. She sat up. She looked around the gallery. At the candles. At the Unlit, sleeping on their benches. At her mother.

She didn't hear anything. She'd never been in the net. She never would be.

But every candle in the Wickhollow, every single one, beeswax and tallow, in jars and tin cups and on bare rock, *leaned*. All at once. In the same direction. Toward something very far away.

And then straightened.

And burned a little brighter than before.

---

In Ashbourne, Tilda Crane was at the great hearth.

She heard it. Clear as anything. A hammer on an anvil.

She knew that sound. She'd know it anywhere. *Tink*. Light and fast and precise. Master Lex's strike. The one he used for the fine work.

She looked down at the hearth. At the fire. And the fire looked back at her.

*Good morning*, it said.

Not in words. But she heard it. For the first time. Clear as a bell.

Tilda Crane sat down on the floor of the Ashbourne Forge with her too-big apron around her knees, and laughed, and cried, and said *good morning* back.

---

At the Hollowmaw, Arion Vey was sitting in front of the sealed door.

He heard it.

And felt something go out of him.

Not taken. Not pulled. *Released*. Like a hand opening. The gold in him, the Root, the thing that had stopped him at thirty-one like a clock and kept him for two hundred years. He felt it go. Out of him. Up. Into the net. Into everyone.

He looked down at his hands.

On the back of the left one, between the knuckles, very faint, there was a spot. Small. Brown. The kind old men get.

He'd never had one before.

He sat in front of the door with Pim beside him and stared at it for a very long time.

And then he started to laugh.

---

At Harrowgate, Corin Bell woke up in the little room under the eaves and found his sister sitting on the end of his bed, glowing.

At Fen Hollow, an old woman named Bess Rook, Spring Kindling, Ceiling twelve, sat up in bed and said, very clearly, to nobody, "*Oh. Oh, there it is.*"

At Thornby, Garret Shale's mother dropped a pot.

At Coldbrook, in a farmhouse with a cow gate, a coat that nobody would admit to owning fell off its hook by the door.

All over the world. Every soul. Every thread. Every ring.

*Letting go*.

---

Lex felt it.

All of it. Through the anvil. Through the hammer. Through the six Wells pouring through him.

Every crimp, in every ring, in every thread, *relaxing*. Uncurling. Like a fist opening. Like a held breath let out. Millions of them. All at once. The Ceiling that had held every soul in the world for eight hundred years. Not broken. Not torn. *Released*. Because someone had asked. Because they'd heard a hammer ring on the anvil they were made on, and remembered they'd been made to breathe.

And the holes. Where the Tithe had run. Closing. Healing. Like wounds. Like the cracks in the sky over the Sundered Lands. One by one. Then all at once.

And the grey tithe-threads, hanging slack from every Stone in the world, *dissolving*. Like frost in sunlight. Like mist. Gone.

And Teo's quench came.

---

From the deep. From under all the oceans in the world. Up through the dark. Up through the Forbidden Lands. Up through the white mountain. A wave. Not of water. Of *Tide*. Of change and memory and the coming in and the going out. Cold and enormous and patient.

It hit the pillar of fire.

And the pillar *sang*.

The same note the Lockward had sung. Pure and high and clear. Like a bell struck once in a silent church. It went out from the Heart. Up through the net. Into every ring. Every newly uncrimped ring, still hot from the forging, still glowing.

*Setting*. Fixing them. The way a quench sets a blade. Teaching the steel its new shape.

Open. Free. Breathing.

*The sea remembers every stone*, Lex thought. Dimly. *And now it remembers this.*

---

And then there was one thing left.

He felt it. Standing at the anvil with the hammer in his hand and the fire roaring and the quench singing. One thing. In the center of the net. At the very bottom. Where every Root began.

*The Root*.

His own. His father's. His brother's. The gold thread that ran from the Veys down to this anvil. That made them Root-heirs. That let them see every thread and set every Sanction and grant every Tier and hold every soul in the world in the palm of their hand.

The center of the net.

*He had to be at the center*, Malachi had said. *Holding the center down. He chose himself. Of course he did.*

*[Lex*,] said Ember. Very quietly. *[It's still there. The Root. At the center. If you leave it, the net still has a center. Someone holding it.]*

*I know*.

*[You could keep it. You'd be the Root of the world. Like him. Like your father. Forever.]*

*I know*.

*[Lex*—]

*No one at the center*, thought Lex Vey. *No one holding it down. Everyone holding everyone.*

And he let go.

---

It went out of him like the Tithe had gone out of the world.

The gold. The Root. Authority. True Sight. Command. Sanction. Unbind. Every Prerogative he'd ever used. The thing that had come up in him the night the seal cracked in the Ashbourne forge four years ago, when Elric Thorne stood over him and said *so it's true*.

He felt it go. Up. Out. Into the net. Not to anyone. To *everyone*. Spreading out through every newly freed ring. A little to each. Not enough for anyone to hold the world. Just enough for everyone to hold themselves.

And beside him, he felt Antho let go too. The same. Gold, going out. Up. Into everyone.

And very far away, at the bottom of a mountain, in front of a sealed door, he felt his father's last threads of it go. Arion. Two hundred and thirty-two years. Letting go.

---

The fire went down.

Slowly. The pillar of gold and red and white sinking. Back down into the anvil. The six Wells drawing back. Kestrel's hands coming out of the flame. Wren's breath running out. The Undertow's quench sinking back into the deep, cold and slow and satisfied. His mother's song going quiet.

And the anvil was just an anvil. Gold. Battered. Scarred. In a round white forge on top of a white mountain at the bottom of the world.

With a little hammer lying on it.

Lex stood there. Swaying. Empty. Lighter than he had ever been in his life.

He looked down at his hands.

The gold was gone. The faint glow that had come off his skin since he was nineteen. Gone. His hands were just hands. Big. Scarred. Callused. A smith's hands.

And then, very faintly, in the hearth at the center of him—

*[Lex*?]

He nearly fell over.

*Ember*?

*[I'm still here*,] said Ember. Wonderingly. *[I'm still here. I thought I'd go. With the Root. I thought I lived in the Root.]* A pause. *[I don't. I live in your hearth. I always did.]*

Lex sat down. On the white floor of the forge. Very suddenly. With his back against the gold anvil, beside Malachi's small brown body.

*[You gave it away*,] said Ember. *[All of it. The whole Root.]*

*Yes*.

*[You could have been the Root of the world.]*

*I know*.

*[Why didn't you*?]

Lex thought about it. Honestly. The way Malachi had taught him.

*Because I'm a smith*, he thought. *Not a king. Smiths make things. And then they give them away.*

The coal in the hearth was quiet for a long time.

*[That's the best answer I've ever heard*,] said Ember. *[I'm putting it in the book.]*

---

The panel came up anyway.

He didn't ask for it. He didn't have True Sight anymore. But the net was new, and the net was everyone's, and it seemed to want to tell him.

He read it. He read it twice.

Then he closed his eyes and leaned his head back against the anvil and breathed.

---

Wren sat down beside him.

She didn't say anything. She leaned against his shoulder. He leaned back.

Antho sat down on his other side. With the Lantern in his lap. Its shutter closed. Its light small and steady. He put his hand on the back of Lex's neck.

And his mother came and knelt in front of them. Faint. Tired. Smiling. And put one hand on each of their faces. Her two boys. Sitting against an anvil at the bottom of the world.

"*Oh, my loves*," said Seren Vey. "*Oh, look at you. Look what you've done.*"

Nobody said anything.

There wasn't anything to say.

---

The Forbidden Lands were *rising*.

Seraphine saw it first. She'd gone to the edge of the forge, between the broken pillars, and was looking out at the empty dark. And she made a sound.

They all looked.

The pieces. The drifting fragments of eaten worlds. The forests and kitchens and schoolrooms and gardens and churches and ships, floating in the empty dark, slowly turning.

They were going *gold*.

One by one. Then dozens. Then all of them. Lighting up from inside, the way the plague-dead had lit up on the green at Millbridge. A soft warm gold glow. And rising. Up. Out of the dark. Like sparks from a forge going up a chimney. Like lanterns released over a river at a festival.

Lex watched a kitchen go past. Small. With a hearth. A table. A chair. And on the chair, a woman. Faint. Grey. Who had been waiting for someone to come home for a very, very long time.

She was gold now. And rising. And smiling.

"*It let them go*," said Antho. Very quietly. "*When it unfolded. Everything it ate.*" His clear grey eyes were wet. "*It's letting them all go.*"

---

They went down the white mountain at what might have been dawn, if there had been a dawn in the Forbidden Lands.

Lex carried Malachi. He was very light. Like a bundle of dry sticks wrapped in an old robe. Lex carried him down the smooth white slope with the old man's head on his shoulder and his straw hat on top of Lex's own head, because there was nowhere else to put it, and the little silver bell in his coat, beside the Ledger and Dorran's book.

It didn't ring. Not once.

At the foot of the mountain, on the black ink, the Ferryman was waiting.

He was standing in his long grey boat. Leaning on his long grey pole. Exactly where they'd left him.

But the lantern at the top of his pole was *lit*.

Gold. Small. Steady. Burning for the first time in nine hundred years.

The Ferryman turned his eyeless face toward them as they came down onto the black ink.

"*My river*," he said. His rain-voice was very strange. "*It's running clean.*"

---

They went home.

Back across the black ink. Back up the waterfall at the edge of the deep, which the Ferryman poled them up as if it were a gentle slope. Back through the Drowned Country.

The drowned city was empty.

All the grey dead were gone. Every one. The woman with the basket. The man leading a horse that wasn't there. The children running without sound. Gone. Gone up. Gold.

Except one.

In the great square at the center of the city, in a ring of six iron lanterns marked with Ardent, burning gold, a little girl was sitting cross-legged on the drowned flagstones.

Waiting.

Antho was out of the boat before it stopped.

He went down into the not-water of the square, the way he had twice before. With the Lantern held high. And the little girl looked up at him.

She was glowing. Faintly. Gold. Like all the others had been, as they rose.

But she hadn't gone.

Antho knelt down in front of her.

"*You waited*," he said.

The little girl looked at him. Then she reached out one small faint hand. And took hold of his sleeve. Between finger and thumb.

And held on.

"*Oh*," said Antho Vey. Very softly. "*Oh. You were waiting for me.*"

She didn't answer. She held his sleeve. And smiled.

And then, slowly, gently, the way a candle goes out in a quiet room at the end of a long evening, she went up. Gold. Rising. Out of the drowned square. Up through the not-water. Up into the soft blue-violet dark above the Drowned Country.

Still holding on to his sleeve. Until she was too high to reach.

And then letting go.

---

At the grey river, the Ferryman set them down on the world's shore.

All of them. Except two.

Seren Vey stood in the long grey boat with Isaura Kell beside her. And didn't get out.

"*Mam*," said Lex.

"*I know, love*."

"*Mam, come on*." His voice cracked. "*It's done. It's over. The Tithe's gone. The hunger's gone. You can come home. Antho's a Threshold Warden, he can let you through any*—"

"*I know*," said Seren. Very gently. "*I know he can.*"

She looked at her eldest son. Standing on the grey shingle with the Lantern in his hand.

"*Not yet*," said Seren Vey. "*Not yet, my loves.*"

---

"*Why*?" said Antho. His voice was very quiet.

His mother looked out across the river. At the far shore. At the soft blue-violet dark of the Hollow. Not hungry anymore. Just dark. Just quiet. Just Shade.

"*Because there are more*," she said. "*Down there. Further than the Drowned Country. Further than the Forbidden Lands.*" She looked back at them. "*Places it ate before the net. Before the Weaving. Long, long before.*" Her faint face was very calm. "*They're rising. All of them. Going home. But some of them don't know the way.*"

"*Like the little girl*," said Antho.

"*Like the little girl*." Seren smiled. "*Someone has to walk them home. Someone who knows the dark. Someone with a light.*" She looked at Kell. "*Isaura's coming with me. She's a Warden. She's good at doors.*"

Kell didn't say anything. But she stood very straight beside Seren in the boat. And her hands, at her sides, were not clenched.

"*And then*?" said Lex.

His mother looked at him. With her grey eyes. Antho's eyes. Ysmir's eyes.

"*And then I'll come home*," said Seren Vey. "*I promise.*" She reached out of the boat and put her faint hand on his face. "*Leave a light on, love. Both of you. In the window.*"

"*Always*," said Antho.

"*I know*." She smiled. "*You always count.*"

---

The Ferryman pushed off.

The long grey boat slid out into the river. Into the mist. Which wasn't grey anymore. Lex saw that, as it closed around the boat. Not grey. *Clear*. Silver. Shining. Like water in sunlight. Like the river in Malachi's stories, before the Tithe. Before the Weaving.

The lantern on the Ferryman's pole burned gold.

And in the boat, getting smaller, getting fainter, a woman in a faded blue dress stood beside a woman in torn white robes, and looked back at the shore.

And waved.

And Lex stood on the shingle with Malachi in his arms and his brother beside him and Wren's hand in his, and waved back. Until the mist closed. Until the gold light of the lantern was only a spark. Until it was gone.

*[She'll come home*,] said Ember. Very softly. In the hearth.

*I know*, thought Lex.

*[How do you know*?]

He looked down at the little silver bell. In his coat. Beside the Ledger. Silent.

*Because she's a Vey*, he thought. *And we always come.*
