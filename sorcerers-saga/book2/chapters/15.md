# Old Allies, New

He told Antho about the stone at dawn, sitting on the rock at the mouth of the Hollowmaw with the valley below them full of smoke.

He told it badly. He'd never been good at telling things. He started in the wrong place and had to go back. He forgot the heron and had to put it in later. He got the willows wrong, then right. And when he got to the part where Antho had picked him up and swung him round, his voice went, and he had to stop and look at the smoke for a while.

Antho listened.

He sat with his arms around his knees and his grey eyes on Lex's face and didn't interrupt once. He listened the way he listened to Shade: completely, with his whole body, as if every word were a sound in the dark that might matter.

When Lex had finished, Antho was quiet for a long time.

"*Five*," he said at last.

"Five."

"*And I said four*."

"You said four. For nine years."

"*Why?*"

Lex looked at him. "Because you'd never done more than four yourself. And you didn't want your little brother beating you on the first day he got it right." He swallowed. "You said you were a petty, jealous fifteen-year-old. You said you were sorry."

Antho thought about that.

"*That sounds like me*," he said. "*At fifteen.*" Something moved at the corner of his mouth. "*It sounds like a good story.*"

"It's not a story. It happened."

"*I know*." Antho looked down at his scarred hands. "*I believe you. I can feel where it was. There's a—a shape. In the dark. Where I used to keep it.*" He was quiet. "*But when you tell it, Lex, it's like hearing about two boys I never met. Nice boys. I'd like them.*" He looked up. "*I just don't remember being one of them.*"

Lex couldn't say anything.

Antho reached out and put his hand on the back of Lex's neck. The way he used to, when they were small. Lex hadn't known he remembered that.

"*Tell it again*," said Antho. "*Tomorrow. And the day after. Until I know it the way you know it.*" His voice was very steady. "*It won't be the same. I know that. But it'll be something. It'll be yours, given to me. That's not nothing.*"

*That's not nothing*. Lex had heard someone say that before. He couldn't think who.

"Every day," he said. "I'll tell it every day."

"*Good*." Antho took his hand away. He looked out at the smoking valley. "*And when this is over, we'll go to the lake. And you can show me. And I'll count.*"

"You always count."

"*Apparently*," said Antho, "*I do.*"

---

Forty-one Keepers had died on the slopes.

Harrow Ves read the names at noon, standing on Hedda's barricade at the mouth of the cave, with her face bandaged and her arm in a sling. Forty-one. Lex didn't know any of them. He made himself listen to every one.

*Alder Penrose. Mattie Crewe. Holm Dacey. Sefton Ward.*

He thought of the Ledger. Of the narrow elegant hand, writing a line under each name. *He brought the cows in every evening. She was learning to read.*

Nobody would write lines under these. They hadn't been unwritten. They'd only died. Their families would remember them, and grieve, and the names would be carved on a stone somewhere and weather away.

*That's better*, he thought. *Isn't it? To be remembered and grieved. That's better than being forgotten.*

He didn't know. He stood at the back of the crowd of grey-coated Keepers with his hands at his sides and listened to the names, and didn't know anything.

Brannoc had broken his arm. Hedda had set it, swearing at him the whole time. *Sixty-one years old, standing on a barricade like a boy, you great bald fool, what were you thinking.* He'd sat on a bench in the hall while she bound it, grinning at Lex through his grey beard like a man who'd just won a bet.

"*Saw you come up that slope, lad*," he said. "*With the fire on you.*" He shook his head slowly. "*Never thought I'd see the like. Never in my life.*"

"You taught me," said Lex.

"*I taught you to make horseshoes*." Brannoc looked at him. "*You did the rest yourself.*"

---

His father came at sunset.

Out of the Stone at Hollow's Foot, the way Lex and Wren had come. Alone. Lex felt him arrive through the Root: a great bright gold thread, coming up out of the net in the valley below, and then *dimming*. Guttering. Like a candle in a draft.

He was halfway down the slope before he knew he'd moved.

Arion was on his knees in the wet grass beside the little Stone. His face was grey. His hands were shaking. He'd walked the threads twice in two days, the second time alone, the first time carrying two people, with Severance still raw on him from the masquerade. He looked up when Lex came running down the slope.

And for the first time in his life, Lex saw his father look *old*.

Not in his face. His face was the same. Lean and dark and forty-five forever. But in his eyes. Something tired. Something that had been carrying a weight for a very long time, and had finally put it down, and couldn't pick it up again.

"*Is he*—" said Arion. Hoarse. "*Antho. The door. Is it—*"

"It's holding," said Lex. "Cracked. But holding."

His father closed his eyes.

"*And Seren*?"

Lex looked at him.

"*Elric told us*," he said. Very quietly. "*Before he left. He said she's alive. On the other side of the door. Holding it. For eleven years.*" His voice didn't shake. He was proud of that, later. "*And he said you've known for three.*"

Arion didn't open his eyes.

"*Yes*," he said.

---

Antho was waiting at the door.

He hadn't come up to the cave mouth when Arion arrived. He hadn't come down the slope. He was sitting on the black floor of the bell-shaped cavern, in front of the cracked door, beside the little clay lamp in its niche. Pim was asleep against his side. The boy had barely left him since the night before.

He looked up when Lex and Arion came down the last long passage into the cavern. He didn't get up.

"*Father*," he said.

Arion stopped at the bottom of the passage. Lex stopped beside him.

"*Antho*."

"*Sit down*," said Antho. "*You look terrible.*"

Arion sat. On the black floor. Across from his eldest son, with the cracked door between them and the lamp burning gold in its niche. Lex sat too. The three of them, in a ring, in front of the door.

Nobody said anything for a while.

"*Three years*," said Antho at last.

"Yes."

"*You knew for three years.*"

"Yes."

"*How?*"

Arion looked at the lamp.

"*The night you lit it*," he said. "*The two of you. Three years ago. After Duskhold.*" His voice was very low. "*When every lantern in the mountain caught fire, I felt it. Through the Root. All the way from Highcrest.*" He swallowed. "*And I felt something else. A thread. Very faint. Very far down. On the other side of the door, where no thread should be.*"

"*Hers*."

"*Hers*." Arion's hands were clenched in his lap. "*I'd have known it anywhere. I've known it since I was a young man.*" He looked at Antho. "*I thought I was mad. I thought it was grief. I went to the Great Stone at the Spire that night and put my hands on it and reached down, as far as I could reach, and—*"

He stopped.

"*And she spoke to you*," said Lex.

"*Yes*."

"*What did she say?*"

His father was quiet for a long time.

"*She said: Don't tell the boys.*"

---

Antho went very still.

"*She said*," said Arion, "*that if you knew, you'd try to come for her. Both of you. You'd find a way. You always find a way.*" His voice cracked. "*And to reach her, you'd have to open the door. And if the door opened, even a little, even for a moment, everything she'd been holding back for eight years would come out. Everything. The whole Hollow. Into the world.*" He looked at his sons. "*She said she hadn't held it for eight years so that her boys could undo it out of love.*"

"*And you agreed.*"

"*I agreed*." Arion looked at the door. "*I sat in my rooms in the Spire every night for three years and talked to her through the Root, and held the door from my side as much as I could from three hundred miles away, and didn't tell you.*" His voice was very flat. "*Wren heard me. Through the door of my rooms. She never asked. I think she guessed.*"

*Hold on, love. Not much longer*. Lex remembered Wren's face on the steps of the Spire. Careful. Knowing more than it was saying.

"*You let me sit here*," said Antho. "*Every night. For three years. Counting lanterns. Saying good evening to her lamp. Thinking it was just a lamp.*" His voice was very quiet. "*And she could hear me. Every word. And you knew.*"

"*Yes*."

"*Why?*"

"*Because she asked me to*," said Arion simply. "*And she's the only person in two hundred years I've never once said no to.*"

Antho stared at him.

Lex heard it. *Two hundred years*. He filed it beside Wren's face on the steps of the Spire.

But Antho didn't seem to hear it at all. He was looking at the door.

---

He got up.

Carefully, so as not to wake Pim. He laid the boy down on the black floor with his coat under his head. Then he went and stood in front of the cracked black glass. And put his hand on it.

"*Mam*," he said.

Nothing. The lamp burned straight up in its niche.

"*Mam. I know you're there. I know you can hear me.*"

Silence.

And then the flame leaned.

*I'm here, love*.

Faint. So faint. Fainter than it had been before the Blade was taken. Like a voice from the bottom of a very deep well. Tired, and strained, and rough as stone.

"*Did you tell him not to tell us?*"

A long pause.

*Yes*.

"*Why?*"

*Because you'd have come for me. Both of you. And I couldn't let you.* The voice was barely a breath. *Don't blame your father, love. Blame me. I made him promise. I knew what it would cost him. I asked anyway.*

Antho stood with his hand on the glass. His shoulders were shaking.

"*I sat here*," he said. "*I sat here every night.*"

*I know. I heard every word.* A pause. *One thousand one hundred and eleven. Every night. Never missed one.* The faintest ghost of something that might have been a laugh. *You always did count, Antho. Even when you were tiny. You'd count the stairs up to the loft. Every night. Out loud.*

Antho made a sound Lex had never heard him make. He bent forward until his forehead touched the glass.

*Lex*, said the voice. *Are you there too?*

Lex was on his feet. He didn't remember standing. He came and stood beside his brother and put his hand on the glass beside Antho's.

"I'm here, Mam."

*You've got so big*. A pause. *Both of you. I can feel it. Through the door. You've got so big.* The voice wavered. *I'm so proud of you. I'm so proud.*

"*Mam*—" Lex's voice broke. "*How do we get you out? How do we—*"

*You don't. Not yet.* Very firm, suddenly. The voice of a woman who had made a decision a long time ago and would not be moved. *The door has to stay shut. He has the Key now. Whole. He'll take it to the lock and turn it. And when he does, everything I've been holding will push from below, and the lid will lift, and the Key will hold it open.* A pause. *You have to stop him turning it. That's all that matters. Not me. Him.*

"*And you?*"

*I'll hold. As long as I can.* The voice was fading. *Light the lamp every night. Keep the door shut. And go.*

"*Mam—*"

*Go, my loves*.

And the flame in the niche straightened, and burned straight up, and she was gone.

---

They stood there for a long time. Two brothers, with their hands on a cracked black door.

Behind them, their father hadn't moved. He sat on the black floor where he'd sat down, with his hands open in his lap. Lex looked back at him once. Arion's face was wet.

At last Antho took his hand off the glass. He turned. He looked down at their father.

"*She's holding it alone*," he said. "*From the other side. She's been holding it alone for eleven years.*"

"Yes."

"*Someone has to hold this side. While we're gone.*"

"Yes," said Arion. And got up. Slowly. Like an old man. And came and stood in front of the door, between his sons, and put both his hands on the black glass.

"*I'll stay*," he said.

Lex looked at him.

"*It's my turn*," said Arion Vey quietly. "*I've stood at the front for two hundred years. I've given orders and made speeches and kept secrets and sent other people into the dark.*" He looked at the lamp. "*Eleven years she's held it alone. Eleven years while I slept in a crystal and then sat in a tower and talked to her through the Root from three hundred miles away.*" He shook his head slowly. "*No more. I'll keep the door. You two go and stop Elric.*"

"*Father*—" said Antho.

"*I'm not asking*." Arion didn't look away from the glass. "*For once in my life, Antho, I'm not asking anyone's permission. I'm staying.*" His voice dropped. "*Close to her. As close as I can get.*"

Antho looked at him for a long, long moment.

Then he did something Lex had never seen him do. Not once. Not in Duskhold, when they'd freed their father from the crystal. Not on the plinth, in front of eleven thousand people, when Antho had said *I forgive you*.

He put his arms around their father. And held on.

Arion stood very still. Then his hands came off the glass, slowly, and closed on his son's back.

Lex stood beside them and looked at the lamp, and let them have it.

*[Lex,]* said Ember. Very quietly. *[He said two hundred years.]*

*I heard.*

*[Twice.]*

*I know.*

*[Are you going to ask him?]*

Lex looked at his father. At the lean unchanged face pressed against Antho's shoulder. At the hands on his brother's back, trembling.

*Not tonight*, he thought.

---

The rest of the Unlit came in two days later, on horses lent by Fenn, riding hard up the long valley from the south road. Dorran on foot beside them, of course. Ilse in the saddle in front of her brother, pale and faded, her head lolling against his chest. Oriel on her mule, entirely unruffled.

They found the Hollowmaw lit, and the slopes scarred and black, and forty-one fresh graves in a row below the cave mouth. And Kestrel Varn, sitting alone on a rock at the edge of the camp, with her brother beside her.

Ivo saw her first.

He was off the horse before it stopped. He handed his sister down to Dorran, very carefully, and then he walked across the camp toward the rock where Kestrel was sitting. Not running. Walking. His face perfectly white.

Kestrel stood up when she saw him coming. She didn't run either. She didn't burn. She just stood there with her hands at her sides, and waited.

He hit her.

Not with a knife. Not with a sling. With his fist, in the face, as hard as he could. He wasn't a big man. He wasn't a fighter. But he put everything he had into it, and Kestrel's head snapped sideways, and she went down on one knee in the grass.

She didn't get up. She didn't hit back. She knelt there with blood running from her lip and looked up at him.

"*She's thirty percent gone*," said Ivo. His voice was shaking. "*My sister. Thirty percent. I wake up in the night and I can't remember what she sounds like when she laughs. I have to go and wake her up and make her laugh so I don't forget.*" His fists were clenched. "*He did that. Your old man. Your* friend. *And you gave him the key.*"

"*I know*," said Kestrel.

"*You gave him the* key, *Kes.*"

"*I know*."

"*For your brother*." Ivo's voice cracked. "*Your brother's whole. Look at him. Standing there. Solid. Whole.*" He jerked his head at Tamsin, who stood beside the rock with his hands at his sides and his face grey. "*And my sister's thirty percent gone. And fifty-five people are still in that book. And the world's about to end.*" He was crying now. "*Was it worth it? Was it worth it, Kes?*"

Kestrel looked at him for a long time.

"*Yes*," she said.

Ivo stared at her.

"*And no*," said Kestrel Varn. Her voice was very low. "*Both. All the time. Every minute.*" She looked at her brother. Then back at Ivo. "*I'd do it again, Ivo. That's the worst of it. I'd do it again. And I'll spend the rest of my life paying for it.*" She swallowed. "*You can hit me again if you want. I won't stop you.*"

Ivo stood over her with his fists clenched and his face wet.

He didn't hit her again.

He turned around and walked back across the camp to his sister. And knelt down beside her where Dorran had laid her on a blanket in the grass. And put his face against her shoulder. And Ilse, faint and pale, put her hand on the back of his head.

---

They met that night in the library. All of them.

Lex. Antho. Arion, grey and quiet in the corner. Wren, back in her own patched coat at last, with her boots up on the table. Seraphine with her spectacles and her maps. Brannoc with his arm in a sling and Hedda beside him with her cudgel. Harrow Ves for the Keepers. The Unlit: Dorran, Ilse and Ivo, Oriel.

And Kestrel and Tamsin, standing by the door. Not sitting. Not invited to.

"*She stays*," said Harrow Ves flatly, "*in a cell. Until the Queen's justice decides what to do with her. Forty-one of my people are in the ground because of what she did.*"

"*Forty-one of your people are in the ground because of what Elric did*," said Dorran.

"*She gave him the blood.*"

"*She gave him a rag*." Dorran's great slab of a face didn't change. "*He'd have found another way. He always finds another way. He found Lex in the glass, and he found the Hollowmaw, and he found my old commander in a hole in the north.*" He looked at Kestrel. "*She made it easier. That's all. She didn't make it happen.*"

"*That's not all*," said Ivo. Very quietly. He was sitting beside Ilse, holding her hand. He didn't look up.

"*No*," Dorran agreed. "*It isn't.*" He was silent for a long moment. "*I put the lid on four thousand one hundred and nine children. Because someone told me it was right. Because I wanted to believe them.*" He looked at Ivo. "*Your sister forgave me. Do you remember? You wanted to kill me. In the Bitter Hills. And she wouldn't let you.*"

Ivo didn't answer.

"*She said I'd only been doing what I thought was right*," said Dorran. "*And then she said the Unlit needed someone big to lift things.*" He looked at Kestrel. "*I've been lifting things ever since. Trying to make up for it. I'll never make up for it. But I try.*"

The library was very quiet.

"*Ilse*," said Lex. "*What do you think?*"

Everyone looked at her.

She was very faint tonight. Faded, like a drawing left out in the rain. Her mouse-brown hair had gone almost grey. But her grey eyes, when she lifted them, were clear.

"*I think*," she said slowly, "*that if Ivo were thirty percent gone, and someone offered me the key to bring him back, I'd have given them anything. Anything.*" She looked at Kestrel. "*I'd have given them the whole world.*"

Ivo's hand tightened on hers.

"*I don't forgive her*," said Ilse. "*Not yet. Maybe not ever.*" Her voice was faint but steady. "*But I understand her. And I'd hate her less if I didn't.*"

---

"*It's not my decision*," said Lex.

They all looked at him.

"It's Antho's," said Lex. "It was his house. His door. His people on the slope." He looked at his brother. "And he nearly died."

Antho was sitting at the far end of the table. He hadn't said anything all evening. He sat with his hands folded on the table and his grey eyes on Kestrel Varn.

He looked at her for a long time.

"*Come here*," he said.

Kestrel came. Slowly. Across the library, past the table, past Harrow Ves's cold stare and Ivo's bent head. She stopped in front of Antho.

He stood up. He was taller than her by a head. He looked down at her face. At the bruise on her cheek where Ivo had hit her. At the split lip. At her amber eyes.

"*Was it worth it?*" he said.

Kestrel opened her mouth.

"*No*," said Antho. "*Don't answer. I heard what you said to Ivo.*" He looked past her, at Tamsin, standing by the door. Solid. Whole. Real. "*I asked my father that once. On a plinth at Duskhold, about a man in a crystal.*" He looked back at her. "*There's never an answer. Not a real one. You just carry the question for the rest of your life.*"

Kestrel's face crumpled.

"*I had a voice in my head for eleven years*," said Antho quietly. "*Telling me things. Telling me what I wanted to hear. Showing me my mother's face in the trees.*" He looked at her. "*Elric's had one too. Telling him he'd always be second. And it found you, didn't it. In the glass. It found the softest place in you and put its hand there.*"

Kestrel didn't answer. She didn't need to.

"*I know what that's like*," said Antho. "*Better than anyone here.*" He was silent for a moment. "*You can come.*"

Harrow Ves made a sound.

"*She can come*," said Antho, without looking at the Keeper-Captain. "*With us. To stop him.*" He looked down at Kestrel. "*But you walk with me. Not with Lex. Not with your brother. With me. Every step. Where I can see you.*" His grey eyes were very steady. "*And if you ever hear that voice again, in your head, telling you what you want to hear, you tell me. At once. Before you do anything.*"

Kestrel stared up at him.

"*Why*?" she whispered. "*Why would you—*"

"*Because somebody did it for me*," said Antho. "*On a woodpile. When I was fifteen.*" He glanced down the table at Lex. "*He was ten. He didn't know what he was doing. He just came and sat beside me in the dark every night, and didn't let go of my sleeve.*"

He looked back at Kestrel.

"*I'm not going to let go of your sleeve*," he said.

---

Correspondence arrived at midnight.

Nobody saw it come up the mountain. It simply walked into the library through the open door, a scruffy grey goat with a yellow eye and a letter tied round its neck with a piece of red string, and stood in the middle of the floor chewing on something it had found in the passage.

Everyone stared at it.

"*Is that*—" said Wren.

"Malachi's goat," said Antho.

"*Malachi has a* goat? *That delivers* letters?"

"Malachi has several goats," said Antho. "Only one of them delivers letters. The others are just goats." He crouched down and untied the string. The goat let him. Then it walked over to Brannoc and began, placidly, to eat the hem of his sling.

Antho unfolded the letter. It was written in a spidery, cheerful, slanting hand that Lex knew at once.

He read it aloud.

*Deep one—and smith, I expect you're there too, and whoever else you've collected; you always collect people, it's a family habit—*

*I felt the Key come whole. Everyone did. My fire went out for the first time in four hundred years. I relit it. It was very upsetting for the goats.*

*Now listen, because I'm old and I don't like writing long letters.*

*The Key can only turn in one lock. The Lock of the World. The place where your ancestor tied the first knot in the net, nine hundred years ago, when he wove the Lattice over the dark. It's beyond the Veil's edge, in the Sundered Lands, in a great crack in the earth that the old texts call the Umbral Rift. He'll have to walk there. The Key can cut through anything in the net, but the Rift is outside the net. It's where the net is tied off. He can't cut a keyhole into it.*

*It's a long walk. Forty days, perhaps. Maybe less. He's very motivated.*

*You can't stop him reaching it. Don't try. He's Tier IV now, and he's got Aurelian's shadow in his hand, and something whispering in his ear that knows the way.*

*But you can stop the Key turning.*

*Remember the verse. Ysmir's verse. The smith told me about it; Correspondence brought me his letter from the Bitter Hills, which I'll admit I read even though it wasn't addressed to me. "Only the blood that forged it can bar the way."*

*A bar, smith. You need to forge a bar. A lockward. Something to go into the Lock beside the Key and hold it fast, so it can't turn. Your ancestor would have known how. He was the best smith who ever lived.*

*But the Lock touches all four Wells. It's where the net was tied to the deep, and the deep is made of all of them. So the bar must be forged of all four, too. Fire from the heart of Flame. Iron from the heart of Stone. Water from the heart of Tide, to quench it. And breath from the heart of Gale, for the bellows.*

*Not from the Wells you know. From the Wellworlds. The places underneath the Wells, where each element is only itself. I've been to two of them. I didn't enjoy either.*

*You'll need someone who Speaks to each Well to open each door. You Speak to Flame, smith. The lightfoot girl Speaks to Gale, if I remember. That leaves Stone and Tide. You'll work it out. You always do.*

*Forty days. Go quickly.*

*—M.*

*P.S. "The blood that bars it, the blood will pay." I know. I read that bit too. I haven't stopped thinking about it. I don't know what it means. I don't think Ysmir did either. Prophecies are like that. They know the shape of things and not the size.*

*P.P.S. The goat's name is Correspondence. Please don't let the dragon eat it. It's very hard to train a new one.*

---

Nobody said anything for a while after Antho finished reading.

Correspondence ate Brannoc's sling. Brannoc let it.

"*Forty days*," said Oriel Sant at last, from her corner. Her white eyes were turned toward nothing. "*I see forty days. Thirty-nine, now.*" She tilted her head. "*Four doors. Four fires. And a dark place at the end, where something old is waiting.*"

"*That's not very helpful, Oriel*," said Ivo.

"*I'm not here to be helpful, boy. I'm here to see.*"

"Four Wellworlds," said Lex slowly. "Flame. Stone. Tide. Gale."

"*I Speak to Gale*," said Wren. She'd taken her boots off the table. She was leaning forward. "*Malachi's right. I got there before Duskhold. Speaking. I can open that one.*"

"And I Speak to Flame."

"*Stone*," said Antho. He was frowning. "*You're at Answering. Not Speaking. And I've never heard Stone at all.*"

"I'll get there," said Lex. "I'll have to."

"*And Tide*." Seraphine was looking at her notes. "*Who Speaks to Tide? Antho, you're only at Listening.*" She looked up. "*Does anyone here Speak to Tide?*"

Silence.

And then, from the doorway, a small, damp, embarrassed voice:

"*Um*."

Everyone turned.

Teo Brask was standing in the doorway of the library in his nightshirt. Dripping. A small puddle forming around his bare feet on the stone floor. He'd obviously been listening at the door for some time. He'd gone bright red.

"*Teo*," said Antho. "*You're supposed to be in bed.*"

"*I know, Master Antho. Sorry, Master Antho.*" Teo swallowed. "*It's only—I don't know if it's Speaking. I don't know what Speaking is.*" He looked down at the puddle around his feet. "*But the water talks to me. All the time. It never stops. And sometimes I talk back.*" He went even redder. "*And sometimes it answers.*"

Antho stared at him.

"*What does it say?*" said Lex.

Teo thought about it. Seriously. A round-faced fifteen-year-old in a wet nightshirt, frowning at the floor.

"*Mostly*," he said, "*it says it misses the sea.*"

---

They left two days later. On the fortieth morning before the end. On dragons.

Vharuun carried most of them. She'd recovered enough to fly now, though not fast, and her vast ragged back could take a dozen people if they didn't mind holding on to scales the size of shields. Dorran and Oriel and the twins and Seraphine (who had refused, flatly, to be left behind: *I'm the only one who's read about the Wellworlds, Antho, the only one, I've read everything there is, which is three paragraphs and a very bad poem*). Teo, wrapped in a borrowed coat, white with terror and excitement, clutching a scale. Brannoc and Hedda stayed behind. Brannoc had wanted to come. Hedda had sat on him.

Ashka carried Antho and Lex. And Kestrel, behind Antho, with her hands on his waist. Where he could see her.

Tamsin rode Vharuun, beside Dorran. He'd been very quiet since the night of the Blade. He looked at his sister, sometimes, across the camp. With an expression Lex couldn't read.

And Wren didn't ride at all. She stepped up into the morning air above the Hollowmaw and stood there on nothing, with her red braid blowing and her bow on her back, grinning down at them.

"*Keep up*," she said.

Arion stood at the mouth of the cave to watch them go. With Pim beside him, and Odile, and the other students. Lex looked down at him from Ashka's back as the dragons lifted off the slope.

His father raised one hand.

Lex raised his.

*Two hundred years*, he thought. *You'll tell me. When we get back. Both of us. You'll tell us everything.*

*[Will he?]* said Ember quietly.

*He'll have to*, thought Lex. *There's nothing left to hide behind.*

The Hollowmaw fell away below them. The great cleft in the mountain, glowing gold. One thousand one hundred and eleven lanterns. And at the bottom of it, a little clay lamp. And a cracked black door. And on the other side of it, their mother. Holding.

And on this side, at last, their father.

They flew south and west. Toward Ashbourne. Toward the first door.

Toward fire.
