# The Awakening Darkness

The Ashbourne Forge had twenty anvils, and for nineteen days not one of them stopped ringing.

Lex had never seen anything like it. Not at Duskhold. Not at the Hollowmaw, the night they forged the Lockward. Forty students at twenty anvils, in shifts, day and night. Brannoc at the great hearth with his arm out of its sling at last, bellowing orders like a sea captain in a gale. Hedda at a long trestle table down the middle of the hall with a dozen pots of glowing paint and a dozen brushes and three apprentices she'd stolen from the Millbridge chandlers, painting runes. *Hush. Hush. Hush*. Twelve to a lantern. Over and over.

And the lanterns. Everywhere. Hanging from every rafter. Stacked on every bench. Packed in straw in crates by the door, waiting for carts. Tin lanterns and iron lanterns and brass ones, little clay lamps like Seren's, bells of copper and bronze with the runes cut into their rims.

Four thousand Stones in Caldor.

They'd made eleven hundred.

---

"*It's not enough*," said Tilda Crane.

She said it at the anvil next to Lex's, on the nineteenth night, with sweat running down her freckled face and her apron scorched black and her arms trembling. She'd been working for nineteen hours. Everyone had. She didn't stop hammering when she said it. *Tink. Tink. Tink*.

"*We're not going to make it*," she said. "*Are we.*"

"No," said Lex.

She looked up at him. Then back down at the lantern-frame on her anvil.

"*Then why are we doing it*?"

He didn't answer for a while. He kept hammering. The frame on his own anvil was a lantern for Harrowgate. The third. The first two had cracked in the night when the song got loud. He'd made this one heavier. Twenty runes instead of twelve.

"Because every one we make," he said at last, "is one village that sleeps through the night."

Tilda thought about that. *Tink. Tink. Tink*.

"*Eleven hundred villages*," she said.

"Eleven hundred villages."

"*That's a lot of people sleeping*."

"Yes."

She nodded. Slowly. And kept hammering.

---

The song was getting louder.

Lex felt it every night. Through the Root. Through every thread. At midnight, when Malachi's fire went out, three hundred miles away in the Stillwater. A whisper. Coming up through the tithe-threads in every Stone in the world. Soft. Beautiful. A man's voice, singing.

The Hush-lanterns helped. Where they hung on the Stones, the song went quiet. Lex could feel it: villages going still in the net, one by one, as the carts rolled out of Ashbourne and the lanterns went up. Queen Lysandra had commandeered every cart in Caldor. Roole's Keepers were riding them, day and night, from village to village. Fenn had ridden four hundred miles in a week.

But where there were no lanterns, the song got through.

And people got up in the night. And walked out to their Stones. And stood there, with their hands on the cold silver, humming.

Not just the sixty. Not anymore. The sixty walked every night now, every one of them, wherever they were. But others had started. People who'd never been unwritten. Never been anywhere near the dark. Unbound people, mostly. Bright ones. The song found them first.

*The bright ones first*.

---

Malachi talked to him while he worked.

The old man sat on an upturned bucket beside Lex's anvil, out of the way of the sparks, with his straw hat pushed back on his head and an apple in his hand. He'd sat there every day for nineteen days. He talked while Lex hammered. And Lex listened. With half his mind on the iron and the other half on the old man's voice.

He was talking about the Weaving.

"*It wasn't weaving*," said Malachi. "*That's the first thing. Everyone calls it the Weaving. The Lattice. The net. As if he sat at a loom.*" He bit into his apple. "*He didn't. He* forged *it.*"

*Tink*. Lex paused. "*Forged*?"

"*Like mail*." Malachi held up his gnarled hand, fingers spread. "*Ring by ring. Link by link. Every thread in the world is a ring, smith. A little ring of the river, bent round and closed. And every ring is linked through four others. And the whole thing hangs from the first knot, down at the bottom of the Rift, where you set your bar.*"

Lex had stopped hammering altogether.

"*On an anvil the size of a mountain*," said Malachi. "*The Old Man of the Ironbacks. That's what it was, before. His anvil. With the river running hot through the Wells for his forge-fire. And his own Root for the hammer.*" He was quiet. "*It took him a year. A year without sleeping. I brought him soup.*"

"*And the Ceiling*?"

"*Came after*." Malachi's face darkened. "*A year after. When the hunger had him. He went back to the anvil and struck every ring again. A second time. Tighter. With a little crimp in it.*" He made a pinching motion with his fingers. "*A Ceiling. And through every crimp, a little hole. For the Tithe to run through.*"

Lex looked at his own hands. At the lantern-frame on the anvil.

"*So to unmake it*," he said slowly. "*To take out the Ceiling. And the Tithe.*"

"*You'd have to strike every ring again*." Malachi looked at him. "*Every one. Every thread in the world. Uncrimp it. Close the hole. And link it back through, clean.*"

"*Millions of them*."

"*Millions of them*." The old man nodded. "*And you'd have to do it from where he did it. With the Root as your hammer. At the place where all the threads come together.*"

"*The first knot*."

"*No*." Malachi's voice was very quiet. "*The first knot's where the net hangs from. Where it's tied off. You've been there. You've seen it.*" He set down his apple. "*The place where the threads* start. *Where the hammer struck. Where the river comes up into the Wells. The Heart.*"

Lex stood very still at his anvil.

"*Every road goes to the Heart, smith*," said Malachi, the Last Listener. "*That's why he chose it. That's why he's buried there. That's why the song says come.*" His shifting eyes were very sad. "*He knows. He's always known. If you ever want to break his net, you'll have to stand exactly where he stood when he made it. With your hammer in your hand.*"

"*Right on top of him*."

"*Right on top of him*."

---

*[It's a trap*,] said Ember. That night. In the loft above the forge, where Lex had been sleeping for nineteen nights, in the gaps between shifts. *[It's a trap all the way down. He wants you at the Heart. And the only way to break the net is to go to the Heart. And he* knows *that.]*

*I know*.

*[He wrote the song so that whichever you choose, you end up there. Save the world from the singing, go to the Heart. Break the net and end the Tithe, go to the Heart.]* A pause. *[There's no choice. There was never a choice. He's built a road with no turnings.]*

Lex lay on his back on the straw pallet and looked at the rafters. Below him, through the gaps in the floorboards, he could see the glow of the great hearth. Hear the hammers. *Tink. Tink. Tink*. The night shift. Tilda and the Millbridge boys.

*Everything that's made has a flaw*, he thought. *Even a road*.

*[What?*]

*The man in the dream. At the gold anvil. He said, look for the flaw. Even it. Even me.* He closed his eyes. *He made the net. And the thing wearing him made the Ceiling and the Tithe. But it made them out of* him. *Out of his rings. His folding.*

*[And he was never very careful with his folding*,] said Ember slowly.

*Then there's a flaw. Somewhere. In the Ceiling. In the Tithe. In the road.*

*[And if there isn't?]*

Lex didn't answer.

---

It came on the twentieth night.

He was at the anvil. The Harrowgate lantern, the third, with twenty runes. He'd just quenched it. It sang in the slack tub, a high clear note, and he lifted it out and held it up to the forge-light, and it was good. The best he'd made.

And every Hush-lantern in the Ashbourne Forge cracked.

All of them. At once. Eleven hundred lanterns waiting in their crates. The ones hanging from the rafters. The ones on the benches. A sound like a thousand panes of glass shattering at the same moment. The runes on every one of them flaring white, and then going dark, and the glass splitting from top to bottom.

The hall went silent.

Tilda had dropped her hammer. Brannoc had turned from the great hearth with his mouth open. Hedda was standing at her trestle table with a brush in her hand and paint dripping onto the floor, staring at the lanterns.

And through the Root, through every thread in the net, Lex felt it.

Not a whisper. Not a song through the tithe-threads.

A *voice*. Coming up.

---

*[Lex*,] said Ember. *[Lex, the Stones. Every Stone. It's—]*

He felt it. Felt the tithe-threads, all over Caldor, all over the world, go *taut*. Like the strings of a harp when someone puts their hand on them. And then *pulled*. From below. Hard. All at once.

And something came up them.

Not the herald. Not the grey thing with the singing mouth. Something *bigger*. Coming up through every tithe-thread in the world at once, like water coming up a thousand pipes. Filling them. Stretching them. And at the top of four of them, in four places, *breaking through*.

He felt where.

Highcrest. The Great Stone. In the square at the foot of the Spire.

The Vale. Millbridge. The Stone on the green, twenty miles from Ashbourne.

The valley below the Hollowmaw. The Stone at Hollow's Foot.

And the fourth—

The fourth was strange. Not a Stone. He felt it come up the tithe-threads and then *veer*. Sideways. Away from the Stones. Toward something else. Something old. Something that wasn't a Stone but had been a breach, once, the oldest crack in the world, held shut for eleven years by a woman with her back against it.

*The door*.

---

"*Malachi*," said Lex. His voice came out strange.

The old man was already standing. He'd dropped his apple. His shifting eyes had gone black, all the way, and he was staring at nothing.

"*Four*," he said. "*All four. They've come up.*" He turned to Lex. "*The Cantor. In the city.*"

"*Highcrest*."

"*The rot. In the Vale. Close. Very close.*" Malachi's face was grey. "*The two-in-one. At your brother's mountain.*"

"*And the fourth*?"

Malachi was silent.

"*The one with no name*," he said at last. "*She's at the door. Your mother's door. On the other side.*" He swallowed. "*With her hand on the glass.*"

---

Lex was on Ashka in four minutes.

He didn't remember getting there. He remembered running out of the forge into the night. He remembered Ashka, who'd been sleeping on the village green beside Vharuun, already awake, already rising, lightning crawling along her flanks. He remembered Wren, coming down out of the dark sky above the forge where she'd been on watch, stepping onto Ashka's back behind him without a word.

He remembered looking back.

At the forge. At Tilda Crane, standing in the doorway with her hammer in her hand and the light of the great hearth behind her. At Brannoc and Hedda. At Malachi, small and brown and barefoot, on the green.

"*The rot*," Malachi had said. "*In the Vale. Close.*"

"*Go*," shouted Brannoc. "*Lad, go. Your brother. We'll hold here.*"

"*Brannoc, it's the Vale, it's twenty miles*—"

"*I know where the Vale is, lad. I've lived in it sixty-one years*." The old smith hefted his hammer. "*Go.*"

He went.

---

They flew north through the dark.

Faster than Ashka had ever flown. Lightning pouring off her. The air screaming past. Wren's arms around his waist. Below them, the dark land of Caldor, rushing past. And every few miles, a village. A Stone.

Lex could feel them. Every one.

Some were quiet. Hush-lanterns, hanging on them, the ones that had already gone out on the carts, cracked now, shattered like the ones in the forge. But the Stones were quiet anyway. Holding. For now.

Some were not.

He felt them. Villages where the song was coming up loud through the tithe-thread. Where people were walking out of their houses in the dark. Dozens. Hundreds. Gathering around their Stones. Hands on the silver. Humming.

And at the bottom of every tithe-thread, very far down, the voice. Singing. Beautiful. Getting louder.

*The bright ones first*.

---

Antho was at the Stone at Hollow's Foot when they came down out of the sky.

Lex saw him from the air. A small dark figure in the valley below the Hollowmaw, standing in front of the Stone with the Keeper's Lantern held high. Its shutter wide open. Its gold light pouring out across the dark valley. Twelve Hush-runes blazing white.

And in front of him, facing him across the Stone, the Twinned Knight.

---

It was enormous.

Taller than Dorran had been. Taller than anyone. A suit of armor, black and silver, old-fashioned, the kind knights had worn in the ballads Brannoc used to sing. A great helm with a narrow slit. A long cloak, torn and rotted. And *wrong*.

*Doubled*.

Lex saw it as Ashka came down. Two shapes. In one suit of armor. Overlapping. Like a drawing someone had traced twice, slightly off. Four arms. Two of them holding swords, long and black. Two of them holding shields. Two heads, inside the one helm; he could see them moving, through the slit, turning slightly different ways. And two voices. Coming out of the helm. Arguing.

"*Left*," said one.

"*No*. *Right*," said the other.

"*Left, brother. The small one's on the left.*"

"*The small one's behind the light.*"

Behind the light. Behind Antho. On the ground, beside the Stone.

Ilse and Ivo.

---

Ilse was on her knees. Her eyes were closed. Her hand was flat on the Stone. She was humming. The song. The beautiful song.

And Ivo was holding her. His arms around her from behind. His face pressed into her hair. Holding on. And *weaving*. Lex could see it. An illusion, flickering around the two of them. A wall. A door. A cage. Anything. To keep her from going down the tithe-thread after the song.

"*She came down here an hour ago*," said Antho. He didn't turn his head. His voice was very calm. "*Sleepwalking. Ivo followed her. He couldn't wake her. He sent Pim up to get me.*" The Lantern trembled in his hand. "*And then that came up out of the Stone.*"

The Twinned Knight's helm turned toward Lex. Both heads. Two pairs of eyes, glinting through the narrow slit.

"*Another one*," said one voice.

"*The heir*," said the other.

"*Not ours. The heir's not ours.*"

"*No. Ours are the two by the Stone.*" The helm turned back. Toward Ilse and Ivo. "*Two who won't let go.*"

"*Like us*," said the first voice.

"*Like us*," the second agreed.

---

"*What do you want with them*?" said Lex.

He'd slid down off Ashka. He was walking across the dark grass toward the Stone. Fire in his hands. Wren was up on the wind above him, her bow drawn, an arrow burning gold on the string.

The Twinned Knight looked at him.

"*We were brothers*," said one voice. Slowly. Almost dreamily. "*Long ago. Knights of the Ford. We held a river crossing. Against an army. Back to back.*"

"*For a day and a night*," said the other.

"*And the river rose*."

"*And we wouldn't let go*." A pause. "*Of each other. Of the ford. We held on.*"

"*And we drowned*," said the first voice. "*Together. Holding on.*" The helm tilted. "*And we went down. Into the dark. Still holding on. And the dark said, you'll never have to let go now. And it poured us into one.*"

"*One armor*," said the second voice.

"*One sword-arm*."

"*One*." Both voices together. "*Forever.*"

The helm turned toward the twins on the ground. Ivo, holding Ilse. Holding on.

"*They're like us*," said the Twinned Knight. Both voices. Almost tenderly. "*Two who won't let go. Our master says we can have them. To keep us company.*"

And it moved.

---

It was fast. Far faster than anything that big should be. Four arms. Two swords. Two shields. It came at Antho like a landslide, and Antho didn't move. He stood in front of the Stone with the Lantern held high, and the gold light hit the Twinned Knight's black armor, and the armor *smoked*.

But it didn't stop.

*Voices can't act through him while it's lit*. That was what the Lantern did. It stopped the song. It stopped the Murmur. It stopped the herald.

But the Twinned Knight wasn't a voice. It was a sword.

Lex was there. He didn't know how. He was between Antho and the swords with his own hands up, fire roaring off them, Flame at Commanding, everything he had. The black swords hit the fire and the fire *held*. Just. Gold and white and furious, a wall of it, and the swords grinding against it like iron on a whetstone.

Wren's arrow hit the helm. Burning gold. Right in the slit. The Knight jerked back. One head screamed. The other laughed.

"*The archer*," said the laughing voice. "*Brother, she's good.*"

"*She's in the way*," said the screaming one.

And one of the four arms swept up and *threw* a shield. At Wren. In the air. Spinning, black and enormous, like a millstone.

She stepped. Sideways. On the wind. The shield went past her by a hand's width and vanished into the dark.

"*She's very good*," said the laughing voice.

---

It was a feint.

Lex understood that too late. The shield, the swords, all of it. Something to look at. While the Knight's other two arms, the ones nobody was watching, reached past Antho. Past the Lantern. Past Lex's fire. Down. To the twins on the ground.

To Ivo.

They closed on him. Black gauntlets. On his shoulders. And *lifted*.

Ivo screamed. Not in pain. In *terror*. Because his arms were coming away from Ilse. Because the Knight was pulling him off her. And she was still humming, still on her knees with her hand on the Stone and her eyes closed, still going down the tithe-thread after the song.

And he couldn't hold on.

"*ILSE*—"

She opened her eyes.

---

Lex saw it. Saw her eyes snap open. Grey. Clear. *Awake*. Saw her look up and see her brother. Lifted off the ground. In the Twinned Knight's black hands. Being carried backward, toward the Stone, toward the black hole that was opening in it.

"*Ivo*—"

She lunged. She got his hand. His wrist. Both her hands, wrapped around his wrist. Holding on.

And for one moment, the Twinned Knight was pulling one twin, and the other twin was pulling back, and neither of them would let go.

"*Oh*," said the laughing voice. Softly. "*Oh, brother, look. Look at them.*"

"*They won't let go*," said the other. Wonderingly.

"*They're just like us*."

And the Knight *pulled*.

---

Ilse held on.

She held on with everything she had. Lex saw her face. White. Furious. Teeth bared. Her heels dug into the frozen grass. Her hands locked around her brother's wrist.

It wasn't enough.

The Knight was too strong. Two dead knights in one armor. It pulled, and Ilse came forward across the grass on her heels, and the black hole in the Stone opened wider.

"*Let go*," said Ivo.

She stared at him.

"*Ilse. Let go*." He was crying. "*You'll go too. It'll take both of us. Let go.*"

"*No*."

"*Ilse*—"

"*NO*. *I'm not letting go. You never let go. You sat up with me every night for six weeks. You held on when I was thirty-six percent gone. You*—"

"*Please*," said Ivo Marrow. "*Please. One of us has to stay.*"

And he did something Lex would never forget.

He *wove*.

An illusion. Small. Simple. The simplest one he knew. Over his own wrist. Where her hands were holding.

He made it look like nothing. Like empty air. The Lampshade. The old trick. *You make it look like the room's dark*.

And for one heartbeat, Ilse's hands believed it. Believed there was nothing there to hold.

And opened.

---

The Twinned Knight went down into the Stone with Ivo in its arms.

Fast. Like smoke sucked down a chimney. The black hole in the silver closing behind it. And Ivo's face, the last thing Lex saw, white and wet and looking back at his sister. Not afraid. *Sorry*.

And then the Stone was silver again. And the valley was dark. And Ilse Marrow was on her knees on the frozen grass, with her hands open in front of her. Empty.

She didn't scream.

She looked at her hands. At the place where her brother's wrist had been.

And then she put her face down on the cold grass at the foot of the Stone, and her whole body shook, and she didn't make any sound at all.

---

From the Hollowmaw, high on the mountain above them, Lex heard a sound.

Faint. A long way up. Through the rock. Through a mile of passage and a thousand lanterns.

A sound he'd heard once before. On the night of the Blade.

A door. Bulging.

"*Father*," said Antho.

He was already turning. Already running. Up the slope. Toward the cave. With the Lantern swinging in his hand.

Lex went after him.

---

The bell-shaped cavern at the bottom of the Hollowmaw was full of gold light.

All the lanterns, burning. One thousand one hundred and eleven. The black glass door at the bottom of the bell. Cracked across its face, the way it had been since the night of the Blade. Holding.

Arion was in front of it. On his knees. With both hands flat on the glass.

And on the other side—

Lex saw it. Through the glass. Faint. A shape. Pressed against the black from the other side. A hand. Long-fingered. Pale. Flat against the glass, palm to palm with Arion's.

And the lamp was leaning.

Toward the door. A finger's width. The way it had leaned every night for three years, when Seren came.

"*Seren*," Arion was saying. Over and over. His face pressed against the glass. Wet. "*Seren. Seren. You came back. You came back.*"

Antho stopped dead at the bottom of the passage.

"*Father*," he said. Very quietly. "*Father, that's not her.*"

Arion didn't hear him.

"*Father*." Antho's voice was steady. Cold. "*Look at the hand.*"

Lex looked.

A woman's hand. Pressed flat against the glass. Long-fingered. Pale.

And grey.

Faint. Blurred at the edges. Like a drawing someone had started to rub out. Like the thinned in the grey valley. Like the man with the rubbed-out face.

Like Isaura Kell, sitting on the stair at the bottom of the Rift, forty-one percent gone.

And behind it, through the glass, a face.

Not a face. A place where a face should be. Pale. Smooth. *Unremembered*. Lex's eyes slid off it. He looked, and couldn't hold it, the way you can't hold a word that's on the tip of your tongue.

And a voice.

Not Seren's. Not low and rough. Dry. Thin. Precise. A voice that had given orders for twenty years.

"*Lex Vey*," said the faceless woman on the other side of the door. "*I've been sent to keep the door. From this side.*" A pause. "*He says it's mine now.*"

The lamp leaned further. Toward her.

"*He says your mother isn't coming back*," said the Unnamed Warden.

And her grey hand on the glass *pushed*.
