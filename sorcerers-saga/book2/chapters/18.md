# The Wellworld of Stone

"*You're hitting it too hard*," said Dorran Vask.

Lex opened his eyes.

He was sitting cross-legged on a ledge of grey rock, high on the south face of the Old Man of the Ironbacks, with his palms flat on the cliff in front of him and the wind tearing at his coat. He'd been sitting there since dawn. It was past noon now. His legs had gone numb an hour ago. His back ached. His hands were raw from the cold stone.

And the mountain had said nothing. Nothing at all.

"I'm not hitting it," he said. "I'm not doing anything. I'm sitting here. *Listening*."

"*You're listening too hard*." Dorran was sitting on a boulder a few paces along the ledge, with his great hammer across his knees and his old leather coat buttoned to the throat against the wind. He'd been there as long as Lex had. He hadn't moved. "*Same thing. You're trying to make it talk. Like a plowshare. Pushing.*"

"I'm not pushing."

"*You are*." Dorran's great slab of a face didn't change. "*I can see it in your shoulders. You've been pushing since sunrise. Pushing and pushing.*" He shifted his hammer. "*You're in a hurry.*"

"*Of course I'm in a hurry*." It came out sharper than he meant. Lex took a breath. "Thirty-three days, Dorran. Oriel says thirty-three. Elric's walking to the Lock, and I've got two pieces out of four, and I can't open the next door until this mountain decides to talk to me, and it *won't*."

"*No*," Dorran agreed. "*It won't. Not while you're in a hurry.*"

Lex stared at him.

"*Stone doesn't hurry*," said Dorran. "*You know that. You said it yourself. In Ysmir's temple. You told me after. The mountain doesn't hurry, but it does, sometimes, agree.*" He looked up at the great grey peak rising above them, its summit lost in cloud. "*It's been here a million years, lad. It'll be here a million more. Your thirty-three days are nothing to it. Less than nothing.*" He looked back at Lex. "*You want it to talk to you? Stop counting.*"

---

He didn't stop counting. He couldn't. It was like telling himself not to breathe.

But he tried.

He sat on the ledge as the afternoon went on and the shadows of the Ironbacks crept across the valleys below. He put his palms on the cold rock. He closed his eyes. And he tried, very hard, not to try.

*[That's a contradiction*,] said Ember.

*I know*.

*[I'm only mentioning it.]*

*Thank you*.

He listened.

He'd been at Answering in Stone for three years. Since the moor, since the Umbral Wardens, since the night in Ysmir's temple when he'd asked the bedrock under the glacier to hold and it had, grudgingly, like an old man getting up from a chair. Answering meant the Well heard him and answered back. A little. When it chose to.

Speaking was more. Speaking was a conversation. Speaking was what he had with Flame: the fire leaning toward him, talking back, telling him things, listening to his answers. The Sunwell had spoken to him. The Hearth had spoken to him.

Stone had never once said a word.

He listened. The wind. The cold. The great grey weight of the mountain under his palms. Deep. Old. Silent.

*Please*, he thought at it. *Please. I need to come in.*

Nothing.

*Please. There's a key. There's a lock. There's something under the world that wants to come out. Please.*

Nothing.

He opened his eyes. The sun was going down. The valleys below were full of blue shadow.

"*Too hard*," said Dorran, from his boulder.

Lex put his face in his hands.

---

Ilse found him there after dark.

He hadn't gone down to the camp. The others were in a hollow below the ledge, around a fire: Kestrel and Tamsin, Antho with Seraphine, Wren, Teo. Oriel on her mule. Ivo, sitting apart, with a blanket around his shoulders. The two dragons were on the next ridge, curled together like a pair of storm-clouds.

Ilse came up the path alone, slowly, leaning on a stick. She was very faint tonight. Fainter than she'd been in the Wellworld days. Lex could see the rock through her shoulders when she passed in front of the stars.

She sat down beside him on the ledge.

"*Ivo's asleep*," she said. "*Finally. He sits up with me every night. Watching me. In case I fade while he's not looking.*" She pulled her coat tighter. "*He thinks I don't notice.*"

"I'm sorry," said Lex.

"*Stop saying that*." She didn't look at him. "*You gave him the bit for me. In the glass. Everyone keeps forgetting that part. You traded the second piece of the key for me.*" She was quiet. "*Nobody's ever traded anything for me before. Except Ivo.*"

Lex didn't say anything.

"*I'm thirty-six percent now*," said Ilse. "*Did you know? It's been going up. Slowly. Since the Hollowmaw.*" She held up her hand against the stars. He could see them through her fingers. "*Ivo's holding me. Every day. Remembering. But he's tired. And the key's whole now, and it's pulling. On all of us. All the fifty-five.*" She lowered her hand. "*I can feel it. Like a hook in my chest. Like someone very far away, slowly reeling in a line.*"

"Elric," said Lex.

"*Elric*." She was quiet. "*He's getting closer to the lock. Every day. And the closer he gets, the harder it pulls.*"

Lex looked at her. At her faint face in the starlight.

"How long?" he said.

"*I don't know*." Ilse shrugged. "*Long enough. Maybe.*" She looked at the cliff. "*So you'd better get this mountain talking, smith. Because I'd quite like to be all here for the end of the world.*"

---

He sat on the ledge all night.

He didn't sleep. He didn't move. He put his palms on the rock, and closed his eyes, and listened.

Not trying. He'd stopped trying around midnight. He was too tired to try. He just sat. And breathed. And let the cold of the stone come up into his hands, and his arms, and his chest.

He thought about Brannoc.

He didn't know why. It just came. Brannoc in the Ashbourne forge, ten years ago, when Lex was twelve and so desperate to be good at everything that he'd broken three hammers in a month.

*You're fighting it, lad*. Brannoc's big hand on his shoulder. Gentle. *Iron's not your enemy. It doesn't want to be a hoe or a horseshoe or a sword. It just wants to be iron. You're not making it do anything. You're asking it nicely to be something else for a while.*

*How do I ask it nicely?*

*You wait. You let it get hot enough. You let it tell you when it's ready.* Brannoc had shrugged his enormous shoulders. *You can't hurry iron. It knows when you're hurrying. It goes stiff on you. Like a horse.*

Lex sat on the ledge with his hands on the mountain and thought about iron. About waiting for the color. About the moment, every time, when the bar in the coals went from red to orange to the yellow of a summer sunset, and you just *knew*. Not because you'd counted. Not because you'd hurried it. Because it told you.

*I'm not making you do anything*, he thought at the mountain. *I'm just here. Sitting. Like Brannoc taught me. I'll wait until you're ready.*

*However long it takes.*

And he let go of the thirty-three days.

It was the hardest thing he'd ever done. Harder than the Lampshade. Harder than not reaching for the gold in the Glass Desert. He felt the count go out of him like a held breath. Thirty-three days. Elric walking. Ilse fading. His mother behind the door. He let go of all of it. Just for a moment. Just for now.

And sat. On a ledge. On a mountain. In the dark. With his hands on the stone.

Waiting.

---

The mountain spoke at dawn.

Not in words. Not in fire, the way Flame spoke. Not in a current, like Tide.

In *weight*.

He felt it come up through his palms. Slowly. So slowly. A pressure, from somewhere very far down, below the roots of the Old Man, below the bedrock, below everything. Rising. Not like a wave. Like a tide of stone. Like the slow, slow heave of a continent shifting in its sleep.

It took a long time to reach him. A very long time. The sun came up while he waited. The valleys below filled with gold. He didn't move.

And then it was there. In his hands. In the rock under his palms.

*...You... waited.*

Slow. So slow. Each word took a breath. A voice like the grinding of boulders in a riverbed. Like a landslide heard from very far away. Like the creak of the earth.

*...Nobody... waits.*

Lex didn't move. He didn't speak. He barely breathed.

*...The one who... wove the net... did not... wait.* A pause as long as a sunrise. *...He came... with his hammer... and his hurry... and he cut my bones... into Stones... and he tied his net... to me... and pinned me... still.*

*...He never... asked.*

*I'm asking*, thought Lex. As slowly as he could. *I'm sorry. I'm asking.*

The mountain was silent for a long time.

*...Yes*, it said at last. *...You are.*

And the cliff in front of him *opened*.

He heard Dorran stand up behind him. Heard the big man's breath go out of him in a long slow *huh*.

"*Told you*," said Dorran Vask. "*Too hard.*"

Lex laughed. He couldn't help it. He sat on the ledge with his legs completely numb and his hands frozen to the rock, and laughed until his eyes ran.

Then he looked at the opening in the cliff.

It wasn't a door. It was a *seam*. A long vertical crack in the grey rock, a pace wide and twenty feet tall, running straight up the face of the mountain. And beyond it, darkness. Not black. Deep grey-brown, like the inside of a stone. And from it, a smell. Cold, mineral, ancient. Like the air in a cave that has been sealed for a thousand years.

"*Four can go*," said Oriel Sant, from somewhere behind him. She'd come up the path on her mule while he wasn't looking. "*Same as the others. Choose.*"

Lex looked around at them. They'd all come up from the camp. The whole band of them, on the ledge in the dawn light, looking at the crack in the mountain.

"Dorran," he said.

The giant nodded slowly. As if he'd known.

"*Me*," said Ivo Marrow.

Everyone turned. He was standing at the back, with the blanket still around his shoulders. His face was grey with tiredness. He had his arm around his sister's waist.

"*And Ilse*," he said. "*Both of us. Or neither.*"

"*Ivo*—" said Ilse.

"*No*." He didn't look at her. He looked at Lex. "*She's thirty-six percent gone. The key's pulling her. I can feel it. Every night.*" His voice cracked. "*Stone holds things. Doesn't it? That's what it does. It holds things still. Holds things in place.*" He swallowed. "*Maybe it can hold her.*"

Lex looked at him. Then at Ilse. Faint and pale in the dawn, leaning on her brother.

"Yes," he said. "Both of you."

---

The Wellworld of Stone was heavy.

That was the first thing. The moment Lex stepped through the seam, he felt it come down on him like a yoke. Not pressure. *Weight*. Everything weighed more here. His arms. His legs. His head. The Heart-Coal in its iron box on his belt. The little glass bottle of the Quench in his coat. He staggered. Caught himself on the wall.

Behind him, Dorran grunted. Ivo swore. Ilse made no sound at all.

"*It's like*," said Ivo, through his teeth, "*carrying a cart. On my back. Uphill.*"

"*Walk slow*," said Dorran. He was moving carefully, deliberately, like a man walking through deep water. "*Don't fight it. Let it hold you.*"

Lex looked around.

They were in a cavern. Or the Wellworld was a cavern. There was no sky. Above them, far above, so far it was only a dim brown-grey haze, there was a ceiling of rock. Below them, a floor of rock. And between them, in every direction, rock. Walls. Columns. Pillars as thick as towers, rising from the floor to the haze above. Some of them were carved. Or worn. Or *grown*; Lex couldn't tell. They had faces in them. Huge, ancient, weathered faces, eyes closed, like the faces of sleeping giants.

And everywhere, running through the rock, light. Veins of it. Crystal. Glowing faint blue and violet and gold, threading through the stone like the veins on the back of a hand. They pulsed. Slowly. So slowly. Once every hundred heartbeats.

*Like a heartbeat*, thought Lex. *A very, very slow one.*

*[It's alive,]* said Ember. Very quietly. *[All of it. The whole world. It's one thing. One enormous, slow, sleeping thing.]*

---

They walked.

It took a long time. Everything took a long time here. Every step was a decision. Lift. Move. Set down. Like walking through mud, except the mud was the air, and the air was made of stone.

And as they walked, Lex noticed something. The light in the crystal veins. It wasn't just pulsing. It was *moving*. Very slowly. Along the veins. Flowing. All in one direction. Down. Toward something.

They followed it.

The pillars grew bigger. The faces in them grew older. More worn. Some of them had opened their eyes. Very slightly. Slits of crystal light, watching the four tiny figures walk past at their feet.

"*Giants*," whispered Ivo.

"*Sleepers*," said Dorran. He didn't sound surprised. "*The old mountain-kings. Before the net. Before anything.*" He was looking up at a face in a pillar the size of a castle. "*My grandmother used to tell stories. About the stone-folk who slept under the hills.*" He was quiet. "*I didn't believe her.*"

---

They found the heart after what felt like a day.

It was a pit. Like the Sunwell. A great round shaft going down into the floor of the cavern, so wide that Lex couldn't see the other side. All the crystal veins in the Wellworld ran down into it, pulsing, flowing. The light poured over the edge of the pit like a slow waterfall of glow.

And at the bottom, very far down, something *shone*.

Not bright. Not like the Heart-Coal. A deep, dull, steady glow. Grey and red and iron-dark. Like the color of metal in a forge, just before it starts to glow. Like the core of a cannonball fresh from the mould.

*...Heartiron*, said the voice.

It came from everywhere. From the floor. From the pillars. From the sleeping faces. From the weight on Lex's shoulders. Slow. Grinding. A landslide heard from very far away.

*...The bones... of the world... The first... iron... From before... the net... From before... the light...*

*...He took... some... once. The one who wove the net. To make... his Stones... his anchors... his pins.* A long, long pause. *...He did not... ask.*

"I'm asking," said Lex. Slowly. "For a little. A piece. To forge a bar. To stop a key from turning."

*...I know... what you want it for.* The voice was very slow and very heavy. *...I felt you... ask me... on the mountain... all night.* A pause. *...You waited.*

"Yes."

*...Then you may... have it.* Another pause, longer. *...If you... can carry it.*

---

Lex looked down into the pit.

It was a long way down. Hundreds of feet. Thousands. The walls were sheer, ridged with veins of crystal light flowing downward. And at the bottom, the Heartiron. Glowing dull red-grey.

"*How do we get down?*" said Ivo.

*...You do not*, said the voice. *...It comes... up.*

And from the bottom of the pit, slowly, something began to rise.

A lump. Rough and dark. The size of a man's fist. Not glowing now, as it came up out of the deep. Just dark. Grey-black and pitted, like a meteorite. Rising slowly through the air of the pit, borne up on nothing, turning a little as it came.

It reached the lip of the pit. It hung there, in the air, in front of Lex.

He reached out to take it.

And it *weighed*.

Not like iron. Not like anything. He got his hand under it and it came down into his palm like a mountain. His arm buckled. His knees hit the floor. His whole body bent toward the stone under him, as if the Heartiron were pulling him down into the earth.

He couldn't lift it. He couldn't even hold it. It was crushing his hand.

*...Heartiron*, said the voice, *weighs... what you carry.*

Lex gasped. "*What*—"

*...Not what it is. What* you *are. What you carry... in you.* The voice was very slow. *...Your guilt. Your grief. Your promises. Your hurry. Every... weight... you have ever picked up... and not put down.*

*...You carry... a great deal... Root-heir.*

---

He tried.

He got his other hand under it. He pushed with everything he had. Stone. Flame. Muscle. He was the strongest man he knew, after Dorran, and he'd swung a hammer every day of his life since he was twelve.

He couldn't lift it.

The weight of it was everything. Forty-eight names in a book against his chest. Fifty-five. Ilse, thirty-six percent gone. Forty-one graves on the Hollowmaw slope. The Bit, thrown across a crater. Kestrel's rag. His mother, behind a door, for twelve years, while he made horseshoes in Ashbourne and didn't know. A promise to the Undertow, binding. Millions of Ceilings. A whole world to remake.

He couldn't lift it. Nobody could have lifted it.

"*Give it here*," said Dorran.

---

Lex looked up.

The giant was kneeling beside him on the stone floor. His great slab of a face very calm. His huge scarred hands held out.

"*Dorran, no. You don't understand. It weighs what you*—"

"*I heard*." Dorran's voice was a slow deep rumble. Like rocks shifting in a stream. Like the voice of the mountain, almost. "*What you carry. Guilt. Grief. Promises.*" He looked at Lex. "*I've been carrying four thousand one hundred and nine children for twenty years, lad. Every one of them. In a book in my coat.*" He held out his hands. "*I know how to carry things.*"

"*It'll crush you*—"

"*Maybe*." Dorran didn't move. "*But I've been practising.*"

And he reached out and took the Heartiron out of Lex's hands.

---

Lex felt the weight leave him. All at once. Like setting down a cart he'd been pulling uphill for years. He fell backward onto the stone floor and lay there, gasping.

And Dorran Vask knelt on the floor of the Wellworld of Stone, with the Heartiron in his hands.

Lex saw it hit him.

Saw the giant's shoulders bow. Saw his arms tremble. Saw his great head bend forward, slowly, slowly, toward the floor. Saw the veins stand out on his shaved scalp like ropes. Saw his face go dark red, then grey.

He didn't drop it.

His knees cracked against the stone. His back bent. He was bowing over it, like a man bowing over a grave. Like a man praying. And the Heartiron sat in his cupped hands, small and dark and pitted, and weighed four thousand one hundred and nine children. And twenty-two years of Kindling-days. And a little girl at a Stone in Kessick, looking up at him and saying, *Why did you put the light out?*

"*Dorran*," said Ilse.

She'd come forward. Slowly. Through the heavy air. Leaning on her stick. Faint as smoke. She knelt down beside the giant on the floor. And put her faint hand on his bowed back.

"*Dorran*," she said again. "*I forgave you. In the Bitter Hills. Do you remember?*"

He couldn't answer. His jaw was locked. His whole body was shaking.

"*I meant it*," said Ilse Marrow. "*I meant it then and I mean it now. You only did what you thought was right. And then you found out it wasn't, and you stopped. And you spent twenty years trying to make up for it.*" Her faint hand pressed on his back. "*You've made up for it, Dorran. You made up for it a long time ago. You can put some of it down.*"

Dorran made a sound. Low. Terrible. Like a mountain cracking.

"*Not all of it*," said Ilse. "*I know you won't. You're too stubborn. But some. Put down some of it.*" Her voice was very gentle. "*Put me down. I forgave you. I'm not yours to carry anymore.*"

And Lex saw the giant's back straighten.

A little. Just a little. An inch. As if something had been lifted off it.

"*Me too*," said Ivo.

He was kneeling on Dorran's other side. Lex hadn't seen him move. His grey eyes were wet.

"*I wanted to kill you*," said Ivo. "*In the Bitter Hills. When we found you. I had my knife out. Ilse stopped me.*" He swallowed. "*I've hated you for a long time, Dorran. Even after. Even when you were carrying things for us and building forges and making us soup.*" He put his hand on Dorran's shoulder. "*I'm done. I'm putting it down too. Both of us.*"

Another inch.

Dorran Vask lifted his head.

His face was wet. His great slab of a face, broken nose, grey beard, all of it running with tears. He looked at Ilse. At Ivo. At the twins he had capped at fourteen, kneeling on either side of him, with their hands on his back.

And he stood up.

Slowly. Slowly. Like a mountain getting up from a chair. With the Heartiron held in both his cupped hands against his chest. Shaking. Grey-faced. Sweat running down his scalp.

But standing.

---

*...Ah*, said the voice.

The whole Wellworld seemed to breathe. The crystal veins pulsed, all at once, bright. The sleeping faces in the pillars opened their eyes. All of them. Hundreds of them. Slits of crystal light, turned toward the giant standing at the edge of the pit with the bones of the world in his hands.

*...He carries... what you could not... Root-heir*, said the voice of Stone. Slow. Grinding. *...Because he has learned... to put things down.*

*...That is... what weight is for. Not to carry forever. To carry... until you can set it down... somewhere it belongs.*

The way out was slow.

Everything here was slow. They walked back through the heavy air, between the pillars with their sleeping faces, following the crystal veins upward instead of down. Dorran walked in the middle, with the Heartiron held against his chest in both hands. He didn't speak. He didn't need to. He walked steadily, one great slow step after another, and the weight of the world in his hands didn't bow him again.

The faces in the pillars watched him go. All of them. Eyes open. Crystal light.

*...Go well*, said the voice of Stone, behind them, very faint now. Sinking back down into the deep. *...Mountain's child.*

They came out through the seam onto the ledge on the south face of the Old Man of the Ironbacks.

And it was night.

Lex stopped. He looked up at the stars. Then at the moon, which was a thin silver crescent low over the western peaks. It had been full. When they went in. At dawn.

"*How long?*" he said.

Wren was there. She'd been waiting on the ledge, sitting cross-legged on Dorran's boulder with her bow across her knees. She stood up when they came out. Her face was very still.

"*Six days*," she said.

Lex went cold.

"*You were gone six days*," said Wren. "*It felt like six hours to you, didn't it?*" She looked at his face. "*It was six days here. Oriel says the Stone runs slow. Slower than anything.*" She swallowed. "*Twenty-six days left, Hammer. Twenty-six.*"

Lex stood on the ledge and looked at the thin crescent moon.

*Six days*.

*[It's all right,]* said Ember. Very firmly. *[It's all right, Lex. You have three of the four. Three. In eleven days. Fire and water and iron.]* A pause. *[One more. And then the forging.]*

*Twenty-six days.*

*[Twenty-six days is a lot of days.]*

He looked at Dorran. The giant was standing at the edge of the ledge, with the Heartiron held against his chest, looking out at the dark valleys of the Ironbacks below. His face was calm. Calmer than Lex had ever seen it.

He looked at Ilse and Ivo. Sitting together on the ledge. Ilse with the little stone pebble held against her heart. Ivo with his arm around her. Both of them asleep, almost at once, leaning against each other, like children after a long day at the fair.

He looked at Wren.

"*Gale*," she said. "*Mine. Tomorrow.*" She lifted her chin. "*And I'm not going to take six days. I'm going to take six minutes.*"

Lex almost laughed.

"Where's the door?" he said.

Wren looked west. Toward the far end of the Greyspine. Toward the Teeth, black against the stars, where the world grew thin. And above them, very faint, a flicker. Lightning. Silent. Walking along the high ridges on long white legs.

"*Up there*," she said. "*Where the storms are born.*"

Behind her, on the next ridge, a vast ragged storm-dragon lifted her head and looked west too. And made a sound. Low and deep and rumbling, like thunder over mountains a hundred miles off.

Like someone who knew the way home.
