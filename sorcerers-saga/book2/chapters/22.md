# A Legacy Forged Anew

It came up out of the crack like smoke out of a chimney.

Not a shape. Not a body. *Absence*. A darkness so complete that the gold light pouring up around it seemed to bend away from it, the way water bends around a stone. It rose in long slow tendrils, curling, reaching. Dozens of them. Hundreds. Coming up out of the widening crack in the lid like the fingers of a hand reaching up through a grate.

And everywhere they touched, things went *out*.

Lex saw one brush the edge of the black glass lid. The glass didn't crack. It didn't break. It simply *wasn't there* anymore, where the tendril had touched it. A hole. A clean round absence in the world, with nothing on the other side.

*[That's it*,] said Ember. Its voice was shaking. *[That's what's under the lid. Lex. That's the hunger. That's the thing the Sunwell burned. That's—]*

*The Unmaker*.

*[Yes*.] Barely a breath. *[And it's wearing your ancestor like a glove.]*

Above the rising dark, standing on the light with his arms spread wide, Aurelian Vey smiled down at them all. Fair and gold and kind. And the darkness rose up around him like a cloak.

"*Don't be afraid*," he said gently. "*It doesn't hurt. Ask the big man on the stair. Ask the shepherd on the White Shore. It never hurts.*"

---

The pressure was gone.

Lex felt it lift off them. The great gentle hand that had held them all still. Aurelian didn't need it anymore. He didn't need them still. He had the dark coming up, and the dark would do what it did.

They moved.

Wren first. She always moved first. She went up into the air, stepping, with her bow already drawn, and loosed. An arrow burning gold. It went straight at the nearest tendril of dark.

And went *through* it.

Not passing through. Going *in*. The gold fire of the arrow vanishing into the black like a coal dropped into deep water. Gone. Not even smoke.

"*It eats it*," Wren shouted. "*Hammer, it eats the fire*—"

Kestrel burned. A great roaring gout of Ashari fire, red and gold, pouring off both her hands at the tendrils rising nearest to Tamsin. The fire hit the dark and *vanished*. Every bit of it. Swallowed.

Seraphine fired her crossbow. The bolt went into the dark and didn't come out.

Antho threw Void Bolt. Shade against hunger. Absence against absence. It was the only thing that *slowed* them. The black bolts hit the tendrils and the tendrils *recoiled*, just slightly, as if they'd touched something that tasted wrong.

But only slowed. Not stopped.

And the dark kept rising.

---

It went for Ilse.

Lex saw it a heartbeat before it happened. Saw a long tendril of absence curling out across the black glass, low, fast, toward the twins. Toward Ilse. Faint and grey, thirty-six percent gone, clutching the little stone from the Wellworld against her heart. The weakest thread in the cavern. The easiest to take.

"*ILSE*—"

Ivo threw himself in front of her. Of course he did. Arms spread. Weaving, desperately, an illusion of a wall, of a door, of anything. The tendril went straight through the illusion as if it wasn't there. Because it wasn't.

And someone else moved.

Grey. Faint. Ragged silver hair. Torn white robes.

Isaura Kell came across the black glass faster than Lex had ever seen anyone move. Faster than Wren. She didn't run. She just *went*. As if she'd been waiting for it. As if she'd known, the whole way down, that this was where she was going.

She shoved Ivo aside. Shoved Ilse aside. Stood where they'd been, with her arms at her sides and her chin up and her sunken eyes open.

And the dark took her.

---

It wrapped around her the way a wave wraps around a rock. Slowly. Almost gently. Black tendrils curling up around her legs, her waist, her chest. She didn't fight. She didn't scream.

She looked at Lex.

"*Vask*," she said. Her voice was very steady. "*Tell his twelve villages. When you stand beside the Stone.*" The dark was at her throat. "*Tell them the High Warden who gave the order was sorry too.*"

"*Kell*—"

"*He carried me*," said Isaura Kell. "*Nobody ever carried me.*"

And the dark closed over her face.

And she was gone. Not into nothing. *Down*. Lex saw it. The tendril drawing back into the crack in the lid, slowly, with something faint and grey wrapped inside it. Going down. Into the gold light. Into whatever was under the bottom of the world.

*She's mine*, said the voice from below. Warm. Pleased. *Nobody remembers her. She's mine.*

And above the crack, Aurelian Vey smiled.

---

Lex looked at the Ledger.

He didn't know why. His hand went to it, against his chest. He pulled it out and opened it to the last page, there on the tilting lid in the gold light, with the dark rising around him.

At the bottom of the page. Under the five soldiers. In the narrow elegant hand.

*Isaura Kell, of Highcrest. Fifty-eight. High Warden. She was carried once.*

He closed the book.

*Not in the Lattice anymore*, he thought. *Not cut. Not unwritten. Taken. Below. Where the Key can't reach.*

*[She saved Ilse*,] said Ember. Very quietly. *[Lex. She chose.]*

*Dorran's anchor*.

*[Some anchors outlast the hand that set them*.]

---

Elric hadn't moved.

He was still kneeling beside the Key. In the middle of the rising dark, with the crack in the lid widening around him and the tendrils curling past him. They didn't touch him. They went around him the way water goes around a post. As if he weren't there. As if he weren't worth eating.

He was looking at the place where Kell had been.

"*Isaura*," he said. Barely a whisper.

Then he looked up. At Aurelian. Standing on the light above the crack, with the dark rising around him like a cloak, smiling down at everything.

"*You said*," said Elric Thorne, "*you said she'd be safe. At the end. You said everyone who followed me would be safe.*"

Aurelian glanced down at him. Mildly. The way you'd glance at a dog that had barked.

"*Did I?*" he said. "*I don't remember. I say so many things.*"

And looked away again.

Lex saw Elric's face.

It wasn't the face of a man who had been told he was second. That had been before. When Aurelian had turned his back and said *you'll be nothing at all*. That face had been broken.

This face was something else.

This face was *awake*.

---

"*Lex*," said Elric.

Lex was on his knees on the tilting lid, ten paces away. The Lockward was on the glass beside him where he'd dropped it. He'd been reaching for it. Antho was beside him, throwing Void Bolt after Void Bolt at the rising dark, his face grey, his Lantern almost out. Wren was above them, shooting arrows that vanished. Kestrel had Tamsin behind her and was burning, burning, pouring fire into the dark and watching it disappear.

"*Lex*," said Elric again. Louder. "*The bar. Give me the bar.*"

Lex stared at him.

"*It won't go in*," said Elric. "*Not while the Key's turned open. You know that. You made it.*" His black eyes were very clear. "*The Key has to be turned back. Closed. And then the ward goes in beside it. And then it can never turn again.*"

"*I know*—"

"*And you can't turn it back*." Elric's voice was flat. Steady. A teacher's voice. The voice that had explained mana-shaping on the Old Founders' Road. "*Not from there. It's my Key. I turned it. My hand's on it. If you try, you'll have to take it from me first, and I won't let go, and by the time you've pried my fingers off it, it'll be too late.*" He looked at the dark rising around him. "*It's already nearly too late.*"

"*Then let go*."

"*No*." Elric looked at him. "*I'm going to turn it back. Myself. And you're going to give me the bar.*"

---

*He's lying*.

Lex felt it. Felt every instinct in him say it. *He's lying. He's always lied. He opened the wards at Fort Caldris. He took Father's crystal. He took fifty-five people. He took Ilse. He gave Kestrel a choice and made her choose wrong.*

*He's lying*.

And under it, faint, from somewhere very far back, a dry warm voice by a campfire in the Greyspine. A clever tired old man in a tattered cloak, explaining the Lattice to a terrified boy who'd just found out he could set things on fire.

*I always tell you exactly what I want. It's the Veys who lie.*

"*Why*?" said Lex.

Elric was quiet for a moment. The dark rose around him. The crack in the lid widened. Gold light poured up past his face.

"*Because it said I'd always be second*," he said. "*And your mother said it was lying.*" He looked up at Aurelian, standing on the light, not looking at him. "*And I'd like to find out which of them was right. Before the end.*"

His black eyes came back to Lex.

"*Give me the bar, Lex. Let me go first.*"

---

*[Lex*,] said Ember. *[The cost. The panel. Cost of setting. The blood pays.]*

*I know*.

*[If he sets it—]*

*I know*.

*[He's not blood. It might not work. It might not take him. It might take you anyway, because you forged it. Or it might—]*

*I know, Ember*.

He looked at the Lockward. On the black glass. Dark grey, almost black. Notched like a mountain range. With a thin line of gold runes down its face. Steadfast. Bulwark. Hedda's nameless one. Arion's Wellscript. And at the very end, near the hilt, in Lex's own hand: *STAY*.

*The bearer cannot be severed from those he holds dear*.

He'd made it to stop Elric. Every strike. Every breath of the bellows. Every drop of the Quench. He'd made it so that Elric Thorne could never turn the Key.

And now Elric was asking for it.

Lex picked it up.

He looked at Elric. Kneeling beside the Key, with his young face and his black eyes and his hands on the bow. Waiting. Not reaching. Just waiting.

"*If you're lying*," said Lex, "*I'll stop you. I don't know how. But I will.*"

"*I know*," said Elric. And something moved at the corner of his mouth. Almost a smile. "*You always do. You and your brother both. It's the most infuriating thing about you.*"

Lex threw him the Lockward.

---

Elric caught it.

And Aurelian turned his head.

Lex saw it happen. Saw the kind fair face turn, slowly, from the rising dark, to look down at the man kneeling beside the Key. Saw the gold eyes see what was in Elric's hand.

And saw the face *slip*.

Not a flicker this time. A *slide*. The beautiful tired features of the smith who sang at his anvil, sliding off the thing underneath like a mask sliding off a wet face. And for three heartbeats, standing on the light above the crack in the world, there was nothing there at all. A hole. A hunger. A shape cut out of reality, Aurelian-sized, with nothing inside it.

"*ELRIC*."

The voice wasn't kind anymore. It was *enormous*. It came up out of the crack and out of the dark and out of the gold and out of every thread in the knot overhead, all at once, so loud that the black glass under Lex's knees cracked. So loud that Wren fell out of the air. So loud that the Lantern in Antho's hand went out.

"*ELRIC THORNE. PUT. THAT. DOWN.*"

Elric looked up at it.

At the hole in the world, standing on the light. At the thing that had whispered in his ear for three years. That had told him where to dig. That had told him he deserved it. That had promised.

"*No*," said Elric Thorne.

And turned the Key.

---

It didn't want to turn.

Lex felt it. Felt the whole Hollow, everything under the lid, *push*. Against the Key. Against Elric's hands. The dark rising out of the crack surged up, toward him, around him, reaching. The tendrils that had gone around him like water around a post turned and came *at* him.

He didn't let go.

He knelt on the black glass with both hands on the bow of the Key, and his young face set, and his black eyes wide, and turned it. Back. Closed. Against the push of a thing that had been waiting under the world for nine hundred years.

It was too strong. Lex could see that. One man against the Hollow. A Tier IV man with stolen Authority and a stolen key, but one man. The Key moved a quarter-inch. Stopped. Started to slide back the other way.

And Elric did something Lex didn't expect.

He *let go* of what he'd taken.

---

Lex felt it through the Root. Like a dam bursting. Like a man opening his fists after three years of holding on.

Fifty-five threads. Sixty. Every soul Elric had cut out of the Lattice and swallowed. Every one that had made him younger, stronger, faster. The Authority they'd given him. The mana. The power. Everything he'd taken from them, he *gave back*. All at once. Through the Key. Not into himself. Into the *turn*.

Every one of them pushed the Key a little further.

*Mella Bell*. A quarter-inch.

*Hollis Penn*. Another.

*Tamsin*, no, Tamsin was already back, Elric had written him back at the Hollowmaw, but Lex felt the place where he'd been, felt it close like a healed wound.

*Odo Ferris. Bram Sowerby. Amira bint Hassa. Tarek al-Nur.*

The Key turned. Inch by inch. Back. Closed. And with every name, Lex felt something else. Felt it far away, through the knot overhead, through every thread in the net. Felt *threads*. Being written. Being woven back into the Lattice, strand by strand, out of nothing, the way Tamsin had been woven back at the Hollowmaw.

He looked down at the Ledger. Still in his hand. Still open.

The names were going.

One by one. From the bottom of the page. Fading. Not like ink washed away. Like a word being *crossed out*, gently, because it isn't needed anymore. *Garret Shale*. Gone. *Wil Brack*. Gone. *Bram Sowerby, of the White Shore. He named every one of his lambs after a star.* Gone.

Back.

---

And Elric was getting older.

Lex saw it. With every name. With every thread he gave back. The young face aging. Thirty. Forty. Fifty. Lines coming back. Grey coming back into his hair, at the temples, at the roots, spreading. His shoulders stooping. His hands, on the bow of the Key, going thin and spotted and old.

Sixty. Seventy.

The Key turned.

And his eyes—

Brown. Coming back. Out of the black. Like the sun coming out from behind a cloud. Brown. Warm. Clever. Tired. The eyes of an old man by a campfire in the Greyspine, explaining the world to a boy.

Eighty.

*ELRIC*, screamed the thing above the crack. *ELRIC, STOP, YOU WILL HAVE NOTHING, YOU WILL BE NOTHING—*

"*I know*," said Elric Thorne. His voice was old now. Cracked. Dry. "*I know. I've always known.*"

And the Key clicked closed.

---

The crack in the lid slammed shut.

Not slowly. Not gently. *Slammed*. Like a door in a gale. The black glass disc tilting back down, flat, the gold light pouring up through the crack cut off like a candle snuffed. The tendrils of dark that had risen out of it severed, all at once, at the base. They hung in the air for a moment, writhing. Then they fell. And dissolved. And were gone.

And Aurelian Vey—

The thing standing on the light above the crack—

The light under its feet went out.

It hung there for a moment. In the air above the closed lid. Fair and gold and beautiful again, the mask back on. And it looked down at Elric, old and grey and kneeling beside the Key, with an expression Lex would never forget.

Not fury. Not hate.

*Surprise*.

As if, in nine hundred years, it had never once been told no by someone it thought it owned.

And then it *fell*.

Down. Into the lid. Through the black glass, as if the glass were water. The beautiful form going down into the dark, slowly, its gold eyes fixed on Elric the whole way. Going. Going.

*This isn't the end*, it said. Very softly. From below. Fading. *Elric. Lex. Antho. All of you.*

*I've waited nine hundred years.*

*I can wait a little longer.*

And it was gone.

---

"*The bar*," said Elric. "*Lex. Quickly. Before it pushes again.*"

He was holding up the Lockward. His hands shaking. Old hands. So old. Lex came across the black glass and knelt beside him.

The Key stood in the Lock. Turned closed. Its looped bow sticking up out of the lid. And beside it, in the black glass, a second slot. Narrow. Jagged. Notched like a mountain range.

The shape of the Lockward.

*It was always there*, Lex realized. *Aurelian made the Lock with a place for a ward. In case. In case someone needed to bar it.*

*Before whatever happened to him happened*.

Elric held the Lockward over the slot. He looked at Lex.

"*Blood of the forger's line*," he said. "*That's what it says, isn't it. On the panel.*"

"*Yes*."

"*I'm not blood*." Elric smiled. A thin, tired, old man's smile. "*Never was. Never will be. Forty years, standing next to your father, and not a drop.*"

He held up his left hand. Still in its glove. The fine grey glove he'd worn at Ysmir and the Glass Desert and the Hollowmaw. And Lex saw the palm of it. Stained. Brown. Dried.

"*I kept the rag*," said Elric. "*Kestrel's rag. With your blood on it. I wiped it on my glove at the Hollowmaw. Before I gave it back.*" He shrugged. A tiny movement. "*I don't know why. I suppose I thought it might come in useful.*" His brown eyes were very bright. "*And I have nine points of your father's Authority. That I stole from him in the crystal, four years ago. The Root still thinks they're his.*"

"*Elric*—"

"*Blood of the forger's line*," said Elric Thorne. "*On my hand. And the Root of the forger's father, in my thread.*" He looked at Lex. "*Close enough, I think. Close enough to fool a lock.*"

"*It'll take you*." Lex's voice cracked. "*The cost. The blood pays. You know what that means.*"

"*I know*."

"*It's supposed to be me*."

"*I know*." Elric looked at him. "*That's why.*"

---

Lex couldn't speak.

Elric looked down at the Lockward in his hands. Dark grey, notched, with its thin line of gold runes. His thumb found the last one. Near the hilt. In Lex's hand.

*STAY*.

"*The bearer cannot be severed from those he holds dear*," said Elric quietly. "*I read it. On the panel at Avaloria, when you put it on your blade. I was standing behind you. You didn't know.*" He traced the rune with his thumb. "*I thought it was the most sentimental thing I'd ever seen. I thought, that boy's going to get himself killed being sentimental.*"

He looked up.

"*I was right*," he said. "*You will. Someday.*" His brown eyes were wet. "*Just not today.*"

And he pressed the Lockward into the slot beside the Key.

---

It went in like a key into a lock.

Smoothly. Perfectly. Every notch finding every gap, every tooth finding every space. Sliding down into the black glass of the lid until only the hilt showed, a short dark-grey bar standing up beside the looped bow of the Key of Shadows. Side by side.

And it *sang*.

The same note it had sung in the Hollowmaw, when Lex quenched it. Pure and high and clear. Like a bell struck once in a silent church. It rang out through the cavern, through the black rock walls, up through the Rift, out across the Sundered Lands.

And the knot overhead *tightened*.

Lex felt it. The great tangled ball of silver and gold, every thread in the world, pulling *in*. Snug. Secure. The first strand, the one that had slipped, settling back into place. And the whole net, everywhere, shivering once, like a horse settling after a fright.

And then still.

---

Elric was fading.

Lex saw it. Not like the Unnamed. Not grey. Not blurred. *Gold*. A soft gold light coming up out of him, out of his old thin hands where they rested on the Lockward's hilt, out of his chest, his face. Rising. Like sparks from a forge going up a chimney.

Going up toward the knot.

"*Elric*." Lex had his hands on the old man's shoulders. He didn't remember putting them there. "*Elric, no. No, there has to be*—"

"*There isn't*." Elric's voice was very faint now. Very old. "*The blood pays. That's the cost. Ysmir wrote it, and she didn't make things up.*" He smiled. "*It's all right, Lex. It doesn't hurt. Ask anyone.*"

"*You don't have to*—"

"*I do*." Elric looked up. At the knot. Gold and silver, pulsing, a hundred feet overhead. "*Someone has to hold it. The bar. Someone has to stay down here and keep it in.*" The gold light was coming up out of him faster now. His hands were going faint on the hilt. "*It'll be me. I'll be in the knot. Holding the ward. Forever.*"

He laughed. A dry, cracked, old man's laugh.

"*First*," he said. "*At last. At the very bottom of the world, where nobody will ever see it.*" He shook his head. "*That's about right, isn't it.*"

---

Antho had come. Lex didn't know when. He was kneeling on Elric's other side. His face was grey. The Lantern in his hand was dark.

"*Elric*," he said.

The old man turned his head. Slowly. And looked at him.

"*Antho*," he said. "*You had the croup. When you were two. Your father was in the Spire and your mother was beside herself. I sat up with you all night. Singing.*" His fading face crinkled. "*I couldn't sing. Neither can you. It must be catching.*"

Antho didn't say anything.

"*I'm sorry*," said Elric. "*For the breach. For Duskhold. For your Clarity.*" His brown eyes held Antho's. "*I knew what it would do to you. When I broke it. I knew. I did it anyway.*"

"*I know*," said Antho.

"*I don't ask you to forgive me*."

"*I know*." Antho was quiet. "*I'm not going to.*"

"*Good*." Elric almost smiled. "*You shouldn't. Your father would. That was always his trouble.*"

He turned back to Lex.

The gold was pouring out of him now. Up toward the knot. His hands were almost gone. His face was going faint at the edges.

"*Tell Arion*," he said.

Lex leaned closer.

"*Tell him I went first*." Barely a whisper. "*Tell him he was right. All those years. I wasn't ready. For Tier IV. For the Root. For any of it.*" A long, slow breath. "*I am now. Tell him that. Tell him, old friend, I'm ready now.*"

"*I'll tell him*."

"*And give him these*."

Lex felt it. Through the Root. Nine small bright points of gold, coming out of the fading old man in front of him. Warm. Familiar. *Arion's*. Rising out of Elric's chest and drifting, slowly, into Lex's hands. Like coals passed from one hearth to another.

"*He'll want them back*," said Elric. "*He's very particular about his things.*"

And then he looked past Lex. Up. At the knot. At the gold light rising out of him toward it.

"*Oh*," said Elric Thorne softly. Wonderingly. "*Oh, look. It's beautiful from down here.*"

And the last of him went up.

---

Lex knelt on the black glass beside the Key and the Lockward, with his hands on nothing.

The old man was gone. Not a body. Not ash. Nothing. Just a kneeling-place on the black glass, and a pair of fine grey gloves lying empty on it, one of them stained brown on the palm.

And overhead, in the great tangled knot of silver and gold, a new thread. Lex could see it. Just one. Thin and gold and steady, running from the very center of the knot straight down to the hilt of the Lockward. Holding it. Holding it in.

He took out the Ledger.

He didn't know why. His hands just did it. He opened it, there on the black glass, in the fading gold light. To the last page.

It was empty.

All of them. Gone. Every name. Mella Bell and Tamsin Varn and Odo Ferris and Hollis Penn and Bram Sowerby and Amira bint Hassa and Tarek al-Nur and Garret Shale and Wil Brack. All sixty. Crossed out, gently, one by one, because they weren't needed anymore. Because they were *back*.

Except two.

At the bottom of the page. In the narrow elegant hand.

*Isaura Kell, of Highcrest. Fifty-eight. High Warden. She was carried once.*

And below it, very faint, in a hand Lex had never seen in the Ledger before. Not Ysmir's. Shakier. Older. A hand he knew from a letter half-burned in a grate at Fort Caldris, four years ago.

*Elric Thorne, of nowhere in particular. Sixty-eight. Second.*

*He went first, once.*

---

Wren found him there.

She came across the black glass with her bow on her back and a cut on her forehead where she'd fallen out of the air. She knelt down beside him. She didn't say anything. She looked at the empty gloves. At the Lockward. At the book in his hands.

She read the last line over his shoulder.

And put her arms around him.

Behind them, Lex heard the others. Coming across the lid. Slowly. Antho. Seraphine, with her crossbow dragging. Kestrel and Tamsin, holding hands. Oriel, on her stick.

And Ivo Marrow, carrying his sister.

He stopped beside Lex. He knelt down on the black glass. He set Ilse down beside him, very carefully.

She opened her eyes.

They were grey. Clear. And *bright*. Her mouse-brown hair was mouse-brown. Her patched green coat was green. Her face was pink with color, and her hands, when she held them up in front of her face, were solid all the way through.

"*Ivo*," she said. Wonderingly. "*Ivo, I can't see through my fingers.*"

Ivo made a sound.

"*I can't see through my fingers*," said Ilse Marrow again. And started to laugh. And cry. And her brother put his arms around her and his face in her hair and held on, and they knelt on the black glass at the bottom of the world, laughing and crying together, the way they'd done on the floor of the Wellworld of Stone.

The Rift was falling.

Lex felt it through Stone. A shudder, far above. The great black walls, already cracked, already crumbling, beginning to come down. The knot had tightened. The lid was sealed. The Rift had been held open by the Key's unlocking, and now the Key was barred, and the Rift was closing. Like a wound healing. Like a mouth shutting.

"*We have to go*," said Antho.

"*Go where*?" Seraphine was staring up into the dark. "*The stair's gone. It's eight days up. And it's falling.*"

Nobody answered.

And then, from very far above, faint, through a mile of falling rock, they heard it.

Thunder.

Not the thunder of the Rift coming down. Something else. Deep and slow and rolling, like a storm over mountains a hundred miles off. Getting closer.

And with it, crackling, bright, a sound like lightning walking.

"*Oh*," said Wren. Her face lit up. "*Oh, Hammer. Look.*"

Lex looked up.

And saw them coming down. Out of the dark. Through the falling rock. Two shapes. One vast and storm-blue and whole, with wings like the sails of a warship, breathing thunder at the boulders that fell toward her and scattering them like leaves. One small and bright as a spark, darting ahead of her, lightning pouring off her in every direction, lighting the whole Rift up white.

Vharuun. And Ashka.

Diving. Straight down. Into the bottom of the world.

*I SAID*, said the storm-mother, and her voice rolled down the Rift like the end of a storm, *THAT WHATEVER WAITED AT THE END, IT WOULD HAVE TO GO THROUGH ME.*

*I DID NOT SAY I WOULD WAIT AT THE TOP.*

---

They came out of the Umbral Rift at dawn.

On dragons. All of them. Clinging to the storm-mother's vast back and her daughter's narrow one, with the Rift collapsing beneath them and the Sundered Lands falling away on every side. Up through the last of the dark. Up past the floating slabs of broken earth. Up past the camp of the Eclipse Concord, which was empty now, its grey tents torn and its men fled.

Up into the sky.

And the sun was rising.

Lex saw it from Vharuun's back. Coming up over the eastern edge of the world, beyond the Sundered Lands, beyond the Veil. Gold. Ordinary. The plain gold light of an ordinary morning, after the longest night of the year.

The cracks in the sky over the Sundered Lands were still there. Black lines across the blue. But fainter. Narrower. Healing.

*[It's over*,] said Ember. Very quietly. *[Lex. It's over. The lid's sealed. The Key's barred. The sun came up.]*

*It's not over*.

*[No*,] Ember agreed, after a moment. *[It said it could wait.]*

*It's waited nine hundred years. It's patient.* Lex watched the sun come up. *And it's still at the bottom of the Root. The door's still open. A crack.* He closed his eyes. *And it's got Kell.*

*[I know*.]

*And Mam's still behind the door at the Hollowmaw.*

*[I know*.]

*And Father's two hundred years old.*

*[Two hundred and thirty-one*,] said Ember. *[I did the sums. From what it said. From what he said. From the portraits Wren won't tell you about.]* A pause. *[He'll tell you. When we get back. He promised.]*

Lex opened his eyes.

Wren was sitting in front of him on the storm-mother's back. Leaning against his chest. Asleep. Her red braid coming undone in the wind, a cut on her forehead, her bow across her knees. Behind him, Antho and Seraphine, holding each other. Kestrel and Tamsin. The twins, Ilse solid and laughing in her sleep. Oriel with her white face turned toward the sun, smiling faintly, as if she could feel it.

And beside them, Ashka, bright as a spark, flying close to her mother's wing.

*Not everyone came back*, thought Lex. *Dorran. Kell. Elric.*

*But most of us did.*

He put his arms around Wren. She murmured something in her sleep and pressed closer.

He held the Ledger against his chest with his other hand. Two names in it. Only two. And Dorran's book beside it. Four thousand one hundred and nine.

*Twelve villages*, he thought. *When we get home.*

*And then the rest of the world.*

The sun came up. And the storm-mother carried them west, toward home.
