# The Dark Alliance

Ember came back on the road north, somewhere in the green hills below the Scald, on an afternoon so ordinary that Lex almost missed it.

He was riding at the front of the little column on a tall golden Sunreach horse that hated him. Ilse was asleep in the saddle behind her brother, her faded head on his shoulder. Dorran walked. Oriel rode her offended mule. Kestrel rode at the back, a long way back, alone, and hadn't said more than ten words in a week.

And in the deep gold, at the bottom of him, something cleared its throat.

*[Well*,] said Ember. *[That was unpleasant.]*

Lex nearly fell off the horse.

*Ember*.

*[Hello, Lex.]* It sounded tired. Thin, like a voice after a long fever. But *there*. Humming, faint and steady, in the place where it had always hummed. *[I'm back. Mostly. I've had to swim up through about a mile of honey, and something very large and very patient sat on me for a week, but I'm back.]*

He didn't say anything. He couldn't. He rode with his eyes stinging and the reins slack in his hands, and the golden horse, sensing weakness, tried to eat a hedge.

*[Don't cry on the horse,]* said Ember. *[It's already got a low opinion of you.]*

*I thought you were gone.*

*[I thought so too, for a bit.]* A pause. *[It didn't hurt me, you know. That's what I keep thinking about. It could have. It had me right there, at the door, in its hand. And it was gentle. It put me down somewhere quiet and dark and soft, like a mother putting a baby in a cradle, and it hummed to me.]* Ember's voice dropped. *[I didn't like that at all.]*

*What was it humming?*

*[A lullaby.]* Very quietly. *[The lantern song. Your mother's song. In a voice that wasn't hers.]*

Lex rode for a long time without saying anything.

*[The door's still open,]* said Ember. *[A crack. I sat beside it the whole time. I can feel it now, under us. Like a draft.]* A pause. *[I think it only opens wider when you touch something of his. The Key pieces. His shadow. Things he made.]*

*The hammer?*

*[No. I checked. The hammer's—clean. Malachi's right about it. It's happy with you.]* Something like a smile. *[Aurelian made it before. When he was still just a smith who sang at his anvil. Before whatever happened to him happened. It doesn't remember the thing at the door. It only remembers the singing.]*

Lex put his hand on the little hammer at his belt. Worn smooth. Warm.

*Good*, he thought.

*[Lex.]* Ember was quiet for a moment. *[Kestrel.]*

*I know.*

*[She's been riding at the back for a week. She hasn't talked to anyone. Not even Tamsin.]* A pause. *[She's holding something. In her coat. She takes it out at night and looks at it when she thinks everyone's asleep.]*

*What is it?*

*[I don't know. I couldn't see. I was a bit busy being sat on.]* Ember's voice was very careful. *[But I don't think it's anything good.]*

---

They came to Highcrest on the eve of the masquerade, at sunset, up the long south road through the river valley with the golden city climbing the mountain ahead of them.

Lex saw her before he saw anything else.

A speck. Far up. Against the red sky, above the Spire. Then not a speck. A figure. Small and slim, in a patched coat, with a red braid streaming out behind her like a banner. Falling. No. Not falling. *Stepping*. Down out of the sky, stair by invisible stair, on the wind, faster than any stair had ever been taken, straight down out of the sunset toward the south road.

He was off the golden horse before he knew he'd decided to be. He was running.

She came down the last fifty feet without stepping at all. She just *dropped*. And hit him in the chest like a sack of grain thrown off a cart, and he caught her and staggered and they both went down in the dust of the road together in a tangle of arms and legs and bow.

"*You*," said Wren Ashdown, on top of him, with her hands fisted in the front of his coat and her face six inches from his. "*You absolute—you stupid, idiot, stubborn, enormous—*"

"I know."

"*A note. On a* pillow. *Like a—like a man in a bad ballad—*"

"I know."

"*Two months. Two months and not a word, and then a letter, and in the letter you say—you say—*" Her voice cracked. Her green eyes were very bright. "*You say it* properly. *In a letter. From a mine. When I can't even hit you.*"

"You can hit me now," said Lex.

She looked down at him. Breathing hard. Windburned and furious and so beautiful he couldn't breathe.

And then she kissed him. Hard. In the dust of the south road below Highcrest, at sunset, with the whole of the Unlit standing around them in a ring.

He kissed her back.

It went on for some time.

"*Ahem*," said Ivo Marrow eventually.

Wren lifted her head. She looked around at them. At the giant with the hammer. At the twin thieves, one of them pale and faded. At the blind old woman on the mule. At the red-haired woman sitting very still on her horse at the back of the column, with her amber eyes narrowed.

"*Who*," said Wren, "*are all these people?*"

"Friends," said Lex. From the dust. "This is Dorran. That's Ilse and Ivo. That's Oriel. And that's—"

"*The fire-witch*," said Wren. She was looking at Kestrel. "*The one who's angrier than I am.*"

Kestrel looked back at her. A long, level, measuring look.

"*He said that?*" said Kestrel.

"*He wrote it in a letter.*"

Kestrel's mouth twisted. "*He's not wrong.*"

"*We'll see*," said Wren.

*[Oh, good*,] said Ember faintly. *[I'm so glad I came back for this.]*

---

His father was waiting at the gate of the Spire.

Arion Vey stood in the great arched doorway of the white tower, in his plain grey coat, with the last of the sunset on his lean face. He didn't come down the steps. He stood at the top of them and watched his son come up the street, with a red-haired archer holding his hand and a band of strangers behind him.

Lex stopped at the bottom of the steps.

They looked at each other.

*[He looks frightened,]* said Ember quietly.

He did. Lex could see it. Not in any one thing. In all of it. In the set of his father's shoulders. In the way his hands hung at his sides, not quite still. In his face, which hadn't changed at all in three years, and which looked older now than Lex had ever seen it.

"You got my letter," said Lex.

"Yes."

"Did you know?"

Arion was silent for a long moment.

"No," he said at last. "I swear it, Lex. I didn't know." He came down one step. Then another. "But I've heard it. For a long time. At night. Whispering. I always thought it was my own mind." He stopped two steps above his son, so their eyes were level. "I should have known. I should have guessed. I'm sorry."

Lex looked at him.

Three years ago, at the first festival, he'd have shouted. A year ago he'd have been coldly, quietly angry. Even two months ago, in the Queen's tower, he'd have stood with Antho and demanded *why didn't you tell us*.

But he'd been to the Glass Desert since then. He'd heard a kind voice in the bottom of himself call him *good boy*. He'd felt something vast and patient sit on Ember for a week. And he understood, now, standing on the steps of the Spire, what it might mean to hear that voice for years. For decades. Alone. At night. And think it was your own.

"It's in me too," he said quietly. "Now. The door's open. A crack."

His father closed his eyes.

"I know," he said. "I felt it. In the Root. The day you touched the bit." He opened them. "I've been sitting in my chair every night since, listening to it breathe."

Behind Lex, Wren made a small sound. He glanced back. She was looking at his father with an expression Lex couldn't read. Something careful. Something that knew more than it was saying.

He filed it away. For later.

"Come in," said Arion. "All of you. There isn't much time. There's something you need to know about tomorrow night."

---

They sat around the round table in the Queen's tower until long after midnight. The Queen. Arion. Elowen Dunmore with Oathkeeper across her back. Fenn, Roole's Keeper-Lieutenant, lean and freckled and very serious, with twenty Keepers waiting in the corridor outside. Seraphine was gone; she'd gone to the Hollowmaw two months ago and stayed. Lex felt her absence like a missing tooth.

And the Unlit. Sitting around the Queen's table in their travel-stained coats and their road dust, looking at the tapestries and the silver candlesticks and the Queen of Caldor herself with expressions that ranged from wonder (Ivo) to suspicion (Dorran) to complete indifference (Oriel, who couldn't see any of it).

Kestrel sat at the end of the table. Silent. Her hands in her lap.

"*The night of the masks*," said Wren, and told them. The three men in ash-grey. The Severance chain. The key-shaped brooch. *The Concord. When it's done, the night of the masks, everything changes.*

"*Sorn*," said Queen Lysandra. Very quietly. "*He's going to try for the Crown.*"

"Not the Crown," said Arion. "Not openly. He's cleverer than that." He laid his hands flat on the table. "He'll declare an emergency. The unwriter on the loose, the Queen too young, the Council deadlocked. He'll ask for a regency. *Temporary*. For the realm's safety. And with Kell's three hundred men around his house and the whole Council inside it, there'll be no one to say no."

"*And the Registration Act?*"

"Passed by decree. The first morning." Arion's voice was flat. "A list of every Unbound sorcerer in Caldor, in Sorn's hands. And Sorn's hands are Elric's hands."

The Queen looked at him for a long moment.

"*Then we don't go*," said Elowen. "*Simple. Majesty stays in the castle. We put the Guard on the walls. Let Sorn have his party.*"

"*If I don't go*," said Lysandra, "*he'll say I'm afraid. He'll say I'm hiding. And half the old houses will believe him, and the other half will wonder.*" She shook her head. "*I've spent three years trying to make them trust me. I can't hide in my tower the first time someone threatens me.*"

"*Majesty—*"

"*I'm going, Elowen.*" The Queen's voice was very calm. "*In a mask, and a gown, and a smile. And you're going to stand behind me with your father's sword. And we're going to let Varric Sorn make his move in front of the whole court.*" Her dark eyes went round the table. "*And then we're going to break it.*"

---

"*There's something else*," said Oriel Sant.

Everyone turned. The old woman had been so quiet all evening that half of them had forgotten she was there. She sat at the far end of the table in her red shawl, with her black thorn stick across her knees and her white eyes turned toward nothing.

"*Two fires*," she said.

Lex went still.

"*I see two fires tomorrow night*," said Oriel. "*One here. In a fine house, full of masks. And one far away. In the mountains. Where the lanterns are.*" Her thin dry voice didn't change. "*The one here is loud. The one there is quiet. The one here is the one he wants you to look at.*"

Silence around the table.

"*The masquerade's a diversion*," said Wren slowly. "*While everyone's looking at Sorn's house—*"

"*Elric goes for the blade*," said Lex.

He was already standing up. He didn't remember standing up.

"*The Hollowmaw*," he said. "*Antho. Seraphine. The students. Father, he's going for the—*"

"I know." Arion was on his feet too. His face had gone grey. "Roole sent three hundred Keepers to the Keep a month ago. They're on the slopes now. And Antho's back. With the storm-mother."

"*Three hundred Keepers against two pieces of the Key*," said Lex. "*Against Elric at Tier IV. Against Umbral Wardens.*" He was breathing too fast. "*I have to go. Tonight. Now.*"

"*It's three hundred miles*," said Elowen. "*Ten days' ride. Four on a dragon. You don't have a dragon.*"

"*I don't care, I'll—*"

"Lex."

His father's voice. Very quiet.

Lex stopped.

"There's a way," said Arion. "Through the Stones. The Lattice is a net. A net has threads. And a Root-holder, at Tier IV, can walk them." He looked at his son. "From the Great Stone in the Spire to the Stone at Kessick Falls, in the Greyspine. A day's ride from the Hollowmaw. I can do it. Once. For two people. It'll take everything I have."

"*Then do it now.*"

"*No*," said Oriel Sant.

Everyone looked at her again.

"*Not now*," said the old woman. "*If you go now, the fire here will burn. And it will burn your Queen, and your father, and everyone in this room.*" Her white eyes moved toward Lex. "*And the fire there will burn anyway. You won't be in time. He isn't there yet. He's waiting. For the masks.*"

"*How do you know?*"

"*I don't know*," said Oriel simply. "*I see. That's all I've ever done.*" She tilted her head. "*Break the fire here first, smith. Fast. Then go. That's the only way both fires go out.*"

Lex stood at the Queen's table with his hands clenched, and looked at his father, and at Wren, and at the Queen.

*[She's been right about everything so far,]* said Ember quietly.

*I know.*

*[I hate it when old women are right.]*

*I know.*

He sat down.

At the end of the table, Kestrel Varn hadn't moved. She was looking at her hands in her lap. And her face, in the candlelight, was the color of ash.

---

He found her on the castle wall, after.

She was standing at the battlements in the dark, looking north. The wind off the mountains lifted her red hair, and there was a faint red glow in it, very low. Tamsin stood beside her. Faint as smoke. Watching her face.

Lex came and leaned on the battlements beside her. He didn't say anything for a while.

"*You know*," said Kestrel at last. Not looking at him. "*Don't you.*"

"I know he offered you Tamsin."

"*Not just offered.*" Her voice was very low. "*He sent me something. In the glass. Before he left. While you were down in your Well.*" She reached into her coat. And took out a scrap of paper, folded small, creased with handling.

She didn't give it to him. She held it in her fist.

"*A place*," she said. "*A time. Tomorrow night. Where to find him.*" Her jaw tightened. "*And a promise. That if I bring him what he needs, he'll write Tam back. Whole. First thing. Before anything else.*"

"What does he need?"

Kestrel didn't answer.

"Kestrel. What does he need?"

She turned her head and looked at him. And her amber eyes were full of tears and fury and something that looked like hatred, though Lex couldn't tell who it was for.

"*You*," she said. "*Your blood. The blade's under a Vey door. Aurelian's own. Under your brother's floor. And the old man can't open it.*" Her voice broke. "*He needs Vey blood. Just a little. Just enough to wet the ward.*"

Lex didn't move.

"*Your hand*," said Kestrel. "*In the glass. When you cut it for the tower door. You wrapped it in a rag after. And threw the rag away, at the edge of the crater, when you'd healed.*" She swallowed. "*I picked it up.*"

He looked at her coat. At the place where her hand had been.

"*I've been carrying it for three weeks*," said Kestrel Varn. "*In my coat. Next to my heart. Every night, I take it out, and I look at it. And I think about Tam. Solid. Whole. Laughing.*" Her voice was barely a whisper. "*And I think about your brother. And his lanterns. And his door.*"

"Have you decided?"

"*No*." She laughed. A terrible little sound. "*No. I haven't. That's the worst of it, smith. I* haven't. *I keep thinking I'll throw it in the fire. And I don't.*"

Beside her, faint and pale, Tamsin Varn had put his face in his hands.

"*Kes*," he whispered. "*Kes, don't. Don't. Not for me.*"

She couldn't hear him.

Lex could. He didn't tell her. He didn't need to.

He looked at her for a long time.

"I'm not going to take it from you," he said at last.

She stared at him.

"I could," said Lex. "I'm bigger than you. And I've got the Root. I could take it." He looked out at the dark mountains. "But it's yours. Whatever you decide. It has to be yours."

"*Why?*"

"Because if I take it, you'll never know what you would have done," said Lex. "And you'll wonder. Every day. For the rest of your life." He pushed himself off the battlements. "I'd rather you chose. Even if you choose wrong."

He walked away down the wall. He didn't look back.

*[Lex,]* said Ember. Very quietly. *[That might have been the bravest thing I've ever seen you do. Or the stupidest.]*

*I know.*

*[Which one?]*

*I don't know yet.*

---

Sorn's house stood at the top of the Crown Terrace, the highest street in Highcrest below the castle itself. A great white palace of a house, with columns along its front and a garden that ran down the mountainside in tiers, and every window blazing with light.

It was full of masks.

Lex had never seen anything like it. Hundreds of people, the whole court of Caldor and half the wealthy merchants of the Lower Town, in silk and velvet and cloth-of-gold, and every face hidden. Masks of white porcelain and black lacquer. Masks of feathers, of gold leaf, of silver filigree. Masks shaped like birds and beasts and moons and suns. A man in a stag's head with real antlers. A woman whose whole face was a mirror, so that when you looked at her you saw yourself.

Lex wore a mask of plain dark leather, a smith's mask, that covered his face from brow to lip. It had been Ilse's idea. *If you're going to hide, hide as what you are. Nobody looks twice at a man pretending to be himself.* He wore a dark coat that Arion had found somewhere, too tight across the shoulders, and Aurelian's hammer in his belt under it.

And beside him, holding his arm, in a gown of deep green silk that made her look like a queen out of an old story, with a fox-mask of copper and red enamel over her eyes, Wren.

"*I hate this*," she said. Through a smile. Not moving her lips.

"You look beautiful."

"*I look like a cake. I can't breathe. There's a knife strapped to my thigh and it's cutting off the blood to my foot.*" Her smile didn't waver. "*And these shoes. Who invented these shoes. I'm going to find them and shoot them.*"

"You'd miss."

"*I would* not *miss*." She tightened her grip on his arm. "*You're enjoying this.*"

"A little."

"*I'm going to put an arrow in you later. Very gently. Somewhere fleshy.*"

He laughed. And she looked up at him from behind the fox-mask, and something in her face softened. Just for a moment.

"*Dance with me*," she said.

"I can't dance."

"*Neither can I. Dance with me anyway.*"

So they danced. Badly. In the middle of the great ballroom of Varric Sorn's house, under a ceiling painted with gods and chandeliers blazing with a thousand candles, surrounded by masks. He trod on her feet twice. She trod on his four times, at least one of them on purpose. Neither of them knew the steps. They just held onto each other and moved, more or less, in time with the music, and for a few minutes, Lex forgot everything. Forgot keys and doors and kind voices. Forgot the Hollowmaw, three hundred miles away in the dark.

Just Wren. In his arms. In a gown she hated. Laughing at him through a fox-mask.

"*I missed you*," she said. Very quietly. Under the music.

"I missed you too."

"*Don't leave me behind again.*" Her green eyes, behind the copper mask, were fierce. "*Promise.*"

"I promise."

"*Properly.*"

"I promise properly," said Lex. "Never again."

She leaned her head against his chest. And they danced.

---

Across the ballroom, the Queen stood in a mask of silver feathers, with Elowen Dunmore behind her in plain black armor and an owl's mask, Oathkeeper on her back. Arion stood at the Queen's right hand in a mask of grey silk, very plain. Fenn's twenty Keepers were scattered through the crowd in borrowed finery, masked, swords under their coats.

And the Unlit were everywhere.

Lex couldn't see them. That was the point. Ilse and Ivo had spent the afternoon weaving illusions, and now Dorran Vask, all seven feet of him, was a footman with a tray of wine glasses, slightly stooped, entirely forgettable. Oriel sat on a bench by the wall disguised as a dowager in a black lace mask, which was barely a disguise at all. The twins themselves were nowhere. Or everywhere. Lex had seen the same pair of servants in three different doorways in the last hour.

Kestrel had not come.

He'd looked for her at the castle gate, when they left. She hadn't been there. Tamsin hadn't been there either.

*[She's gone,]* Ember had said quietly.

*I know*.

*[Where?]*

*Where the paper said.*

---

At the stroke of midnight, Varric Sorn stood up on the gallery above the ballroom and raised his glass.

He wore no mask. He was the host; it was his privilege. He stood at the gallery rail in black velvet with his silver hair swept back and his silver beard neatly pointed and a single grey band on his sleeve, and the music stopped, and five hundred masked faces turned up toward him.

"*My friends*," he said. His beautiful voice filled the ballroom effortlessly. "*My lords and ladies. Majesty.*" A graceful bow toward the Queen. "*Thank you for coming tonight.*"

Polite applause.

"*We live in frightening times*," said Sorn. "*I don't need to tell you. Fifty-five of our people, gone. Their families don't even remember their names. And every week, more.*" His voice was grave and sorrowful. "*And our Council deadlocked. Our Queen, young and brave, but alone. Our protector, the Order, broken. The Ceiling that kept us safe for nine hundred years, gone.*"

Murmurs. A few nods, behind the masks.

"*I have been asked*," said Sorn, "*by many of you, privately, to do something. To act. To protect the realm while there is still a realm to protect.*" He set down his glass on the gallery rail. "*And tonight, I am answering.*"

The doors of the ballroom closed.

All of them. At once. Every great double door, on every side, swinging shut with a single heavy *boom* that shook the chandeliers.

And behind the doors, Lex heard boots. A great many boots. Marching.

"*By the authority of the Eclipse Concord*," said Lord Chancellor Varric Sorn, "*and for the safety of the realm, I hereby declare a regency over the Crown of Caldor. Her Majesty will be placed under my protection, for her own safety, until the present danger has passed.*" He smiled down at the Queen. Kindly. "*No one will be harmed. Everyone will be home by morning.*" A pause. "*Except, I'm afraid, Lord Arion Vey.*"

The great doors at the far end of the ballroom opened.

And Isaura Kell walked in, in the white of a High Warden, with a hundred men in ash-grey behind her.

---

Things happened very fast after that.

Lex saw Kell raise her hand. Felt the great cold silver pressure come down over the ballroom like a lid. *Inspection*. The Order's oldest power. *Show yourself. Show your thread. Show what you are.*

He felt the Unlit's illusions tear. All of them. All at once. The footman with the wine tray was suddenly seven feet tall and holding a hammer. The dowager in black lace was a tiny blind old woman in a red shawl. Two servants in two different doorways flickered and became two mouse-haired thieves in patched coats, one of them faded and pale.

And Lex's own Lampshade, which he'd been holding all night, ripped away like a curtain in a gale, and his thread blazed out naked into the net, gold and silver, bright as a beacon.

"*There he is*," said Kell. Quietly. Looking straight at him across the ballroom. "*Take the father. I'll hold the son.*"

The men in ash-grey came in.

And someone came out of the crowd behind Arion with a coil of silver-black chain and threw it over his shoulders.

---

Lex saw it happen.

Saw his father turn, too late. Saw the chain fall across his shoulders and wrap itself around him, link after link, like a snake. Saw the gold go out of his eyes. All at once. Like a candle snuffed. Saw him stagger, and go down on one knee on the polished ballroom floor, with the silver-black chain wound around his chest and arms.

*Severance*.

Everything the Lattice gave him. Every skill. Every stat. The Root. The Authority. Two hundred and thirty-one years of power. Gone. Cut off. Leaving a lean tired man in a grey silk mask kneeling on a dance floor.

"*FATHER—*"

Kell's hand turned toward Lex.

And the silver pressure came down on him. Not Inspection now. Something heavier. Something he'd felt once before, in the Spire, years ago, when the Order had tried to lock his class. *Hold*. *Be still*. *Obey*. The voice of the High Warden, Tier IV, the highest Sanction the Lattice had ever granted, pressing down on him with nine hundred years of authority behind it.

He felt his knees start to bend.

*[Lex,]* said Ember. *[Lex, she's using a Sanction. Her own. The one the Lattice gave her when she was raised to High Warden.]* A pause. Very fast. *[And Sanctions are* granted. *By the Root. Lex. You're the Root.]*

He understood.

He stopped fighting the pressure. He let it push him down, onto one knee, like his father. And he reached, not out at Kell, but *down*. Into the gold. Into the Root. Into the deep place where every Sanction in the Lattice was written, every Tier permission, every flag and lock and grant, the whole great ledger of who was allowed what.

And found hers.

*ISAURA KELL — HIGH WARDEN — TIER IV SANCTION — granted by the Order under Root delegation.*

*Granted*.

*Under Root delegation*.

And he was the Root.

He felt the door at the bottom of the gold stir as he reached. Felt something open its eye a crack wider, warm and interested, watching what he did.

*Let it watch*, he thought.

Kell screamed.

Not loudly. A sharp short cry, like someone who's put her hand on a hot stove. The silver pressure over the ballroom *vanished*. Just—gone. Like a lid lifted off a pot.

She staggered. Her hand fell. She stared at it. Then at Lex. Her pale grey eyes, for the first time since he'd known her, wide with shock.

"*You can't*," she whispered. "*You can't do that. That Sanction was granted by the Order. By the High Council. By—*"

"By the Root," said Lex, standing up. "Through the Order. On loan." He looked at her. "I'm calling in the loan."

And then he reached again. Not at Kell. At the giant standing frozen in the middle of the ballroom, with a hammer in his hands and a faded silver brand on his forearm, still glowing faintly with the last of her command.

*DORRAN VASK — WARDEN OF THE KINDLING — Sanction to Ceiling, granted under Root delegation.*

Across the ballroom, the brand on Dorran's forearm flared silver one last time. And went dark. And then it was just a scar. An old burn on a big man's arm. Nothing else.

Dorran looked down at it.

Then he looked up. At Kell. And something came into his great slab of a face that Lex had never seen there before.

Not anger. Not quite. Something older and heavier and much more patient. Like a mountain deciding to move.

"*Commander*," said Dorran Vask. "*I've been waiting twenty years to say this.*"

And he dropped his hammer, and walked across the ballroom toward her.

---

After that, it was a fight.

The men in ash-grey were hired swords. Good ones. A hundred of them, armored, trained, filling the ballroom from three doors. But they'd been told they'd be facing a frightened court and an old man in chains. They hadn't been told about the Unlit.

Ilse and Ivo went up the walls. Literally. They'd brought ropes, under their coats, and grapnels, and they swarmed up the tapestries like squirrels up a tree and perched on the gallery rail and began to *weave*. Illusions. Dozens of them. Fifty Queens of Caldor appeared in the ballroom at once, all in silver feather masks, all running in different directions. The men in ash-grey stopped, bewildered, swinging their swords at Queens who dissolved like smoke.

Wren went up too. Not on a rope. On the wind. She kicked off her shoes, hiked her green silk gown up to her knees, pulled a bow out from behind the tapestry where she'd hidden it that afternoon, and *stepped* up into the air. She stood on nothing, twenty feet above the ballroom floor, under the painted gods, with her fox-mask pushed up on her head and her red hair coming down, and began to shoot. Not to kill. To stop. Arrows through sword-hands. Through boots. Through the straps of shields. Every one exactly where she wanted it.

Elowen Dunmore drew Oathkeeper.

Lex had never seen her fight. He'd heard about it. He hadn't believed it. She stood in front of the Queen, the real one, in her plain black armor with her owl-mask torn off, and swung her father's sword, and men in ash-grey simply went backwards. Like waves breaking on a rock. She didn't speak. She didn't shout. She just stood there, square and steady and immovable, exactly the way Garrick had stood at the gate of Fort Caldris, and nobody got past her.

And behind her, the Queen of Caldor tore off her silver feather mask and pulled a slim straight sword out of the folds of her gown, and stood at Elowen's shoulder with it raised. Her dark eyes were blazing.

"*SORN*," she shouted. Up at the gallery. "*You called this a regency? I am the Queen of Caldor, and I was Kindled a Sovereign, and I* will not be protected.*"

Lex had forgotten. *Sovereign. Epic*. He felt it come off her, a great bright gold-silver wave, rolling out across the ballroom. Not an attack. A *command*. The way the old kings of Caldor had commanded, before the Order ever set a Ceiling on them. *Stand with me*.

And all around the ballroom, behind the masks, people stood.

Not all of them. But many. Lords and ladies in silk and velvet, merchants in cloth-of-gold, pulling off their masks, picking up chairs and candlesticks and the swords of fallen men in ash-grey. Standing with their Queen.

---

Lex went for his father.

He went through the fight like a man wading through a river in flood. He didn't use his gold. He didn't use Flame. He didn't need to. He was the biggest man in the room except Dorran, and he'd swung a hammer every day for ten years, and when a man in ash-grey came at him with a sword, he simply caught the man's wrist and squeezed until the sword fell out of it.

He reached Arion. His father was still on his knees. The silver-black chain wound around him. His face grey. His eyes ordinary and dark and very, very tired.

"*Go*," said Arion. Hoarse. "*Lex, go. The Hollowmaw. Don't waste time on—*"

"Be quiet," said Lex.

He knelt. He took out Aurelian's hammer.

He looked at the chain. Not with True Sight. With a smith's eye. Fine silver-black links, each one perfect, each one forged by someone who knew what they were doing. Severance chains. Made by the Order three hundred years ago in a secret forge under the Spire, to cut sorcerers off from the Lattice. Very well made.

But *made*.

And everything that was made had a flaw.

*Find the maker's flaw*, he thought. *Strike there*.

He listened. With the hammer in his hand. The way he'd listened to the Umbral Wardens on the moor. The way he'd listened to iron in the coals since he was twelve years old.

There. In the forty-third link. A place where the smith who'd made it, three hundred years ago, had been tired, or distracted, or thinking about his supper. A seam. A tiny place where the metal hadn't quite fused.

*Tink*.

The chain fell apart.

All of it. Every link. Unfolding, uncoiling, sliding off Arion's shoulders onto the polished floor in a jangling silver-black heap, like a dead snake.

And the gold came back into his father's eyes.

Arion took a breath. A long shuddering breath, like a man coming up from deep water. He looked down at the chain on the floor. Then at his son.

"*How did you—*"

"Everything that's made has a flaw," said Lex. "Even you." He held out his hand. "Get up, Father. We've got to go."

---

Dorran had Kell.

Lex saw it as he pulled his father to his feet. The giant had walked straight through the fight to her, ignoring the men in ash-grey, ignoring the swords that glanced off his old leather coat. Kell had tried to stop him. With her hand. With her voice. With the Sanction that wasn't there anymore. Nothing happened.

And Dorran Vask had picked her up.

Not violently. Not cruelly. He'd simply put his huge hands under her arms and lifted her off her feet, the High Warden of the Order, the most powerful woman in Caldor for twenty years, and held her there. At eye level. Like a child.

"*Four thousand one hundred and nine*," said Dorran. His voice was very quiet. "*Children. I put my hand on their shoulders and closed their lids. Because you told me it kept them safe.*"

Kell didn't answer. She hung in his grip, her ragged silver hair falling in her face, her pale eyes staring at him.

"*It didn't keep them safe*," said Dorran. "*It kept them small. It kept them yours.*" He looked at her for a long moment. "*I'm not going to hurt you, Commander. I've hurt enough people for you.*"

And he set her down. Gently. On her feet. And stepped back.

Kell stood there. Swaying. Looking at him.

And then the air behind her opened.

A keyhole. Black. Cutting through the world. And a gloved hand came out of it, and closed on her arm, and *pulled*. And she went backwards into it, into the dark, without a sound. And the keyhole closed.

Up on the gallery, Lex saw another one open. Behind Sorn. The Lord Chancellor was already backing toward it, his elegant face white, his beautiful voice silent. He looked down at the ballroom one last time. At the Queen with her sword raised. At the court of Caldor standing with her. At his ruined coup.

He went into the keyhole. It closed.

---

It was over in less than an hour.

The men in ash-grey threw down their swords when Kell vanished. Most of them, anyway. A few ran, and Fenn's Keepers caught them on the Crown Terrace. The rest knelt on the polished ballroom floor with their hands on their heads while the court of Caldor stood around them in torn silk and broken masks, breathing hard, staring at each other as if they'd never seen each other before.

Wren came down out of the air and landed beside Lex. Barefoot. Her green gown torn to the knee. Her hair coming down in a wild red tangle. Her fox-mask hanging from one ear.

"*Well*," she said. "*That was a party.*"

He laughed. He couldn't help it. And she grinned at him, wild and fierce, and kissed him, quick and hard, in the middle of the wrecked ballroom.

"*Now go*," she said. "*Your brother.*"

He was already turning. To his father. Arion was standing by the heap of broken chain, gold back in his eyes, but pale. Very pale. The Severance had taken something out of him that hadn't come back yet.

"Can you still do it?" said Lex. "The Stones. Walk the threads."

"Yes." His father's jaw was set. "It'll cost more now. I'm weaker. But yes. For two."

"Me and Wren."

"*Me and Wren*," Wren agreed.

"*And me*," said Dorran Vask, coming up behind them with his hammer. "*I'm not as fast. But I'll follow.*"

Lex looked around for the others. The twins on the gallery rail. Oriel on her bench by the wall, entirely unruffled, in her red shawl.

"*Kestrel*," he said. "*Has anyone seen—*"

And then it hit him.

Not a sound. Not a sight. A *feeling*. Through the Bloodbond. Through the deep gold thread that ran from him across three hundred miles of mountains and valleys to the other end, where his brother was.

Pain.

Not Lex's. Antho's. Sudden and enormous, like a blow to the chest. And under it, rising up through the Bloodbond like water through a cracked floor, something cold. Something dark. The Hollow. Pressing.

And a voice. Antho's. Not words. Just—his brother, in the Bloodbond, calling. The way he'd called once before, years ago, in the dark of the Hollowmaw, when the breach had nearly taken him.

*Lex*.

Lex staggered. Wren caught his arm.

"*What—*"

"*He's there*," said Lex. His voice came out strange. "*Elric. He's at the Hollowmaw. He's—*"

He felt it then. Through the Bloodbond. Faint and far but unmistakable. A cut. A slice through a Vey ward, deep under a mountain, under a door of black glass. Something that had been sealed for nine hundred years by Aurelian's own blood, opening.

Because someone had wet it with *more* of Aurelian's blood.

Lex's blood.

On a rag. From the edge of a crater in the Glass Desert.

"*Kestrel*," he whispered.

Across the ruined ballroom, on her bench by the wall, Oriel Sant turned her white eyes toward him. Her face was very sad.

"*Two fires*," she said softly. "*I told you, smith. I'm sorry.*"
