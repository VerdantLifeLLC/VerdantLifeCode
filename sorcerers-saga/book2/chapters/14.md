# The Gathering Storm

*Tonight*.

The voice through the door said it at dusk. Not singing. Not *not yet*. Just the one word, low and rough, through the black glass, while Antho stood with his palm pressed flat against it and the clay lamp leaning in its niche.

*Tonight, love. He's coming tonight.*

Antho took his hand away from the glass.

He didn't ask how she knew. He'd stopped asking. For three weeks since he came back from the Drowned Country with a storm-mother and a hole in his memory, the voice had spoken to him every night at the end of the count. And every night it had been right. *Rain tomorrow*. *There's a crack in the north passage; mend it*. *The little one who doesn't talk is having nightmares; sit with him.* Small things. True things. As if whoever was on the other side of the door could see the Hollowmaw better than he could.

As if she'd been watching it for a long time.

"How many?" he said to the glass.

Silence.

Then, very faintly: *All of them.*

---

He went up the long passage counting.

*One thousand one hundred and eleven. One thousand one hundred and ten.* Backwards. Up through the deep part of the mountain, past the bell-shaped cavern and the library and the cold-room and the great carved hall, toward the mouth of the cave. Every lantern lit. Every lantern steady. Gold light on black rock.

*Four hundred. Three hundred.*

The students were in the hall. Nine of them, and Seraphine, and Brannoc and Hedda, who'd arrived two weeks ago on a cart full of iron and opinions and had been arguing with each other about the Keep's drainage ever since. They all looked up when Antho came in.

He didn't have to say anything. They saw his face.

"*Tonight*," said Seraphine. Not a question.

"Yes."

Brannoc got up from the bench. He was sixty-one now, bald as an egg, his great beard gone grey, and his knees cracked when he stood. But his shoulders were still the shoulders of a man who'd swung a hammer every day for fifty years, and the hammer in his hand was the biggest in the Ashbourne forge.

"*Right*," he said. "*Where d'you want me, lad?*"

---

He put them where he'd planned. He'd planned for three weeks. Every night after the count, at the table in the library with Seraphine's maps spread out and Roole's Keeper-Captain, a scarred quiet woman named Harrow Ves, sitting across from him.

The Keepers on the slopes. Three hundred of them, in their grey coats, in three lines across the long green shoulder of the mountain below the cave mouth. Pikes and crossbows and Ward-scribers. Good soldiers. Roole had sent his best.

The students in the deep hall, with the door shut and barred. Odile in charge. *If anything comes through that door that isn't me or Seraphine, you go down the north passage to the cold-room and out through the old water-shaft. All of you. Don't come back.*

*We're not children, Master Antho*, Odile had said.

*No. You're my students. That's worse.*

Brannoc and Hedda at the cave mouth, with the forge-iron they'd brought. Bars and spikes and a great barricade of it, built across the mouth of the Hollowmaw in two weeks of hammering. Hedda had painted runes on every bar. *Steadfast* and *Bulwark* and one Antho didn't recognize, which she said was her own, and which she refused to explain.

Seraphine with him. Down at the door. With the steel crossbow Lex had made her four years ago, the one that wouldn't load upside down.

And in the sky, the dragons.

Vharuun had recovered, a little. Three weeks of mountain air and sun and Ashka bringing her goats (not Correspondence; Antho had been very firm about Correspondence). Her scales were storm-blue again in great patches, and her one good eye was bright gold. She still couldn't fly well. But she could rise. And she could breathe thunder.

And Ashka had grown again. Antho didn't know how. Something her mother had given her in the drowned square, in that long slow breath of storm. She was bigger. Brighter. Lightning didn't just crawl along her sides now. It *lived* there. When she flew, the air behind her crackled and smelled of rain.

They perched on the crags above the Hollowmaw, mother and daughter, black against the dusk. Watching the sky.

---

He sat with Pim before it started.

The boy was in the hall with the others, on his bench at the end of the front row, knees pulled up. Antho sat down beside him. Didn't say anything. Pim didn't either.

They sat for a while.

"*Is it the old man*?" said Pim.

It was the third thing he'd ever said. Antho kept count.

"Yes."

"*The one who makes people disappear.*"

"Yes."

Pim was quiet. Then: "*Will he make you disappear?*"

Antho looked at him. Thin and grave and twelve years old. With eyes that had seen a man in Kessick take off his belt.

"I don't know," said Antho honestly. "He'll try."

Pim nodded. Slowly. As if this were what he'd expected.

Then he reached out, without looking, and put his small cold hand on Antho's wrist. And left it there. The way Antho had put his hand on Corin Bell's shoulder on Ashka's back, months ago. The way Lex used to put his hand on Antho's sleeve, on the woodpile.

He didn't say anything else. He didn't need to.

---

It started at midnight.

Antho was at the door. Seraphine beside him with her crossbow. The cavern quiet. The clay lamp burning straight up in its niche.

And then every lantern in the Hollowmaw flickered.

All of them. At once. All one thousand one hundred and eleven. A single great shudder of gold light running up the walls and across the ceiling of the bell-shaped cavern and away up the passage, like a breath of wind across a field of candles.

They didn't go out. But Antho felt it. Felt something pass *through* the mountain. Something cold.

*He's here*, said the voice through the door.

And from far above, faint through a mile of rock, he heard the Keepers' horns.

---

Elric didn't come up the mountain.

He came *through* it.

Antho learned that later, from Harrow Ves, who saw it from the slopes. Keyholes. Dozens of them. Opening in the air all over the long green shoulder of the mountain below the cave mouth, black and jagged, like rips in a painted cloth. And through them, shadows. Umbral Wardens. Not forty. Not a hundred.

*Hundreds*.

"*He's been making them*," Harrow Ves said, afterward, with blood drying on her face. "*Folding them. Out of something. I don't know what. They just kept coming out of the holes.*"

And behind the shadows, men. In ash-grey. Two hundred of them, Kell's last loyal swords, coming through the keyholes in disciplined ranks with shields locked and crossbows raised.

The Keepers held. For a long time, they held. Three hundred against two hundred men and an endless river of shadows. The Ward-scribers drew glyphs in the air that burned silver and held the shadows back. The crossbowmen fired volley after volley. The pikemen stood their ground.

And above them, the storm broke.

Vharuun rose off the crags with a sound like the mountain splitting. Not fast. Not graceful. But *enormous*, a ragged storm-blue shadow against the stars, wings like the sails of a warship. And she breathed.

Thunder. A great rolling wall of it, pouring down out of her jaws onto the slope. Not lightning. *Sound*. A pressure-wave of storm that hit the advancing shadows like a hammer hitting glass. The Umbral Wardens didn't burn. Thunder couldn't burn them. But it *scattered* them. Tore them apart, the way wind tears smoke. They came back together, slowly, rippling. But it bought time.

And Ashka came down behind her mother like a spear of lightning.

She didn't breathe. She *dove*. Straight down into the mass of men in ash-grey, crackling white from nose to tail, and lightning leapt off her in every direction, forking, branching, finding steel. Shields. Swords. Helmets. Men went flying.

From the cave mouth, Brannoc Holt watched a storm-drake the size of a barn come down on two hundred armored men like the wrath of the sky, and said, very quietly, to no one: "*Well. I'll be damned.*"

Hedda Vorne, beside him, hefted her cudgel. "*Language*," she said.

---

Down at the door, Antho felt all of it.

Not with his ears. With Shade. The great cold quiet hearing that went out through the mountain like water through sand. He felt the Keepers on the slope, their fear, their resolve, the shape of the silence around each of them. He felt the shadows, hundreds of them, cold and empty. He felt the dragons, burning.

And he felt the moment Elric came into the mountain.

Not through the cave mouth. Not through Brannoc's barricade. Through the *rock*. A keyhole opened in the passage halfway down, between the library and the cold-room, cutting through solid stone like a knife through cheese. And Elric Thorne stepped out of it into the gold-lit passage of the Hollowmaw, with two pieces of the Key of Shadows hanging black at his throat.

And beside him, red-haired, burning, a woman Antho had never seen.

"*Seraphine*," said Antho. Very quietly. "*Go to the hall. Now. Get the students out.*"

"*Antho—*"

"*Now*."

She looked at him. Then she went. Running, up the long passage, with her crossbow in her hands. He heard her footsteps fading.

He turned back to the door. And the lamp.

*Here he comes*, said the voice through the glass. *Oh, my love. Here he comes.*

---

Elric didn't hurry.

Antho heard him coming down the passage. Unhurried footsteps. And the lanterns going out.

One by one. As Elric passed them. Not blown out. *Cut*. Antho felt each one go. A small gold thread, snipped. *Four hundred and twelve. Four hundred and eleven. Four hundred and ten.*

He was counting them backwards. He couldn't stop.

*Three hundred. Two hundred and ninety-nine.*

The passage behind Elric went dark. A long tongue of black, coming down into the mountain, swallowing the gold.

*Two hundred.*

And then Elric came around the last bend into the bell-shaped cavern, and stopped.

He looked at the cavern. The high round walls, hung with lanterns. The black floor. The door of black glass at the bottom. The little clay lamp in its niche. And Antho, standing in front of it, with his staff in one hand and the Keeper's Lantern in the other.

"*Antho*," said Elric. Almost gently. "*You look well. Better than the last time.*"

The last time had been Duskhold. A black corridor. Antho on his knees, with his Clarity shattered, and Elric standing over him.

"*You don't*," said Antho.

It was true. Elric looked *younger*. Forty, perhaps. His hair was dark at the roots now, the grey growing out. His face was firm. But something about it was wrong. Too smooth. Too still. Like a mask of his own face, slightly too tight.

And his eyes. They'd always been brown. Warm, clever, tired brown.

They were darker now. Much darker. Almost black.

"*The Key has been kind to me*," said Elric. "*Fifty-five threads. Every one of them gave me a little of what they had.*" He touched the black thing at his throat. "*I feel thirty again. Do you know what that's like? To be old, and tired, and slow, and then not?*"

"*No*."

"*Your father does.*" Elric's mouth twisted. "*Your father has never once in his life been old.*"

Behind him, the red-haired woman had stopped at the edge of the cavern. She was staring at Antho. At the door. At the lamp. Her face was white. And beside her—

Antho went very still.

Beside her stood a young man. Faint. Pale. Barely there. Thin and dark-haired, in a farmer's smock. Antho could see the black rock of the cavern wall through his chest.

And with Grave-Speech, with the old cold hearing that let him speak to things at the edge of being, Antho could *hear* him.

"*Please*," the faint young man was saying. Over and over. To the red-haired woman. To Antho. To anyone. "*Please. Stop her. Don't let her do this. Not for me. Please.*"

The red-haired woman couldn't hear him. Antho could see that. She stood with her fists clenched at her sides and fire burning low in her hair and stared at the door.

---

"*I'm going to open the floor now*," said Elric. "*Under the lamp. That's where he put it. Aurelian. He buried the blade right under the door, so he could guard it from both sides.*" He looked at the clay lamp in its niche. "*Your mother's lamp is sitting on top of it. Did you know? Nine hundred years of buried shadow, and a little clay lamp from the Ashbourne market sitting on top of it like a paperweight.*"

"*Don't touch it.*"

"*I won't have to.*" Elric held out his hand toward the red-haired woman, without looking at her. "*Kestrel.*"

She didn't move.

"*Kestrel*," said Elric again. Patient. "*We had an arrangement.*"

Slowly, she reached into her coat. And took out something small and brown and stiff. A rag. Twisted and filthy, stained dark.

Antho knew what it was before he could see it properly. He felt it. Through the Bloodbond. A tiny piece of his brother. His blood. Dried brown on a scrap of cloth.

"*Where did you get that*," he said. Very quietly.

Kestrel looked at him. Her amber eyes were wet.

"*The Glass Desert*," she said. "*He cut his hand on a door, and wrapped it in this, and threw it away. And I picked it up.*" Her voice cracked. "*I'm sorry. I'm so sorry. He took my brother.*"

"*He took fifty-five brothers*," said Antho. "*And sisters. And mothers. And—*"

"*I know*." It came out of her like a sob. "*I KNOW. Don't you think I know?*"

Beside her, Tamsin Varn was weeping. *Kes, Kes, please, Kes*.

She held out the rag to Elric.

---

Antho moved.

He didn't think. He didn't plan. He'd planned for three weeks, and none of it mattered now. He came across the cavern floor in three long strides with the Lantern swinging in his hand and his staff coming up, and he threw everything he had at Elric Thorne.

Void Bolt. Not one. Six. A hail of them, black and silent, the cold empty nothing-stuff of Shade, hurled from his staff like stones from a sling. Shadowbind, wrapping around Elric's legs, his arms, his chest. Gale, a great howling gust of wind, slamming across the cavern to tear the rag out of Kestrel's hand.

Elric turned the Key.

And it all stopped.

Not blocked. Not deflected. *Stopped*. The Void Bolts hung in the air, frozen, halfway across the cavern. The Shadowbind went slack and fell away like cut rope. The wind died in mid-howl.

"*Oh, Antho*," said Elric. Very gently. "*Don't you understand yet? It's a* key. *Everything the Lattice gives you, it can lock. Or unlock.*" He looked at the frozen Void Bolts. "*Your skills are just doors. And I have the key to every door in the world.*"

He turned it again. The other way.

And the Void Bolts turned around in the air and came back at Antho.

---

He got the Lantern up in time. Just. The Hush-runes on its glass flared white and the Void Bolts hit the circle of gold light around him and shattered, one after another, like black glass on stone. Each one hit like a hammer. He went backwards. One step. Two. His boots skidding on the black floor.

*[He's going to kill you*,] said the Murmur. Calmly. *[He's going to kill you, and then he's going to open the floor, and then he's going to have the whole key. And you'll have done nothing. Again.]*

"*Be quiet*."

*[I'm only saying—]*

He held the Lantern up. *Voices can't act through you while it's lit*. The Murmur went silent.

And Elric turned the Key toward *him*.

He felt it. The hook. Going into his chest. Into his thread. Not the gold-and-silver cord Lex had. Something stranger. Antho's thread was twisted. Gold, yes, the Root, the blood of the Veys. But wound through it, the old dark of the Hollow. The link he'd carried for eleven years. Dormant now. Quiet. But *there*.

The Key found it. And *pulled*.

He went to his knees.

It didn't hurt. Lex had told him that, in a letter: *it doesn't hurt, that's the worst of it, it's like falling asleep*. He'd thought he understood. He hadn't. It was like the grey river. Like walking into the forgetting mist with no Lantern and no fee paid. His name going soft. His edges blurring.

*Anthoren. Anthoren V—*

He tried to hold. The way he'd held the breach. The way he'd held the Hollow-chains in the drowned square while Ashka bit through them. Hold *still*. Hold *on*.

But he was holding against two pieces of the Key of Shadows. And Elric had fifty-five people's worth of strength behind him.

He felt himself start to fade.

---

A hand closed on his wrist.

Small. Cold. A boy's hand.

He hadn't heard Pim come. He never heard Pim come. The boy was just *there*, kneeling beside him on the black floor of the cavern, in front of the door. He must have slipped away from the others in the hall. Come down the dark passage alone, past the cut lanterns, through the black.

He had his hand on Antho's wrist. And his eyes were closed. And he was *listening*.

And around Antho, the silence changed shape.

Antho felt it. The way he felt Shade. The great cold quiet that lived in the space between heartbeats. But this wasn't his. This was Pim's. A boy who'd spent his whole life listening to the dark because it was the only thing that had ever been kind to him. And now he was pulling that dark around Antho like a blanket. Like a cloak. Like a lampshade over a light.

*Hiding him*.

The hook in Antho's thread slipped.

Not out. But *sideways*. As if it had been pulling on a rope, and the rope had suddenly gone slack and dark and hard to find. Elric's face changed. He frowned. Turned the Key. Searched.

"*What*—" he said.

And from behind Antho, from the other side of the door, through the black glass, a voice spoke.

Not to Antho. Not to Pim. To Elric.

Low and rough and very, very cold.

*Let go of my son.*

---

Elric froze.

Antho saw it. Saw him go white. Saw the dark eyes go wide. Saw his hand on the Key go still.

"*Seren*," Elric whispered.

*Let go of him, Elric Thorne. You were my husband's friend for forty years. You held my boys when they were babies. You sang to Antho when he had the croup.* The voice through the glass was shaking. *Let go.*

"*You're dead*," said Elric. His voice was strange. "*You're—you went into the dark. Eleven years ago. You're—*"

*I'm on the other side of a door*, said the voice. *Holding it shut. With everything I have. Against the thing that's been whispering in your ear.* A pause. *Do you know what it is, Elric? Have you ever asked yourself? What's been telling you where to dig?*

Elric didn't answer. He stood in the middle of the cavern with the Key at his throat and his face white.

*It's not Aurelian*, said the voice through the door. *Not anymore. Not for eight hundred years. It's wearing his face. It's using his voice. And it's using* you. *The way it's tried to use every one of us.*

For one long moment, Elric Thorne stood absolutely still.

And Antho, on his knees on the black floor with Pim's hand on his wrist, saw something in the man's face crack. Just a little. Like the first fine line in a sheet of ice.

And then the other voice came.

Not through the door. From *inside* Elric. From the Key at his throat. Warm and kind and gentle as a hand on his hair.

*She's lying, Elric. She's been in the dark too long. It's changed her. She wants the door shut because she wants you to stay small. Like Arion did. Like they all did.* A pause. *They've always wanted you to stay second.*

Elric's face closed.

Like a door slamming.

"*Kestrel*," he said. Very quietly. Not looking away from the black glass. "*The rag.*"

---

She gave it to him.

Antho saw her do it. Saw her hand shaking. Saw her face, white and wet and twisted with something that looked like hatred, though Antho couldn't tell who it was for. Saw Tamsin beside her, screaming silently, *Kes, Kes, no*.

She put the rag in Elric's gloved hand.

And Elric knelt. In front of the niche. In front of the little clay lamp burning there. And pressed the rag, stained brown with Lex's blood, flat against the black stone floor beneath it.

The floor *opened*.

Not cracked. Not broke. Opened, like a mouth. A circle of black stone, a pace across, directly beneath the niche, folding back on itself in a spiral, like the petals of a flower opening in reverse. And under it, darkness. Deep. Cold. Breathing.

And from the darkness, rising slowly on a column of something that wasn't stone and wasn't shadow, the Blade.

Long. Thin. Black. A shaft as long as Antho's forearm, perfectly straight, perfectly smooth, so dark it seemed to drink the light from the lanterns around it. The part of a key that connects the bow to the bit. The part that goes into the lock.

Elric reached out and took it.

---

The lamp went out.

Antho felt it before he saw it. A small gold thread, snapping. The little clay lamp in the niche, the one he and Lex had carried down the long passage three years ago, the one that had lit every lantern in the mountain. Its flame shrank. Flickered. Went blue.

And went out.

And on the other side of the door, Antho heard his mother scream.

Not in pain. In *effort*. A long, terrible, straining cry, like someone holding up something far too heavy for far too long, and feeling it start to slip.

The black glass of the door *bulged*.

Toward him. Outward. Like a soap bubble pushed from the inside. And through it, faint and terrible, he felt the Hollow. Pressing. Hungry. *Awake*. The thing at the bottom of the world, that had been sealed under the Lattice for nine hundred years, leaning on the door with all its weight.

Because the Blade had been holding it shut. Nine hundred years under the floor. A piece of Aurelian's shadow, pinning the lid down from this side, while Seren held it from the other.

And now it was gone.

*Antho*. His mother's voice. Strained. Breaking. *Antho, the lamp. Light the lamp. I can't—I can't hold it alone—*

---

Elric stood up with the Blade in his hand.

He didn't look at the door. He didn't look at Antho. He held the Blade up to his throat, where the Bow and the Bit hung joined on their black chain. And brought them together.

*Click*.

Three pieces. One key. Long and black and perfect, as long as a man's arm, with a looped bow and a straight shaft and a bit of jagged teeth like a mountain range. Exactly as Lex had described it, from Ysmir's window of ice. Exactly as Aurelian had forged it nine hundred years ago, on a gold anvil, under stars too close and too bright.

The Key of Shadows. Whole.

And every lantern in the Hollowmaw went out.

---

All of them. One thousand one hundred and eleven. At once. Not cut. *Swallowed*. Like candles in a gale. The gold light of the cavern vanishing all at once and the dark coming down like a lid.

Antho couldn't see. He couldn't see anything. Just black. Absolute. The black of the inside of the world.

And in the black, Elric's voice. Very quiet. Very calm.

"*Thank you, Antho*," said Elric. "*And thank you, Kestrel. I said I would keep my word. I always keep my word.*"

Antho heard the Key turn.

Not toward him. Toward the place where the red-haired woman was standing.

And in the black, a light. Faint. Silver. A thread, being written. Being *woven*, strand by strand, out of nothing, the way a spider spins silk. Coming together. Getting brighter.

And a young man's voice. Not faint. Not a whisper. *Real*. Rough and shaking and *loud*.

"*KES*—"

---

Somewhere far away, Antho heard the Keepers' horns change.

Not the alarm. The *other* call. The one Harrow Ves had taught them for if things went very badly or very well. Three long notes, rising.

*Reinforcements*.

And through the Bloodbond, so close now he almost cried out, he felt his brother.

*Lex*.

---

They came out of the Stone at Hollow's Foot at a dead run.

Lex had never walked the threads before. His father had done it for them. Standing at the Great Stone in the Spire with his hands on its silver face and the gold coming up in him so bright that Wren had had to look away. *Hold on to me. Don't let go. Whatever you see, don't let go.* And then the Spire had gone away. And Lex had been *in* the net. Rushing. Silver threads blurring past on every side like rain seen from a galloping horse. Wren's hand in his, crushing his fingers. And somewhere behind them, getting fainter, his father's voice: *Go. Go. I'll hold the thread as long as I can*.

And then the Stone at Hollow's Foot. The little Stone in the valley below the Hollowmaw, that Lex had Unbound himself two summers ago. Coming up around them like water. And spitting them out onto the wet grass of a valley full of fire and thunder.

He saw the mountain.

Saw the long green slope below the cave mouth, black now with fighting. Grey coats and ash-grey coats. Shadows, hundreds of them, swarming. Two dragons in the sky, one vast and ragged and one bright as a lightning bolt, pouring storm down onto the slope. And at the top of it, the great cleft of the Hollowmaw.

Dark.

Not a single lantern. Not one.

"*Antho*," he said.

Wren was already stepping up onto the wind.

---

He didn't remember the climb.

He remembered fire. His own. Flame, pouring out of him the way it had poured out of the Sunwell, gold-white and roaring. Not borrowed this time. *His*. Every channel that the Sunwell's fire had blistered and burned in the Glass Desert had healed thicker, stronger, the way a smith's hands callus. He went up the slope through the shadows like a forge-fire through kindling.

He remembered Wren. Above him. Stepping on the wind, her bow singing, arrows burning gold. She was still wearing the torn green gown from the masquerade. He'd remember that later and laugh. He didn't laugh now.

He remembered Brannoc at the cave mouth. Grey and bloody, standing on Hedda's barricade with his great hammer in both hands, and his face when he saw Lex coming up the slope. Something breaking open in it. *Lad*. *Lad*.

He didn't stop.

He went into the dark of the Hollowmaw with fire in his hands, down the long passage where every lantern was out. Down past the hall, where nine children were huddled behind a barred door with Seraphine and a steel crossbow. Down past the library and the cold-room. Down and down.

Into the bell.

---

It was dark.

His fire lit it. The great round cavern, black-walled. Hung with a thousand dead lanterns. And at the bottom, the door.

Bulging.

Lex had never seen anything so wrong. The smooth black glass of the sealed breach, bowed outward like the belly of a sail, straining, creaking, a web of fine cracks spreading across its surface. And from behind it, a sound. Low. Enormous. Like the breathing of something the size of the world.

In front of it, on his knees, Antho. With a small dark-haired boy kneeling beside him, gripping his wrist. Both of them with their hands pressed flat against the bulging glass. Holding it. Antho's face grey and running with sweat. The boy's eyes screwed shut.

To one side, a red-haired woman on her knees on the black floor. Clutching someone. A young man. Thin and dark-haired, in a farmer's smock. *Solid*. *Whole*. Real enough to touch. She had her arms around him and her face buried in his shoulder, and she was sobbing, great broken sobs that shook her whole body. And Tamsin Varn, *real*, with color in his face and blood in his cheeks, was holding his sister and staring over her head at the bulging door with his face full of horror.

And in the center of the cavern, Elric Thorne.

With a key in his hand. Long and black. Whole.

He turned when Lex came in. He looked at him. At the fire pouring off his hands.

"*Lex*," he said. "*You're late.*"

"*Put it down*."

"*No*." Elric looked at the Key in his hand. At its long black shaft. Its teeth. "*No, I don't think I will.*" He looked up. "*You should help your brother. The door won't hold much longer. Not without the blade. Not without the lamp.*" A pause. "*Your mother's been holding it alone from the other side for eleven years. She's very tired.*"

Lex went cold.

"*She's—*"

"*Alive*." Elric's mouth twisted. "*In a manner of speaking. Ask your father. He's known for three years.*" He turned the Key, very slightly, and a keyhole opened in the air behind him. "*I'll see you at the end, Lex. At the lid. When I turn this, and everything changes.*"

"*Elric—*"

"*And Lex.*" Elric looked back. And for a moment, just a moment, his strange dark eyes were brown again. Tired. Clever. Old. "*It said I'd always be second. Your mother said it was lying.*" He was quiet. "*I'd like to know which of them was right. Before the end.*"

He stepped backwards into the keyhole.

It closed.

---

Lex didn't go after him.

He couldn't. The door groaned. A sound like a ship's timbers breaking. A crack ran across the black glass from side to side, and through it, for one terrible instant, Lex felt the Hollow. The real Hollow. Not the shallows Antho had walked in. The deep. The *hungry*. A cold so enormous it wasn't cold anymore, just *absence*, pressing against the thin black skin of the door like a flood against a dam.

He dropped to his knees beside his brother. Put his own hands flat on the glass.

And *pushed*.

With Stone. With the mountain. With the deep old patient bones of the Greyspine, which had held this breach before, and didn't want to see it open again. With Flame, pouring gold-white heat into the black glass until it glowed red and steamed. With the Root, Authority, everything he had left after Kell and the Spire and the long walk through the threads.

It wasn't enough.

He felt it. The door was bending. Slowly. Inch by inch. Three of them holding it, two Veys and a boy who heard the dark, and it wasn't enough.

*The lamp*, said a voice through the glass. Strained. Breaking. A voice Lex hadn't heard in eleven years, and had never stopped hearing. *Lex. Lex, love. Light the lamp.*

He looked at the niche. At the little clay lamp. Dark. Cold. A chip in its spout and a thumbprint in its base.

His mother's lamp. That he and Antho had carried down this passage three years ago, and sung to, and watched light every lantern in the mountain.

He reached out, with one hand still on the bulging door. And touched it.

And *Spoke* to Flame.

Not Commanded. Not asked. *Spoke*. The way he'd spoken to the Sunwell. The way he talked to the forge-fire every morning when he lit it. *Hello. It's me. I need you.*

*Please*.

And the lamp lit.

Not with his fire. With its own. A small, steady, golden flame, rising up out of the cold clay as if it had only been sleeping. The same flame. The same warm summer-evening smell.

And one by one, all around the cavern, then up the passage, then all through the mountain, the lanterns of the Hollowmaw came back.

---

The door stopped bulging.

Slowly. Like a breath let out. The black glass sinking back. Flattening. The crack across its face still there, a long jagged line from side to side. But holding. *Holding*.

From the other side, faint, so faint Lex almost didn't hear it:

*Thank you, love.*

And then nothing.

Lex knelt in front of the door with his hands on the glass and his brother beside him and a small boy holding his brother's wrist, and the lanterns burning gold all around them, and couldn't move.

*Mam*.

*She's alive. She's on the other side of the door. She's been holding it for eleven years.*

*And Father knew.*

He turned his head and looked at Antho.

His brother was grey. Exhausted. His face slick with sweat, his hands trembling on the glass. But his eyes were open. Grey and dark and steady. Looking at Lex.

"*You came*," said Antho.

"I came."

"*Late*."

"I know. I'm sorry." Lex's voice cracked. "There was a party."

Something moved at the corner of Antho's mouth. Almost a smile.

They knelt together in front of the cracked door for a long time. Not speaking. Breathing. The lanterns burning gold around them. Pim had let go of Antho's wrist and slumped against his side, asleep, or something like it.

And then Lex said, because it was the thing he always said, the thing they always came back to, the thing that had been in his head the whole long run through the threads and up the burning slope:

"When this is done. When it's over. We'll go to your lake. On the other side of the Teeth. And skip stones." He tried to smile. "I'll beat you this time. I'll get six."

Antho looked at him.

Puzzled. Gently puzzled. The way you look at someone who's mentioned a friend you've never met.

"*Six*?" he said. "*Six what?*"

Lex stopped.

"Skips," he said. "The stone. At the millpond. When I was ten. You told me it was four, and it was five, and you told me the truth on the Founders' Road, under the stars, and I laughed until I couldn't—" He stopped again. "Antho. You remember. The five-skip stone. You—you *counted*. You always count."

Antho's face didn't change. Grey and dark and steady. And very, very sad.

"*I paid the Ferryman*," he said quietly. "*To cross. To find Ashka's mother.*" He looked at his brother. "*He takes the memory you'd least want to lose.*"

He put his scarred hand over Lex's on the black glass of the door.

"*I don't remember, Lex*," said Antho. "*I'm sorry. Tell me. Tell me about the stone.*"
