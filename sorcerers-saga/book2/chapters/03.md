# Unveiling Darkness

The north was empty.

Not empty the way the Pale Wastes had been empty, white and dead and silent. Empty the way a house is empty when someone has just left it. The villages were still there, scattered up the long green valleys toward the Ironbacks. The fields were still planted. Smoke still rose from chimneys. People still worked and talked and laughed and sang.

But there were gaps.

Lex started noticing them on the third day out from Highcrest. A cottage at the edge of a village, its door standing open, its hearth cold, its garden going to weeds. When he asked the neighbors who lived there, they looked at him with polite, puzzled, kindly expressions and said *no one, as far as I know. It's always been empty.*

A chair at a tavern table, pulled out, with a mug of ale on the table in front of it, going flat. Nobody sitting in it. Nobody remembering who'd ordered it.

A child's wooden horse lying in the middle of a lane. A spinning wheel with half-spun wool still on the spindle. A pair of boots by a door, too big for anyone in the house.

Small things. Things nobody noticed. Things nobody could explain, and so nobody tried to.

*[It's like walking through a room after someone's taken the furniture,]* said Ember quietly, on the fifth day. *[You can see the marks on the floor where it used to be.]*

*How many?*

*[I've been counting the holes. In the net. As we pass.]* A pause. *[Nineteen, so far. Since Highcrest.]*

*Nineteen.*

*[And every one of them Unbound. Every one in a bright place.]* Ember's voice was very quiet. *[He's not just picking the orchard anymore, Lex. He's harvesting it.]*

---

He used True Sight on every village Stone he passed.

It cost him a point of Authority each time. He had a hundred, and the Wells fed it back four times faster than the old way, so it didn't matter. He laid his palm on each grey lichened Stone in each village square and let the gold rise behind his eyes and looked down into it, into the threads, the way he'd looked into the Lower Town Stone in Highcrest three years ago and seen the flags.

And he saw the holes.

Not in the Stone itself. In the *record*. The Stone held every thread that had ever been Kindled at it, the living and the dead, a great bundle of gold and silver running down into the net like the roots of a tree. And here and there, among the roots, there was a place where a root *should* be and wasn't. A gap. Not a dead thread. Not a broken one. A *nothing*. As if someone had reached into the soil and pulled a root out so cleanly that the earth had closed over the hole behind it.

He found the half-man on the seventh day.

He was crossing a high moor above Coldbrook, in the last of the evening light, with the Ironbacks rising grey and snow-streaked ahead of him. The wind was cold. The heather was purple. A curlew was crying somewhere over the bog.

And something was following him.

He felt it first. The way he'd learned to feel things, these three years, the way he felt slag in iron. A wrongness behind him. A flaw in the grain of the evening.

He stopped. He turned.

There was nothing there. Just the moor, and the heather, and the long grey line of the Ironbacks behind it.

He walked on.

A hundred paces later, he felt it again. Closer.

He stopped. He turned. He used True Sight.

The world went gold.

And there was a man standing in the heather twenty paces behind him.

Or most of one. Lex could see him only with True Sight. A young man, perhaps twenty, thin and dark-haired, in a farmer's smock and boots. But *faded*. Pale. Soft at the edges. Like a chalk drawing left out in the rain. Lex could see the heather through his legs. Through his chest. Through his face.

But not all the way. Not like the baker in the square, who'd gone from solid to nothing in the space of a breath. This man was stuck. Halfway. As if he'd started to fade and then something had caught him and held him there.

He was looking at Lex. His eyes were dark, and very frightened, and very, very lost.

"*You can see me*," he said.

His voice was faint. Like a voice heard from the next room. Like a voice heard in a dream.

"Yes," said Lex.

The faded man made a sound. It might have been a laugh. It might have been a sob.

"*Nobody can see me*," he said. "*Nobody. Not for weeks. I walk into villages and stand in front of people and shout, and they walk through me. They don't hear me. They don't see me.*" His faint voice cracked. "*I thought I was dead. I thought I was a ghost. But ghosts know their names.*"

"You don't know your name?"

The faded man looked at him for a long moment.

"*No*," he whispered.

---

Lex made a fire.

He did it with Flame, not flint. A ring of stones in a hollow in the heather, sheltered from the wind, and a few sticks of dead gorse, and a thought. *I'm cold. There's someone here who's lost. I want to keep him warm.* The fire came up gold and steady and leaned toward the faded man sitting on the other side of it, the way it leaned toward Lex.

The faded man stared at it.

"*It sees me*," he said. "*The fire. It sees me.*"

"Fire sees most things," said Lex. "It's honest that way."

He looked at the man properly. With True Sight. Deep. The way he'd looked at Seraphine on the moor, three years ago, when Elric had wanted him to doubt her.

*[Someone's holding him,]* said Ember softly. *[His sister. Someone named Varn. She remembers him. And because she remembers him, he can't fade all the way.]* A pause. *[It's like the boy. Corin. He remembered Mella. But Mella faded anyway.]*

*Why didn't this one?*

*[I don't know.]* Ember was quiet for a long moment. *[Maybe the sister remembers him harder. Maybe she's doing something. Fighting for him.]* Another pause. *[Maybe she's a Listener.]*

"Your sister," said Lex aloud. "Do you remember a sister?"

The faded man looked at him through the fire. His dark eyes were very far away.

"*Fire*," he said slowly. "*There's fire. Someone with fire. Red hair. Laughing.*" His face creased, as if trying to see something through fog. "*She burned the barn down once. When we were small. By accident. Da was so angry.*" He was almost smiling. "*She laughed and laughed. She said it was the prettiest fire she ever saw.*"

"What's her name?"

The faded man opened his mouth.

Nothing came out.

He tried again. His face twisted with effort. His faint hands clenched on his faded knees.

"*I can't*," he whispered. "*I can see her. I can hear her laughing. I can't—her name's gone. Mine's gone. They're both gone.*" His voice broke. "*I'm forgetting her face. A little more every day. Every day, she's a little less. And when she's gone, I'll be gone too.*" He looked at Lex across the fire. "*Won't I.*"

Lex didn't answer.

He didn't have to.

---

The faded man followed him into the Ironbacks.

Lex hadn't asked him to. He hadn't said no. In the morning, when he kicked out the fire and shouldered Anvilsong and started up the long slope toward the mountains, the faded man was simply there, walking a few paces behind him, faint as mist in the early light.

"*You're going north*," the faded man said. "*Following the holes.*"

"How did you know?"

"*I can feel them. Like you can.*" He was quiet for a moment. "*I'm one of them, aren't I. A hole. Or most of one.*" His faint voice was very calm. "*I can feel the others. The ones who went all the way. There's a pull. Like a current in a river. Going north and east.*" He looked up at the Ironbacks. "*Toward whatever took me.*"

"Do you remember it? What took you?"

The faded man walked in silence for a long time.

"*A man*," he said at last. "*Old. In grey. With a black key.*" His voice dropped. "*I was in the field. Bringing the cows in. And he was just there. At the gate. He didn't say anything. He just looked at me. With such sad eyes.*" He shuddered. "*And then he touched the key. And I felt something pull. Here.*" He pressed his faint hand to his chest. "*Like a hook in a fish's mouth. And then I didn't know my name.*"

*[Elric,]* said Ember.

*I know.*

"*He said something*," said the faded man. "*When I was fading. Before I stopped being able to hear.*" He frowned. "*He said, I'm sorry. Very quietly. Like he meant it.*" He looked at Lex. "*And then he said: It's nothing personal. I'm only taking what I'm owed.*"

---

The Ironbacks were cold and grey and very old.

Older than the Greyspine, Seraphine had told him once. Older than anything in Caldor. Worn down by ages of wind and ice into great rounded shoulders of grey rock, with long scree slopes and black tarns and snow that never melted in the high corries. Few people lived up there. A few shepherds. A few miners. A village here and there, tucked into a valley, clinging on.

Lex followed the holes.

He could feel them more clearly up here. The pull that the faded man had described. A current in the net, flowing north and east, carrying the mana of every unwritten thread away toward something. He followed it like a tracker follows a trail, up through the high passes, over the grey shoulders, past tarns as black and still as Mirrormere.

And on the tenth day, he found the scars.

They were hanging in the air above a high pass. A dozen of them. Thin black lines in the empty air, the shape of keyholes, healed over. Some old and faint. Some fresh and dark. In a line. Like footprints. Like the marks a man leaves when he's been walking the same path over and over.

"*He came this way*," said the faded man. He was standing very still, looking at the scars. "*Many times.*"

Lex used True Sight on the nearest.

The shadows came at dusk.

Lex was making camp in the lee of a great grey boulder below the pass, with the faded man sitting faint and silent on the other side of a fire. The sky over the Ironbacks had gone from grey to deep blue. The first stars were coming out. The wind had dropped.

And the shadows of the rocks began to move.

Not the way shadows move when the sun goes down, slowly, stretching. They moved *wrong*. They peeled up off the ground. Off the boulders. Off the scree. They rose up into the air like smoke rising off a fire, and folded, and took shape.

Not Gloam. Lex knew Gloam. Gloam was oily and black and stank of wet ash and nothing. These were different. Thinner. Cleaner. Almost *beautiful*. Like shadows cast by a candle on a wall. Tall, and slender, and shaped like men in long cloaks, and their faces were smooth and featureless, like the faces of the Lumen Wardens at Avaloria, only dark instead of light.

Lex was on his feet with Anvilsong in his hands before the first one moved.

He hit it with an Ember Strike. A Tier III Forgeheart's Ember Strike, Well-fed, Flame at Speaking. Three years ago, at Duskhold, it would have torn a hole through a Hollow-knight and the three behind it.

It went through the Umbral Warden like a hand through smoke.

The shadow didn't burst. It didn't scream. It *rippled*, the way a reflection ripples in water, and reformed, and turned its smooth dark face toward him.

*[It's not Gloam,]* said Ember fast. *[Fire doesn't eat it. It's not hungry. It's not the Hollow. It's* shadow*. Aurelian's shadow. Forged.]* A pause. *[It's a* made *thing, Lex. Like your hammer.]*

The shadow struck back.

Not with a weapon. It reached out with one long dark hand and touched Lex's chest, and something *pulled*. Like a hook in a fish's mouth. Like a thread being drawn out of a hem.

He felt it go for his thread. His bright gold Root-thread, running out of him into the net. Felt the cold dark fingers close on it. Felt them *tug*.

*[LEX—]*

He didn't let them. He reached down for Stone. The old slow heavy hum of the mountain under his feet, the oldest mountain in Caldor. And he *rooted*. Like a tree in a gale. Like an anvil on a stump. He made himself heavy. Immovable. The Umbral Warden tugged at his thread and the thread didn't come, because Lex was holding it, and Stone was holding Lex, and the mountain was holding Stone.

The shadow tugged harder.

And behind it, the other five were reaching for the faded man.

He was backing away across the heather, his faint hands raised in front of his face, and the five tall dark shapes were drifting after him, and Lex could see it: their long dark fingers stretching out toward the faint, faded thread still running out of the man's chest. The seventy-one percent that had already been cut. The twenty-nine that was still holding on, anchored by a sister's memory.

They were going to finish it.

"*NO*—"

Lex tore free of the shadow that held him. He didn't know how. He just *pulled*, with Stone, with the mountain, with everything, and the cold dark fingers slipped off his thread, and he was running. Across the heather. Toward the faded man. Toward the five shadows reaching for him.

He didn't have anything that worked on them. Flame went through them. Stone held him but didn't hurt them. They weren't Gloam, they weren't Hollow, they weren't anything he'd ever fought.

*They're made things*, Ember had said. *Like your hammer.*

He was a smith.

---

He threw himself between the faded man and the shadows.

He didn't use Ember Strike. He didn't use Flame. He reached for Aurelian's little hand-hammer, the one with the open hand and the flame on its head, the one he kept in his belt and had never once used in a fight. It was a tool. A fine-work hammer. For coaxing runes into clay lamps and silver wire.

He pulled it out, and he did the only thing a smith knows how to do with a made thing he doesn't understand.

He looked for the flaw.

He used True Sight. And Forge Sense. And Stone. All at once. On the nearest Umbral Warden. And he *listened* to it, the way he listened to iron. For the dull note under the ring. For the place where the maker had been careless.

And he found it.

It was very small. A seam. Deep in the dark weave of the shadow, at the place where a heart would be. A place where, nine hundred years ago, a smith had folded his own shadow on an anvil, and folded it again, and again, and somewhere in all that folding, he'd been in a hurry, or tired, or afraid. And he'd left a tiny gap. A slag pocket. A flaw.

Lex reached out with Aurelian's hammer and tapped it.

*Tink.*

The Umbral Warden came apart.

Not burst. Not shattered. *Unfolded*. The way a badly-made blade unfolds along its seams when you strike it in the wrong place. The tall dark shape simply opened, like a flower blooming in fast motion, and its folds fell away from each other, and fell, and lay on the heather.

Just shadow. Ordinary shadow. The shadow of a boulder, lying on the ground where it belonged.

The other four stopped.

They turned their smooth dark faces toward him. Toward the little hammer in his hand. And Lex thought, though they had no faces to show it, that they *recognized* it.

*His*, they seemed to say. *His hammer. The maker's hammer.*

"*Yes*," said Lex. "*His*." He raised it. "*And I know where he was careless.*"

---

He unmade them. All five. And the one that had held him by the boulder.

It wasn't fast. It wasn't easy. Each one had its flaw in a different place, and he had to find each one, listening, while they reached for him and he rooted himself in Stone and held. One had its seam in the throat. One at the wrist. One, the last one, so deep in its core that he had to let it get its cold fingers on his thread before he could get close enough to hear it.

But he found them all.

*Tink. Tink. Tink.*

And when it was over, there were six shadows lying on the heather in the starlight, where shadows belonged. And the moor was quiet. And the faded man was sitting in the heather with his faint arms wrapped around his faint knees, shaking.
