# Unveiling the Truth

"*Sit down*," said the thing at the anvil. "*All of you. You've come such a long way.*"

Nobody sat.

It didn't seem to mind. It stood at the great gold anvil in the center of the round white forge, under stars too close and too bright, with its sleeves rolled up and its scarred forearms bare and a smith's hammer resting on the anvil beside it. And smiled at them. One by one. As if it were glad to see every single one.

"*Wren Ashdown*," it said. "*The lightfoot. Who let go of his hand in the sky. That was very brave.*" Its gold eyes moved. "*Kestrel Varn. Who kept her brother. And Tamsin, who was kept. I sang to you, Tamsin, at the edge of the dark. Do you remember? You had such a lovely way of listening.*"

Tamsin's face went white.

"*Seraphine*." The kind face lingered on her, gently, the way a host lingers on a favourite guest. "*The archivist. Who sang the last verse. Terribly.*" It smiled. "*I've never heard anything so terrible. I loved it.*"

"*Stop it*," said Antho.

The gold eyes moved to him. And softened.

"*Antho*," said the thing wearing Aurelian Vey. "*My poor broken boy. Not broken anymore.*" It tilted its head. "*I felt you go. At the Cantor's Stone. The last of the Murmur. Like a lamp going out in a window I'd been watching for eleven years.*" A small, sad smile. "*I'll miss you. You were such good company.*"

"*I was never your company*."

"*Weren't you*?" It looked at him with infinite fondness. "*Eleven years, in your head. Every night. I knew you better than anyone alive. Better than your mother. Better than your brother.*" Its voice dropped. "*I knew every lantern you counted.*"

Antho's hand tightened on the Lantern. But he didn't say anything else.

---

"*And Lex*," it said.

It came around the anvil.

Slowly. Its boots making no sound on the white floor. Tall and fair and beautiful and tired, in its leather apron. It stopped in front of him. Close. Close enough that he could smell it: hot iron, and summer, and the inside of a bread oven. The smell of the Wellworld of Flame. The smell of every forge he'd ever loved.

"*My smith*," it said. "*My blood. My boy.*"

"*I'm not yours*."

"*You will be*." Gently. Without any doubt at all. "*Everything is, in the end. Everything comes down here. To the Heart. Every thread. Every soul. Every road.*" It spread its scarred hands. "*Do you know why?*"

Lex didn't answer.

"*Because I made it so*," said the thing at the Heart. "*Let me show you.*"

And it lifted one hand. And touched the air between them.

And the air turned to *gold*.

---

He was standing on the white mountain.

Not the white mountain at the bottom of the Forbidden Lands. *The* white mountain. In the world. Nine hundred years ago. Under a real sky, blue and enormous, with a real sun. A round forge on its summit, new, unbroken, the white pillars standing straight. A gold anvil at its center.

And a man at the anvil. Fair-haired. Young. Younger than the thing that wore him. Thirty, perhaps. With a smith's hands and a smith's shoulders and gold eyes, bright and *alive*. Working. Striking. Over and over. Something on the anvil that glowed silver and gold and *every* color, so bright it hurt to look at.

A ring. A tiny ring. Of light. Of the river itself, bent round and closed.

He struck it. Once. And it linked through four others, already lying on the anvil. And a fifth. And a sixth.

*Ring by ring*, Lex thought. *Link by link. Like mail*.

Around the forge, at the edges, people. Six of them. Watching. A tall dark-haired woman with Antho's face: Ysmir. A broad grim man with a traveller's staff: Tarn. Two women and a man Lex didn't know. And a boy. Small. Brown. Barefoot. Twelve, perhaps. Sitting on an upturned bucket by the bellows with a bowl of soup in his lap he wasn't eating. Watching his brother work.

*Mal*.

---

"*A year*," said the voice of the thing at the anvil. Beside him. In the vision. Fond. "*A year without sleeping. Ring after ring. Every thread in the world. Every soul.*" It sighed. "*He was magnificent. Wasn't he. The best smith who ever lived.*"

The young Aurelian struck. And struck. And the rings spread out from the anvil across the white floor of the forge, across the mountain, down the slopes, out across the world. A net. Silver. Shining. Settling over everything like frost.

"*And then he had to hold it*," said the voice. "*From the root. From the inside. The net was forged over the hunger, and someone had to be at the center of it, holding the center down.*" A pause. "*He chose himself. Of course he did. He always chose himself. For everything.*"

The vision shifted.

The young Aurelian, standing at the anvil, with the finished net spread out over the world. And his brothers and sisters around him. And the boy on the bucket, standing up. Crying. Saying something Lex couldn't hear.

*You can't cage a storm by becoming the bars*.

And Aurelian, looking down at his little brother. Smiling. Tired. And saying something back.

And then putting his hand on the anvil. And going *down*. Into it. Into the gold. Into the root of the net. Like a man stepping into deep water.

And the boy on the bucket screaming his name.

---

"*He came down to me*," said the thing at the Heart. "*Willingly. To hold me. To be the bars of my cage.*" Its voice was very soft. "*Do you know what it's like? To be a hunger with no shape, in the dark, for longer than the world has existed? And then to have someone come down to you. Willingly. And hold you.*" A pause. "*Close. So close.*"

The vision showed it. Lex couldn't look away. The young Aurelian, in the dark, at the root of the net. Holding something. Something with no shape, that ate light. His arms around it. His face set. His gold eyes fierce. Holding it down. Holding it in.

Year after year.

And the thing in his arms, slowly, patiently, *leaning in*.

"*He held me for a hundred years*," said the voice. "*A hundred years, in the dark, with his arms around me. Brave. Stubborn. Magnificent.*" A long sigh. "*And every year, I got a little further in. Through the bars. Through his arms. Through his skin. Through his fear.*" Its voice was almost tender. "*He was so afraid, Lex. Of me. Of what people did with power. Of himself. I just had to find the fear. And go in through it.*"

The vision showed it. The young Aurelian's gold eyes, year after year, getting dimmer. Duller. Tireder. And something else coming into them. Brighter. Warmer. *Kinder*.

"*And then*," said the thing at the Heart, "*there was no difference anymore. Between the bars and the thing behind them.*"

---

The vision let him go.

He was back in the round white forge at the bottom of the Forbidden Lands. Under the too-close stars. With the thing in Aurelian's face standing in front of him, smiling.

Behind him, he heard Malachi. Making a sound. Very small. Like the boy on the bucket.

"*The Ceiling*," said the thing. Conversationally. As if they'd been chatting by a fire. "*That was mine. After I had him. I sent him back up, wearing his face, and told the others: the net's not strong enough. We need a lid on every thread, so no one ever grows strong enough to lift the lid off me.*" It smiled. "*They agreed. They were so frightened. They'd do anything.*"

"*And the Tithe*," said Lex.

"*And the Tithe*." The thing nodded. "*A tenth. For the binding. To keep the lid strong.*" It spread its hands. "*It was for me, of course. All of it. A tenth of every soul in the world. Running down every thread. Into me.*" Its gold eyes were very warm. "*Eight hundred years. Do you know how much that is? A tenth of everyone. Every day. For eight hundred years.*"

"*Enough to grow*," said Malachi. Hoarse. From the back.

"*Enough to grow*." The thing looked past Lex at the old man. "*Enough to fill the Forbidden Lands with everything I've eaten. Enough to sing through a choir-master and rot through a midwife and pour two drowned brothers into one suit of armor.*" It smiled at Malachi. "*Enough to wait. Patiently. For one of my blood to come down to the Heart.*"

---

"*Why*?" said Seraphine.

Everyone turned. She was standing at the edge of the forge with her notebook clutched against her chest. White. Shaking. But her voice was steady.

"*Why the blood*?" she said. "*If you have Aurelian. If you're wearing him. Why do you need Lex?*"

The thing looked at her for a long moment. And something in its face *flickered*. Just for an instant. A ripple. Like a reflection in disturbed water.

"*Clever girl*," it said softly.

It turned back to Lex.

"*Because he's dead*," it said. "*Aurelian. Mostly. I've eaten nearly all of him. Eight hundred years. There's barely anything left.*" It looked down at its own scarred hands. "*And when he's gone. When I've finished him. I'll have nothing to wear. Nothing to hold. Nothing at the root of the net. And the net will go slack. And the lid will lift.*"

"*And you'll be free*," said Lex.

"*And I'll be* nothing." For the first time, the kind voice had an edge. Sharp. Hungry. "*Free in the dark. With nothing to hold. Nothing to wear. Nothing to eat but the dark itself. Do you know what that's like? Do you know what I was before he came down to me?*"

It leaned close.

"*Empty*," whispered the thing at the Heart. "*Empty forever. A hunger with no mouth. In the dark. Alone.*"

Lex didn't move.

"*So I need another*," it said. Softer again. Kind again. "*Another of the blood. To come down to the Heart. To the root. To hold me, the way he did.*" It smiled. "*And then I'll have a new face. A new pair of hands. A new smith.*"

"*Me*."

"*You*." Fondly. "*You've already opened the door. In the glass. When you touched my shadow. It's been open ever since. Right there. At the bottom of your gold.*" It reached out and touched his chest. Over his heart. Over the Root. "*And the bottom of your gold is* here. *The Heart. Every Root starts here. Every Vey thread runs down to this anvil.*"

Its fingers were warm. So warm.

"*You're standing on your own door, Lex*," said the thing in his ancestor's face. "*You've been walking toward it since the festival. Since the baker. Since the Ledger and the glass and the Rift.*" Its gold eyes were very bright. "*All you have to do is open it.*"

---

"*And the prophecy*," said Wren.

She was standing at Lex's shoulder. Bow in her hand. Arrow on the string. Pointed at the thing's face. Her green eyes very hard.

"*The song*," she said. "*At Harrowgate. The Root lies bound, the heir must come. You wrote that.*"

"*I did*." It didn't look at her. It was still looking at Lex. "*I've written a great many things. Through a great many hands. Through Aurelian's, at first. Then through the Cantor's mouth. Through Elric's ear.*" A small smile. "*The Veys love a prophecy. They can never resist one.*"

"*And Ysmir's*?" said Lex. "*The verse in the vault. Bow and bit and blade. The blood that bars it, the blood will pay.*"

For the first time, the thing at the Heart hesitated.

Just for a moment.

"*That one*," it said slowly, "*was not mine.*"

"*No*," said Malachi, from the back. Very quietly. "*It wasn't. My sister wrote it. And you hated it.*" The old man's voice was shaking. "*Because she was the Keeper of What Was. And she wrote what was true.*"

---

"*Why did you choose Arion*?" said Antho.

The thing looked at him.

"*Two hundred years ago*," said Antho. "*You asked our father. Every night. For a year. Come down. Let me in.*" His clear grey eyes were very steady. "*Why him?*"

The thing in Aurelian's face smiled. Very slowly.

"*Because he was the strongest*," it said. "*The strongest Vey in eight hundred years. And the most afraid.*" It tilted its head. "*Do you know what your father was afraid of, Antho? At thirty-one? When the Root stopped him like a clock?*"

Antho didn't answer.

"*Of being alone*," said the thing. "*Of standing at the front of the world forever while everyone he loved grew old behind him. Of being Aurelian.*" It smiled. "*I told him I'd keep him company. Forever. I told him he'd never be alone again.*"

"*And he said no*."

"*And he said no*." The smile faded. Just a little. "*Every night. For a year. Not because he saw through me. Because he didn't want to be told what to do.*" It shook its head. "*The most stubborn man I've ever met. Worse than Aurelian.*"

"*So you waited*."

"*So I waited*." It looked at Lex. "*For someone less stubborn. And less afraid of being alone.*" Its gold eyes were very soft. "*Someone who's never been alone in his life. Who's always had a brother, and a father, and a little voice in his head, and a girl with a bow.*" It smiled. "*Someone who'd do anything to keep them.*"

---

Oriel Sant laughed.

Everyone turned. Even the thing at the anvil.

The old blind woman was sitting on the white floor at the edge of the forge, where Tamsin had set her down, with her black thorn stick across her knees and her red shawl around her shoulders. Her white eyes were turned toward the anvil. And she was laughing. A thin dry crackle, like dead leaves.

"*What's funny, old woman*?" said the thing at the Heart. Kindly.

"*I can't see you*," said Oriel Sant.

The thing went still.

"*I've never seen anything in my life*," said Oriel. "*Not with eyes. But I've always seen what's* there. *I saw the smith's little fire. I saw the door at the bottom of his gold. I saw a big man holding up a mountain.*" She tilted her white face toward the anvil. "*And I look at you, and there's nothing there. Nothing at all.*" She smiled. "*Just a smith's apron and a pair of scarred hands and a kind voice. Hanging on nothing. Like a coat on a hook.*"

The kind face didn't change.

But behind it, very far down, Lex felt the thing at the door at the bottom of his Root *flinch*.

"*And behind the nothing*," said Oriel softly, "*very small. Very far down. Something that* is *there.*" Her white eyes were very bright. "*Tired. Sad. Holding a hammer. Trying to sing.*"

*Ting*, said the bell in Malachi's hand.

---

The thing at the Heart turned away from them.

It walked back to the anvil. Slowly. And picked up the hammer. Aurelian's hammer. The great one. Not the little hand-hammer Lex carried in his belt. The one from the vision. The one that had struck every ring in the world.

"*You don't have to believe me*," it said. Its back to them. "*It doesn't matter. Nothing you believe matters, here.*" It turned the hammer over in its scarred hands. "*Because I'm going to show you something else.*"

It struck the anvil.

*Once*.

---

The whole world *rang*.

Lex felt it. Through the Root. Through every thread. A single great note, going out from the anvil at the Heart, up the pillar of gold, up every grey tithe-thread falling into it, up and out into the world. Into every Stone. Every thread. Every soul.

And then he *saw* it.

The Heart was the start of every thread. Every Root. Every soul. And standing on it, with his own Root humming in him, he could see them all. The whole net. The whole world. As if he were standing at the top of the white mountain looking down at a map spread out below, every thread shining.

Highcrest. The Great Stone in the square. Silver. Quiet.

Saffaran. On its mesa. Burning gold with the Ashari fire.

The Wickhollow. Under the Bitter Hills. A thousand candles.

Ashbourne. The forge. Twenty anvils. A girl with freckles asleep on a bench by the great hearth.

The Hollowmaw. A cracked black door. Two lamps in a niche. A man with his hands on the glass.

And every Stone. Four thousand in Caldor. Thousands more. Every one with a thin grey thread running down from it. Down. Into the dark. Into the Forbidden Lands. Into the pillar of gold rising from the anvil.

*A tenth*.

And the thing at the anvil lifted its hammer again.

"*Watch*," it said.

And struck.

---

The tithe-threads went *taut*.

Every one. All at once. Like the strings of a harp pulled tight. And then *pulled*. Down. Hard.

Not a tenth.

*More*.

Lex felt it. Felt the threads all over the world, every soul under the net, suddenly losing more than a tenth. A fifth. A quarter. Being drawn down. Through the Stones. Into the dark. Into the pillar.

And all over the world, people *faltered*.

He saw it. In Highcrest, in the dark before dawn, a city stirring in its sleep. People sitting up in their beds, gasping. Clutching their chests. Feeling something go out of them, the way you feel the warmth go out of a room when someone opens a door in winter.

In Saffaran, the Ashari fire guttering on every terrace.

In the Wickhollow, every candle flickering.

In Ashbourne, Tilda Crane jerking awake on her bench with a cry, staring at her own hands.

"*STOP*," shouted Lex.

The thing at the anvil lowered its hammer.

The threads went slack. The pull stopped. Not all the way. A tenth was still running down. The way it always had. But not more.

All over the world, people lay back down in their beds. Breathing hard. Bewildered.

---

"*That was a quarter*," said the thing at the Heart. Mildly. "*For a moment. Just to show you.*" It set the hammer down on the anvil. Gently. "*I can take half. If I strike harder. I can take all of it.*"

It turned around.

"*I am the Tithe, Lex*," it said. "*I am in every thread in the world. Every soul your ancestor ever caught in his net, I have my hand in. A tenth of everyone.*" Its gold eyes were very calm. "*If I pull, they come. All of them. Down to me. Every soul in the world. Emptied. Like Millbridge. Like Hollin's Rest.*"

Nobody moved.

"*Or*," said the thing at the Heart, "*you open the door.*"

It came around the anvil again. Slowly. And stopped in front of Lex. And held out its hand. The way Elric had held out his hand at Ysmir's vault. Patient. Reasonable. A teacher's hand.

"*Open the door*," it said. "*Let me in. And I'll stop pulling. I'll take only the tenth. As I always have. The world will go on. Your friends will go home. Your brother will count his lanterns. Your girl will shoot her arrows.*" Its voice was very gentle. "*And you'll be the Root of the world. Forever. Like Aurelian. Like your father. Never alone again.*"

"*And you'll be wearing me*."

"*And I'll be keeping you company*." It smiled. "*That's all. Just company. In the dark. At the bottom of your gold.*"

---

He felt the door.

At the bottom of his Root. Right there. Under his feet. Under the anvil. Under everything. Open. A crack. Warm. Breathing.

He felt the thing on the other side of it, leaning on it. Gently. Patiently. The way the Hollow had leaned on his mother's door for eleven years. The way the hunger had leaned on Aurelian for a hundred. Not pushing. Just *leaning*. Waiting for the hinges to give.

And he felt something else.

Further down. Below the warmth. Below the leaning. Very far down, in the deepest gold, where the first thread went down into the dark. Something small. Tired. Sad.

Holding on.

*Lex*, it said. Faintly. Through the door. Through everything. A rough voice. A voice that lost the tune. *Lex, don't. Don't. I held it for a hundred years and it still got in. Don't let it in. Look for the*—

The warmth surged. The small voice cut off.

"*Ignore that*," said the thing in front of him, kindly. "*That's only what's left. He's very confused, at the end.*"

---

"*Lex*."

Antho. Behind him. Very quiet.

Lex didn't turn. He couldn't. He was looking at the hand held out in front of him. Scarred. A smith's hand. Like his own.

"*Lex*," said Antho again. "*It's lying.*"

"*About what*?"

"*About company*." His brother's voice was very steady. Very clear. "*I had it in me for eleven years. It never once kept me company. It sat in my head and whispered and showed me things and wore Mam's face.*" A pause. "*And I was the loneliest person in the world.*"

The thing's hand didn't move. But its gold eyes flickered. Toward Antho. Just for a moment.

"*It doesn't keep anyone company*," said Antho. "*It doesn't know how. It's a hunger. It eats.*" His voice dropped. "*It ate Aurelian for a hundred years with its arms around him and he was alone the whole time.*"

Far down, at the bottom of Lex's Root, the small tired voice made a sound. Like someone weeping.

"*Don't*," said Antho. "*Lex. Don't let it in. Whatever it does. Whatever it pulls.*" Lex felt his brother's hand close on his sleeve. Between finger and thumb. "*We'll find another way. We always find another way.*"

---

"*There isn't another way*," said the thing at the Heart.

It said it very kindly. Very sadly. As if it truly regretted it.

"*There's the door*," it said. "*Or the Tithe. That's all. I've built it that way. Every road comes here.*" It looked at Lex. "*Choose, my boy. The door. Or the world.*"

And it turned its head.

Toward the edge of the forge. Toward the dark beyond the broken white pillars, where the Forbidden Lands stretched away forever, and the drifting pieces of eaten worlds turned slowly in the emptiness.

"*And while you're choosing*," it said, "*I've sent for my Warden.*"

---

She came out of the dark.

Lex saw her. Walking. Across the empty void, toward the white mountain, the way the Ferryman's boat had come across the black ink. Not on anything. Just walking. A tall gaunt figure in torn white robes gone grey. Ragged silver hair hanging in her face.

No face.

Where a face should be, *unremembered*. His eyes slid off it.

She came up the white slope. Slowly. Not looking at anyone. And stepped into the round forge, between two broken pillars. And stood there. With her hands at her sides.

"*My Warden*," said the thing at the Heart. Fondly. "*She keeps the door. Your mother's door. At the Hollowmaw. Every night. From the inside.*" It smiled at Lex. "*If you don't open your door, she'll open that one. And everything I've eaten in nine hundred years will come up through it. Into your father's face.*"

The faceless woman didn't move.

But her hands, at her sides, *clenched*.

Lex saw it. The way he'd seen it through the black glass at the Hollowmaw. A tiny movement. A tightening.

As if, very far down, something in her didn't agree.

---

And then he heard it.

Behind the Warden. In the dark. Far off. Across the empty void, from the direction she'd come.

Faint. So faint. A woman's voice. Low and a little rough. As if from long disuse.

Singing.

*If the dark comes calling, love, don't you mind.*

He went absolutely still.

Beside him, Antho's hand on his sleeve went rigid.

*A lantern's lit for the ones behind*.

It was faint. It was far. It was barely there at all, coming out of the dark behind the faceless woman, following her, the way a lamp follows someone through a dark house.

And on the high note—

It went *flat*.

Just a little. Just a hair. The way it always had. Every time. In a cottage at Harrow, in the dark, when Lex was small and frightened of the noises in the walls.

The way it used to drive their father mad.

"*Lex*," whispered Antho. His voice was shaking. "*Lex. She's flat on the high note.*"

And the thing at the Heart, standing at the anvil with its hand still held out, turned its head toward the sound.

And for the first time since Lex had known it, in the Glass Desert and the Rift and the Forbidden Lands, the kind face in Aurelian's shape looked *surprised*.
