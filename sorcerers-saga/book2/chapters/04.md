# The Temple of Ysmir

The world ended in ice.

Lex had known it would. Seraphine's maps said so. North of the Ironbacks, the land fell away in long grey slopes to a coast that no one in Caldor had visited in living memory, and beyond the coast, the sea froze. Not in winter. Always. A white sea, flat and endless, stretching away under a pale sky to a horizon that shimmered with strange lights at night. The old texts called it the White Shore. The maps just stopped.

He came down to it on the fourteenth day out of Highcrest.

He didn't talk much anymore. When he did, his voice was so faint Lex had to stop walking to hear it.

"*There*," he said, on the fourteenth evening.

Lex looked.

The coast curved away to the east in a long white arc. And at the far end of the arc, where a great river of ice came down out of the mountains and met the frozen sea, there was a glacier. Blue-white and enormous, a wall of ice a thousand feet high. And carved into its face, the way the Barrow-King's door had been carved into the side of the mound, there was a temple.

Columns. Arches. Stairs. All carved from the living ice of the glacier itself, blue and white and glittering in the last of the sunset. A great arched doorway, three hundred feet tall, opening into darkness. And above it, carved into the ice in letters taller than a man, Wellscript.

*[Ysmir,]* read Ember. *[Temple of Ysmir. Keeper of What Was.]*

And the scars led straight to it.

A line of them. Dozens. Keyhole-shaped, black, healed over, hanging in the air above the frozen shore like footprints. Old and new. Faint and dark. Leading up the long white slope of the shore to the great arched doorway in the ice.

And the doorway was cracked.

Lex could see it from a mile off. A great jagged split running up through the carved ice of the arch, from the threshold to the keystone. Black. Fresh. And spreading out from it, smaller cracks, a web of them, running through the columns and the stairs and the carved Wellscript like the cracks in a dropped plate.

*[He's been breaking it,]* said Ember quietly. *[Every time he comes. A little more.]*

*Why?*

*[I don't know. Looking for something, maybe. Or just—not caring what he breaks to get it.]*

---

The inside of the temple was blue.

Blue ice. Blue light. The light came from the ice itself, the way the white stone had glowed at Avaloria, a soft blue radiance that came from everywhere and cast no shadows. The great doorway opened into a hall so vast that Lex couldn't see its ceiling, only a blue dimness overhead, like the inside of a frozen wave. Columns of ice as thick as oaks rose up into it. The floor was ice too, smooth and clear and so deep that Lex could see things frozen in it far below his feet.

He stopped. He looked down.

There were *things* in the ice. Under the floor. Deep down, suspended like flies in amber.

A book, open, its pages spread. A sword. A child's toy, a little wooden boat. A woman's comb. A loaf of bread. A letter, folded. Hundreds of things. Thousands. Scattered through the deep blue ice under the floor of the hall like stars in a night sky.

*[Ysmir,]* said Ember slowly. *[Keeper of What Was. She was one of the Founders. Aurelian's sister. The second oldest. Malachi mentioned her, once.]* A pause. *[The Founders' records say she kept the memories of the world. Things that were lost. Things that were forgotten. She froze them here, so they'd never be lost again.]*

"*Memories*," whispered the faded man. He was standing very still beside Lex, looking down into the ice. "*They're memories.*"

He knelt. He laid his faint hand on the ice of the floor. Lex saw his fingers go through it, a little. Not all the way. Just into the surface, the way a hand goes into deep water.

"*I can feel them*," said the faded man. "*Every one. A boy who lost his boat in a river. A woman who lost her comb on her wedding day. A man who wrote a letter to his brother and never sent it.*" His voice cracked. "*They're all here. Everything anyone ever lost.*"

Lex knelt beside him.

"Is yours here?" he said quietly. "Your name?"

The faded man looked at him. Then down into the ice. Searching. Deep down. Further.

"*No*," he whispered. "*Not here. Not yet.*" He swallowed. "*But maybe deeper.*"

---

They went deeper.

The great hall opened at its far end into passages. Dozens of them. Branching away into the glacier like the roots of a tree. Blue and glowing and silent. And every passage had things frozen in its walls and floor and ceiling. Memories. Lost things. A pair of spectacles. A wedding ring. A drawing, in a child's hand, of a house with a lantern in the window.

The scars led them. The black keyhole-shaped scars, hanging in the air of the passages. Elric had come this way. Many times. And every time he'd come, he'd broken something. The cracks were everywhere here. Running through the walls. Splitting the frozen memories inside them. Lex passed a crack that ran straight through a frozen letter, splitting it in half, and the two halves had drifted apart in the ice like two pieces of a broken boat.

*[He doesn't care,]* said Ember. Its voice was very cold. *[About any of it. He walks through here and breaks it as he goes.]*

The faded man walked close beside Lex, and every time they passed a cracked memory, he flinched.

And then they heard the fire.

---

It came from ahead. Down a passage. Around a bend. A roar. Not the roar of a wind or a beast. The deep throaty crackling roar of a fire. A *big* one. Bigger than anything should be able to burn in a temple made of ice.

And a voice. A woman's. Shouting.

"*Come on, then! Come ON! I've burned a dozen of you already! I'll burn a dozen more! Come and get it, you faceless—*"

The rest of it was lost in the roar.

Lex ran.

He came around the bend into a round chamber of blue ice, and stopped.

There was a woman in the middle of it.

She was tall and lean and fierce, in a long red coat scorched black at the hem, with a mane of red hair so bright it looked like it was burning. It *was* burning. Lex saw that a heartbeat later. Little flames were dancing along the ends of it, licking up toward her ears, and she didn't seem to notice. Her face was sharp and freckled and twisted in a snarl. And her hands—

Her hands were on fire. Both of them. To the wrist. Great roaring gouts of orange-red flame pouring off her fingers, and she was flinging them in great arcs around her, a whirling storm of fire that lit the whole round chamber orange and set the blue ice walls streaming with meltwater.

And around her, closing in, the Umbral Wardens.

Eight of them. Tall and dark and featureless, their long shadowy fingers reaching. And her fire was going *through* them. The way Lex's Ember Strike had gone through them on the moor. They rippled. They reformed. They kept coming.

"*Burn*, *damn* you!" the red-haired woman was screaming. "*Why won't you BURN?*"

One of them reached her. Its long dark fingers touched her chest.

And *pulled*.

Lex saw her stagger. Saw her face go white with shock. Saw her fire flicker and gutter.

And then saw something that made his heart stop.

Nothing happened.

The shadow's fingers were on her chest, pulling, the way they'd pulled on Lex's thread on the moor, the way they'd pulled on the faded man's. But there was nothing there to pull. No thread. No silver. No gold. Nothing running out of her into the net at all.

*[She's not in the Lattice,]* said Ember sharply. *[Lex. She's not in it at all. She's never been Kindled.]*

The shadow pulled at nothing. And the red-haired woman, finding herself still standing, still whole, threw back her burning head and *laughed*. A wild, delighted, furious laugh.

"*Ha!*" she shouted. "*Can't get me, can you! Nothing to hook! Nothing to—*"

And the other seven shadows, finding nothing to pull, simply raised their long dark hands, and struck her.

Not her thread. *Her*. Her body. Seven blows at once, from seven directions, cold and hard as black ice. And she went down.

---

Lex was there before she hit the floor.

He didn't think about it. He went in under the reaching shadows with Aurelian's little hammer already in his hand and True Sight already blazing behind his eyes, listening for the flaws. *Tink.* One. *Tink.* Two. They came apart around him like flowers blooming in fast motion, unfolding, falling, lying on the blue ice floor as ordinary shadows.

*Tink. Tink. Tink.*

The last one had its flaw so deep he had to step into its arms to find it. Cold. So cold. Its dark fingers on his chest, on his thread, pulling. He rooted himself in Stone and held. And reached in, past the cold, past the dark, and found it. A tiny seam. A place where a smith nine hundred years ago had folded too fast.

*Tink.*

It unfolded.

And the round chamber of blue ice was quiet. And full of steam. And there were eight ordinary shadows lying on the floor, and a woman in a scorched red coat lying among them, and a young man with gold eyes standing over her with a little hand-hammer in his fist.

The woman rolled over. She looked up at him.

Her eyes were amber. Bright, fierce, burning amber, like a fire seen through honey. They took in his face. His gold eyes. The hammer in his hand. The eight dead shadows around them.

And her face twisted.

"*You*," she snarled. "*You're with HIM.*"

She came up off the floor like a striking snake. Fire roared off her hands. She swung a burning fist at his head.

---

He caught it.

He didn't know he was going to. His hand just came up, the way it came up to catch a falling tool, and closed around her burning wrist. And the fire, which should have taken the skin off his palm, which would have taken the skin off anyone else's palm, *leaned*.

Toward him.

The way the fire in Malachi's hearth leaned. The way the eternal fire under the water at Qel'Varran leaned. Her fire, her wild red-orange fire, pouring off her fingers, bent toward his hand like a flower toward a window. And curled around his fingers. And didn't burn.

The red-haired woman stared at her own hand.

At her fire, wrapping itself around a stranger's fingers like a cat around an ankle.

"*What*," she said. Very quietly. "*What are you?*"

"A smith," said Lex.

"*That's not what I asked.*"

"It's the only answer I've got." He let go of her wrist. Slowly. Her fire stayed curled around his fingers for a moment, then drifted back to her, reluctantly. "I'm not with him. The old man with the key. I'm hunting him."

"*Liar*." But she didn't swing again. She was staring at him. At his eyes. "*You've got gold eyes. Root eyes. Like him. Like his key. I've seen them in the dark, when he cuts people out.*" Her voice cracked. "*And you unmade those things like you knew where they'd been made. Like you* made *them.*"

"They were made by my ancestor," said Lex. "Nine hundred years ago. I know where he was careless."

"*Your—*" She stopped.

*[Lex,]* said Ember. *[Look at her. Properly.]*

He looked. With True Sight. Deep.

And there was nothing there.

No panel. No silver. No gold. No threads. Like Malachi. The Lattice looked at her and saw nothing at all. She'd never been Kindled. She'd never been in the net.

But there was something else. Not in the Lattice. *Under* it. In the place where the river ran. He could feel it, the way he felt Flame in Malachi's hearth. A fire. Wild and bright and furious and *hers*. Answering her. The way it answered him.

And then Lex felt something else.

Behind him. In the doorway of the round chamber. Faint as mist.

The faded man.

He'd come in. Lex hadn't noticed. He was standing in the doorway with his faint hands at his sides and his faint face turned toward the red-haired woman. And he was *brighter*.

Not much. Not solid. But brighter. More there. The blue ice of the doorway showing a little less clearly through his chest. And his dark eyes were fixed on the woman's face, wide and wondering.

"*Red hair*," he whispered. "*Laughing. Fire.*"

The woman didn't turn. She couldn't see him. She couldn't hear him. She was still staring at Lex.

But her hand, the one that had been on fire, came up to her chest. To the place over her heart. And pressed there. As if something had hurt her.

"*What*," she said, "*did you just do?*"

"Nothing," said Lex. "I didn't do anything." He didn't look away from her. "What's your name?"

She hesitated. "*Kestrel*," she said. "*Kestrel Varn.*"
