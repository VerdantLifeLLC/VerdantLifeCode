# The Twinned Knight

Seraphine knew the last verse.

Of course she did. Lex should have guessed. She stood up in the long grey boat on the black ink of the Forbidden Lands, with her notebook clutched against her chest and her spectacles fogged and her voice shaking, and said:

"*I found it*."

Everyone turned.

"*In the Spire*," said Seraphine. "*In the sealed vaults. When I was cataloguing.*" She swallowed. "*There's a copy of the ballad. The Knights of the Ford. The oldest one. Written down two years after the battle by a priest who was there. Who saw them pulled out of the river.*" She looked at Ilse, standing alone on the black ink ten paces from the Knight. "*Everyone sings it wrong. Everyone. Brannoc. Every tavern in Caldor. They all end it at the wrong verse.*"

"*How does it end*?" said Ilse. Without turning.

Seraphine took a breath.

And sang. Badly. Her voice cracking on every note. A thin, wavering, terrible voice, in the dark at the bottom of the world.

*Oh the river rose and the river ran,*
*and the brothers stood as one,*
*and Cael said, Corran, let me go,*
*and live to see the sun.*

*And Corran said, I'll never go,*
*I'll hold you to the last,*
*and the water rose above their heads,*
*and held the brothers fast.*

She stopped. Swallowed. Kept going.

*But under the water, the old priest said,*
*where no one else could see,*
*Cael let go of his brother's hand*
*and pushed him to the lee.*

*And Corran felt the current take him,*
*felt the bank beneath his heel,*
*and turned around, and dove back down,*
*for the brother he could not feel.*

*And so they drowned, the ballad says,*
*holding each other tight.*
*But they did not drown because they held.*
*They drowned because each tried to let the other go,*
*and the other one came back.*

The last line didn't scan. It didn't rhyme. It was barely a verse at all. Seraphine spoke it more than sang it, in a whisper, with tears running down behind her spectacles.

The black ink was very still.

---

The Twinned Knight had not moved.

It stood at the foot of the white mountain with its four black arms wrapped around Ivo, and its helm tilted down toward Ilse, and it didn't move at all.

But inside the helm, through the narrow slit, Lex could see the two heads. Overlapping. Slightly off. And they were *turning*. Slowly. Toward each other. As if looking at each other for the first time.

"*Cael*," said one voice. Very quietly.

"*Corran*," said the other.

"*You let go*." The first voice was shaking. "*Under the water. You let go of my hand.*"

"*You came back*." The second voice was shaking too. "*I pushed you to the bank. I felt you get there. I felt your heel touch. And you came back.*"

"*Of course I came back*."

"*You could have lived*."

"*Without you*?"

The helm trembled.

"*I let go so you could live*," said Cael. Inside the armor. Three hundred years too late. "*And you came back for me. And we both drowned.*"

"*And the dark said*," said Corran, "*you'll never have to let go now.*"

"*And poured us into one*."

"*So we'd never have to choose again*."

The four black arms were shaking now. All of them. Around Ivo Marrow. Holding him. Pouring him in.

---

"*He did the same thing*," said Ilse.

She took a step forward. Nine paces.

"*My brother*," she said. "*At the Stone. When you took him. I was holding his wrist. I wouldn't let go.*" Her voice was very steady. "*And he made his wrist look like nothing. So my hands would open. So you'd only take one of us.*"

Eight paces.

"*He let go*," said Ilse Marrow. "*So I could live.*" She looked up at the helm. At the two heads inside it, overlapping, turning toward each other. "*And I came back.*"

Seven.

"*I came all the way down here*," said Ilse. "*Across the grey river. Through the Drowned Country. Over the edge of the world. For him.*" She stopped. Six paces. Close enough to see her brother's face, white and faint and half-poured into the black armor. "*And now I don't know what to do. Because if I take him back, I'm doing what Corran did. Coming back. And if I don't, I'm leaving him.*"

She looked up at the Twinned Knight.

"*So you tell me*," she said. "*You've had three hundred years to think about it.*" Her voice cracked, just once. "*Which one was right? The one who let go, or the one who came back?*"

---

Nobody moved.

On the black ink, at the foot of the white mountain, under a pillar of gold light, a girl in a patched green coat stood six paces from a knight made of two dead brothers. And waited.

And the Twinned Knight answered.

Not with words. With its arms.

The four black arms loosened. Slowly. One by one. The way a man's hands loosen when he's been gripping something so long he's forgotten how to open them. The black gauntlets came away from Ivo's shoulders. His side. His arm.

And where they came away, he came *back*. Solid. Whole. The faint overlapping blur of him sharpening, like a drawing being inked in. His face. His hands. His mouse-brown hair.

The Knight lowered him. Very gently. Onto the black ink. At its feet.

And stepped back.

"*Both*," said Corran's voice. Very softly. Inside the helm.

"*Both were right*," said Cael's.

"*The one who lets go is right*." The first voice.

"*And the one who comes back is right*." The second.

"*It's only*," said both voices together, "*that you have to let the other one choose.*"

---

Ilse was on her knees beside her brother.

She had his face in her hands. She was saying his name. Over and over. *Ivo, Ivo, Ivo*. And his eyes opened. Grey. Clear. Confused.

"*Ilse*?" he said. Hoarse. "*You're—where are we? What*—"

"*You made your wrist look like nothing*," said Ilse Marrow. "*You stupid—you absolute—you made your* wrist *look like* nothing.*"

"*Oh*." Ivo blinked up at her. "*Yes. I did, didn't I.*" A pause. "*Did it work?*"

"*It worked*." She was crying. Laughing. Both. "*It worked perfectly. My hands just let go. They believed you.*" She pressed her forehead against his. "*It's the best illusion you've ever done. I hate you.*"

"*I know*," said Ivo. And put his arms around her. "*I hate you too. You came all the way down here.*"

"*Of course I came all the way down here*."

"*You could have stayed. You could have lived*—"

"*Without you*?" said Ilse Marrow.

And Ivo stopped. And looked at her. And Lex saw him understand.

He didn't say anything else. He just held on.

---

The Twinned Knight was coming apart.

Lex saw it. Standing in the boat with Wren's hand crushing his. The black and silver armor, so solid, so enormous, beginning to *separate*. Not breaking. Not cracking. *Untangling*. Like two threads that had been twisted together for three hundred years, slowly unwinding.

Two shapes. Stepping apart. Out of the one armor.

Two knights. Young. Younger than Lex had imagined. Twenty, perhaps. Fair-haired, both of them, with the same face, the same jaw, the same eyes. In old-fashioned mail, rusted and torn, the way it must have looked when they pulled them out of the river. Faint. Grey. Translucent. Ghosts.

But *two*.

They stood side by side on the black ink, where the Twinned Knight had stood. And looked at each other.

"*Corran*," said one.

"*Cael*," said the other.

And then they laughed. Both of them. At the same moment. The same laugh. Startled and helpless, like two boys who've just got away with something.

"*Three hundred years*," said Corran. "*In one suit of armor. With you.*"

"*You snore*," said Cael.

"*We don't sleep*."

"*You'd snore if we did*."

They looked at each other. And then, very simply, the way you'd reach for your brother's hand on any ordinary day, they reached out. And took each other's hands.

Not gripping. Not holding on for dear life. Just holding. Loosely. Easily. The way you hold something you've chosen to hold, and could let go of, and don't.

"*Shall we*?" said Cael.

"*Let's*," said Corran.

And the Knights of the Ford walked away together across the black ink of the Forbidden Lands. Hand in hand. Not toward the Heart. Away from it. Out into the empty dark, between the drifting pieces of eaten worlds. Getting fainter. Getting smaller.

Until they were gone.

---

They sat on the black ink at the foot of the white mountain for a long time after.

Nobody wanted to move. Ilse and Ivo sat together, a little apart, holding each other. Not talking. Seraphine had sat down in the bottom of the boat with her notebook in her lap and her face in her hands. Antho was beside her, with his arm around her shoulders.

"*You sang*," he said quietly. "*In front of everyone.*"

"*Don't*," said Seraphine, through her fingers. "*I know. I was terrible. I've never been so terrible at anything in my life.*"

"*You were*," Antho agreed. "*You were absolutely terrible.*" He pressed his face into her hair. "*It was the best thing I've ever heard.*"

Wren had come to sit beside Lex on the black ink. She leaned against his arm. He leaned back.

"*Hammer*," she said. Very quietly. "*Can you feel it*?"

He could.

He'd been feeling it since the boat stopped. Since they came in sight of the white mountain. Growing. Getting closer. Not from the mountain. From *inside*.

The door.

At the bottom of the gold. At the bottom of the Root. The door he'd found in the Glass Desert, when he touched the Bit. The door that had swung wide at the Rift, when the kind voice said *come home*. The door that had been open a crack ever since.

It was *right there*. Under him. Under the black ink. Under the white mountain. As if the bottom of his own Root and the foot of this mountain were the same place.

*Every road goes to the Heart*, Malachi had said. *Including the one at the bottom of your gold.*

He couldn't feel Ember. Not since the waterfall. Not since the net went out. He was alone in his own head.

Except for the warmth. At the door.

Breathing.

---

"*Up*," said Malachi.

The old man had climbed out of the boat. He was standing on the black ink at the foot of the white mountain, small and brown and barefoot, with his straw hat in one hand and the little silver bell in the other.

It was ringing. Louder now. Steadily. *Ting. Ting. Ting.*

"*Up*," he said again. "*He's up there. At the forge. At the anvil.*" He looked up at the white slope, rising out of the black ink into the empty dark, toward the round open forge at its summit and the pillar of gold light. "*Both of him.*"

"*Both*?" said Kestrel.

"*The hunger*." Malachi didn't look away from the summit. "*And my brother.*" He was quiet. "*I can feel them. Through the bell. The bell's ringing for him. And something's answering.*" His shifting eyes were wet. "*Faintly. Very faintly. But something's answering.*"

The Ferryman stood in his long grey boat on the black ink. He hadn't moved since they stopped. His eyeless face was turned up toward the summit.

"*I will wait*," he said. "*Here. At the foot. For whoever comes back down.*" A long pause. "*If anyone comes back down, I will take them home. Without a fee.*"

"*And if no one does*?" said Wren.

The Ferryman was silent.

"*Then I will take the river home alone*," he said at last. "*And it will run grey forever.*"

---

They climbed.

The white mountain was smooth. Like bone. Like the white stone of Avaloria, which glowed from within. It rose steeply out of the black ink, and there was no path, but it wasn't hard to climb. It was as if it *wanted* them to climb. As if every step had been worn smooth for them, a long time ago, by someone who'd known they'd come.

Lex went first. Then Antho, with the Lantern. Then Wren. Then the others, in a long straggling line. Kestrel and Tamsin. Seraphine. Ilse and Ivo, holding hands. Teo. Oriel, on Tamsin's back now; her mule had stayed in the boat with the Ferryman. And Malachi last, slow and barefoot, with the bell ringing in his hand.

The pillar of gold light rose ahead of them. From the summit. Into the dark. Lex could feel it now. Warm. Gold. Like the knot at the bottom of the Rift. Every grey tithe-thread in the Forbidden Lands converging on it. Falling into it. Feeding it.

And under it, the warmth at the bottom of his own Root. Getting stronger. With every step.

*Come up, child*, it said. Very softly. For the first time since the Rift. *Come up. I've been waiting.*

He didn't answer.

He kept climbing.

---

Halfway up, Antho caught his sleeve.

Lex stopped. Turned.

His brother was standing on the white slope just below him, with the Lantern in his hand and his grey eyes very dark. Not the soft black of Shade. Something else. Something Lex had never seen in them before.

*Clear*.

All the way through. Clear as glass. As a still pool. As the sky over the Wellworld of Gale.

"*It's talking to you*," said Antho. "*Isn't it. Again. At the door.*"

"*Yes*."

"*What's it saying*?"

"*Come up*." Lex swallowed. "*It's been waiting.*"

Antho looked at him for a long moment.

"*At the Rift*," he said. "*I held your sleeve. And pulled you back through the Bloodbond. With Shade. Quenched it.*" He was quiet. "*It worked because I had the Murmur in me. Eleven years of the Hollow. I knew what it felt like. I knew how to hold a door against it.*"

"*And now*?"

"*And now the Murmur's gone*." Antho's clear grey eyes held his. "*All the way. Clarity a hundred. Hollow-link severed.*" His mouth twisted. "*I don't have a door in me anymore, Lex. I've got a house. An empty clean house with nothing in it but me.*"

Lex didn't say anything.

"*So I don't know*," said Antho. "*If I can do it again. Pull you back. I don't know if I've still got the trick of it.*" He looked down at the Lantern in his hand. "*I've spent eleven years learning how to keep a door shut. And now I haven't got one.*"

"*You've got mine*," said Lex.

His brother looked up.

"*The door at the bottom of my Root*," said Lex. "*It's right there. Under us. I can feel it. Like a draft.*" He swallowed. "*You know doors, Antho. Better than anyone alive. You held one for eight years from this side, and Mam held it from the other, and neither of you knew.*" His voice cracked. "*If it opens. When it opens. Up there. You're the only one I'd trust to hold it.*"

Antho stood on the white slope and looked at him for a very long time.

Then he reached out. And took hold of Lex's sleeve. Between finger and thumb.

"*I'm not letting go*," he said.

"*I know*."

"*Not this time. Not ever.*"

"*I know*."

They stood like that for a moment. On the white mountain. Under the pillar of gold. Two brothers. One holding the other's sleeve.

And then Antho let go. And they climbed on.

---

The summit was a forge.

Lex had known it would be. He'd seen it in his dream, at the Hollowmaw, months ago. He'd seen it through a window of ice nine hundred years deep, in Ysmir's vault.

A great round forge, open to the dark. A floor of white stone. A ring of white pillars, broken, some of them, fallen. No roof. Above, where the sky should be, the empty dark, and in it, impossibly, stars. Too close. Too bright. Lex could almost hear them.

And in the center of the forge, an anvil.

Gold. Huge. The size of a cart. Battered and scarred by a thousand thousand blows. And from the anvil, rising straight up into the dark, the pillar of gold light. Every grey thread in the Forbidden Lands falling into it.

*The Heart*.

He felt it. The moment he stepped onto the white floor of the forge. The Root. All of it. Every thread in the world, the whole Lattice, the net Aurelian had forged ring by ring nine hundred years ago, *starting here*. Not tied off, like the knot at the bottom of the Rift. *Starting*. As if every thread in the world had been struck, the first time, on this anvil. And still remembered it.

And at the anvil, a man.

---

Tall. Fair-haired. In a smith's leather apron over a robe of white and gold, with his sleeves rolled up and his forearms scarred. Working a bar of iron. Light and fast and precise. *Tink. Tink. Tink*.

Not singing.

He looked up.

His eyes were gold. Root gold. Bright and warm and loving. And when they found Lex, standing at the edge of the forge with his brother and his friends behind him, they *lit up*. Like a father seeing his son come home.

"*Lex*," said Aurelian Vey. And set down his hammer. "*Oh, my boy. You came.*"

Behind Lex, the little silver bell in Malachi's hand rang.

*Ting*.

And Aurelian Vey's head turned.

Very slightly. Toward the sound. A small movement. A flicker. The way a sleeping man turns his head toward a voice he half recognizes.

And for one heartbeat, just one, the bright warm loving gold of his eyes went *dull*. Tired. Red-rimmed. Sad.

And his lips moved. Without sound. One word.

*Mal*?

And then the gold came back. Bright. Warm. Loving. And the kind face smiled.

"*Malachi*," said the thing wearing Aurelian Vey. "*Little brother. You brought your bell.*" Its gold eyes found the old man at the back of the group. "*How lovely. I always did like that bell.*"

Malachi stood very still on the white floor of the forge. The bell ringing in his hand.

"*That's not you*," he said quietly. "*You never once called it lovely. You said it was the worst thing you ever made. You said the clapper was crooked and it rang flat.*"

The kind face didn't change.

But behind it, very far down, Lex felt something *stir*. At the door at the bottom of his Root. Something that wasn't warm. Something that was tired and sad and couldn't sing.

*Mal*, it said. Very faint. From very far away. *Mal. You kept it.*

And the bell rang louder.
