# The Umbral Rift

The world ended at the Veil.

Not like the White Shore, where the land simply ran out into a frozen sea. Not like the Teeth, where it grew thin and grey. Here, the world *broke*.

Lex saw it from Vharuun's back on the sixth morning out of the Hollowmaw, with the sun coming up behind them and the storm-mother's vast wings beating slow and steady over the last of the eastern hills. The land below was ordinary. Green and brown. Farms, at first, then forest, then moor. Then rock. Then a line.

A long ragged line across the earth, running north and south as far as he could see. Like a crack in a dropped plate. And on the far side of it, the land wasn't land anymore.

It was *pieces*.

Huge slabs of earth and rock, broken off and tilted, hanging at angles in the air. Some of them were the size of villages. Some were the size of mountains. They floated, slowly turning, above a darkness so deep it had no bottom. Between them, the sky was wrong. Cracked. Like a window someone had thrown a stone through. Lines of black ran across the blue, branching, spreading. And through the cracks, very faint, stars. In the middle of the morning.

"*The Sundered Lands*," said Seraphine. She was sitting behind Lex on the storm-mother's back, with her notebook clutched to her chest and her spectacles fogging in the cold. She'd refused to stay behind. She'd refused so firmly that Antho had stopped arguing after the first hour. "*Where the net was tied off. The edge of the Lattice.*" She swallowed. "*The old texts say that when Aurelian wove the net, he pulled it so tight that the world at the edges* tore. *Like cloth stretched over a frame. And the torn bits just—hung there.*"

"*For nine hundred years*," said Ivo.

"*For nine hundred years*."

*[It's like the grey beyond the Teeth*,] said Ember quietly. *[But broken instead of thin. Lex. Look at your panel.]*

He looked.

*Eleven percent*. Less than the grey river. Less than anywhere he'd ever been.

He looked down at his hands. They were steady. The gold glow on them was steady. He felt the Root in him, deep and strong and humming, and Ember in the Root, and under Ember, very far down, the door. A crack of warmth. A faint sound of breathing.

*It's closer here*, he thought. *Isn't it.*

*[Yes*,] said Ember. *[Much closer. The door's at the bottom of the gold, and the gold's at the bottom of the net, and the net ends here.]* A pause. *[We're flying toward the place where it starts, Lex. The very first knot. The root of the Root.]*

---

They saw the Rift at noon.

It was in the middle of the Sundered Lands, where the floating slabs were thickest. A great crack in the world. Not a crack in the earth; the earth here was all cracks. A crack in the *darkness* below. A line of deeper black, running through the bottomless dark under the floating slabs, a mile long and very straight. Like a seam. Like a scar.

And around its edge, on the largest of the floating slabs, a camp.

Grey tents. Hundreds of them. Banners of ash-grey, with a silver key stitched on them. Cookfires. Men. And shadows. Lex could see them from here, even without True Sight: tall dark featureless shapes, standing in ranks around the edge of the slab like the teeth of a comb. Hundreds. Maybe a thousand.

*The Eclipse Concord*, he thought. *What's left of it. Kell's men. Sorn's swords. And Elric's shadows.*

And at the edge of the slab, where it hung over the black seam of the Rift, a stair. Carved into the rock. Going down.

"*He's already gone in*," said Oriel Sant.

Everyone turned. The old woman was sitting on Vharuun's back near the storm-mother's shoulders, wrapped in her red shawl, with her white eyes turned toward the Rift.

"*Yesterday*," she said. "*Down the stair. With the Key. Into the dark.*" Her thin dry voice didn't change. "*He's going down to the Lock. It's a long way. Very long. He won't reach it until the longest night.*"

"*Midwinter*," said Seraphine slowly. "*Midwinter's in—*"

"*Sixteen days*," said Oriel. "*He'll reach the Lock on the longest night. And turn the Key at midnight.*"

"*Then we go down after him*," said Lex. "*Now. Today.*"

"*Through that*?" Wren was standing in the air beside the storm-mother's head, her bow in her hand, looking at the camp. At the ranks of shadows. "*Hammer, there's a thousand of them.*"

"*Then we go through a thousand of them*."

Behind him, Dorran Vask laughed. A low slow rumble.

"*He's in a hurry again*," said the giant.

---

They didn't go through a thousand of them.

They went *over*.

It was Antho's idea. And the twins'. And Vharuun's, in the end, though she didn't say so in words. They came down out of the cracked sky in the late afternoon, out of the west, with the sun behind them, and the storm-mother *breathed*.

Thunder. A great rolling wall of it, pouring down out of her jaws onto the camp of the Eclipse Concord. Not lightning. *Sound*. The pressure-wave hit the ranks of shadows like a hammer hitting glass and scattered them, tore them apart, flung them out across the slab like smoke in a gale. Tents went flying. Men went flying. The grey banners with their silver keys tore loose and spun away into the bottomless dark.

And Ashka came down behind her mother like a spear of lightning, white from nose to tail, and lit the camp up like the noon sun.

And in the confusion, in the thunder and lightning and screaming, nobody noticed a dozen people climbing down off a storm-dragon's back at the top of the stair.

Because there was nobody there. Just rock. Just shadow. Just an empty patch of ground at the edge of the slab, where the stair went down.

Ilse and Ivo stood together at the head of the stair with their hands joined and their eyes closed and sweat running down their faces. Weaving. The Lampshade, the old trick from the Wickhollow, stretched as wide as it would go. Not over one thread. Over twelve. Over everyone.

"*Go*," said Ilse, through her teeth. She was faint. Very faint. The little stone pebble the Wellworld had given her was clutched against her heart. "*Go, go, go, we can't hold it long*—"

They went.

Down the stair. Into the dark. Lex at the front with fire in his hands. Wren behind him with her bow. Antho with the Lantern. Kestrel beside Antho, where he could see her. Tamsin behind his sister. Seraphine with her crossbow. Oriel, riding on Dorran's shoulders like a child on its father's, her thorn stick tapping the rock wall as they went. Dorran with the Lockward in a leather case on his back. He'd carried it from the Hollowmaw. He'd insisted. *I'm good at carrying things*.

And last, the twins. Backing down the stair, still holding hands, still weaving, until the stair turned and the camp was out of sight.

Above them, the thunder rolled on. The storm-mother and her daughter, holding the rim.

Holding the door.

---

The Rift went down a long way.

Lex had thought the Sunwell was deep. He'd thought the Wellworld of Stone was deep. They were nothing. The stair went down and down and down, carved into the black rock of the Rift's wall, spiralling. Narrow. No rail. On one side, the wall. On the other, nothing. Darkness. Going down forever.

They walked for a day. Then another. They slept on the stair, in turns, roped together, with Antho's Lantern hanging from a spike in the wall. They ate what they'd carried. They didn't talk much.

And as they went down, the Lattice thinned.

Lex felt it. Every hour. The silver threads of the net, which had always been there, humming at the edge of his mind, getting fewer. Fainter. Farther apart. His panels flickered. Ember's voice came and went, like a voice heard through a storm.

*[Seven percent]*, it said, on the second day. *[Lex. Seven.]*

And on the third:

*[Four]*.

And something else was getting *stronger*.

Not the net. Not the Root. Something underneath both of them. A warmth. Gold. Coming up from below, the way heat comes up through the floor of a forge. And with it, very faint, a sound. Like breathing.

*The door*, he thought. *The door at the bottom of the gold. We're walking down toward it.*

*[No,]* said Ember. Very quietly. Barely there. *[We're walking down toward the thing on the other side of it.]*

---

On the fourth day, they found the first of the dead.

Men in ash-grey. Five of them. Lying on the stair in a heap, as if they'd fallen together. Their armor was crushed. Their swords were broken.

And they were *grey*. All the way through. Like the thinned in the valley beyond the Teeth. Like the man with the rubbed-out face. Their faces were blurred. Their bodies were faint. Lex could see the stair through their chests.

"*Unwritten*," said Seraphine. Very quietly. "*He's unwriting his own men.*"

"*He's feeding*," said Antho. He was kneeling beside the bodies with the Lantern held low, his face grey. "*On the way down. The deeper he goes, the more the Key needs. He's taking whatever's nearest.*"

"*His own people*," said Kestrel. Her voice was strange.

"*His own people*."

Lex took out the Ledger. He opened it to the last page.

Five new names. In the narrow elegant hand. Very faint.

*Garret Shale. Soldier. He sent half his pay home to his mother in Thornby.*

*Wil Brack. Soldier. He was afraid of the dark.*

He read them all. Then he closed the book and put it back against his chest.

"*Sixty*," he said.

---

On the sixth day, they found Kell.

She was sitting on the stair. At a place where it widened into a ledge, a flat shelf of black rock jutting out over the dark. She was sitting with her back against the wall and her knees drawn up and her ragged silver hair hanging in her face. Alone.

She looked up when the light of Antho's Lantern came round the bend.

Lex barely recognized her. She'd been gaunt at the masquerade. She was skeletal now. Her white robes were torn and filthy. Her face was hollow, her eyes sunk deep in dark circles. Her hands, resting on her knees, were shaking.

And she was *faint*.

Not as faint as the dead men. Not yet. But the edges of her were blurring. The white of her robes was going grey. When Lex looked at her with True Sight, the panel over her head flickered and stuttered like a candle in a draft.

"*I don't know what to do with that*," said Isaura Kell. "*I don't know what to do.*"

"Neither do I," said Lex.

He turned away. He looked down. Into the dark. Into the bottom of the Umbral Rift, where something gold was glowing, very faint, very far below. Where something was breathing.

*Eight days*, he thought. *Eight days to the longest night.*

*[Lex,]* said Ember. So faint now he could barely hear it. Two percent. One. *[I'm losing you. The net's too thin. I'm—]*

*I know. It's all right.*

*[I'll be here. At the bottom of the gold. I'll be right here.]* A pause. Fainter. *[Lex. He was a good man. The big one.]*

*I know.*

*[He hit things too hard.]*

*I know.*

*[So do you.]*

And then Ember was gone. Not swallowed, this time. Not taken. Just out of reach. Like a voice on the far side of a river.

Lex was alone in his own head for the first time in four years.

He slung the Lockward higher on his back. He took the Lantern from Wren. He looked at all of them, on the ledge. His brother. Wren. Seraphine. Kestrel and Tamsin. The twins. Oriel. Kell.

"*Down*," he said.

And they went down into the dark, toward the Lock of the World.
