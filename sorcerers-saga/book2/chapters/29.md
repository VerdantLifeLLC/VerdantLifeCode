# The Cantor

Highcrest was singing.

Antho heard it from forty miles out. From the storm-mother's back, over the river valley, with the sun going down behind them and the golden city climbing its mountain ahead. Not with his ears. Not yet. With Shade. With the deep cold hearing that listened to silences.

There was no silence in Highcrest.

That was what he heard. A city of a hundred thousand souls, and not one quiet place in it. Every street. Every terrace. Every room. Full of sound. The same sound. One sound. Rising up off the mountain like heat off a forge.

"*Can you hear it*?" said Seraphine, behind him. Her voice was strained.

"*Yes*."

"*I can't*." She swallowed. "*Not with my ears. But I can feel it. In my teeth.*"

In front of him, Pim sat very still on the storm-mother's neck. His thin shoulders hunched. His hands over his ears. He'd put them there an hour ago, when they first crossed the river, and he hadn't taken them down.

Antho put his hand on the boy's back.

"*Too loud*?" he said.

Pim nodded. Once.

"*I know*," said Antho. "*It's too loud for me too.*"

---

They came down on the castle wall at dusk.

The castle was empty. The Queen's guard was gone. Elowen's knights were gone. The servants, the cooks, the grooms. Gone. Down into the city. Down to the square.

The Queen walked along the battlements of her own castle in the twilight with her face like stone. She didn't say anything. She stopped at the place where the wall looked down over the city, and stood there with her hands on the cold parapet, and looked.

Antho came and stood beside her.

---

The square at the foot of the Spire was the biggest open space in Highcrest. He'd seen it at the festival, three years ago and then again in the summer: lanterns everywhere, stalls, dancing, the Queen's speech.

It was full of people.

All of them. More than at any festival. Packed shoulder to shoulder from wall to wall, filling the square, spilling up the streets that led into it, up the stairs, onto the rooftops. Thousands. Tens of thousands. In nightclothes, mostly. Barefoot. With their faces turned up.

And all of them singing.

He could hear it now, with his ears. Even from the castle wall. A great soft swelling sound, rising up out of the square like a tide. Not words. Not a tune he knew. Just *singing*. Tens of thousands of voices, perfectly together, perfectly in tune, the way no crowd has ever sung, in all of history.

It was the most beautiful thing he had ever heard.

And in the middle of the square, on top of the Great Stone of Highcrest, a man.

---

He was tall. Very tall. Thin, like a heron. In a long dark robe that hung to the Stone and spilled over its edges like water. His arms were raised. His hands were long and pale and moving, slowly, gracefully, the way a choir-master's hands move. Conducting. Shaping the sound. Lifting it, softening it, sending it swelling out across the square and back.

His head was thrown back. His eyes were closed.

And his mouth was open. Wide. Too wide. Singing.

Antho couldn't hear his voice. Not separately. It was *in* the sound. Under it. The thread that all the other voices hung from. The note they were all tuned to.

"*Brother Aldous*," said Seraphine quietly. Beside him. With her notebook clutched to her chest. "*Of Hollin's Rest.*"

The Queen made a sound. Low. Furious.

"*That's my bakers*," she said. "*Down there. My grooms. My guards. My council*." Her hands were white on the parapet. "*That's the Duke of Harlowe. In his nightshirt. On the steps of the Spire. Singing.*"

"*He can't hear you*," said Antho. "*None of them can.*"

"*Then I'll make them hear me*."

---

She tried.

From the castle wall. Standing at the parapet with her arms spread. And Antho felt it come off her: the Sovereign's command. Gold-silver. A great bright wave, rolling out over the city the way it had rolled out over the ballroom at the masquerade. *Stand with me. Hear me. I am your Queen.*

It hit the singing like a wave hitting a sea-wall.

And broke.

Antho felt it break. Felt the Queen's command go out over the square and *vanish* into the song. Swallowed. The way the Hollow had swallowed Kestrel's fire at the Rift. Not resisted. Absorbed. Made part of it.

And the song swelled. A little. As if someone had added a new voice to the choir.

The Queen staggered back from the parapet. Elowen caught her.

"*It took it*," said Lysandra. Her face was white. "*It took my command. It* sang *it.*"

---

Antho lit the Lantern.

He did it on the castle wall, in the falling dark, with the song pouring up from the square below. The shutter clicked. The gold light spilled out. Twelve Hush-runes, blazing white.

The song went quiet. Inside the circle. Around him. Around Pim, and Seraphine, and the Queen, and Elowen, and Roole. A small circle of silence on a castle wall, in a city full of singing.

Pim took his hands off his ears.

He looked up at Antho. His thin grave face very pale.

"*It's beautiful*," said Pim.

Antho looked at him.

"*The singing*," said Pim. "*It's beautiful. That's why they go.*" He was quiet. "*Nobody ever sang to them like that before.*"

It was the longest thing the boy had ever said.

---

"*Hollin's Rest*," said Seraphine.

She'd sat down on the battlements, inside the circle of gold light, with her notebook open on her knees. She was turning pages fast. Her spectacles were sliding down her nose.

"*The records*," she said. "*Everything I could find. There's not much. A tax roll. A letter from the Warden of the north to the Spire. A ballad, a bad one.*" She stopped on a page. "*And this.*"

She held it up. A copy, in her own neat cramped hand, of something older.

"*The letter from the Warden*," she said. "*Written the spring after. When they went back to Hollin's Rest and found it empty.*" She read it aloud. "*The village is deserted. The Stone is black. Every house is empty. The church door stands open. We have found no one.*" She looked up. "*And then, at the bottom. Almost as an afterthought.*"

She read:

"*Save one child. A girl of seven, found in the loft of the miller's house, alone, half-starved. She did not go with the others into the snow. When asked why, she could not answer, for she has been deaf from birth.*"

---

The circle of gold light was very quiet.

"*Deaf*," said Antho.

"*She couldn't hear him*." Seraphine's voice was shaking. "*Every soul in Hollin's Rest followed him into the hills, singing. Every one. Except the one who couldn't hear the song.*"

"*Children are hard to sing to*," said Antho slowly. "*That's what the herald said. At Harrowgate. They don't listen properly. They're always thinking about something else.*"

"*He can't sing to someone who isn't listening*," said Seraphine.

They both looked at Pim.

The boy was standing at the parapet. Inside the circle of light. With his small hands on the cold stone and his dark careful eyes looking down at the square. At the tens of thousands of people, singing. At the tall thin man on top of the Stone, conducting.

He wasn't covering his ears anymore.

He was *listening*.

Not to the song. Antho could see it. Feel it. With Shade. The boy was listening *past* the song. Under it. Through it. To the silence. To the shape of the quiet underneath all that sound. The way he'd listened to the dark his whole life, in a cottage in Kessick with a man who took off his belt, because the dark was the only thing that had ever been kind to him.

There was silence under the song. Antho felt it. Very small. Very deep. Like the space between heartbeats.

And Pim had found it.

---

"*No*," said the Queen.

She'd understood. Antho saw it on her face. She was looking at the boy at the parapet. Twelve years old. Thin. Silent.

"*No*," said Lysandra again. "*You're not sending a child down there. Into that.*"

"*I'm not sending him*," said Antho. "*I'm taking him.*"

"*He's twelve*."

"*I know how old he is*." Antho didn't look away from Pim. "*I know exactly how old he is. I was fifteen when the voices started. On a woodpile. And the only thing that kept me from walking into the trees was a ten-year-old boy holding my sleeve.*"

The Queen stared at him.

"*Pim*," said Antho.

The boy turned his head.

"*The man on the Stone*," said Antho. "*He sings, and people follow. Because they listen. Because it's beautiful and nobody ever sang to them like that before.*" He crouched down. So they were eye to eye. "*But you don't listen to the singing. Do you. You listen to the quiet underneath.*"

Pim looked at him for a long moment.

Then nodded.

"*Can you make the quiet bigger*?" said Antho. "*Like you did at the door. On the night of the Blade. When you held my wrist and hid me from the Key.*"

Pim thought about it. Seriously. The way he thought about everything.

"*I don't know*," he said. "*It's very loud.*"

"*I know*."

"*I'd have to be close*," said Pim. "*To the Stone. To him.*" He looked down at the square. At the tens of thousands of people. "*They're in the way.*"

"*Then we'll get you through them*," said Antho. "*All of us. We'll get you to the Stone.*" He held out his hand. "*And then you just listen. That's all. Listen to the quiet. As hard as you can.*"

Pim looked at the hand. Then at Antho's face.

Then he put his small cold hand in Antho's.

"*Don't let go*," he said.

"*I won't*."

---

They went down into the square at midnight.

Six of them. Antho, with Pim's hand in his and the Lantern held high in the other, its shutter wide, its twelve runes blazing. Seraphine at his shoulder, with her crossbow, which she knew she couldn't use. Roole, with his sword sheathed. Elowen, with Oathkeeper on her back. And the Queen of Caldor, in her plain grey cloak, with her face set.

The circle of silence went with them. Gold. Small. Around them like a bubble. And outside it, the song.

It hit them like a wall the moment they stepped off the last stair into the square. Even inside the Lantern's light. Antho felt it pressing against the edges of the gold, the way the grey had pressed in the valley beyond the Teeth. Thirsty. Patient. Beautiful.

And the people.

Packed shoulder to shoulder. Tens of thousands. Facing the Stone. Faces turned up. Eyes closed. Singing. They didn't move aside. They didn't see the little band of six with the gold light. They just stood there, swaying, singing, a wall of bodies between them and the Stone.

"*We can't push through*," said Roole. Low. "*Not without hurting them. There's too many. They'll crush each other.*"

"*Then don't push*," said the Queen.

---

She stepped to the front.

Outside the circle. Out of the gold light. Into the song.

Antho saw it hit her. Saw her face go slack. Saw her eyes start to close. Saw her lips part, the way every lip in the square was parted.

And then saw her set her jaw.

"*I was Kindled a Sovereign*," said Lysandra of Caldor. Through her teeth. To no one. To herself. "*At sixteen. At the Great Stone. With a flag on it that weighted me down so I'd never be strong enough to rule on my own.*" Her voice was shaking. "*And a smith from Ashbourne took the flag off. And I have been my own Queen for three years.*"

She lifted her head.

"*I will not sing your song*," said the Queen.

And she didn't command. She'd learned that, on the castle wall. The song swallowed commands. Took them. Sang them.

She did something else.

She *spoke*. Quietly. Not to the crowd. To the man in front of her. A baker, in his nightshirt, with flour still on his arms. Singing with his eyes closed.

"*Hollis*," said the Queen. "*Hollis Penn. You make the burnt loaves for the children by the fountain. You sing in the Lower Town choir. Badly. Your wife's name is Ada.*" Her voice was very gentle. "*Hollis. It's Lysandra. Step aside, please. Just a little. Just for me.*"

The baker's singing faltered.

His eyes flickered. Didn't open. But flickered.

And he stepped aside. A half-step. Swaying. Still humming. But aside.

The Queen walked into the gap.

---

She did it all the way across the square.

Antho would never forget it. A young woman in a grey cloak, walking through a crowd of tens of thousands of her own people, outside the circle of gold light, in the full force of the song. Not commanding. Not shouting. *Naming them*. One by one. Every face she passed.

"*Mistress Orrin. You keep the bookshop on the Crown Terrace. You lent me the Founders' Chronicles when I was twelve and never asked for them back. Step aside, please.*"

"*Ser Hadley. You taught me to ride. You put me on a pony called Biscuit and told me if I fell off you'd tell my grandfather I'd jumped. Step aside.*"

"*Little Wenna. You're six. You gave me a daisy at the festival. Step aside, love.*"

And they did.

Half a step. A step. Swaying. Still singing. Eyes still closed. But aside. Opening a path. A narrow winding path through the singing crowd, from the foot of the stair to the Great Stone, a person wide. And the Queen of Caldor walking down it, with tears running down her face, naming every single soul she passed.

"*How does she know them*?" whispered Seraphine. "*All of them. How does she know*—"

"*She's their Queen*," said Elowen Dunmore. Very quietly. Her square face was wet. "*She's spent three years learning their names.*"

---

They reached the Stone.

The Great Stone of Highcrest. Taller than a man. Silver once. Black now. A hole cut out of the night. And on top of it, the Cantor.

Close up, he was bigger. Taller. Thinner. His dark robe pooled over the edges of the black Stone like ink. His long pale hands moved above the crowd, slowly, endlessly. His head thrown back. His eyes closed. His wide mouth open.

And the song was *enormous*. Even inside the gold light. Antho felt it batter at the circle. Felt the Lantern shake in his hand. Felt the twelve Hush-runes straining.

And then the Cantor opened his eyes.

They were silver. Pale silver. Like the Lattice. Like the threads of the net.

And they looked down at the little circle of gold light at the foot of his Stone. At Antho. At the Lantern. At the boy holding his hand.

The song changed.

---

It came straight at Antho. Through the gold. Through the Hush-runes. As if they weren't there.

And it wasn't the song anymore.

It was a woman's voice. Low and a little rough. Flat on the high note.

*If the dark comes calling, love, don't you mind*.

His mother's voice.

*A lantern's lit for the ones behind*.

He stood at the foot of the black Stone with the Lantern in his hand and felt his knees start to go. The way they'd gone on the woodpile at fifteen. The way they'd gone every night for eleven years when the Murmur sang to him in the trees.

*Come home, love*, sang his mother's voice. *Come home. I'm right here. I've been waiting so long.*

The Lantern was shaking.

And from somewhere very deep inside him, from the last whisper of the four percent, the one percent, the place where the Murmur used to live, a voice said:

*[...oh, that's a beautiful voice*.]

He closed his eyes.

*It is*, he thought. *It's the most beautiful voice I've ever heard.*

*[...you could go. You know. It wouldn't hurt*.]

*I know*.

*[...then why don't you?*]

He opened his eyes. And looked up at the Cantor, on top of the black Stone, singing to him in his mother's voice.

"*Because you got the tune wrong*," said Antho Vey.

---

The song stuttered.

Just for a moment. A missed note. A crack in the sound. The Cantor's silver eyes widened.

"*She's flat*," said Antho. "*On the high note. Always. Every time. It used to drive Father mad.*" His voice was very steady. "*You sing it perfectly. You sing everything perfectly.*" He held the Lantern higher. "*You've never once heard her sing. You've only heard the song.*"

The Cantor's mouth closed.

For the first time in three hundred years, Brother Aldous of Hollin's Rest stopped singing.

And in the silence, in the tiny crack of quiet between one note and the next, Pim let go of Antho's hand.

---

He walked to the Stone.

Antho didn't stop him. He'd promised not to let go. But Pim had let go first. And Antho understood, watching the boy walk out of the circle of gold light into the full roaring force of the song, that it wasn't his to hold anymore.

Pim put his small hand on the black Stone.

And *listened*.

---

The silence came out of him like a breath.

Antho felt it. With Shade. With every part of him. A silence. Not the absence of sound. Something *deeper*. The silence under everything. The quiet at the bottom of the world, before the world, before the net, before the hunger. The space between heartbeats. The pause at the top of a breath. The stillness in a room where someone has just stopped crying.

It went out from the boy's hand through the black Stone.

And down.

Down the tithe-thread. The great wide pipe the Cantor had been holding open for twenty days. Down and down, through the dark, to the bottom of the world, where something gold and kind and hungry had been singing through a choir-master for three hundred years.

And *out*. Through the Stone. Into the square. Into every thread.

And the square went quiet.

---

Tens of thousands of voices. Stopping. Not all at once. In a wave. Like wind dying across a field of wheat. From the Stone outward. Rippling. Every mouth closing. Every voice going still. The great soft swelling sound that had risen off Highcrest for twenty days, dying away, dying away, until the only sound in the whole of the square was the wind off the mountain and somebody, somewhere, very quietly, beginning to cry.

And on top of the black Stone, the Cantor stood with his long pale arms still raised, and his mouth closed, and his silver eyes wide.

Listening.

For the first time in three hundred years.

---

Antho saw it happen. Saw it go through the tall thin man on the Stone. The silence. Going into him the way the song had gone into everyone else.

And in the silence, Brother Aldous heard something.

Antho didn't know what. He would never know exactly. But he saw the choir-master's face change. Saw the wide mouth tremble. Saw the silver eyes fill.

*Snow*, Antho thought. *He's hearing the snow.*

*A village in the hills on a midwinter night. Everyone following him. Singing. Out of the church, out of the village, up into the hills. In the snow. In the dark. Singing.*

*And then the singing stopping. One voice at a time. As the cold took them.*

*And him, still singing. Alone. On a hillside. In the snow. Because he didn't know how to stop.*

"*I only wanted them to hear it*," said the Cantor.

His voice was small. Cracked. An old man's voice. Not a choir. One voice. Speaking, not singing, for the first time in three hundred years.

"*I only wanted them to hear the music*," he said. "*It was so beautiful. I wanted everyone to hear how beautiful it was.*" His long pale hands came down. Slowly. "*And they followed me. And I couldn't stop. If I stopped, it would end. And I couldn't bear for it to end.*"

He looked down at the boy with his hand on the Stone.

"*Is it quiet*?" said Brother Aldous. "*Is it really quiet?*"

Pim looked up at him.

"*Yes*," said Pim. "*You can stop now.*"

---

The Cantor knelt down on top of the Stone.

Slowly. The way an old man kneels in a church. His dark robe pooling around him. His long pale hands folding in his lap. His silver eyes still wet.

"*It's been so long*," he said. "*Since I heard nothing.*"

"*I know*," said Pim.

"*It's lovely*." The Cantor closed his eyes. "*I'd forgotten it was lovely.*"

And then, very softly, so softly that only the people closest to the Stone heard it, he hummed. Just a little. Not the song. Something else. A simple tune. Three notes. Rising.

The kind of tune a choir-master hums to give the note before a hymn begins.

And then he stopped. And smiled. And was quiet.

And the black Stone under him went grey.

And then silver.

And Brother Aldous of Hollin's Rest went with it. Not down. Not into the dark. *Out*. Like a candle going out in a quiet room. The tall thin shape on top of the Stone, fading. Grey, then pale, then nothing. Gone. Leaving only the Great Stone of Highcrest, silver and plain and ordinary, with a twelve-year-old boy's small hand flat against its side.

---

Pim took his hand off the Stone.

He turned around. He looked at Antho. Standing in the circle of gold light with the Lantern in his hand.

And then the boy's knees gave out, and he sat down very suddenly on the cobbles of the square, and started to shake.

Antho was there before he hit the ground. He knelt. He put his arms around the boy. Small and thin and cold, shaking like a leaf.

"*It was so loud*," said Pim. Into his coat. "*It was so loud and I had to find the quiet and it was so small.*"

"*I know*."

"*And then he heard it*." Pim was crying. Antho had never seen him cry. Not once. Not in all his months at the Keep. "*He heard it and he was so sad.*"

"*I know*."

"*Is he gone*?"

"*Yes*."

"*Is he all right*?"

Antho held the boy and looked at the silver Stone.

"*I think so*," he said. "*I think he's somewhere quiet.*"

---

All around them, the square was waking.

Tens of thousands of people, blinking. Looking around. In their nightclothes. Barefoot. On the cobbles of the square at the foot of the Spire, in the middle of the night. Staring at each other. At their own hands. At the silver Stone.

Someone started to laugh. Shakily. Someone else started to cry. A child was calling for her mother. A dog was barking, somewhere in the Lower Town, as if it had been holding its breath for twenty days.

And a man in a nightshirt with flour on his arms was standing a few paces from the Stone, holding a loaf of bread he didn't remember picking up, and looking around in total bewilderment.

"*Your Majesty*?" said Hollis Penn. Staring at the Queen. "*What are you doing in the square in the middle of the night? In your* cloak?*"

Lysandra of Caldor looked at him.

And laughed. A great helpless startled laugh, with tears running down her face. And put her arms around the baker, flour and bread and all, and held on.

"*Oh*," said Hollis Penn, very faintly, into the Queen's shoulder. "*Oh. All right then.*"

---

Antho sat on the cobbles at the foot of the Great Stone with Pim in his arms and the Lantern beside him, and didn't move for a long time.

Seraphine came and sat beside him. She didn't say anything. She leaned against his shoulder.

And somewhere very deep inside him, in the last place where the Murmur had lived, something stirred. Very faint. Like a dry leaf moving in the wind.

*[...it was a beautiful voice*,] it said. *[...wasn't it.*]

*It was*, thought Antho.

*[...you didn't go*.]

*No*.

A long silence. And then, so faint he almost didn't hear it:

*[...good*.]

And it was gone.

Not quiet. Not asleep. *Gone*. Like a breath let out. Like a door closed for the last time on an empty room. He felt the place where it had been, for eleven years, in the space between his heartbeats. Empty. Clean. *His*.

He read it. He read it twice.

Then he put his face in Seraphine's hair, with the boy still in his arms, on the cobbles of the square in the city that had stopped singing, and wept.

---

A raven came at dawn.

Not Quill. A Keeper's raven. Grey and sleek. It found them on the castle wall, where they'd gone to watch the sun come up over a silent city, and dropped a scrap of paper at Roole's feet.

Roole read it. His hatchet face went grey.

He handed it to Antho.

*Commander—*

*Millbridge is gone.*

*Not burned. Not fallen. Gone soft. The Stone went black at midnight three nights ago and a woman came up out of it in a midwife's apron and everything she touched began to rot. Houses. Fields. Wells. People.*

*It's spreading. Through the Vale. Toward Ashbourne.*

*Teodra Hale's company is holding the road at the Millbridge ford. The Forgeheart arrived last night with the fire-witch and the old man. They're going in at dawn.*

*Teodra says to tell you: if you've got a dragon to spare, send it. Now.*

*—Fenn*

Antho looked south. Toward the Vale. Toward a forge with twenty anvils, and a village where his brother had grown up, and an old bald smith with a broken arm who'd said *lad, go*.

"*Vharuun*," he said.

On the castle wall behind him, the storm-mother lifted her vast head.

*I HEARD*, she said.
