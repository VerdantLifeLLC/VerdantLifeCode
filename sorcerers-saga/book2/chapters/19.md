# The Wellworld of Gale

Wren Ashdown had never been afraid of heights.

She'd been told so, often. By her mother, who used to find her at the top of the tallest pine in the forest at five years old, singing. By Garrick, who'd watched her walk along the parapet of Fort Caldris with her arms out like a tightrope dancer and said, *Ashdown, if you fall off that wall I will personally kill you*. By Lex, who'd held onto her on the back of a dragon over the Greyspine at night, and felt her laughing, and said, *How are you not terrified?*

She didn't know. She'd just never been. The ground was a place you started from. The sky was where you were going.

So when they came up through the high passes of the Teeth on the twenty-sixth morning before the end, on dragons, into the place where the storms were born, she wasn't afraid.

She was *home*.

---

The storms were born at the top of the world.

That's what it looked like. The highest peak of the Teeth, a black spike of rock so tall it punched through the clouds, and around it, the sky was alive. Lightning walked along the ridges on long white legs. Clouds boiled up out of nothing, grey and black and purple, churning, and tore themselves apart, and boiled up again. The wind howled. Not one wind. A hundred winds. Coming from every direction at once, crashing together over the peak, spiralling up into the sky in great invisible towers.

And at the very center of it, directly above the black spike, a hole.

A hole in the storm. A perfect circle of calm, clear, deep blue sky, ringed by churning cloud. Like the eye of a hurricane. Like the pupil of an enormous eye, looking down.

"*There*," said Wren.

She was standing in the air beside Ashka, with the wind tearing at her coat and her braid whipping round her face. She hadn't needed to step for an hour. The wind here *held* her. Like hands under her feet. Like the whole sky was a floor.

"*That's the door*," she said. "*Can you feel it, Hammer? Can you hear it?*"

Lex was clinging to Ashka's neck with both hands. His face was green. He looked at the eye in the storm. Then at her.

"I can hear the wind," he said. "That's all. Very loud wind."

"*It's singing*," said Wren.

---

It was. She'd never heard anything like it.

At Duskhold, when she'd reached Speaking, Gale had spoken to her for the first time. A voice in the wind. Light and quick and laughing, like a girl running through long grass. She'd heard it every day since. Every time she stepped into the air. A little whisper at her ear: *up, up, up, lightfoot. Higher. Faster. Come on.*

This was that voice. But a thousand times bigger. A thousand voices. Singing. All around the peak. In the churning clouds and the walking lightning and the crashing winds. A great wild wordless chorus, rising and falling, like every wind that had ever blown in the world singing at once.

And under it, very faint, from the eye of the storm, one voice. Older than the others. Deeper. Calling.

*Come up*.

Wren stood in the air and listened, and felt tears freeze on her cheeks in the high cold wind.

"*Wren*," said Antho.

He was behind Lex on Ashka's back. Holding on with one hand. His other hand was stretched out toward the vast ragged shape of Vharuun, flying beside them. Pushing wind under her torn wings. Holding her up, the way he'd held her up all the way from the Drowned Country. His face was grey with the effort.

But the storm-mother wasn't struggling now. That was the strange thing. As they'd come up into the storms, Vharuun had flown easier. Stronger. Her ragged wings had caught the winds and *held* them. And now she was gliding beside them in great slow arcs, her head lifted toward the eye in the sky, her one good gold eye wide.

"*Wren*," said Antho again. "*Look at her.*"

Wren looked.

And saw that Vharuun was crying.

Great slow tears. Rolling out of her gold eye. Freezing in the wind. Falling away down into the clouds like hailstones.

*HOME*, said the storm-mother. Wren didn't hear it. She felt it. Through the wind. A great slow rumble of thunder that came through the sky and into her chest. *THIS IS WHERE I WAS BORN. A THOUSAND YEARS AGO. IN THE EYE OF THE STORM.*

*I THOUGHT I WOULD NEVER SEE IT AGAIN.*

---

They went up.

Not on the dragons. The dragons couldn't take them through the eye; Wren knew that somehow, the way she knew which way the wind was going to turn. The eye was a door, and doors were for people. Dragons could follow. But people had to go first.

"*Four*," she said. "*Oriel said four.*"

"*Me*," said Antho. "*I hear Gale. Answering.*" He looked at Vharuun. "*And somebody has to keep her up.*"

"*And Lex*." Wren looked at him. Still clinging to Ashka's neck. Still green. "*He's carrying the coal and the water. And the iron's with Dorran on the ridge below.*" She hesitated. "*And because it's his bar. He has to see where the breath comes from.*"

"Who's the fourth?" said Lex.

Wren looked down. At the ridge far below them, where the others were waiting. Dorran with the Heartiron. Seraphine with her notebook. Teo. Oriel. The twins. And Kestrel, sitting on a rock a little apart, with her brother beside her.

"*Nobody*," said Wren. "*Three's enough.*"

Lex looked at her.

"*Gale doesn't like crowds*," said Wren. "*I can hear it. It's a door for one, really. Two at a push. Three if they hold hands.*" She held out her hand. "*Come on, Hammer.*"

He looked down at the drop. Four thousand feet of empty air, straight down into the boiling clouds.

"*You're going to tell me to jump*," he said.

"*I'm going to tell you to step*," said Wren. "*Off the dragon. Into the air. Holding my hand.*" She grinned at him. "*And I'm not going to drop you. I promise. Properly.*"

He looked at her for a long moment.

Then he let go of Ashka's neck.

And took her hand.

And stepped.

---

She didn't drop him.

She'd never carried anyone before. Not really. Windstep was for one. It held her up, light as a leaf, but she'd always known it wouldn't hold anyone else. She'd tried, once, with Seraphine, at Fort Caldris, and they'd both nearly broken their necks.

But this wasn't Windstep. This was the sky at the top of the world. And the sky *wanted* them.

She felt it. The moment Lex stepped off the dragon's back, the moment his weight came onto her hand. The winds came. All of them. From every direction. Crashing together under him, under her, under Antho stepping off behind, and *lifting*. Like a hand. Like the whole storm reaching down and cupping them in its palm.

Lex made a sound. Not a scream. Something between a gasp and a laugh.

"*Wren*," he said. "*Wren, I'm flying. I'm—*"

"*I know*," said Wren. She was laughing too. "*I know, Hammer. Isn't it wonderful?*"

And they went up into the eye of the storm.

---

The Wellworld of Gale had no ground.

That was the first thing. Wren had half expected it, the way she'd half expected everything about this place. But it still took her breath away.

There was sky. Only sky. Endless. In every direction. Above, below, around. Blue and gold and white and silver, going on forever. And in it, *clouds*. Huge ones. Islands of cloud, floating in the endless sky, white and grey and pink and gold, with mountains on them and valleys and rivers of mist pouring off their edges into nothing. Some of them were small, the size of a house. Some were bigger than Caldor. They drifted slowly through the sky, turning, colliding, merging, tearing apart.

And between them, the winds.

She could *see* them. Here, the winds were visible. Long rippling streams of silver light, flowing between the clouds like rivers in the air. Some were gentle, slow, broad as lakes. Some were fierce, narrow, shrieking, twisting around each other like ropes. They crossed and wove and tangled, a great endless shining web of moving air, filling the sky.

And in the winds, *things*. Living. Shapes of air and light. Birds, but not birds. Long and slender, trailing streamers of silver, diving and rising through the winds faster than anything Wren had ever seen. Fish, but not fish. Swimming through the sky in great shimmering shoals. And far off, very far, something enormous. A shape like a whale, made of storm-cloud and lightning, drifting slowly through the endless blue.

"*Oh*," said Antho, behind her. His voice was very small. "*Oh. It's free.*"

Wren looked back at him.

He was standing on the wind. On his own. Not holding her hand anymore. His grey eyes were wide and wet. His black hair was blowing wild. And on his face was an expression she'd never seen there in four years.

Joy. Plain, uncomplicated, open joy. Like a child's.

"*There's no net here*," said Antho. "*Can you feel it? No net. No Lattice. No Ceiling. No door. Nothing holding anything down.*" He laughed. A short wild startled sound. "*It's the only place I've ever been where nothing's holding anything down.*"

---

They flew.

There was nothing else to do. There was no ground to walk on. You either stood on the wind or you fell. And the wind didn't want them to fall. It wanted them to *fly*. Wren felt it pulling at her, tugging, laughing. *Come on, lightfoot, come on, come on*.

So she went. With Lex's hand in hers. With Antho flying beside them on his own, clumsy, laughing, his long coat flapping like a crow's wings. Down a long silver river of wind between two cloud-islands, so fast the air screamed past her ears. Up through a waterfall of mist pouring off the edge of a cloud. Around and around a column of rising air, spiralling up, up, up, until the clouds below were tiny and the blue above went on forever.

Lex stopped being green. Somewhere in the first hour. He stopped clutching her hand like a drowning man and started just holding it. And then, near the top of the spiral, he started laughing too.

"*This is mad*," he shouted over the wind. "*This is completely mad. I'm a smith. I'm a smith from Ashbourne. I'm supposed to be on the ground. Next to an anvil.*"

"*Do you want to go back?*"

"*No*!"

Wren laughed so hard she nearly lost the wind.

---

They found the heart in the afternoon. If there was afternoon here.

It wasn't a place. It was a *wind*. The oldest one. Wren felt it before she saw it: the deep slow voice she'd heard from the eye of the storm, calling. *Come up*. Getting louder. Closer. Until they came around the edge of a cloud-island the size of a mountain range, and there it was.

A wind. A single wind. Wider than a river. Wider than a lake. Flowing through the endless sky in a great slow circle, around and around, so wide that Wren couldn't see the other side. Silver and gold and white, shining, like a ring of light. And in the middle of the ring, empty sky. Perfectly still. Perfectly calm. Perfectly blue.

The First Breath.

She knew its name the way she knew the wind's direction. Not because anyone told her. Because it was *true*.

*Lightfoot*, said the First Breath.

Its voice was everywhere. In every wind. In every cloud. In her own lungs, going in and out. Light and deep and very, very old. Like the first breath of a newborn, and the last breath of a dying man, and every breath in between.

*You came. You heard me calling. Through the eye.*

"*Yes*," said Wren. Her voice came out small. "*I heard you.*"

*Not many hear me. Not anymore. The net is very loud. It drowns me out.* A pause. Like a sigh. *The one who wove it heard me once. Long ago. He came here, and stood on my wind, and asked me for breath. For his bellows. To forge his great net.*

*I gave it to him.*

*And he wove the net. And laid it over the world. And the world below stopped breathing.*

---

Lex had gone still beside her. She felt his hand tighten.

"*Stopped breathing*?" said Wren.

*Not the people*, said the First Breath. *Their souls. Their threads. Every soul is meant to breathe. In and out. To grow and change and become. That is what I am. Breath. Life. Freedom. The air that moves.*

*And the net held every soul still. Capped. Ceilinged. Thus far and no further.* A long sigh. *A soul with a Ceiling is a soul holding its breath. Forever. Never letting go. Never taking in.*

*I have felt them below me for nine hundred years. Millions of them. All holding their breath.*

Wren looked at Lex.

He was staring into the empty blue at the center of the great ring of wind. His jaw was tight. She felt it through his hand: the same thing she'd felt from the Undertow. The weight of it. The whole world, holding its breath, because his ancestor had asked.

"*I promised the Undertow*," he said. Quietly. "*At the bottom of the sea. That I'd break the net. All of it. Every Ceiling. And weave a new one.*"

*I know. I heard. The sea and I talk. All the Wells talk, in the deep.* A pause. *That is why I let you up through my eye.*

"*Then you'll give us the breath?*"

*I will give the lightfoot the breath*, said the First Breath. *Not you, Root-heir. Her.*

Wren went still.

*You cannot carry it*, said the First Breath. *It is not a coal, or a drop, or a stone. It is breath. It must be carried in a chest. In lungs. Held, and carried, and breathed out into the fire when the bar is forged.* A pause. *Only one who Speaks to me can hold my breath without being torn apart by it.*

*And there is a price.*

---

Wren had known there would be. There always was. She'd watched Seraphine pay her fear to the sea. She'd watched Antho pay a stone that skipped five times. She'd watched Dorran carry the weight of four thousand children and set some of it down.

"*Name it*," she said.

The First Breath was quiet for a long time.

*You hold on*, it said at last. *Too tight.*

Wren didn't move.

*You hold on to him*, said the First Breath. *To the smith. You are afraid. Not of heights. Not of falling. You have never been afraid of falling.* A pause. *You are afraid of being left.*

Something cold went through Wren's chest.

*Your mother*, said the First Breath. Very gently. Like a breeze through long grass. *When you were eight. She went into the dark. For a year. Into her own head, behind her own eyes, and did not come out. You sat beside her bed every day. And held her hand. And she did not know you.*

Wren couldn't speak.

*And then the smith*, said the First Breath. *Who left you a note on a pillow. Who walks into the dark again and again without you. You hold on to him so tight. You follow him into burning forges and Founders' temples and the Pale Warden's fortress. Because if you let go, even for a moment, he might go into the dark and not come out.*

*Like her.*

---

She was crying. She didn't know when she'd started. The tears were blowing away off her face in the wind, little silver drops, scattering into the endless blue.

Lex's hand was very still in hers.

"*What do you want*," she said. Her voice was shaking. "*What's the price.*"

*Let go of his hand*, said the First Breath.

Wren stared into the empty blue.

*Just for a moment*, said the First Breath. *Here. In my sky. Where nothing holds anything down.* A pause. *Let go of his hand, and trust that he will not fall. Trust that the sky will hold him. Trust that if he goes into the dark, he will come back out. Not because you held on. Because he will.*

*That is the price. One moment. Of letting go.*

---

Lex turned his head and looked at her.

She could see it in his face. He'd heard it all. Every word. He was looking at her with his brown-gold eyes wide and wet and *stricken*, as if someone had shown him something he'd never seen before. As if he'd never once, in four years, understood why she followed him everywhere.

"*Wren*," he said. "*I didn't know. I didn't—*"

"*I know you didn't*." Her voice cracked. "*That's the point, Hammer. You weren't supposed to.*"

"*Your mother*—"

"*Is getting better*. *She knows me now. Most days.*" She swallowed. "*It doesn't matter. It's not about her. It's—*" She stopped. Looked down at their hands. His big scarred forge-callused hand, wrapped around hers.

She'd held it on the back of a dragon over the Greyspine. She'd held it in the black hall at Duskhold, when Garrick died. She'd held it in a wrecked ballroom with her shoes off. She'd held it in the dust of the south road below Highcrest.

She'd never once let go first.

*If I let go*, she thought, *and he falls—*

*If I let go, and he goes into the dark—*

*If I let go, and he doesn't come back—*

"*Wren*," said Lex. Very quietly. "*It's all right. Let go.*"

She looked at him.

"*I'll come back*," he said. "*I always come back. I came back from Duskhold. I came back from the glass. I came back from the masquerade, and the Hollowmaw, and the bottom of the sea.*" His voice was rough. "*I'll come back from this. And from the Lock. And from whatever's at the end.*"

"*You don't know that*."

"*No*," said Lex. "*I don't.*" He looked at her. "*But I'll try. Every time. I'll always try. Because you're there.*" He swallowed. "*Not because you're holding my hand. Because you're* there. *Waiting. With your bow. On the roof of the Spire.*"

Wren made a sound she'd never made before. Not a sob. Not a laugh. Something in between.

And opened her hand.

---

He fell.

Not far. A foot. Two feet. She saw his face change, saw him drop away from her through the shining air, saw his arms go out and his eyes go wide.

And then the sky caught him.

The winds came. All of them. Gentle, now. Like hands. Lifting him. Holding him. Setting him down on a long slow river of silver air a few feet below her, where he stood, swaying, with his arms out like a tightrope walker, staring up at her.

"*I'm all right*," he said. Wonderingly. "*Wren. I'm all right. It's holding me.*"

She looked down at him. Standing on the wind. On his own. Without her.

And something in her chest, something that had been clenched tight since she was eight years old and sitting beside a bed, holding a hand that didn't know her, *let go*.

She breathed out.

All the way out. Every bit of air in her lungs. A long, shaking, endless breath, going out of her into the endless sky.

*Thank you*, said the First Breath.

And then she breathed in.

---

It came into her like a storm.

She felt it. The First Breath. The oldest wind in the world. Pouring into her lungs from the great ring of silver air around the still blue center. Not cold. Not warm. *Alive*. Like breathing lightning. Like breathing the whole sky.

She thought she'd burst. She thought her chest would split open. It filled her lungs and kept coming, filled her throat, her head, her fingertips, her toes. She felt herself lifting. Off the wind. Up into the blue. Light as a leaf. Lighter. Light as air.

*Hold it*, said the First Breath. *Hold it in. Don't let it out. Not until the fire.*

She held it.

She clamped her mouth shut and held it, the whole sky in her chest, and floated in the blue with her eyes streaming and her hair standing out around her head in a crackling halo, and *held*.

She couldn't read it. Her eyes were full of tears and sky. But she felt Lex read it, below her, on the wind. Felt his face change.

*Commanding*.

She'd skipped past him again.

She wanted to laugh. She couldn't. She'd have let the breath out.

So she grinned instead. A huge, wild, tear-streaked grin, at the smith from Ashbourne standing on the wind below her.

And he grinned back.

---

They didn't go back alone.

When they came back down through the eye of the storm into the churning sky above the Teeth, Vharuun was waiting.

But not the Vharuun they'd left. Wren saw that at once. The storm-mother was hanging in the air above the black peak with her vast wings spread, and they weren't ragged anymore. They were *whole*. Storm-blue and silver, every tear mended, stretched out across a hundred feet of sky like the sails of the greatest ship ever built. Her scales were blue all over. No grey. No patches. And her eyes—

Both of them. Gold. Bright. Clear.

*THE STORM HEALED ME*, said Vharuun. Her voice rolled through the sky like a thunderclap. *WHILE YOU WERE ABOVE. I FLEW INTO THE CLOUDS WHERE I WAS BORN, AND THE STORM REMEMBERED ME.*

Ashka was beside her. Small, beside her mother. Bright as a spark. She was flying in circles around Vharuun, tight and fast and joyful, the way a puppy runs around its mother's legs.

*I AM WHAT I WAS*, said the storm-mother. *A THOUSAND YEARS OLD. LIGHTNING AND THUNDER. AND I HAVE A DEBT TO PAY.*

Her gold eyes found Antho.

*TO YOU, KEEPER OF MY CHILD. WHO SANG TO HER IN THE DARK. WHO CROSSED THE GREY RIVER AND PAID A PRICE FOR ME HE CANNOT REMEMBER.*

Antho, standing on the wind beside Wren, didn't say anything. His face was wet.

*WHEN YOU GO TO THE LOCK*, said Vharuun, *I WILL CARRY YOU. AND WHEN YOU FIGHT, I WILL FIGHT. AND WHATEVER WAITS AT THE END, IT WILL HAVE TO GO THROUGH ME.*

---

Wren held the breath for three days.

It was the hardest thing she'd ever done. Harder than letting go of Lex's hand. She couldn't speak. She couldn't eat. She couldn't drink, except in tiny sips, through her nose, with her mouth clamped shut. She couldn't sleep properly, because every time she started to drift off, she felt the breath start to slip, and jerked awake with her heart pounding.

She didn't need to breathe. That was the strange thing. The First Breath was in her lungs, and it was enough. It kept her alive. But it wanted *out*. It pushed against her ribs and her throat and her teeth, all the time, like a bird beating against the bars of a cage.

*Not yet*, she told it. *Not yet. Not until the fire.*

They flew east. Back along the Greyspine. On Vharuun's great whole back, all of them together, with Ashka darting around her mother like a swallow. Back to the Hollowmaw.

Lex held her hand the whole way.

She let him.

---

He forged it on the twenty-second night before the end.

In the Hollowmaw. In the bell-shaped cavern at the bottom of the mountain. In front of the cracked black door, and the little clay lamp in its niche.

Brannoc had built the forge. In the three weeks they'd been gone, the old smith had hauled an anvil down the long passage on a sled, with his arm still in a sling and Hedda swearing at him the whole way, and built a hearth of black stone against the cavern wall, and a chimney cut up through the rock. *Knew you'd need it*, he said, when Lex stared at it. *Knew you'd come back with something to make. You always do.*

Arion was there. He'd been at the door for three weeks. Sitting in front of it, day and night, with his hands on the black glass. He looked thinner. Older around the eyes. But when his sons came down the long passage into the cavern, he stood up, and the lean unchanged face did something it almost never did. It smiled.

Everyone was there. Wren looked around the cavern and saw them all, ringed around the forge in the gold light of a thousand lanterns. Antho and Seraphine. Brannoc and Hedda. Dorran, with the Heartiron in his hands. Kestrel and Tamsin. Ilse and Ivo. Oriel on her stool. Teo. Pim and Odile and the other students, crowded in the mouth of the passage.

And Lex. Standing at the anvil. In his leather apron. With Aurelian's little hammer in his hand.

He looked at her.

She nodded. She couldn't speak.

---

He laid the Heart-Coal in the hearth.

It didn't need coal around it. It didn't need wood. It sat in the black stone fire-pit on its own, white and gold and singing, and the heat came off it like the heat off the sun. The whole cavern lit up. Every lantern flared. The black glass of the door glowed red.

"*Iron*," said Lex.

Dorran came forward. Slowly. With the Heartiron held against his chest. He knelt beside the hearth and laid it on the white coal.

It glowed. Not red. Not orange. *Grey*. A deep, strange, iron-grey light, like the light at the bottom of the Wellworld of Stone. It didn't melt. It didn't soften. It just sat there on the Heart-Coal, glowing grey, getting heavier.

"*It's not taking the heat*," said Brannoc. Frowning. "*It's too cold. Too dense. It needs more.*"

Lex looked at Wren.

And she understood.

*Not until the fire*.

She came forward. To the bellows. A great old leather bellows, Brannoc's, patched and cracked, as long as her body. She took hold of its handles. She looked at the Heart-Coal, white and singing, and the Heartiron on it, grey and stubborn.

She opened her mouth.

And breathed out.

---

The First Breath went into the bellows like a storm into a sail.

She felt it go. All of it. Three days of it. The whole sky, pouring out of her lungs. She pushed the bellows down and the breath went *through* it, into the hearth, onto the Heart-Coal, and the coal *roared*.

Not a fire. A *sun*. White and gold and blinding, rising up out of the hearth in a column that hit the roof of the cavern and spread out across it like the burning sky of the Wellworld of Flame. The whole cavern went white. Wren couldn't see. She felt the heat like a hand pressed against her face. She heard the lanterns ringing, all thousand of them, like bells.

And the Heartiron *gave*.

She heard it. A sound like ice cracking on a river in spring. Like a mountain sighing. The grey glow went red, then orange, then gold, then the white of the sun.

"*NOW*," said Lex.

---

She'd seen him forge before. A hundred times. In the Ashbourne forge. In the Frostvault. In a dozen camps on a dozen roads. She'd sat cross-legged by the fire and watched him strike, and thought: *this is when he's most himself*.

She'd never seen this.

He took the Heartiron out of the fire with the tongs, white-hot, and laid it on the anvil. And Dorran Vask stood across the anvil from him with his great hammer, the striker's place, the place Brannoc had stood for Lex when he was twelve. And they began.

*Strike*. Dorran's hammer, heavy, slow, steady. A mountain's blow.

*Tink*. Lex's little hammer. Aurelian's. Light and fast and precise.

*Strike. Tink. Strike. Tink.*

The rhythm filled the cavern. Like a heartbeat. Like a song. Brannoc, standing at the side with his arm in a sling, was humming it under his breath. A hymn. Badly. Hedda was beside him with a brush and a pot of something that glowed, and every time Lex turned the bar, she leaned in and painted a rune on it. Fast. Sure. *Steadfast*. *Bulwark*. And her own one, the one she wouldn't explain.

And Arion. Standing behind his son. With his hands on Lex's shoulders. Gold pouring off him. Root-runes. Wellscript. Writing them into the iron through his son's hands, the way he'd once written Ember into a seal.

Wren watched the bar take shape.

It wasn't a sword. It wasn't a key. It was short, as long as her forearm, and thick, and flat, and its edge was *notched*. Jagged. A row of teeth. A mountain range.

She knew that shape. Lex had drawn it for her a dozen times on the road north, in the dust beside the campfire, as if he were trying to get it out of his head. The Bit. The second piece of the Key. Black and jagged, in a cup of glass, at the top of a column in a burning Well.

*The negative of it*, she realized. *The opposite. Where the Bit has a tooth, the bar has a gap. Where the Bit has a gap, the bar has a tooth.*

*It's a ward. Like in a lock. It goes into the lock beside the key and fills every space the key needs to turn.*

*He's forging it from memory. From the one time he held the Bit in his hand.*

---

They forged until dawn. Wren worked the bellows the whole time. Not with the First Breath; that was gone, all of it, poured into the first great roar. But the fire remembered it. Every time she pumped the old leather bellows, the Heart-Coal roared up white and gold, and she heard, very faint, a sound like wind in long grass. Like a girl running.

At dawn, Lex held up his hand.

Dorran stopped.

The bar lay on the anvil. Glowing. White-gold.

Lex looked at it for a long time.

Then he took his knife from his belt. And cut his palm. And held it out over the little glass bottle Seraphine had given him at the bottom of the sea. And let his blood drip into the Quench. Three drops. The silver water went red, then gold, then silver again.

*Only the blood that forged it can bar the way*.

He poured the Quench over the bar.

---

It didn't hiss. Wren had expected it to hiss. Quenched iron always hissed. Steam and spit and fury.

This *sang*.

A single note. Pure and high and clear. Like a bell struck once in a silent church. It rang out through the cavern, through the mountain, up the long passage and out of the cave mouth and across the valleys of the Greyspine. Every lantern in the Hollowmaw rang with it. The black glass of the cracked door rang with it. Wren felt it in her bones, in her teeth, in the place in her chest where the First Breath had been.

And the bar on the anvil cooled. From white to gold. From gold to silver. From silver to—

Grey. Dark grey, almost black. Smooth. Heavy. Notched along one edge like a mountain range. With runes running down its face in a thin line of gold. Steadfast. Bulwark. Hedda's nameless one. And Wellscript, Arion's, too small to read.

And one more. At the very end, near the hilt. In a hand Wren knew. Lex's hand. A rune she'd seen him cut once before, into Anvilsong's blade, at Avaloria. *The bearer cannot be severed from those he holds dear.*

*STAY*.

*Cost of setting*.

Wren read it over Lex's head. In gold. And the last line was black. Blotted out. Like the Lattice itself didn't want to say.

*And the blood that bars it, the blood will pay*.

She looked at Lex. He was looking at the panel too. His face was very still.

Then he looked at her. And smiled. Tired. Soot-black. Sweat running down his face.

"*Twenty-two days*," he said.

She came around the anvil. She took the Lockward out of his hands and laid it down on the anvil. Very gently. Then she took his hands. Both of them. His big scarred bleeding forge-callused hands.

And held them.

Not tight. Not like she was afraid he'd fall. Just held them. The way you hold something you've chosen to hold.

"*Twenty-two days*," she said.

Behind them, at the cracked black door, the little clay lamp leaned in its niche. And from the other side of the glass, so faint that only Antho and Arion heard it, a voice said:

*Oh, love. Oh, it's beautiful.*
