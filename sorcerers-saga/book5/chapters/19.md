# The Empty Chair

Lex didn't let go of her hand for three days.

Not really. Not literally. You couldn't. She was fourteen months old and she went everywhere at the speed of a falling sack of potatoes and she had opinions about being held. She wanted to walk. She wanted to climb the anvils. She wanted to eat coal. She wanted to put her fist in the quench-trough and see what happened.

So he didn't hold her.

He just didn't let go.

---

He worked with her on his back.

In a sling. The one Wren had made, oiled wool lined with fleece. He worked at the long anvil in the Forge with his daughter strapped to his back, her fists in his hair and her chin on his shoulder, watching everything he did. Every heat. Every stroke. Every quench.

She liked the quench best. The hiss. The steam going up in a white cloud.

"*Hot*," she said. Every time. Pointing.

"*Hot*," agreed Lex.

"*More*."

"*In a minute*."

---

He slept with her in the loft at the Forge. In the little room under the roof where the apprentices used to sleep, before the twenty anvils, before the Academy. On a cot. With her in the cradle beside him. With his hand through the bars.

He didn't sleep much.

He lay in the dark with his hand on her back and felt her breathe. In. Out. Small. Steady. And listened. To the Forge. To the rain on the slates. To the twenty hearths, banked, ticking. To Kestrel's fire in each one—she'd lit them all before she left with Wren, *so there's light everywhere and nothing to see in it*—small and white and steady.

To the water barrels. Lidded. Every one.

To nothing.

---

On the second night, he heard the stream.

Not the Forge's. The Forge didn't have a stream. A well. In the yard. Deep. Stone-lined. With a wooden cover over it that Brannoc had made in the autumn and nailed down at the edges.

He heard the water in it. Far down. In the dark. Moving.

Then not moving.

*Still*.

---

He was out of the cot before he knew he'd moved. With Lark in his arms. Asleep. Heavy. Warm. Her head on his shoulder.

He stood at the little window under the roof. Horn. Blurred. Looking down at the yard. At the well. At the wooden cover, nailed down, in the rain.

He couldn't see anything. Horn doesn't let you see anything clearly. That was the point.

He stood there all night anyway.

The water didn't move again.

---

On the third day, at dusk, Wren came home.

---

He heard the horses first. On the Ashbourne road. Then in the lane. Then in the yard. Two of them. Grey and bay. Muddy to the hocks. Steaming.

He was at the door before they'd stopped.

She got down off the grey horse. Slowly. Stiffly. Mud to the knees. A cut on her cheek. Her braid coming apart. Her bow over her shoulder.

She saw him. In the door. With Lark on his hip.

She didn't say anything.

She walked across the yard and put her arms round both of them. Him and the baby. And held on.

---

He held on too.

He didn't say anything either. He stood in the door of the Forge with his wife's arms round him and his daughter between them and the rain coming down, and held on.

After a while Lark said, muffled, from somewhere between them: "*No*."

Wren laughed into his shoulder.

"*Hello, chick*," she said. "*I know. I know. I'm squashing you.*"

She didn't let go.

---

Kestrel got down off the bay horse. Looked at them. Rolled her eyes.

"*I'll put the kettle on*," she said. "*Someone has to.*"

---

They sat in the kitchen behind the Forge. At the long table. With the fire lit—Kestrel's fire, small and white—and every pot lidded and every window horn and the rain on the roof.

Wren told it.

The islands. The line. The glass. Elowen fighting her own face. Eleven lanterns broken. Ada Fell. The mist rolling over the glass, and the glass going blind, and the Keepers breaking it. Kestrel's black heat. The glass things walking into it and melting.

And herself.

She told that part last. Quietly. Looking at the table. A glass woman in a ditch with a glass bow drawn and five clear fingers curled round the grip.

*This is what you are*.

"*I missed*," she said. "*By a foot.*"

Lex didn't say anything.

He reached across the table and took her left hand. Her gold one. And held it. In both of his. The three gold fingers. Cold. Smooth. Curled.

He didn't look at them like they were his fault.

He just held them.

---

Kestrel was watching. From the end of the table. With her tea. She didn't say anything either. But Lex saw her look away, after a moment. Out of the horn window. At nothing.

---

Seraphine came at midnight.

---

Down from the Forge proper. In her nightgown and her coat and her boots, unlaced, with a candle in one hand and her notebook in the other and no spectacles. Squinting. Her hair down. Her face white.

"*It's moving*," she said.

They all looked at her.

"*The cold*," said Seraphine. "*In the middle. At the bottom. I can feel it. Through Mal.*" She put her hand on her chest. "*It was still. Since noon. Since the Stones went dark. Just—sitting there. In the middle. In front of the crown.*" She swallowed. "*It's moving now.*"

"*Where*?" said Lex.

"*Nowhere*." Seraphine sat down. Heavily. At the end of the bench. "*Not moving like walking. Moving like—*" She stopped. Tried again. "*Like someone sitting down.*"

---

The fire went out.

---

All of them. At once. Kestrel's fire in the kitchen hearth. Small and white. Out. Like a candle snuffed.

And through the door to the Forge proper, Lex heard it. Saw it. The light going out. Twenty hearths. One after another. Down the long hall. Like lanterns in a row going dark.

Black.

---

Kestrel was on her feet. Her hand up. Fire in it—Lex felt her reach for it, felt the heat come up in her—

Nothing.

Her hand stayed dark.

"*What*—" She stared at it. "*What—*"

Lex reached for his own.

---

It was there.

He felt it. Under his breastbone. Where the Wells were. Fire. Heat. Weight. The roar of a forge. Flame Commanding. He'd had it since he was nineteen. It had never once not come.

It was there. He could feel it.

It wasn't coming *out*.

It was like reaching for a hammer and finding your hand wouldn't close. Like opening your mouth to shout and having no breath. The fire was in him. It just wouldn't—

Lark started to cry.

---

Not her usual cry. Not the one long furious howl she'd made on the rubble the night she was born, or the indignant squawk when someone took the coal away. A thin high frightened wail. The cry of a baby in the dark.

Wren had her. Lex felt her take her. Off his lap. Into her arms. Felt her turn away. Toward the wall. With the baby against her chest. Shielding her.

And then—

---

Light.

---

Not fire. Not lantern-light. Not anything warm.

*White*.

Cold. Fine. Hard. Like frost on a window under the moon. Hanging in the air in front of Lex. In front of Wren. In front of Kestrel, and Seraphine. In front of every one of them. At the same height. At the same distance. In the dark kitchen.

Panels.

---

Lex hadn't called it. He hadn't thought *status*. It just opened. In front of him. By itself.

The way the World Notices opened.

---

:::error
▓▓▓ WORLD NOTICE ▓▓▓
A CLAIMANT SITS AT THE HEART.
:::

---

He stared at it.

Not silver-blue. Not the Lattice's own clean cold glyphs. Not the ember-gold of the Root bleeding through. *White*. Frost-letters. Fine and hard and glittering. The letters the Ashbourne Stone had shown Posy Tull, on her sixteenth birthday, under her own face.

*THIS IS WHAT YOU ARE*.

---

The letters melted.

Re-formed.

---

:::error
▓▓▓ WORLD NOTICE ▓▓▓
THE NET IS STILL.
:::

---

"*Still*," said Seraphine. Very faintly. In the dark. "*Still. She's—oh. Oh, no.*"

---

The panel changed again.

Not a World Notice. *His*. His own panel. The one he'd looked at every day for seven years. The frame of it. The lines. Silver-blue. Coming up out of the white the way a face comes up out of frost on a pane when you breathe on it.

But wrong.

---

:::error
LEX VEY
Class: WORLDFORGER (Unique)
  TIER IV
Level 99
Experience: STILL
:::

---

*Still*.

He'd never seen that word on a panel. Not in seven years. Not in any panel anyone had ever shown him. Experience was a number. It went up. Slowly, at the top. A hair at a time. But up. Always up. That was what experience *was*.

*STILL*.

---

And under it. Smaller. At the bottom. In the corner. Where, for seven years, there'd been a line of warm gold text.

---

:::error
YOU ARE WHAT YOU ARE.
:::

---

"*Antho*," said Lex.

He didn't know why he said it. His brother was three hundred miles away. At Mal's pool. In the green circle.

He felt him anyway. Through the Bloodbond. Faint. Far. The way you feel a candle through a curtain.

*Ninety-eight*. *Still.*

And Wren—he looked at her. In the white light of her own panel. Her face. Her eyes moving over the frost-letters.

"*Seventy-three*," she said. Very quietly. "*Still.*"

"*Mine too*," said Kestrel. "*Still.*" Her voice cracked. "*And I can't—Lex, my fire—*"

"*It's not the fire*," said Seraphine. "*It's not the fire. It's the net.*"

---

They all looked at her.

She was sitting at the end of the bench in the dark with the white panel hanging in front of her face and her notebook open in her lap, and she wasn't writing anything. She was staring at her panel.

"*She's sitting in it*," said Seraphine. "*At the Heart. In the middle. In the chair. Where the Root was. Where you were, Lex.*" She looked at him. "*She can't pick it up. Not without the hand. But she can sit in it. Like—like a regent. Like someone sitting on a throne that isn't theirs, holding it for someone else.*"

"*And she's holding it still*," said Lex.

"*She's holding it still*." Seraphine's voice shook. "*The whole net. Every thread. She's got her hand on it and she's holding it the way you'd hold a—a mirror. Flat. Steady. So nothing moves. So nothing changes.*" She looked down at her panel. "*No experience. No levels. No growth. Nothing new in the net. Everyone exactly what they are.*"

"*And the fire*?" said Kestrel. "*My fire. Lex's.*"

"*The fire's not the net*." Seraphine shook her head. "*The fire's yours. The Wells. Attunement. UNTRACKED.*" She looked at Kestrel's empty hand. "*But you've been reaching for it through the net. All your life. Like everyone does. The net's the way the fire gets from you to the world. And she's holding the net still.*"

"*So the way's blocked*," said Lex.

"*So it's stuck*."

---

He reached for it again.

Under his breastbone. The fire. *There*. Heat. Weight. Roar.

He didn't reach through the net.

He didn't know how. He'd never thought about it. Seven years. It had always just come. He reached and it came. Through his hand. Through the panel. Through the threads.

He tried to reach *round*.

---

The way Malachi had taught him. In the Stillwater. Seven years ago. Barefoot, laughing, on a rock in the middle of a hot spring. *Before the Lattice, mages listened. To the Wells. Directly. The Lattice only channels it. Attunement is invisible to it.*

*Listen*.

He listened.

Not to the net. Not to the panel. To the fire. Under his breastbone. To the heat and the weight and the roar. To the Well of Flame. Directly. The way you'd listen to a friend's voice in a crowded room, past everyone else.

*Hello*, he thought.

And the fire—

---

Answered.

Not through his hand. Not through the net. *Through him*. Up from under his breastbone. Through his chest. Through his arm. Out of his palm.

Small. White-gold. A flame the size of a candle-flame.

In the dark.

---

Everyone stared at it.

Lex stared at it.

It burned in his palm. Small. Steady. Not through the net. *Round* it. The way water goes round a stone in a stream. The way the grey went round the lanterns.

"*UNTRACKED*," said Seraphine. Faintly. "*It's untracked. She can't hold what the net doesn't carry.*"

---

Kestrel was staring at the flame. At his hand.

"*How*—"

"*Listen to it*," said Lex. "*Not through the net. Round it. Like Malachi said. Like before the Lattice.*" He looked at her. "*You learned black heat in the deep. Without the net. Brannoc taught you. In the dark. Remember? Fire with no light.*"

Kestrel looked at her own hand. Dark. Empty.

She closed her eyes.

And after a long time—a very long time, long enough that Lark stopped crying and just hiccupped against Wren's chest—a flame came. In Kestrel's palm. Small. White. Shaking.

She opened her eyes. Looked at it.

"*Oh*," said Kestrel Varn. And sat down. Hard. On the bench. And put her other hand over her mouth.

---

The panels changed.

All of them. At once. In front of every face in the kitchen. And Lex knew—he didn't know how he knew—in front of every face in Caldor. Every person with a thread. Every Keeper on the line. Every farmer in the Westmarch. Every child in Ashbourne. Every lord in Highcrest. The Queen.

White. Frost. Melting. Re-forming.

A face.

---

Narrow. Pale. Clear. Not pale like skin. Pale like ice. You could see through it, faintly, to the dark behind. White hair, fine, pulled back off a high brow. Ice-grey eyes. A crown of glass, thin as a line of frost along the edge of a leaf.

Lex had seen it in his own panel once. Over his daughter's cradle. Wearing his face.

It wasn't wearing his face now.

It was wearing its own.

Or trying to.

---

"*Hello*," said the Glass Queen.

---

Her voice came out of the panel. Out of every panel. Cold. Clear. Like a bell under ice. Lex heard it from his own, in front of his face. From Wren's. From Kestrel's. From Seraphine's. All at once. The same voice. The same word. A little out of time with itself, like a choir.

"*Hello, everyone*," said Ysenne of the Glass. "*I'm sitting in your net.*"

---

Nobody in the kitchen moved.

"*You don't know me*," said the face. "*Most of you. Some of you have seen me. In a window. At night. In a bucket. In a puddle.*" The ice-grey eyes moved. Looking out. At each of them. At everyone. "*I'm sorry if I frightened you. I don't mean to frighten anyone. I only show people what they are.*"

A pause.

"*And now you can all see me*," said the Glass Queen. Very softly. "*In your panels. At the same time. Everyone.*" Something moved across the clear face. Quick. Like the shadow of a bird across a pond. "*That's never happened before.*"

---

Seraphine made a small sound.

Lex looked at her. She was staring at her panel. At the face in it. At whatever was in it.

"*She likes it*," whispered Seraphine. "*Oh. Oh, she likes it. Being looked at. By everyone.*"

---

"*Something is coming into your world*," said the Glass Queen. "*From the west. You've seen it. Some of you. The grey. It drinks. Bread won't rise. Seeds won't sprout. Cuts won't close.*" The face was very still. "*It's coming faster now. Because I'm holding your net still. Because nothing in it is new. And the grey drinks what's new.*"

She let that sit.

"*By midsummer*," she said, "*it will be in your Vale. By the autumn, your capital. By next winter—*"

She didn't finish.

"*Everything*," she said. "*It will drink everything. And you'll be like my country. Grey. Empty. Nothing growing. Nothing new. Forever.*"

---

"*Or*," said Ysenne.

---

"*I can keep you*," said the Glass Queen. "*All of you. Every thread. With the net. Like glass. Clear. True. Exactly what you are, forever.*" Her voice was very gentle. "*The grey can't drink glass. Nothing can. Glass doesn't change. Glass doesn't grow. Glass is never empty, because it's always full of whatever's looking at it.*"

The ice-grey eyes moved over them. Over everyone.

"*No grey*," she said. "*No hunger. No bread that won't rise—there won't be any bread, you won't need it. No cuts that won't close—you won't be cut. No old women in beds by windows. No old men. No doors.*" A pause. "*Nobody will ever have to go through a door again. Not ever.*"

---

Lex couldn't breathe.

He sat at the long table in the dark with the white panel in front of his face and the flame in his palm and the Glass Queen's voice coming out of the panel, and couldn't breathe.

Because it was *true*.

That was the thing. Everything she said was true. She never lied. Harrow lore. His mother. *The Glass Queen never lies.*

The grey *was* coming. Faster. The net *was* still. By midsummer the Vale. By autumn Highcrest.

And glass didn't grey.

---

"*I can't hold it*," said Ysenne. "*Not properly. Not yet. I'm only sitting in the chair. I can hold it still, but I can't hold it forever. Not like this.*" Her voice dropped. "*The net doesn't know me. It wasn't made for me. It was made for his blood. Aurelian's. The house remembers its builder.*"

And Lex knew what was coming.

He knew before she said it. He felt it come. The way you feel the hammer coming down before it lands.

"*I need a hand*," said the Glass Queen. "*A Vey hand. On the crown. Freely given. Or near enough.*"

Wren's arms tightened round Lark.

"*There are three left in the world*," said Ysenne. "*Three Vey hands. The smith's. The warden's.*" A pause. "*And a small one.*"

---

Nobody in the kitchen moved.

Nobody in Caldor moved. Lex knew that. He felt it. Every person with a thread, in every house and field and barracks and palace in the kingdom, with a white panel hanging in front of their face and the Glass Queen's voice in their ears.

*And a small one*.

---

"*The smith won't give me his*," said Ysenne. "*He gave the net away once. He'd do it again. He'd rather watch you all go grey than let anyone hold it.*" Her voice was very cold. "*He's very proud of that. Giving things away. He thinks it makes him good.*"

Lex's hand closed. On the flame. It went out.

"*The warden won't either*," said the Glass Queen. "*He keeps doors. He'd rather keep a door than open it.*"

A pause.

"*But the small one*," she said, "*doesn't know anything yet. The small one hasn't decided anything. The small one just needs someone to lift her hand and put it on the crown.*" The ice-grey eyes moved. And Lex felt them. Felt them find him. Through the panel. Through the dark. Felt them look past him. At Wren. At the bundle in Wren's arms. "*Her father won't. Her mother won't.*"

"*So I'm asking you*," said Ysenne. To everyone. "*All of you.*"

---

"*By midsummer*," said the Glass Queen. "*The shortest night. That's how long I can hold it. That's how long I can keep the grey from drinking you all at once.*" Her voice was very soft. "*Bring me the child. Bring her to any Stone. Put her hand on the glass. I'll do the rest.*"

"*And I'll keep you*," she said. "*All of you. Safe. Forever. Exactly as you are.*"

"*Or don't*," said Ysenne. "*And on the morning after midsummer, I'll let go of the net. And stand back. And let the grey have you.*"

A pause.

"*It's your choice*," said the Glass Queen. "*I never lie. I'm telling you the truth. That's all I ever do.*"

---

The face in the panel looked at them.

At everyone. For a long moment. Lex felt it. Felt her looking. Out of every panel in Caldor. At every face. Every one of them, at the same time, looking back at her.

And something moved across the clear face. Again. Quick. The shadow of a bird.

Then the panels went out.

---

Dark.

Total. The kitchen. The Forge beyond. Twenty hearths, black. The rain on the roof.

Lark started to cry again.

---

Lex sat in the dark.

He couldn't move. He sat at the long table with his hands flat on the wood and the dark all round him and his daughter crying, and couldn't move at all.

*Bring me the child*.

*Bring her to any Stone*.

*Every person with a thread in Caldor heard that*.

---

Somebody struck a light.

Not fire. Not from any Well. Flint and steel. From the tinderbox on the mantel. *Scratch*. A spark. Another. A curl of char catching, and a splint held to it. Yellow. Small. Ordinary. The kind of light anyone could make.

Brannoc.

He was standing in the doorway to the Forge. Huge. Bald. In his nightshirt and his apron, with his gold fingertips round the splint. Lex hadn't heard him come. He'd been asleep in the apprentices' room at the back. He must have heard. Seen. His own panel.

He lit a candle on the table. An ordinary candle. Tallow. From the box by the hearth.

Then he lit another.

Then he went to the kitchen hearth, and knelt, with his knees cracking, and blew on the coals. The way you'd wake someone who'd been up all night.

They were warm. Underneath. The fire hadn't gone *out*, not all the way. Only the light of it. Only the part that came through the net.

They caught.

Small. Red. Ordinary.

*Good evening*, said the fire.

Lex heard it. Faintly. A warmth. A lift. A small bright gladness.

The fire didn't care about the net.

It never had.

---

Brannoc stood up.

He looked round the kitchen. At Seraphine, white-faced, with her notebook. At Kestrel, on the bench, with her hand over her mouth. At Wren, by the wall, with the baby. At Lex.

"*Well*," said Brannoc Holt. "*That's that, then.*"

---

Nobody said anything.

"*She says midsummer*," said the old smith. He sat down. Heavily. At the end of the table. With the candle in front of him. "*Bring her the child, or she lets go and the grey has us.*" He looked at his gold fingertips. In the candlelight. "*And she says it's true.*"

"*It is true*," said Seraphine. Barely a whisper. "*She never lies. Everything she said is true.*"

"*Everything she said*," agreed Brannoc. "*Aye.*"

He looked at Lex.

"*But she didn't say everything*," said Brannoc.

---

Lex looked at him.

"*She said bring her the child or the grey has us*," said Brannoc. "*She didn't say there wasn't another way. She just didn't mention one.*" He folded his arms. "*That's not lying, lad. That's leaving something out.*" He looked at the candle. "*I've sold enough horseshoes to know the difference.*"

Lex stared at him.

"*The door*," said Wren. From the wall. Quietly.

Everyone looked at her.

"*She didn't say anything about the door*," said Wren. "*The grey's coming because there's no edge. Because there's nothing between us and it. The door's an edge. A door that asks.*" She held Lark tighter. "*If there's a door by midsummer, the grey stops. And she's got nothing to offer anyone.*"

---

Seraphine was very still.

"*She didn't mention it*," she said slowly. "*She knows. She must know. She's been in every window in Caldor for a year. She's seen the planks in the yard. The drawing on the table.*" She looked down at her notebook. "*And she didn't mention it. To anyone. To the whole kingdom.*"

"*Because she can't lie about it*," said Kestrel. "*So she didn't say it at all.*"

"*Because if everyone knew there was another way*," said Brannoc, "*nobody'd bring her the child.*"

---

Lex looked at his hands.

At the flat of them. On the table. In the candlelight. Burned. Scarred. A smith's hands.

He thought about the door.

Seven feet high. Three and a half wide. Planked. Apple wood. Eleven planks in the yard, blooming. A threshold-stone, wrapped in chains on the green, with a Glass Queen behind it. Three pieces of black iron in a basket. A bell on a cord round his neck.

A latch.

*Latch:* And nothing after it. Underlined twice.

*It has to say yes and no. Both. And know which. And when.*

He didn't know what it was. He didn't know where to find it.

He had until midsummer.

---

"*Twelve weeks*," said Seraphine. She'd been counting. Of course she had. "*Midsummer's in twelve weeks. Eighty-four days.*"

"*And the grey*?"

"*Faster*." She looked at her notebook. At columns of figures. "*She said it. The net's still. Nothing new in it. And the grey drinks what's new.*" She looked up. "*It was a mile a day. With the lanterns, less. Without the net—*" She stopped. "*I don't know. Two. Three. More.*"

"*Ashbourne*?"

"*Midsummer*," said Seraphine. "*Or before.*"

---

Nobody said anything.

The candles burned. The fire ticked. Outside, the rain.

Lark had stopped crying. She was sitting in Wren's lap, hiccupping, with her fist in her mouth and her eyes very wide in the candlelight. Looking at the flame.

"*Hot*," she said. Round her fist. Pointing.

"*Hot*," said Wren. Softly.

---

Lex got up.

He went round the table. To his wife. To his daughter. And knelt. On the kitchen floor. In front of them. In the candlelight.

Lark looked at him.

Grey-green eyes. Steady. Her round face. Her fluff of dark hair. Her fist in her mouth.

He held out his hand.

She took her fist out of her mouth. And put it in his. Square. Small. Too big for the rest of her. Wet.

A Vey hand.

He closed his fingers round it. Very gently.

"*No*," said Lark. Thoughtfully. To nobody. To the candle.

"*No*," agreed her father.

---

He looked up at Wren.

She was looking at him. Over the baby's head. With the cut on her cheek and her braid coming apart and her gold fingers lying on Lark's back.

He didn't say anything.

He didn't need to.

*We're making a door*.

*By midsummer*.

*With a latch I can't find, and a threshold behind a Glass Queen, and the whole of Caldor with a white panel in front of its face and a voice in its ears saying* bring me the child.

She nodded.

---

They sat on the floor of the kitchen behind the Forge at Ashbourne for a long time.

The three of them. In the candlelight. With the rain on the roof and the fire ticking and every pot lidded and every window horn.

And far off—very far off, very deep, at the bottom of the world, under the grey river, under the drowned country, in a round chamber where every thread in the world came in and crossed and met—

A woman of glass sat in an empty chair.

In front of a crown nobody could see. Holding a net still. Waiting.

Looking out of every window in the world.

And in none of them, herself.
