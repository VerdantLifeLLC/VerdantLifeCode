# Brannoc

Brannoc Holt had been asked a lot of things in sixty-five years, and most of them he'd said yes to without thinking, and a few of them he'd said no to and been sorry, and one or two he'd said nothing to at all and been sorry for longer.

Nobody had ever asked him if they could come into his forge.

People just came in. It was a forge. The doors were always open. Light came in, and air, and anyone who needed a horse shod or a pot mended or a word. That was what a forge was for. You didn't ask. You came in.

He stood in the middle of the long hall with a hammer in his hand and looked at the King in the doorway.

Gold. Enormous. With his head bent under the lintel and a cup in his hand and his other hand still raised from knocking on a door that was already open.

*May I come in*?

---

Everyone was looking at the lad.

Brannoc could feel it. The whole Forge. Tilda and Hedda and the fire-witch and the old desert woman and the dripping boy. The carpenter from the Westmarch and his son. Hollis with his bread. Wren, with the little one. Seren, with her pot of blue. The thorn-woman beside her, who wasn't a thorn-woman anymore. Seraphine and Antho.

All of them. Looking at Lex. Waiting for him to say.

And the lad was standing by the door at the end of the hall. The new one. Apple wood. Blooming. With his hand still on it. With his face white.

He didn't say anything.

Brannoc watched him not say anything. Watched him look at the King in the doorway. Watched him think about a gold floor in an old woman's cottage. About a yard at Harrow in the rain. About a cup with a crack in it.

About his daughter. On her mother's hip. Twenty feet away.

---

So Brannoc said it.

He didn't decide to. It came out of him the way most things did. Before he'd thought about it.

"*Mind the floor*," said Brannoc Holt. "*It's been swept.*"

---

The King looked at him.

Down the long hall. Over the anvils. Past all of them. At the old bald soot-bearded man in the middle of the floor with a hammer in his hand. Furnace eyes. Dimmed. Banked. Like a fire for the night.

"*You're the smith*," said the King. "*The big one. With the fingers.*"

"*I'm a smith*," said Brannoc. "*He's the smith.*" He jerked his chin at Lex. "*I just taught him.*"

The King looked at Lex. Then back at Brannoc.

"*I gilded your fingers*," he said.

"*Your steward did*." Brannoc held up his left hand. In the light from the hearths. The fingertips. All four. And the thumb. Gold. To the first knuckle. Smooth and bright and cold. "*On the green. In the spring. I was standing in front of a cart with six children in it.*"

"*I know*," said the King. "*I felt it. Every grain of it.*" He was very still. "*I'm sorry.*"

"*Are you*?"

"*Yes*."

Brannoc looked at him for a long time. At the gold face. At the furnace eyes. At whatever was in them.

"*Well*, *come in, then*," he said. "*Don't stand in the door. You're letting the heat out.*"

---

He came in.

It took a long time. He came the way he must have come into the old woman's cottage. Bent nearly double. Sideways between the anvils. Putting each foot down so carefully it might have been glass. The flags went gold where he stepped. A path. Gold. Down the middle of the long hall, from the great doors toward the hearth.

People moved out of his way.

Not running. Not screaming. Brannoc was proud of them for that. They just moved. Quietly. The way you'd move out of the way of a cart. Tilda stepped back with her gold hand in her apron. Hedda gathered up her chisels. The carpenter put his arm round his boy and stepped behind the saw-bench.

Wren didn't move.

She stood where she was. With the little one on her hip. With her bow over her shoulder. And watched the King come.

The little one watched too. With her fist in her mouth. Very seriously.

---

The King stopped.

Halfway down the hall. In front of Wren. A yard off. Bent. Looking down.

At the child.

The child looked up.

Brannoc held his breath. Everyone did. He heard it. The whole Forge.

The King put his hands behind his back. Both of them. With the cup. And held them there.

"*Hello*, *little one*," he said.

The child took her fist out of her mouth.

Brannoc waited for it. *No*. They all did.

The child looked at him. At the great gold face bent over her. At the furnace eyes. For a long time.

Then she put her fist back in her mouth.

And didn't say anything at all.

---

The King straightened up.

Slowly. Brannoc saw his face. Saw something go across it. Quick. Like a cloud across the sun.

He didn't say anything either.

He walked on. Down the hall. Past Wren. Past the child. Toward the hearth.

Toward the door.

---

He stopped in front of it.

The apple wood door in its apple wood frame at the end of the Forge. Shut. Blooming. With its dark hinges, that had been a key. With petals drifting off it onto the flags round the King's gold feet. And staying white.

He looked at it.

He looked at it the way Brannoc had seen men look at the sea. The first time. Lads from the Vale who'd never been further than Thistledown, standing on the cliffs at Saltreach looking out at all that grey water going on forever. Looking at something too big to understand.

Except it was a door. Seven feet high. Three and a half wide. A cottage door.

---

"*It's very small*," said the King.

"*It's the size of a door*," said Brannoc.

"*I thought it would be*—" The King stopped. "*I don't know what I thought. Big. Like Hes's curtain. Like the gate at the palace. Like the black door at the bottom of your mountain.*" He looked at it. "*It's the size of the door on the hedge-witch's cottage.*"

"*That's the point*," said Seren. Quietly. From behind him. With her pot of blue. "*Seraphine says. If it's big and terrible, nobody'll knock.*"

The King didn't turn round.

"*No*," he said. "*No. I suppose they wouldn't.*"

---

He looked at the hinges.

Brannoc saw him look. Saw the furnace eyes go to the dark straps on the apple wood. The sheen like a crow's wing. The knuckles curled round the pintles. Breathing. Very faintly. A slow turn inside the curl, like something rocking in a cradle.

"*That's Aurelian's*," said the King.

Nobody answered.

"*That's the Key*," said the King. "*Isn't it? The little man's key. That locked the pool. That Cad went down the thread for. That the grey one turned.*" He was very still. "*I'd know it anywhere. I felt it made. On the shingle. In chains. Waiting for the boat.*"

"*Yes*," said Lex. From beside the door. Hoarse.

"*And now it's a hinge*."

"*Yes*."

The King looked at it for a long time.

"*Cad would have laughed*," he said. "*Cad never laughed. But he'd have laughed at that.*"

---

He looked at the leaf.

At the apple wood. Pale gold. Blooming.

"*That's the old woman's tree*," he said.

Lex didn't answer.

"*I sat under it*," said the King. "*Two weeks. On the gold path. With the children. She talked to me from the window. She said it was the only thing in the village that never got any older.*" His voice was very low. "*She said the flowers were new every morning.*"

"*She gave it to me*," said Lex. "*At the end. She said make something with it.*"

"*I heard*," said the King. "*I was holding her hand.*"

---

He reached out.

Brannoc saw it happen. Saw the King's great gold hand come out from behind his back—not the one with the cup, the other one—and lift. Toward the door. Toward the apple wood. Slowly. Shaking.

"*Don't*," said Lex.

The hand stopped.

An inch from the wood. Hanging there. Gold. Enormous. Shaking.

"*It'll go gold*," said Lex. Very quietly. "*If you touch it. It'll stop growing. It'll be kept.*" He swallowed. "*It won't be a door anymore. It'll be a wall.*"

The King looked at his hand. An inch from the wood.

He didn't take it away.

He didn't put it down.

He just held it there. An inch off. The way he'd held it over the lad's shoulder on the floor of the old woman's cottage. Not touching. Not keeping.

A petal came down off the door. And landed on the back of his gold hand.

And stayed white.

---

The King made a sound.

Not a word. Not a laugh. Brannoc didn't know what it was. Something between the two. Something that came up out of the great gold chest like a breath let out after a long time under water.

He stood there with his hand an inch from the door and a white petal on it and looked at it.

Then he took his hand back. Slowly. With the petal still on it.

And looked at the petal.

---

"*Smith*," he said.

Brannoc thought he meant Lex. Then he saw the furnace eyes. On him.

"*Aye*?"

"*Can I sit*?" said the Gilded King. "*Somewhere. Anywhere. On the floor. I've been walking for three days.*"

Brannoc looked at him.

Then he looked round the Forge. At all of them. Standing. Still. Watching. The lad by the door, white-faced. Wren with the little one. Seren with her pot. The thorn-woman. Antho and Seraphine.

"*Go home*," said Brannoc. To all of them. "*It's late. Go and eat. Go to bed.*"

Nobody moved.

"*Go on*," said Brannoc. Louder. "*It's my forge. I'm banking the fires. Go home.*"

---

They went.

Slowly. One at a time. Looking back. Tom Pruett and his boy. The Arley lads. Hollis. Hedda. Tilda, last of the Forge folk, with her gold hand in her apron, looking at Brannoc with her eyebrows up. He shook his head at her. *Go*. She went.

Kestrel stopped in the door. Looked at the King. Looked at Brannoc.

"*If he gilds your forge*," said the fire-witch, "*I'm not helping you sweep it.*"

"*Noted*," said Brannoc.

---

The family didn't go.

He'd known they wouldn't. Seren and the thorn-woman sat down on the bench by the long table with the pot of blue between them. Antho and Seraphine stood by the wall. Wren went to the far end, to the corner by the quench-trough, and sat on an upturned bucket with the little one in her lap, and didn't take her eyes off the King.

And the lad stood by the door.

With his hand on it. On the apple wood. As if he could hold it shut by himself.

Brannoc let them be.

---

He banked the fires.

He did it the way he'd done it every night for forty-five years. The great hearth first. Then the others. Down the long hall. One by one. Rake the coals in. Pile the ash over. Leave a red eye in the middle. Enough to wake in the morning.

The King watched him.

He'd sat down on the floor in front of the great hearth. Cross-legged. With his back to the door. With the cup in his lap. The flags under him going gold in a circle.

He watched Brannoc rake and pile and bank. All the way down the hall and back.

He didn't say anything until Brannoc had finished.

---

"*Why do you do that*?" he said.

Brannoc looked at him.

"*The ash*," said the King. "*You cover them. All of them. Every night. You don't put them out.*"

"*You don't put out a forge fire*," said Brannoc. "*Not if you can help it. Takes an hour to light from cold. More, for the big hearth.*" He sat down. On the edge of his own anvil. The first one. The one he'd swung the lad up onto at eleven. "*You bank it. Cover it. Keep it low. And in the morning you rake off the ash and blow on it and it wakes.*"

"*Keep it*," said the King.

"*Not keep it*," said Brannoc. "*Bank it.*"

"*What's the difference*?"

---

Brannoc thought about it.

He wasn't a thinking man. He'd never pretended to be. He was a smith. He thought with his hands. With heat and weight and the ring of iron. When the lad was small and asked him things—*why does iron go red*, *why does water make it hard*, *why did my mother go into the fire*—he'd never had words. He'd shown him instead. At the anvil. With his hands.

He didn't have an anvil for this.

He tried anyway.

"*If you kept it*," he said slowly, "*it'd be the same fire. All night. Forever. Never going down. Never going out. The same flame.*" He looked at the great hearth. At the red eye under the ash. "*And in the morning you couldn't do anything with it. Because it'd just be—the same. Burning. Like it was.*"

The King was listening.

"*Banked*, *it goes down*," said Brannoc. "*It rests. It goes low and red and quiet. And in the morning it's a new fire. Out of the old one. You blow on it and it comes up different. Every morning.*" He shrugged. "*That's the difference. Kept's the same fire. Banked's a new one every day.*"

---

The King was quiet for a long time.

He looked at the hearth. At the red eye under the grey ash.

"*The old woman said that*," he said at last. "*About the flowers. New every morning.*"

"*Mag said a lot of things*," said Brannoc. "*Most of them rude.*"

The King laughed.

Cracked. Wet. Astonished. Brannoc heard it go up into the rafters of his forge and come back down.

"*She was rude to me*," said the King. "*On the green. In the snow. She said I was a lump.*"

"*You are a lump*," said Brannoc.

The King laughed again.

---

"*Tell me about your fingers*," said the King.

Brannoc looked down at them. At his left hand. In his lap. The gold tips. In the red light from the banked hearths.

"*Nothing to tell*," he said. "*The dust came down. I was standing in front of a cart. Six kiddies in it. Little Jenna Tull and the Arley twins and the rest. The lad was making room in front of them. Couldn't make it in front of me as well.*" He turned the hand over. "*So it came down on me.*"

"*Does it hurt*?"

"*No*."

"*Do you mind*?"

Brannoc thought about it.

"*I mind I can't hold a pair of tongs right*," he said. "*I mind I can't feel the iron. Fifty years I've felt iron with these. Felt it through the tongs. Hot. Cold. Ready. Not ready.*" He looked at the gold. "*Can't now. Not with this hand. Have to look. Have to guess.*"

"*I'm sorry*."

"*You said*."

"*I am*," said the King. "*I'm sorry for all of them. Your fingers. The girl's hand. The miner's arm. The wife's—*" He looked toward the corner. At Wren. On her bucket. With the little one in her lap. "*The boy in the field. The man in the gate. The old shepherd's wife in her chair.*" He looked down at his own hands. "*Nine thousand of my own. All of them. I'm sorry for all of them.*"

"*Then let them go*," said Brannoc.

---

The King went still.

Brannoc watched him go still. The way iron goes still when you pull it out of the fire and lay it on the anvil and it stops glowing and starts to set.

"*I can't*," said the King.

"*Why not*?"

"*Because if I let go*—" The King stopped.

"*If you let go*," said Brannoc, "*my fingers come back. The lass's hand. The miner's arm. Wren's. The boy in the field finishes his step. The Keeper walks out of his gate.*" He looked at the King. "*And your nine thousand come apart. Nine hundred years. All at once. In front of you.*"

The King stared at him.

"*The lad told me*," said Brannoc. "*In the winter. After the green.*" He folded his arms. "*And you go too. Don't you? It's you that's holding them. If you let go, there's nothing holding you.*"

---

The King didn't answer.

He sat on the floor of the Forge with the cup in his lap and the banked hearth behind him and the door behind that, and didn't say anything at all.

Brannoc let him not say anything.

He'd learned that. Not from anyone. From the lad. From a boy of eleven, sitting on the floor of this same forge at night, after the fire, after his mother went into the dark and his brother went mad and the whole of his life went up like dry thatch. Sitting on the floor. Not crying. Not talking. Just sitting.

Brannoc had sat with him.

Every night. For a month. On the floor. Not saying anything.

That was all. That was the whole of it.

---

"*I had a wife*," said Brannoc.

He didn't know he was going to say it. It came out of him.

The King looked up.

"*Mattie*," said Brannoc. "*Martha Holt. Married at twenty. She was a weaver. Up at the mill. Red hair. Freckles. Laughed like a goose.*" He looked at the banked hearth. "*Couldn't have children. We tried. Ten years. Never happened.*" A pause. "*She'd have liked the lad. She'd have spoiled him rotten.*"

The King didn't say anything.

"*Wasting sickness*," said Brannoc. "*Same as your father, sounds like. In the chest. Took a winter. Twenty-five years ago.*" He looked at his gold fingertips. "*Started in the autumn. A cough. By Midwinter she couldn't get up the stairs. I carried her. By the thaw she couldn't get out of bed.*"

"*Did you*—" The King stopped.

"*Go in*?" said Brannoc. "*Aye. Every night. Sat by the bed. Held her hand.*" He shrugged. A small heavy movement. "*Didn't say much. Didn't have much to say. She did the talking. She always did.*"

"*Was it*—"

"*Terrible*?" said Brannoc. "*Aye.*"

The King was very still.

"*Worst thing I ever did*," said Brannoc Holt. "*Sitting there. Every night. Watching her go. A bit more every night. Like a fire going down. And nothing to do about it. Not a thing. A smith. Fifty years of fixing things. And I couldn't fix that.*" He looked at his hands. "*I'd sit there holding her hand and think, I could beat iron into anything. Anything. And I can't do this.*"

---

The forge was very quiet.

Brannoc heard the banked fires ticking. The rain on the slates. Somewhere at the end of the hall, by the quench-trough, the little one making a small sleepy sound in her mother's lap.

"*Would you do it again*?" said the King. Very quietly.

Brannoc looked at him.

"*Every night*," he said. "*Every night of my life. If I could have her back for a winter to do it.*"

---

The King put his face in his hands.

Brannoc didn't move.

He sat on his anvil and watched the biggest man in the world sit on the floor of his forge with his face in his hands and his shoulders shaking, and didn't move, and didn't say anything.

That was all. That was the whole of it.

---

After a long time, the King lifted his head.

His face was wet. Gold tears. On gold cheeks. Falling. Onto his robe. Onto the cup in his lap. Onto the floor.

The floor was already gold.

"*I couldn't go in*," he said. "*For my father. A month. Every night. I stood outside. With my hand on the latch.*" His voice was a ruin. "*Cad went in. He was eleven. He held Father's hand. Every night. And I stood outside the door. And I thought—if I drink, I'll never have to stand outside a door again. Not ever. For anyone.*"

"*And now you're standing outside nine thousand of 'em*," said Brannoc.

The King stared at him.

"*Aren't you*?" said Brannoc. "*All those folk of yours. Walking across the fields. Humming. Home.*" He leaned forward. "*They're not home. They're in a room with no door. And you're standing outside it. With your hand on the latch. Every night. For nine hundred years.*"

---

The King didn't say anything.

He sat on the floor with his wet gold face and his cup in his lap and looked at Brannoc.

And Brannoc saw it. He didn't have Seren's eyes, or the Glass Queen's, or anyone's. He was a smith. He saw iron. He saw heat. He saw when a thing was ready and when it wasn't.

He saw it now.

---

"*You leave iron in the fire too long*," said Brannoc Holt, "*it burns.*"

The King looked at him.

"*You don't know that*," said Brannoc. "*You've never held a pair of tongs.*" He got up. Off his anvil. Slowly. His knees cracking. He went to the rack on the wall and took down a pair of tongs. Old. Worn. The handles polished by fifty years of his hands. He came back and sat down again and held them up. In the red light. "*You put a bar in the fire. You let it come up. Red. Orange. Yellow. White.*" He moved the tongs. "*And then you take it out. And you work it. And you quench it. And it's what it is. A nail. A hinge. A horseshoe.*"

"*And if you don't take it out*?"

"*It keeps going*," said Brannoc. "*White. Past white. It starts to spark. Little stars. Coming off it.*" He looked at the tongs. "*Pretty. Looks pretty. Like a firework.*" He looked at the King. "*That's the iron burning. That's the iron going. Into sparks. Into nothing. You leave it in long enough, there's nothing left on the end of your tongs but a lump of slag.*"

The King was very still.

"*You've got to take it out*," said Brannoc. "*You've got to let it cool. You've got to let it be what it's going to be. Even if it's not what you wanted. Even if you'd rather it stayed white and shining in the fire forever.*" He laid the tongs across his knees. "*Or there's nothing left.*"

---

The King looked at the tongs for a long time.

Then he looked down at his own hands. Gold. Enormous. In his lap. Round the cup.

"*They're burning*," he said. Very quietly. "*Aren't they. My household. They've been in the fire nine hundred years.*"

Brannoc didn't answer.

"*Kept's the oven*," said the King. "*Finished is the cake.*" He laughed. Cracked. "*Kept's the fire. Finished is the horseshoe.*" He looked up at Brannoc. "*Everything's a cake. Everything's a horseshoe. You're all the same.*"

"*Most of us*," said Brannoc. "*Aye.*"

---

The King got up.

Slowly. Carefully. The way a very big man gets up off a floor he's afraid of breaking. One knee. Then the other. Then all of him. Up and up. Until his head was among the rafters and the red light from the banked hearths was on his gold face from below.

He turned round.

To the door.

The apple wood door in its frame at the end of the Forge. Shut. Blooming. With its dark hinges breathing.

The lad was still standing beside it. With his hand on it. White-faced.

The King looked at the door. Then at the lad. Then at the door again.

"*If I let go*," he said. "*If I open my hand. All the way.*" His voice was very low. "*I go through that. Don't I.*"

---

Nobody said anything.

The lad didn't. Seren didn't. The thorn-woman didn't. Antho didn't. Wren didn't, in her corner, with the little one asleep in her lap.

Brannoc didn't.

He looked at the King's back. Huge. Gold. Facing the door.

He thought about Mattie. In a bed. By a window. In the thaw. Holding his hand. *Don't you dare sit in that chair moping after, Brannoc Holt. You go and find someone to look after. You're no good on your own.*

He'd found the lad.

Ten years later. In the ashes of a cottage in a birch wood. Eleven years old. Not crying. Not talking.

He'd swung him up onto the anvil.

*This is yours now, lad*.

---

"*Aye*," said Brannoc Holt. "*Most of us do. In the end.*"

The King didn't turn round.

Brannoc got up off his anvil. Slowly. His knees cracking. He walked down the hall. Past the hearths. Past the long table. Past Seren and the thorn-woman on the bench. To the door.

He stood beside the lad. On the other side of the frame. And put his own hand on the apple wood. His left. With the gold fingertips.

The wood was warm.

He couldn't feel it. Not with those fingers. He felt it anyway. Through the rest of his hand. The palm. Warm. Alive. Growing. Making room.

He looked up at the King.

"*I'll hold it for you*," said Brannoc. "*When it's time. The door.*" He shrugged. "*Someone'll have to. Might as well be a smith.*"

---

The King looked down at him.

For a long time. The furnace eyes. Banked. Low. Red under the gold. Like a forge fire under ash.

Then he did something Brannoc would think about for the rest of his life.

He bowed.

Not low. Not the court bow. Not the steward's bow, with one arm across the middle. A small bow. A nod, really. A tilt of the great gold head. The way one man nods to another across a forge, when the other's said something that'll take a while to think about.

"*Thank you*," said Oszric of the Crowned. "*Smith.*"

---

He went out at midnight.

Down the long hall. Down his own gold path. Out through the great doors into the yard, where the rain had stopped and the stars were out over the Vale.

He stopped in the yard.

Brannoc watched him from the door. Watched him stand there. Huge. Gold. Glowing faintly in the dark. Looking up at the stars.

Then he lifted his hand. The one without the cup. And looked at it.

There was a petal on the back of it. Still. White. Pink at the edges. Off the apple wood door. It had stayed there. All night. Through all of it. Not gold.

The King looked at it for a long time.

Then he closed his hand round it. Very gently. The way you'd close your hand round a moth.

And walked away into the dark.

---

Brannoc went back in.

The family was still there. All of them. Seren and the thorn-woman on the bench. Antho and Seraphine by the wall. Wren on her bucket with the little one asleep. The lad by the door.

Nobody said anything.

Brannoc went to the great hearth. Knelt. With his knees cracking. And looked at the banked fire. At the red eye under the ash.

He'd banked it an hour ago. He'd banked it every night for forty-five years.

He blew on it anyway. Gently. The way you'd wake someone who'd been up all night.

It woke. A little. A flicker. A lift.

*Good evening*, said the fire. Not in words. A warmth. A small bright gladness.

Brannoc had never heard it before. Not once in forty-five years. The lad heard it. The lad said good morning to his fire every day. Brannoc had always thought that was his father in him. The mad streak. The Vey in him.

He heard it now.

He knelt there on the hearthstone with his gold fingertips on his knees and looked at the little red flame and didn't say anything for a long time.

"*Evening*," said Brannoc Holt. At last. Gruffly. To the fire.

It seemed rude not to.
