# The Rose Goes Home

Seren Harrow met her great-aunt, nine hundred times removed, in the kitchen of the Coldwater house, on a wet grey morning at the end of winter, over a pot of tea.

She was smaller than Seren had expected.

That was the first thing. In the songs, the Crowned were tall. Taller than people. Glittering. Terrible. Gold for the eldest. Glass for the vain. Iron for the soldier. In the songs, they filled doorways.

The woman at the end of the long table in the Coldwater house didn't fill anything.

She sat wrapped in two of Kit's blankets and a sheepskin, on a bench by the fire, with her knees drawn up and her arms round them. Thin. So thin. Grey—not pale, not white, *grey*, like the inside of a dead tree, like ash—with grey hair hanging lank round a grey face, and a crown of dead brown thorns on her head, crooked, with one broken place on it near the front where something had been.

And two green eyes.

They came up when Seren came in. And stayed on her. Wide. Very green. The only green thing in the room.

"*Oh*," said Rhosyn. A whisper. "*Oh. You look like her.*"

---

Seren had come east on the dragon.

Antho had come for her. Four days ago. Down out of a grey sky onto the road outside Westerling, where she'd been helping Brannoc and Tilda set the last of seven hundred and twelve lanterns in the snow, and said *Mam. Pim's back. He's brought her. She's asking for you.*

*Who*?

*Rosie*, her son had said. *Rhosyn. The Thorn-Crowned.* He'd looked at her with her own brown eyes. *She says she wants Hes's girl. She won't do it without you.*

*Do what*?

*Give it back*, said Antho.

---

She sat down at the long table.

Across from the grey woman on the bench. With the fire between them, and the rain on the horn windows, and Kit hovering by the hearth with the kettle.

She didn't know what to say.

She'd thought about it all the way east. Four days on a dragon in the rain. What you say to your great-aunt, nine hundred times removed, who drank from a Well and made herself deathless and spent nine hundred years in a grey country with no sun, growing thorns.

*Hello*. *I'm sorry*. *How could you*.

*Thank you for the rose*.

"*Tea*?" said Seren.

---

Rhosyn looked at the pot.

Brown. Glazed. Chipped at the spout. Kit's mother's. Steam coming off the top.

"*I haven't*," she said. "*Not in nine hundred years. There wasn't—in the Court, there wasn't anything. No water. No leaf. Nothing grew.*" She looked at it. "*I used to dream about tea. Isn't that strange? Of all the things. Not feasts. Not wine. Just tea. In the morning. In the garden. With Hes.*"

Seren poured.

She poured it the way she poured it every morning. The way Arion had taught her, thirty years ago, on the Spire steps, when she didn't know him. Warm the pot. One for each and one for the pot. Water just off the boil.

She set the cup on the table. In front of the grey woman.

And took her hand away. And looked out of the window.

---

She didn't watch. She'd learned that. From her son, in a cave full of lanterns, a month ago. *That's lending with your eyes*.

She looked at the rain on the horn panes. At the grey light. At the green circle beyond, under the last of the snow, with the dark pool in the middle and the ring of lanterns round it a mile out, small and gold, five hundred and fifty-six of them.

She heard the cup lift.

She heard a breath. A small one. Shaking.

She heard a sound. Very quiet. Like a woman putting her face in her hands.

She didn't look.

---

"*Hes did that*," said Rhosyn. After a long time. Muffled. "*Put things down and looked away. Bread. Flowers. A cup. She'd put it down by you and go and look out of the window like she'd only just noticed it was there.*" A wet laugh. "*I used to think she was shy.*"

"*She wasn't shy*," said Seren.

"*No*." The grey woman lifted her head. Her face was wet. "*No. I know that now.*"

---

They talked.

All morning. With the rain on the windows and the fire ticking and Kit making more tea and going away and coming back. About Hesper.

Rhosyn talked, mostly. Seren listened.

About a girl of nine with a bell on her belt so the nurses would know where she was. About a girl of twelve who'd hidden in the kitchen garden all day, among the cabbages, and been found at dusk by a barefoot boy from the Listeners' house who'd climbed the wall to steal turnips. About a girl of seventeen who'd sat on the edge of Rhosyn's bed, the night before the pool, in the dark, and held her hand.

*Don't, Rosie. Please. It isn't ours*.

"*I listened*," said Rhosyn. "*I did. All night. I lay there and thought about it till the window went grey.*" She looked at her hands. Grey. Thin. With red marks round the wrists, fading. "*And in the morning I went down to the garden. And they were all there. Kneeling. Round the pool. Oz first. Laughing. Like it was a game.*" She swallowed. "*And Hes standing apart. With her crown in her hands. Looking at me.*"

"*And you knelt*."

"*I knelt*," said Rhosyn. "*Between Cad and Ysy. And cupped my hands. And Hes said Rosie. Just my name. Rosie. Across the garden.*" Her voice broke. "*And I drank anyway.*"

---

Seren didn't say anything.

She sat across the table from her great-aunt, nine hundred times removed, and thought about the old verses. Sung at the end of summer. In thirty houses. In a half-packed cottage, with the fire low.

*Thorns for the gardener, who listened—and drank*.

It didn't rhyme. It wasn't supposed to. It broke right there, right in the middle, on that word. So you'd always remember. So you'd never forget that's where it went wrong.

"*Mam sang about you*," she said.

Rhosyn looked up.

"*The old verses*," said Seren. "*At the end of summer. Every summer. Gold for the eldest. Glass for the vain.*" She looked at the grey woman. "*Thorns for the gardener. Who listened. And drank.*"

Rhosyn went very still.

"*It doesn't rhyme*," said Seren. "*I asked Mam why. When I was seven. She said it's meant to break there. On that line. Your line. So you'd always remember where it went wrong.*"

---

The grey woman sat very still for a long time.

"*Yes*," she said at last. Very quietly. "*Yes. That's right. That's where it went wrong.*" She looked down at the cup in her hands. "*Hes would have made it break there. She'd have known.*"

"*Mam said something else*," said Seren.

Rhosyn waited.

"*She said it's the only line in the song where somebody listened*," said Seren Harrow. "*All the others—gold, glass, iron, silver. They never listened. Never even heard. They just drank.*" She looked at her great-aunt. "*You heard. You lay awake all night. You knew.*"

"*And I drank anyway*."

"*And you knew*," said Seren. "*Mam said that's why it breaks. Not because you drank. Because you knew, and drank anyway, and you've been knowing it ever since.*" She reached across the table. Slowly. And put her hand over the grey woman's. On the cup. "*Mam said that's the only one of them that could ever come back.*"

---

Rhosyn stared at her.

At the hand on hers. Brown. Freckled. Square. A hedge-witch's hand.

"*She said that*?"

"*Every summer*."

"*Your mother*," said Rhosyn. "*Wenna.*" Her voice was very small. "*She came. Thirty-two years ago. To the tenth tower. In the dark. Behind the man with the boat. With the crown.*" She swallowed. "*I watched her. Through my thorns. She didn't see me. She stood in front of the tenth chair and set the crown on it and said, for Hes. From your girls. We kept the rule.*"

Seren's hand tightened.

"*And then she turned round*," said Rhosyn. "*And looked straight at my thorns. Straight at me. Though she couldn't have seen. And she said—*"

She stopped.

"*What did she say*?" said Seren.

"*She said, I know you're there, Rosie*," said Rhosyn. "*She said, Mam told me about you. She said, if you ever want to come home, the door's open.*"

---

Seren couldn't speak.

She sat with her hand on her great-aunt's hand on a cup of tea in the kitchen of the Coldwater house, with the rain on the windows, and couldn't say anything at all.

*I know you're there, Rosie*.

Her mother. Who never cried. Who never left a light on. Who never stayed anywhere longer than a year. Who'd paid the Ferryman the memory of the man she loved to carry a crown to the far side of the world. Standing in a dead tower in a grey country with no sun, speaking into a wall of thorns.

*If you ever want to come home, the door's open*.

---

"*I didn't*," said Rhosyn. "*I didn't come. For thirty-two years. I sat outside the tenth tower and grew my thorns round it so Oz wouldn't find the crown and I didn't come.*" She looked at Seren. "*And then the Harrow boys came through the crack. In the summer. And I took them to the tower. And they took the crown. And I held my thorns so they could run.*" Her voice cracked. "*And Ysy chained me.*"

"*And you sent a rose*," said Seren. "*To Harrow. To the grave.*"

"*It was the last green thing I had*," said Rhosyn.

"*I know*," said Seren. "*I saw the place.*" She looked at the crown. At the broken spot near the front. Raw. Pale. "*Thank you.*"

The grey woman looked at her.

Then she turned her hand over. Under Seren's. On the cup. And held on.

---

They went to the pool at noon.

---

The rain had stopped. The sky was low and grey and wet. The snow was going—not melting, not in the grey-snow parts, but going everywhere else, soft and slushy, with green showing through. Real green. Grass. Clover. The new river running fast and brown and loud.

And the lanterns.

A ring of them. A mile out from the pool. All round. Five hundred and fifty-six. Small and gold. Each one in its own little circle of real ground. And between them, and inside them, between the lantern ring and the rim—

Grey.

Not snow anymore. The snow had gone. Grey *grass*. Grey clover. Grey earth. Flat and still. The way the bread had been still in the bowl. The way the boy in the field was still. A mile of it. All the way in to the rim.

And the dead.

---

Seren had never seen them.

Antho had told her. Ivy. Pim, in his three words at a time. *Thousands*. She'd thought she understood.

She stood at the edge of the lantern ring with her great-aunt leaning on her arm and looked at them.

Grey. Columns of dust. Tall as a man, some of them. Some as small as a child. Thousands. Standing in the grey grass between the lantern ring and the pool, close together, so many that she couldn't see the water through them. Swaying. Very slightly. In a wind that wasn't blowing.

Saying their names.

She couldn't hear them. Not like Antho could. But she could feel it. A murmur. Under everything. Like a congregation praying, very quietly, in a very large church.

---

Rhosyn stopped.

Seren felt her stop. Felt the thin grey arm on hers go rigid.

"*Oh*," said Rhosyn. "*Oh, I know them.*"

She was looking at the dead.

"*That's Wenlock*," she said. "*The under-gardener. He taught me to graft roses. He came apart in the second year, in the glass hall, sitting on the floor.*" Her voice was shaking. "*And that's Bess, from the dairy. And Hollin. Hollin of the stables. He had a dog. The dog came apart first and he sat with it and then he—*"

She stopped.

"*Rosie*," said Seren.

"*I walked past them*," said the Thorn-Crowned. "*Nine hundred years. In the dust. On the white road. I walked past them every day and I never once stopped.*"

---

Antho met them at the lantern ring.

He came out through the dead. Tall. Thin. In his long dark coat, with his black hair tied back and his scarred hands at his sides and a coin of gold on the back of the left one. He walked through the dead the way you'd walk through a field of tall grass. Carefully. Not touching. Saying something to them, as he went, under his breath.

He stopped in front of Rhosyn.

He looked at her for a long time.

"*Highness*," he said.

"*Don't*," said Rhosyn. "*Please. Not that.*"

"*Rosie*, *then*."

She looked at him.

"*You held the thorns*," said Antho. "*In the summer. So we could run.*" He looked at her wrists. "*Ysy chained you for it.*"

"*Yes*."

"*Thank you*," said Antho Vey.

Rhosyn looked at him. At his face. At whatever was in it.

"*You're the one who told them*," she said. "*At the edge. In the summer. The four cousins. I heard you. Through the roots.*" She swallowed. "*There's a pool in a green field. Full of room. It's still very little.*"

"*Yes*."

"*Is it still very little*?"

Antho looked back at the dead. At the pool beyond them, invisible behind them.

"*Yes*," he said.

"*Then let's make it bigger*," said Rhosyn.

---

They walked in through the dead.

Antho first. Then Seren, with Rhosyn on her arm. Then Pim. Then Hask, with Kell's staff, and Kit. And Seraphine, who'd come out of the Coldwater house at the last moment with her spectacles on and her notebook in her hand, and fallen in behind them without a word.

The dead didn't move.

They swayed. That was all. Tall grey columns, fine as flour, in the grey grass. Saying their names. As Seren passed them, close, she felt it—the murmur—go through her. Under her skin. Like a hum.

And she felt them notice.

Not her. *Rhosyn*.

Felt them turn—not turn, they didn't have faces to turn—*lean*. Very slightly. All of them. Toward the thin grey woman on her arm. The way grass leans in the wind. The way a crowd leans when someone it knows walks into a room.

Rhosyn felt it too.

Seren felt her shake.

---

The pool was very small.

Smaller than Seren remembered. She'd seen it once. Two winters ago. On the night it came home. A great dark shape the size of a country rising up through the breach at Duskhold in the Blightmarch, enormous, soft, frightened, and folding itself down, and down, and down, into a pool the size of a pond, with three gold lights at the bottom.

It was smaller now.

She could see it. The rim of it. The black water. Soft. Dark. Reflecting nothing. And round the edge of the black—a ring. Grey. A hand wide. Like frost on a window. Like the beginning of a cataract in an old man's eye.

And on the rim, on a folded blanket, a girl on a crutch. Talking.

"*—and she's here now, Mal. She's come. The thorn lady. With the lamp-lady. And everyone.*" Ivy Moss looked up. Saw them. Saw Rhosyn. Her face went white. Then pink. Then she turned back to the pool. "*She's here, Mal. She's really here.*"

---

The pool didn't answer.

Seren heard it not answer. A silence from the dark water. Not empty. *Tired*. Like a child who's cried itself out and is lying very still in the dark, too worn out to sleep.

Then—

*[...lamp-lady...]*

Faint. So faint. Seren had heard that voice in the deep, thirteen years in the dark, all round her, as big as a sky. It was a thread now. A whisper. Like a voice from the bottom of a well.

*[...lamp-lady...you came...]*

"*I came*," said Seren. She knelt. On the rim. Beside Ivy. And put her hand flat on the cold stone. "*I came, little one. I brought someone.*"

---

Rhosyn knelt beside her.

Slowly. Her knees cracking. Her thin grey hands on the rim. She knelt the way she must have knelt, Seren thought, nine hundred years ago, in a hidden garden, between her brother and her sister, at the edge of a black pool.

And looked down into the dark.

---

*[...oh...]* said Mal.

The dark water moved. Seren saw it. A ripple. From the middle. Out. To the grey ring at the edge. As if something very far down had turned over.

*[...oh...it's you...I know you...]*

Rhosyn didn't move.

*[...you were there...in the garden...you were the one who cried...]*

The grey woman's breath caught.

*[...they all laughed...when they drank me...all of them...the gold one...the glass one...the iron one...the moth...they laughed...]* The voice was very small. Very slow. *[...but you didn't laugh...you cried...I remember...I tasted it...when you drank me...you were crying...]*

---

Rhosyn put her face in her hands.

Kneeling on the rim. With the thorns crooked on her head. Shaking.

"*I'm sorry*," she said. Into her hands. "*I'm sorry. I'm sorry. I knew. She told me. She sat on my bed and told me and I knew and I did it anyway—*"

*[...I know...]* said Mal.

"*Nine hundred years*," said Rhosyn. "*Nine hundred years you were hungry. You were empty and starving and hungry in the dark. Because of us. Because of me.*"

*[...I know...]*

"*And I've had you*," said the Thorn-Crowned. "*All that time. In me. What I drank. I've had it the whole time.*" She took her hands from her face. Looked down into the dark. Her face was ruined. Wet. Grey. "*I want to give it back.*"

---

The pool was very quiet.

Then—

*[...will it hurt...]* said Mal. *[...you...]*

Rhosyn stared at it.

*[...the iron one...]* said the Well. Faintly. *[...when he came home...it was cold...and heavy...and he was gone...]* A ripple. *[...I don't want you to be gone...you cried...]*

---

Seren felt it go through all of them.

Antho. Seraphine. Pim. Hask. Kit. Ivy. Even the dead, round them, swaying.

The Well that had been drunk dry. Starved for nine hundred years. Hungry in the dark. Asking the one who drank it if giving it back would hurt.

*I don't want you to be gone*.

Rhosyn made a sound. Not a word. A sound like something breaking.

"*I don't know*," she said. "*I don't know if it'll hurt.*" She looked at Seren. Beside her. On the rim. "*Hes didn't die. She poured hers back and walked away and lived. But she'd only had it a moment. One mouthful. I've had it nine hundred years.*" Her voice shook. "*I don't know what happens.*"

"*Nobody does*," said Seren.

Rhosyn looked at her.

"*Will you*—" she said. And stopped. And started again. "*Will you hold my hand? While I do it?*"

---

Seren thought of a gold king kneeling on a gold floor in an old woman's cottage. With her son beside him holding the room open with his bare hand. Shaking. For an hour.

*Sit with me, lad. When it's time*.

She took her great-aunt's hand.

Grey. Thin. Cold as a branch in winter. She held it in her own brown square one. On the rim. And didn't let go.

"*Yes*," she said.

---

Rhosyn turned back to the pool.

She knelt there. With one hand in Seren's. And the other—

She lifted it. Her free hand. And held it out. Over the water. Palm down. Cupped. The way you'd cup your hand to drink from a stream.

The way she'd cupped it nine hundred years ago.

Only upside down.

---

"*Hes*," she said. To the dark water. Very quietly. "*I'm sorry it took so long.*"

And opened her hand.

---

Seren felt it go.

Through the hand in hers. Through the thin grey fingers. A *pull*. Not out of Seren. Out of Rhosyn. Out of the very middle of her. Like a tide going out over a mile of sand. Like water going out of a cracked jar. Slow. Steady. Endless.

And out of the cupped hand over the pool—

Light.

Not gold. Not silver. *Green*. Soft. Dim. Like light through leaves. Like the first green of spring through a window. It came out of Rhosyn's open palm in a thin bright thread and fell. Into the dark water. And didn't splash. Didn't ripple. Just went *in*. Down. Like rain into dry ground.

And kept coming.

---

It went on for a long time.

Seren didn't know how long. She knelt on the rim with her great-aunt's hand in hers and watched the green light pour out of Rhosyn's palm into the pool. A thread. Then a stream. Then more. Pouring. The way water pours out of a jug when you tip it all the way.

Nine hundred years of it.

And Rhosyn—

---

Seren watched her change.

She didn't want to watch. She couldn't not. She knelt beside her with their hands joined and watched it happen.

The grey went out of her.

Not all at once. From the edges in. Like colour coming back into a face after a faint. Her hands first. The thin grey fingers in Seren's going—not pink. Not pale. *Brown*. Weathered. A gardener's hands. With dirt in the creases that had been there nine hundred years and was suddenly real dirt again.

Then her wrists. Her arms. Her neck. Her face.

The grey hair, lank and lifeless round her face—it moved. Lifted. In a wind that was suddenly there, from the east, warm. And it wasn't grey anymore. It was brown. Dark brown. Shot through with grey—real grey, the grey of a woman of forty who's worked in the sun all her life. Thick. Curling at the ends.

And her face.

Thin still. Hollow-cheeked still. But not the hollow of something starved and stopped. The hollow of a woman who's been very ill and is getting better. Lines round the eyes. Laughter lines. Lines from squinting at the sun in a garden. Freckles—Seren saw them come, a scatter across the nose, the way they come on a child in summer.

And the eyes.

Still green. Still the greenest thing in the circle.

But *wet*. And alive. And looking down at her own hands in wonder.

---

The light stopped.

The last of it went out of her palm. A thread. Then a drop. Then nothing.

Rhosyn knelt on the rim with her hand open over the water. Empty.

She was breathing hard. Like someone who's run up a hill.

She turned her head and looked at Seren.

"*Oh*," she said. And her voice was different. Lower. Rougher. A real voice. With breath in it. "*Oh. I'm tired.*"

And she laughed.

---

Seren had never heard anything like it.

It came up out of her great-aunt like water out of a spring. Rough. Cracked. Astonished. A woman of forty laughing on her knees by a pool with dirt in the creases of her hands. Laughing because she was tired. Because she'd never been tired in nine hundred years. Because tired meant something was happening. Meant time.

Meant *new*.

---

And her crown—

Seren saw it. On Rhosyn's head. The crown of thorns. Dead. Brown. Dry. Crooked.

A bud.

At the front. Where the broken place had been. Where the last green thing she had had grown, and been spent, and gone. A bud. Green. Pushing out of the raw pale scar.

And another. Beside it.

And another.

All round. On every stem. Every thorn. Green buds pushing out of the dead brown wood. Swelling. Splitting.

*Opening*.

---

Roses.

Red. Dark red. Nearly black at the heart. One. Then ten. Then all of them. All round the crown. Blooming. In a breath. The dead thorns hidden under a ring of dark red flowers, petals falling, on her brown hair, on her shoulders, on the rim.

On the water.

Rhosyn reached up. Slowly. And touched them. The roses. On her own head.

Then she took the crown off.

And looked at it. In her brown gardener's hands. A ring of thorns, blooming.

And set it down.

On the stone. On the rim of the pool. Beside her knee.

The way a girl had set hers down nine hundred years ago, in a hidden garden, at the edge of a black pool, while her brothers and sisters knelt round it laughing.

And took her hands away.

And didn't look at it again.

---

*[...OH...]*

---

The pool *moved*.

Not a ripple. Not a shiver. A *heave*. The whole dark disc of it lifting, like a chest drawing a great breath, rising up against the rim, against their knees, cold, soft, black—and settling. And the grey ring round the edge—

Gone.

Seren saw it go. The hand's width of grey frost round the black. Shrinking. Fading. Like breath fading off a window. Gone. And the black water *bigger*. Not much. A yard. More. Spreading out past the old rim, over the stone, soft and dark, so that Ivy had to snatch her blanket back and Hask stepped away with a grunt.

*[...I'm BIGGER...]* said Mal.

Not a whisper. A *voice*. Young. Astonished. Like a child who's just found out it can whistle.

*[...I'm bigger...I can feel it...there's ROOM...]*

---

Ivy was crying.

Kit was crying. Seraphine had her spectacles off and her hand over her mouth. Pim was kneeling on the rim with his hands cupped and his face turned up.

Antho was standing very still.

Seren looked at him. And saw his face. And saw what he was looking at.

Behind them. Round them. On every side.

The dead.

---

They were leaning.

All of them. Thousands. In the grey grass. Every grey column, swaying, leaning toward the pool. Toward the black water that was bigger now. Toward the room.

And the grey grass under them—

Seren saw it go. From the rim out. Like a stain washing out of cloth. Grey to green. A yard. Ten yards. Out from the new edge of the pool. Through the feet of the dead. The grass coming back. Green. Real. Wet with rain. Clover. A daisy, closed, in the grey light.

Not all the way. Not to the lantern ring. Not even halfway. But *back*.

---

*[...brother...]* said Mal.

Antho looked down at the water.

*[...brother...there's room...not for all of them...not yet...but some...some...]* The voice was shaking. With joy. With fear. *[...can you...will you...the door...]*

Antho closed his eyes.

Seren saw him do it. Saw his face change. The way it changed at the black door, at the bottom of the Hollowmaw, when he raised his fist and knocked. The Threshold Warden. The man who stood in doorways and decided.

He opened his eyes.

And turned round. To the dead.

And held out his hand.

---

"*Corvin*," he said.

---

Seren didn't know the name.

She felt it go out from him, though. Into the dead. A word. Spoken quietly. With something behind it she'd never heard in her son's voice. Not command. Not asking. *Opening*. Like a door swinging wide.

And one of the columns moved.

Not grey. *Bronze*. Seren saw it now. Among the grey. Taller than the others. The colour of old bronze, dull and warm. Swaying. Stepping forward. Out of the crowd. Toward Antho's hand.

"*Idris*," said Antho.

Another. Pale. The colour of old bone. Stepping out.

"*Taddeo*."

Copper. Red-brown. Like a kettle by a fire.

"*Merrow*."

And the last. Coral. Pink and white and pale orange, like the inside of a shell. Stepping out of the grey dead and standing with the others, in the green grass, at the edge of the pool.

The four cousins.

Who came apart against the edge of the new net nine hundred years ago, on the night the boat went through, because they'd drunk the least and the net didn't know them and pushed them till they broke.

---

Rhosyn made a sound.

Seren turned. Her great-aunt was on her knees on the rim, with her brown hands pressed flat on the stone, staring at the coral column.

"*Merrow*," she said.

The coral dust swayed.

"*Merrow*," said Rhosyn. "*Oh. Oh, it's you. It's been you. All this time. At the edge.*" She was shaking. "*I didn't know. I didn't know which one you were. I couldn't tell. In the dust. They all looked the same.*"

The column leaned toward her.

"*He was my husband*," said Rhosyn. To Seren. Without looking at her. Without looking away from the coral dust. "*For three months. Before the pool. He was a fool and he couldn't sing and he brought me shells. From the sea. Every week.*" She laughed. Or tried to. "*He drank because I drank. He said, if you're going to live forever, Rosie, I'm not letting you do it alone.*"

The coral column swayed. Close. A yard off.

"*And the net broke him*," said Rhosyn. "*At the edge. On the first night. And I watched.*"

---

She got up.

Slowly. Stiffly. Seren helped her. A woman of forty, tired, with dirt on her hands and petals in her hair. She stood on the green grass at the edge of the pool.

And held out her hand.

To the coral dust.

It came. Close. Closer. And for a moment—just a moment, Seren thought, she'd never be sure—there was a shape in it. A man. Young. Tall. Laughing. With something in his hand. Small. Pink and white. A shell.

He held it out.

Rhosyn didn't take it. You couldn't take dust.

She held out her own hand. Brown. Open. Under his.

And the shape in the coral dust leaned down. And put its hand on hers. Just for a moment.

Then turned.

---

"*Go home*," said Antho. Quietly. To all four. "*There's room now. For you. Go home.*"

The bronze went first.
