# The Hinge

"*Shut your eyes*," said Antho.

His brother shut his eyes.

They were standing at the end of the cave at the Hollowmaw. At the very back. Past all one thousand one hundred and thirteen lanterns, burning on nothing, gold and small, from the mouth to here. At the black door.

It was taller than a man and wider than a cart and made of nothing. Black. Smooth. Cold. Like a slab of night set into the rock. For thirteen years it had been shut, with their mother on the far side of it, holding it closed against a starving Well. Then their father had sat in front of it for three years with a lamp. Then Antho had knocked, and it had opened, and they'd gone down into the deep and brought her home.

It was shut again now. Not locked. *Shut*. The way a door is shut in a house where everyone's asleep.

On the hook beside it, the last three lanterns. Clay. Iron. Tin.

"*Are they shut*?" said Antho.

"*Yes*."

"*Properly*?"

"*Yes*, *Antho.*"

"*Then why are you squinting*?"

A pause.

"*Because I don't like it*," said Lex. "*I don't like not seeing.*"

"*I know*," said Antho. "*That's why we're here.*"

---

It had been four days since the Stones.

Four days since Posy Tull and her blue dress with the lace at the cuffs. Four days since thirty-one children all over Caldor put their hands on the glass and saw their own faces. Four days since the Keepers started wrapping Stones in sacking and chains, and Seraphine started sending ravens, and the Queen sent word from Highcrest that no Kindling was to happen anywhere in the kingdom until further notice.

Four days since Lex came down the loft ladder at dawn with his eyes red-rimmed and his hands black at the fingertips, scorched, where he'd held fire in them all night with nowhere to put it.

He'd told Antho about the panel. In the forge. In a flat quiet voice, with his back turned, hammering a nail that didn't need hammering.

*Hello, Smith*.

*Your daughter's hands are very square*.

And his own face. Crowned. With every thread in the world coming out of his head like the rays of the sun.

*I wonder if you'd give it away again*.

---

"*Breathe*," said Antho.

"*I'm breathing*."

"*You're not*. *You're holding it and letting it out in little bits so I won't notice.*" Antho put his hand flat on his brother's chest. Over the bell. Over the place under the breastbone. "*Breathe. All the way. Like Teo's sea.*"

Lex breathed.

Antho felt it go in. All the way. And out.

"*Again*."

In. Out.

"*Again*."

In. Out. Slower. Antho felt the heart under his hand. Hammering. Then less.

"*Good*," said Antho. And knocked on the door.

---

He didn't use his fist. Not this time. He used two fingers. The way their father used to tap on a table. *Tap*. *Tap*.

The door listened.

He felt it listen. The way you feel a dog listen in the dark, its ears coming up. The black slab of night in front of him, cold, smooth, attending.

*Who is it*? said the door. Not in words.

*Antho*, he said. Not in words. *The Warden. And my brother.*

*Where to*?

*Down*, said Antho. *Not all the way. Not to the deep. To the dark. The starlit one. Where Shade lives.*

The door considered this.

Then it opened.

---

Not wide. A crack. A slit of deeper dark in the dark, the width of a man. And through it, cold. Not the grey cold of the Ferryman's river. Not the white cold of the Stones. A *soft* cold. Like a cellar. Like a well at night. Like the hour before dawn in midsummer, when everything's still and the stars are out and nothing's moving and nothing needs to.

Antho took his brother's hand.

"*Keep them shut*," he said. "*All the way down. Don't open them till I say.*"

"*Antho*—"

"*Trust me*," said Antho Vey.

And led him through.

---

Down.

Not stairs. Not a slope. Just *down*. The way you go down in a dream, without your feet doing anything. The dark closing over them like water. The lanterns of the Keep going away behind them, a long way up, small and gold, like the lights of a village seen from a mountain at night. Then gone.

Antho didn't need his eyes here.

He never had. Not in the mad years, when he'd come down here alone with a voice in his head that wanted to eat the world. Not in the Wellworlds, or the deep, or the bottom of the world. The dark wasn't dark to him. It never had been. It was just another kind of room.

He could feel his brother's hand in his. Hot. Burned. A smith's hand. Gripping too hard.

"*It's all right*," he said.

"*I can't see anything*."

"*Your eyes are shut*."

"*I can't see anything through my eyelids*," said Lex. "*There's always something. Light. Red. Something. There's nothing.*"

"*I know*," said Antho. "*That's the point.*"

---

They stopped.

Antho felt them stop. Not hit the ground. There wasn't any ground. Just—*stopped*. The way a leaf stops when it's fallen to the bottom of a still pool.

He let go of his brother's hand.

Lex grabbed for it. Antho stepped back. Out of reach.

"*Antho*—"

"*Open your eyes*."

---

He heard his brother open his eyes.

He knew what he'd see. He'd seen it a hundred times. A thousand. The first time, at seventeen, a year after the fire, he'd lain on his back in it for three days and cried until he couldn't anymore.

Stars.

Not overhead. Everywhere. Above and below and on every side. Small and white and very far off, scattered across the black like frost on a window, like salt on a slate, like sparks going up a chimney and stopping, all at once, forever. Millions of them. No moon. No sun. No ground. No sky. Just the dark, soft and deep and endless, and the stars hanging in it.

And silence.

Not the silence of the grey snow at Westerling, where things had stopped. A *living* silence. The silence between heartbeats. The silence in a room after a song ends, before anyone claps. Full. Waiting.

Shade's dark.

The Well of absence. Of hush. Of the space between.

---

Lex didn't say anything for a long time.

Antho watched him. Hanging in the dark a yard off, turning slowly, his head going back, his mouth open, the starlight on his face. On the handprint. On his father's eyes.

"*Oh*," said Lex.

"*Yes*."

"*It's*—" He stopped. "*Antho, it's beautiful.*"

"*I know*."

"*You never said*."

"*You never asked*," said Antho. "*You've been coming down here for three years. Through me. Through the Bloodbond. Answering. And every time, you came down with your eyes screwed shut and your hands in fists, like a boy in a cellar.*" He shrugged. In the dark. "*You never once looked.*"

---

Lex turned. Slowly. In the starlight. Looking.

"*It doesn't feel like Shade*," he said.

"*What does Shade feel like*?"

"*Cold*. *Quiet. The thing that takes the heat out of things.*" He looked at his hands. "*The opposite of me.*"

"*It's not the opposite of you*," said Antho. "*It's the room you're in.*"

Lex looked at him.

"*Hold up your hand*," said Antho. "*Make a light.*"

---

Lex held up his hand. And the fire came.

Small. He made it small. A flame in his palm the size of a candle-flame. White-gold. Steady.

It lit the dark.

A little. A circle. A yard across. His hand. His face. His coat. Antho, at the edge of it. And past that—

The stars.

Still there. Still shining. Small and white and far. Not drowned out. Not washed away by the flame. Just *there*. Round it. On every side.

"*Look at it*," said Antho.

Lex looked at the flame in his hand.

"*Now look at the dark round it*," said Antho.

Lex looked.

"*That's Shade*," said Antho. "*Not the cold. Not the quiet. Not the thing that puts the fire out.*" He came closer. Into the circle of light. "*The dark round the flame. That lets you see it. If the whole world were fire, you'd never see a candle.*"

---

Lex looked at the flame for a long time.

"*A lantern*," he said slowly.

"*A lantern's just a dark box with a light in it*," said Antho. "*A forge is a dark room with a fire in it. A window at night is a light in the dark, so someone coming home can find it.*" He looked at his brother. "*You've been making lanterns since you were eleven. You've hung one in every window at Harrow. You cut HERE in the base of one and TOO LATE in another.*" A pause. "*Every one of them's half Shade, Lex. You just never noticed the half that wasn't fire.*"

---

They sat.

There wasn't anything to sit on. They sat anyway. In the dark. Hanging in it. Cross-legged, side by side, with the stars all round them and the little flame in Lex's palm making its circle of light.

Antho didn't say anything.

He'd learned that. From their father, in the last year. From Pim, for seven years. From Seraphine, every day. Sometimes the best thing to say was nothing. Sometimes the best thing to do was sit beside someone in the dark and let them find what they'd come down for.

So he sat.

---

"*I saw myself*," said Lex. After a long time. "*In the panel. Crowned.*"

"*You told me*."

"*I wanted it*."

Antho didn't move.

"*Just for a breath*," said Lex. Very low. Looking at the flame. "*I saw my face with the net coming out of it. Every thread. And I thought—I could fix it. All of it. Wren's hand. Brannoc's. The bread. The boy in the field. The dead at the rim.*" He swallowed. "*I could reach down a thread and find a flame in a lantern and make it remember me.*"

"*I know*."

"*How do you know*?"

"*Because I'd want it too*," said Antho. "*I want it now. Just hearing you say it.*" He looked at his left hand. At the coin of gold on the back of it. In the starlight. "*I want it every time I look at this.*"

---

Lex looked at the coin.

"*Does it hurt*?"

"*No*. *It doesn't do anything. That's the worst of it.*" Antho turned his hand over. Back again. "*It's a place in me that's stopped. A door that won't open. I'm the Threshold Warden. I open doors. I opened the deep. I opened a breach a hundred miles wide by asking.*" He laughed. Short. "*I can't open a coin of gold on my own hand.*"

"*I'm sorry*."

"*Don't*," said Antho. "*Don't be sorry. Wren's right. You're always sorry.*" He looked at his brother. "*You looked at her fingers every day for six months like you'd put the gold there yourself. You look at this the same way. You look at Brannoc's. At Hask's arm.*" He shook his head. "*You didn't do it, Lex. He did. Orrin did. The grey country did. Nine hundred years of people did.*"

"*I know*."

"*Do you*?"

Lex didn't answer.

---

They sat.

The flame in Lex's hand burned. Small. Steady. The stars hung round them.

"*I said no*," said Lex at last. "*In the loft. To the face. To the crown. I didn't think it. I felt it. Under here.*" He touched his chest. "*All the Wells at once. No.*"

"*Good*."

"*But I don't know if I'd say it again*." His voice was very quiet. "*If she showed me again. If I had to watch Lark put her hand on a Stone and see her own face. If the grey came up the Vale and over the hill and into the yard.*" He looked at the flame. "*If it was that or the crown.*"

Antho didn't say anything.

"*Father said no*," said Lex. "*For two hundred years. He had the Root. He could have done anything. And he said no, every day, and hid it, and got old.*" He swallowed. "*Elric said no. At the bottom of the world. With the whole net offering him everything. Not me. Not ever.*"

"*He had Hesper's crown on*," said Antho. "*It said it for him.*"

"*He said it too*. *I heard him.*"

"*I know*," said Antho. "*I was there.*"

---

"*I don't have a crown that says no*," said Lex. "*I just have me.*"

He looked at the flame. At the dark round it.

"*And Father's gone*," he said. "*And Ember's gone. And Elric. And everyone who ever said no and meant it.*" His voice cracked. "*There's nobody left to show me how.*"

---

Antho looked at him.

At his little brother. Twenty-six. Burned. Grey-eyed. Holding a flame in his palm in the middle of the dark, with the stars all round him, saying *there's nobody left*.

He thought about a boy of eleven in a burning cottage. Screaming. *Antho*. *Antho*. With fire in the thatch and a door in the dark that Antho's broken power had torn open, and their mother walking into it.

He'd run. That night. Into the Greyspine. Mad. With a voice in his head. And left his little brother in the ashes.

For eight years.

---

"*Look at the dark*," said Antho.

"*What*?"

"*Not the flame*. *The dark. Round it.*" He leaned in. "*Look.*"

Lex looked.

"*What do you see*?"

"*Stars*."

"*Look closer*."

---

Lex looked closer.

Antho watched him do it. Watched him narrow his eyes. Watched him lean forward, past the little flame, into the dark beyond its circle. Watched him go still.

He'd seen it himself. The first time. At seventeen. Lying on his back for three days.

The stars weren't all stars.

---

Most of them were. Small and white and far. Scattered across the black like frost.

But between them, here and there—scattered too, but fewer, much fewer—there were places where there were no stars.

Not darker. The dark was all the same dark. Just—*gaps*. Holes in the scatter of light. Small round places where a star should have been and wasn't. As if someone had reached into the frost on the window and pressed a fingertip against it, and left a clear round print.

Shapes.

---

"*What are they*?" said Lex. Barely a whisper.

"*What do you think they are*?"

Lex didn't answer. He was looking at one. Close. A yard off. Just past the edge of the flame's light. A gap in the stars. Round. The size of a man's head. Or—

Not round.

The size of a man. The shape of a man. A tall thin man, sitting. In a chair. With his hands folded. A gap in the stars exactly that shape.

---

"*That's Father*," said Lex.

"*Yes*."

"*Antho*—"

"*It's where he was*," said Antho. "*That's all. Not him. He's gone. He went with the Ferryman. On the boat.*" He looked at the gap. At the shape of a man in a chair, made of no stars. "*It's the place he left. In the dark. When he went.*"

---

Lex was shaking.

The flame in his hand guttered. Antho saw it. Saw it shrink. Go blue at the edges.

"*Don't*," said Antho. "*Don't let it go out. Hold it.*"

"*I can't*—"

"*Hold it*. *And look.*"

---

Lex held it.

He looked at the gap in the stars. At the shape. His father. Sitting. Hands folded. The way he'd sat in the wheeled chair by the west window at Harrow every morning for a year, with a cup of tea going cold beside him and his grey eyes on the birch wood.

The way he'd sat at the Spire, at his great desk, drawing the world from the inside with a pendulum.

The way he'd sat at the Hollowmaw, in front of the black door, for three years, with a lamp.

"*It's the same shape*," said Lex. Wondering. Hoarse. "*It's exactly the same. It's—*"

"*It's his shape*," said Antho. "*The shape he made in the world. In the dark. By being in it.*" He looked round. At the other gaps. Scattered. Few. Here and there among the stars. "*Everyone leaves one. When they go. A place where they were.*"

---

He showed him.

The others. Moving through the dark, the two of them, slowly, with the little flame in Lex's palm making its circle of light. Past the gaps.

A big one. Huge. The shape of a giant, kneeling. *Dorran*. Who'd carried the weight of the Wellworld of Stone for them and died with Ilse and Ivo's forgiveness in his ears.

A square one. Broad-shouldered. A man with a sword, standing. *Garrick*. In the breach at Duskhold. Seven years ago.

A small one. Very small. A little bent man, barefoot, laughing—you could see the laugh, somehow, in the shape of the gap, in the tilt of the head. *Malachi*.

A woman with a staff. *Kell*.

A man in a cloak, with a bird on his shoulder. Grey. The gap faintly grey at its edges, the way the cloak had been. *Elric*.

And one more. Very small. Just a bump in the stars. Round. Like a coal. Like an ember.

---

Lex stopped.

He hung in the dark in front of it with the flame in his palm and looked at it.

A small round gap in the stars. The size of a coal. The size of a piece of a man the size of an ember, pressed into a sleeping boy's blood like a maker's mark.

"*That's*—"

"*Yes*."

"*But it's not gone*," said Lex. "*It's not dead. It's in the lantern. In the Ferryman's—*"

"*It's not here*," said Antho. "*It used to be here. In you. And now it isn't.*" He looked at the little gap. "*That's all an absence is, Lex. A place where something used to be.*"

---

Lex looked at it for a long, long time.

Then he did something Antho didn't expect.

He reached out.

With his free hand. His right. Not the one with the flame. He reached out into the dark, past the edge of the light, toward the little round gap in the stars. And put his fingertips against it.

Against nothing. Against the shape of a coal.

And held them there.

---

"*It's a mould*," said Lex Vey.

---

Antho didn't understand.

"*It's a mould*," said Lex again. Louder. Wondering. He was looking at his fingertips. At the gap. "*Antho. Look. It's the shape of it. Exactly. Every edge. Like a mould is the shape of what you're going to cast. Like a hollow in sand.*"

He turned. In the dark. With the flame in one hand and the other still out, touching the little gap.

"*You make a mould*," he said. Fast. The way he talked about iron. "*You press the thing into sand. Or clay. You take it out. And there's a hole. A gap. The exact shape of what was there.*" He looked at the gap. "*And then you pour. Fire. Metal. Whatever you're making. And it fills the gap. And it takes the shape.*"

"*Lex*—"

"*That's what these are*," said Lex. He looked round. At all the gaps. Dorran. Garrick. Malachi. Kell. Elric. His father. "*They're not just holes. They're moulds. The shape of everyone who was here. In the dark. Kept. Not kept. Held.*" His voice cracked. "*So you can see what they were. So you can make something in their shape.*"

---

Antho stared at him.

He'd come down here for seven years. He'd lain in the dark for three days at seventeen and looked at the gaps in the stars and cried until he couldn't. He'd come back a hundred times since and floated among them and thought *that's where they were*. *That's what's left*.

He'd never once thought *mould*.

---

"*Every new thing needs somewhere to be*," said Lex. Quietly now. "*That's Hollow. That's room. Wren taught me. A womb's a room. A mould's a room.*" He looked at the little gap under his fingertips. "*But a mould's a dark room, Antho. It's always dark. It has to be. You can't cast anything in the light. You cast it in the sand, in the dark, in the shape of something that was there and isn't.*"

He looked at his brother.

"*Shade's not the opposite of fire*," said Lex Vey. "*Shade's the mould.*"

---

The dark changed.

Antho felt it. All round them. The stars. The silence. The soft endless cold. It didn't move. It didn't brighten. Nothing so obvious.

It *listened*.

The way the door at the Keep had listened when he tapped on it. The way Ashka listened when he whistled. The whole of Shade's dark—the Well of absence, of hush, of the space between—turning toward his brother.

Attending.

---

Lex closed his hand.

Not on the gap. You couldn't close a hand on nothing. He closed it round the flame. The other one. The small white-gold fire in his left palm. Folded his fingers over it.

And the light went out.

---

Dark. All round. Total. The stars. The gaps. His brother, a yard off, invisible now.

Antho waited.

He heard Lex breathe. In. Out. In the dark. Slow. The way he'd breathed at the door, with Antho's hand on his chest. Like Teo's sea.

*Out*. *And back*.

And then—

---

Light.

Not from Lex's hand. From *everywhere*. From the gaps.

Antho saw it. He saw it and didn't believe it. All round them, in the dark, in the scatter of stars—the gaps. The shapes. The places where people had been. Dorran. Garrick. Malachi. Kell. Elric. Their father.

*Glowing*.

Faintly. Gold. From inside. The way a mould glows when the metal's just been poured. The way sand glows round a casting in the dark of a foundry. Each shape filled, for a moment, with a soft warm light, exactly its own shape, every edge. A giant kneeling. A man with a sword. A little laughing barefoot man. A woman with a staff. A man with a bird.

A man in a chair. With his hands folded.

And a small round coal.

---

It lasted a breath.

Then it faded. Gently. The gold going out of the shapes the way heat goes out of iron. Back to dark. Back to gaps in the stars.

But they weren't just gaps anymore.

Antho could see it now. He'd never seen it before. He'd never *looked*. Every one of them had an edge. A clean, sharp, careful edge. Like the inside of a mould. Like the hollow a hand leaves in clay. Shaped. Kept—not kept. *Held*. By the dark. So that whatever had been there could be seen, and remembered, and—

Made again. In its shape.

He stared at the panel. Silver-blue. Hanging in the dark in front of him where only he could see it.

Antho could see it. Through the Bloodbond. Faintly. The way you see a candle through a curtain.

*Commanding*.

Two stages. In one breath. In the dark. With his hand out to a gap in the stars the shape of an ember.

---

"*You skipped one*," said Antho. His voice wasn't steady.

Lex looked at him.

"*Speaking*," said Antho. "*You went straight through it. Nobody does that. Malachi said nobody does that. You have to stay at each one. Seasons. Years. You have to—*"

"*I had a good teacher*," said Lex.

Antho couldn't speak.

He hung in the dark with the stars all round him and his little brother in front of him, with a flame in his palm and wet on his face, and couldn't say anything at all.

---

He laughed.

He didn't mean to. It came out of him cracked and wet and sudden, the way their mother's laugh came out. He put his scarred hands over his face and laughed into them.

"*What*?" said Lex.

"*Nothing*," said Antho. Through his fingers. "*Nothing. Showed someone the dark. That's all it says. That's the reason. Seven years down here, and that's what it gives me a level for.*"

"*It's a good reason*."

"*It's a terrible reason*," said Antho. "*It's the best reason I've ever had.*"

---

They went up.

Slowly. Through the dark. Hand in hand. Not because Lex needed leading. Because neither of them wanted to let go.

Past the stars. Past the gaps. Past the shape of a man in a chair.

Lex stopped, there. For a moment. And put his hand out. And touched the edge of it. The clean, careful edge of the place where their father had been.

He didn't say anything.

Then they went on.

---

The lanterns of the Keep came back overhead. Small and gold. One thousand one hundred and thirteen. Like a village seen from a mountain at night.

The black door. The crack of grey cave-light. The hook with the last three lanterns. Clay. Iron. Tin.

They stepped through.

And the door closed behind them. Gently. The way a door closes in a house where everyone's asleep.

---

Seraphine was waiting.

At the back of the cave. By the last lanterns. With her coat on and her spectacles pushed up on her head and a raven on her shoulder. Not Quill. One of the Keepers' birds. Black. Ruffled. Furious at being indoors.

Her face was white.

"*How long*?" said Antho.

"*A day*," said Seraphine. "*And a night. You've been gone a day and a night.*" She looked at Lex. At his face. At whatever was in it. "*Did it—*"

"*Yes*," said Lex.

"*Commanding*?"

"*Commanding*."

She closed her eyes. Just for a moment. Then opened them.

"*Good*," she said. "*Good. Because this came. Two hours ago.*" She held out a paper. Small. Tied with black thread. "*From Hask.*"

---

Antho took it.

Hask's hand. Big. Square. Pressing too hard. A miner's hand, that had learned its letters late and never liked them.

Five lines.

---

*Grey's spread. A mile out from the rim now. All round.*

*Grass is grey under the snow. The new river's stopped. Not frozen. Stopped.*

*Kit went to fetch water from it this morning and the bucket came up and the water in it didn't move.*

*Mal's stopped talking. Ivy's been on the rim two days. Won't come in. Says she can't hear it anymore.*

*Come.*

---

Antho read it twice.

He looked at his brother.

Lex was looking at the paper. At the five lines. At *Mal's stopped talking*.

He didn't say anything.

He didn't reach for fire. He didn't clench his hands. He didn't turn away.

He looked at the paper. And at the lanterns. All of them. One thousand one hundred and thirteen. Burning on nothing, gold and small, from the black door to the mouth of the cave.

Lights in the dark.

Every one of them half Shade.

"*Get Mam*," said Lex Vey. "*It's time she taught me Radiance.*"
