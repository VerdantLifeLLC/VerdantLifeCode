# Seven Wells

The Forge Academy at Ashbourne had twenty anvils, and on the sixth day after Midwinter every one of them had somebody sitting on it.

Lex stood in the doorway and looked at them.

The great hall of the Forge. Long and high and smoke-dark, with a slate roof on oak trusses and the snow sliding off it in slabs. Twenty hearths down the two long walls, banked low. Twenty anvils in front of them, black and battered. Racks of tools. Water barrels, every one of them lidded now. A smell of coal and iron and wet wool and too many people.

And on the anvils, and on the benches, and on upturned buckets and coal sacks and the edge of the great quench-trough at the far end—

Everyone.

---

Queen Lysandra of Caldor sat on the anvil nearest the main hearth. In a grey travelling cloak with the hood pushed back and snow melting in her dark hair. Thirty now. Sharp-faced. With her grandfather's nose and her own eyes, which missed nothing. She'd ridden down from Highcrest in four days through the drifts with a dozen guards and Seraphine's letter in her saddlebag, and she looked it.

Beside her, on a coal sack, cross-legged, in a red coat with the sleeves singed, eating an apple—

"*You look terrible*," said Kestrel Varn.

"*Thank you*."

"*It wasn't a compliment*." She took another bite. "*You've lost weight. You've got that look. The one where you haven't slept since the moon was full and you think nobody's noticed.*" She chewed. "*Everyone's noticed.*"

"*Hello, Kestrel*."

"*Hello, smith*." She looked at him over the apple. Dark-eyed. Sardonic. Fire-witch of the Unlit, who'd crossed the grey river with him, and walked the Wellworld of Flame, and learned black heat in the deep. Who'd been at Harrow on Lantern Night with her fire. "*I'm sorry about your father.*"

She'd said it at the grave too. On Lantern Night. In the snow. She said it again now. Exactly the same. Plainly. Not looking away.

He found he didn't mind.

---

He knew all of them.

Brannoc, on his own anvil, the first one, the one he'd swung Lex up onto at eleven. Arms folded. Gold fingertips tucked into his armpits. Tilda beside him on a bucket, twenty years old and square-built and soot-faced, with Brannoc's old hammer in her belt and her right hand in her lap. Gold. All of it. From the wrist to the nails. She'd learned to swing left-handed in the summer. She was better at it now than most people were with their right.

Hedda, the rune-carver from Thistledown, grey-plaited, with her chisels in a roll on her knees. Teo Brask, the Tide Speaker, down from the coast—eighteen now, gangling, with his hair dripping as it always dripped, though it hadn't rained in a week and he'd come four hundred miles. A puddle spreading round his boots on the flags. Grandmother Asha of the Ashari, very small, very old, wrapped in desert silks and three Vale blankets, sitting on the edge of the quench-trough with her bare brown feet over the cold water as if it were a hot spring.

Wren, by the wall. With Lark on her hip. Lark was asleep, for once, with her fist in her mouth and her other fist round Wren's braid.

His mother. On a bench near the door. In her grey shawl. With her hands folded.

Seraphine. Standing. At the far end. By the great hearth. With a table in front of her that she'd dragged there herself, and on the table, books. Eleven of them. Lex counted. Open. Stacked. Bristling with paper markers. And in the middle, the biggest, very old, bound in cracked brown leather with a clasp of tarnished silver.

The Listeners' Codex.

And beside her, leaning on the wall with his arms folded and his long black hair tied back and the face he wore when he was very frightened and pretending to be bored—

Antho.

He'd come in from the east that morning on the dragon. Lex hadn't had time to speak to him. He looked across the hall now and met Lex's eyes.

*Mal*? thought Lex.

His brother shook his head. Slightly. *The same*.

---

"*Well*," said the Queen of Caldor. "*Now we're all here.*"

She didn't stand up. She didn't need to. Lysandra had never needed to stand up to make a room look at her.

"*I've had sixty-one letters since Midwinter*," she said. "*From the Westmarch. From lords and reeves and Keepers and a great many frightened farmers. I've read every one.*" She looked round the hall. "*They all say the same three things.*"

She held up a finger.

"*The gold ones are coming. Nine thousand of them. Walking east. Very slowly. Through everything. Nobody can stop them and nobody knows what they want.*"

A second finger.

"*The bread won't rise. The cuts won't close. The eggs won't hatch. And it's moving. East. Toward us.*"

A third.

"*And the Gilded King is walking through my kingdom drinking tea with old women and asking them how their grandmothers died.*" She lowered her hand. "*I'd like to know what to do about any of it. I'm told this is the room to ask.*"

---

Nobody said anything.

Then Seraphine cleared her throat.

---

She was terrified. Lex could see it. She'd been terrified of rooms full of people for as long as he'd known her—since a burned archive in the Thornwood, when she was a fugitive Warden scribe with ink on her fingers and a price on her head, and she'd stood up in front of Elric and Garrick and Lex and Antho and stammered out the truth about the Tithe.

She'd stood up in front of a lot of rooms since. She'd stood up in front of the Queen. She'd stood up in the deep in front of a Moth Queen and read Wellscript off the walls.

She was still terrified. Every time.

She did it anyway. That was the thing about Seraphine.

"*I'm going to start with the bread*," she said. "*Because it's the easiest. And because I think it's the same as the other two.*"

---

She talked for an hour.

Lex had heard most of it. At the rim. On the dragon. In letters. But he listened anyway, because she was good at it, because she put things in order the way a smith lays out tools, and because when she talked about something it stopped being a terror in the dark and became a thing you could pick up and turn over in your hands.

The grey country. A place the Crowned had emptied. The way they'd emptied the seventh Well. Nine hundred years with no room in it. No new thing. Nothing growing, nothing born, nothing changing. Not dead. *Empty*.

And empty is hungry.

The Veil had held it back. Not a wall, exactly. A seam. The edge of the exile. Woven into the net by Aurelian on the night he wove everything else, and tied into the First Knot at the bottom of the world, and holding, for nine hundred years, the line between a world full of room and a country with none.

And on Lantern Night, Elric Thorne had put on Hesper's crown and turned the Key of Shadows against it. And it had unwritten. And fallen. Like a cloth.

"*And now there's nothing*," said Seraphine. "*Between us and it. No edge at all. And it's doing what anything empty does when you open the door.*"

"*Drinking*," said Lex.

"*Drinking*." She looked at him. "*The room. Out of everything. The space where the next thing happens. That's why the bread won't rise. There's nowhere for it to rise into. That's why the boy's hand won't close. That's why the eggs—*" She stopped. Swallowed. "*It's not killing anything. It's just taking the room away. And without room—*"

"*Nothing new*," said his mother. Quietly. From the bench by the door.

Seraphine looked at her.

"*Nothing new*," she said.

---

"*How fast*?" said the Queen.

"*A mile a day*. *A little more. And faster every week.*" Seraphine turned a page in a notebook. Her own. Columns of figures in a tiny neat hand. "*Elowen's stakes. I've been charting them. It started slow—three hundred yards a day, the first week. It's been speeding up.*" She looked up. "*I think because the more it drinks, the stronger the pull. The way a fire draws harder the hotter it gets.*"

"*How long till here*?"

Seraphine looked at the figures for a long moment.

"*Midsummer*," she said. "*Perhaps a little after. If it keeps on as it is.*" She closed the notebook. "*Six months. Then Ashbourne. Then the Vale. Then Highcrest. Then—*"

She didn't finish.

---

Nobody said anything for a while.

Lark woke up. On Wren's hip. Looked round the hall at all the silent people. Took her fist out of her mouth.

"*No*," she said. Loudly. To nobody in particular.

Somebody laughed. Kestrel. Short and startled. Then Brannoc. Then half the hall. Not because it was funny. Because it was the only thing anyone had said in a minute that wasn't terrible.

Lark looked pleased with herself. Put her fist back in her mouth.

"*She's right*," said the Queen of Caldor. Dryly. "*No. We're not doing that. So—*" She looked at Seraphine. "*What do we do instead?*"

---

"*Two things*," said Seraphine. "*And then a third.*"

She put her hand on the Codex.

---

"*The first is the dead*," she said. "*At Mal's rim.*"

She told it. Antho told it, partly—he came off the wall and stood beside her and told the part about the names, and his voice didn't shake until the end. Thousands of them. The Outer Court's dead. The ones who came apart in the first years, with nothing to drink. Drifting east on the first wind, to the only room there was.

And the room too small.

*If I let them all in I'll stop*.

"*And the snow's grey where they stand*," said Antho. "*The grass under it too. They're not doing anything. They don't mean anything by it. But they've been in the grey country so long they've brought it with them. And Mal's frightened. And every week they wait, the grey round the rim gets a little wider.*"

"*So there's grey coming from the west*," said Kestrel. Slowly. "*And grey starting in the east.*"

"*Yes*."

"*Lovely*." She threw her apple core into the nearest hearth. It hissed. "*Go on. I can tell there's worse.*"

---

"*Mal grew*," said Seraphine. "*On Lantern Night. When Caddoc fell.*"

She told that too. The heavy cold piece of the Well coming home from the bottom of the world. *Hesper's*. And the room getting bigger. Like a breath.

"*They drank it*," she said. "*The Crowned. Nine hundred years ago. All nine. They drank the seventh Well dry, and it's still in them. That's what makes them deathless. That's why they can't make anything new. They're full of it.*" She looked round the hall. "*And Mal's empty. Or nearly. And the grey country's empty. And the dead have nowhere to go. All for the same reason.*"

"*And if they gave it back*—" said the Queen.

"*Then Mal would grow*," said Seraphine. "*Enough, maybe. Enough to take in the dead. Enough to—*" She hesitated. "*I don't know how much. Nobody's ever measured a Well. But Caddoc was one of nine, and the four cousins came apart at the edge nine hundred years ago, and Maelis poured hers home at the Blightmarch. So what's left is in three of them.*" She counted on her fingers. "*The King. The Glass Queen. The Thorn-Crowned.*"

"*And they're deathless*," said Lysandra.

"*Yes*."

"*So we can't take it from them*."

"*No*," said Seraphine. "*Nobody can. Caddoc stepped off the thread himself. Maelis came apart because she let herself be warm.*" She looked at Lex. "*It has to be given.*"

---

He felt the hall turn to look at him.

He'd said it. At the rim. *We ask.* Antho must have told them.

"*Hesper did*," he said. "*At the pool. She poured hers back. And it was hers, and she gave it, and nobody made her.*" He looked at the Queen. "*That's the only way it works. We can't take it. So we ask.*"

"*Ask the Gilded King*," said Lysandra. "*To stop being deathless. Politely.*"

"*Yes*."

"*And the Glass Queen*."

"*Yes*."

"*Who has spent the last six months reaching out of my subjects' windows at night to show them their own faces*." The Queen looked at him. "*And whom nobody has seen since the edge fell.*"

"*Yes*."

Lysandra was quiet for a moment.

"*Arion always said you were the most stubborn person he'd ever met*," she said. "*Including himself. Including me.*"

Lex didn't answer that. He couldn't.

---

"*And the second thing*?" said Brannoc. From his anvil. Gruffly. "*You said two things. Then a third.*"

Seraphine opened the Codex.

---

The pages crackled. Old vellum. Brown and soft as cloth, written over in Wellscript—the Founders' glyphs, gold-brown with age, crowding the margins in five or six different hands.

She turned to a page Lex knew.

Seven circles. Drawn in faded ink. Six in a ring, each with its glyph: Flame, Stone, Tide, Gale, Radiance, Shade. And in the middle, a seventh. Empty, once. Filled black now, with a glyph like a ring that doesn't quite close.

*Hollow*.

And in the margin, in a cramped old hand, slanting hard to the right, the hand of a man writing in anger:

*The one they drank. The one we caged. Forgive us.*

Malachi's.

And below it, in a different hand—neater, smaller, older, the ink almost gone—the line Seraphine had found in the summer:

*Unwrite it when there is someone on this side who can make room for what is on the other. Not before.*

Aurelian's.

---

"*He left the Veil one last turn*," said Seraphine. "*We knew that. We found it in the summer. And we used it. We had to.*" She touched the second line. "*Not before, he said. Not until there's someone who can make room. And there wasn't. Mal wasn't ready. Mal said so. Too big, still little.*" She swallowed. "*We did it anyway.*"

"*We didn't have a choice*," said Antho.

"*I know*. *I'm not—I know.*" She turned the page. Carefully. "*But I went back. After. In the autumn. To see if he'd left anything else. And he hadn't.*"

She put her finger on the next page.

"*But his brother had*."

---

It was on the back. On the reverse of the page with the seven circles. In the bottom corner, so small and faint and cramped that Lex had to lean over the table and squint.

The same hand as *Forgive us*. Slanting hard. Angry. Malachi's.

Two lines.

---

Seraphine read it aloud. Translating as she went. The way she always did. Slowly. Carefully. With her finger under each glyph.

"*Brother*," she read. "*You built a wall because a wall needs only one Well. Stone. A wall only has to say no.*"

She moved her finger.

"*A door needs all seven. It has to say no and yes both, and know which, and when. You could not hear all seven. No one can, yet. When there is a smith who can hear every one, let him make a door.*"

---

The hall was silent.

Lex stood at the table with his hands flat on it on either side of the old book and looked at the two lines in the corner of the page.

*A smith who can hear every one*.

He felt everyone in the hall look at him again.

He didn't look up.

---

"*A door*," said the Queen. Slowly. "*At the edge.*"

"*At the edge*," said Seraphine. "*Not a wall. Not a Veil. Aurelian's was a wall. Woven into the net. Tied into the Knot. Held with the Key.*" She shook her head. "*There's no Key anymore. It's in three pieces in a basket at Harrow. And even if there were—the net can't make a door. It's a net. It can only hold.*"

"*Then how*—"

"*Forged*," said Lex.

He looked up.

"*Not woven*," he said. "*Forged. Not out of the net. Out of the Wells.*" He looked down at the corner of the page. At the cramped angry glyphs. "*That's what he means. Malachi. He wasn't a weaver. He was a Listener. He never had the Root. He never touched the net. He thought his brother was wrong to build it.*" He swallowed. "*He's saying—don't weave a wall. Forge a door. Out of everything. All seven.*"

"*And what does a door do*," said Kestrel, "*that a wall doesn't? Apart from let things through. Which is rather the opposite of what we want.*"

---

"*It asks*," said Antho.

Everyone turned.

He was standing beside Seraphine. With his scarred hands on the table. Looking at the Codex. And his face had changed. Lex knew the look. He'd seen it in the deep, at the black door at the bottom of the Hollowmaw, when his brother raised his fist and *knocked*.

"*A wall doesn't ask*," said the Threshold Warden. "*It just stands there. Saying no. To everything. Forever. And everything piles up against it. The grey on one side. The room on the other. Pushing.*" He looked up. "*And when it comes down, it comes down all at once, and everything rushes through.*"

"*Like now*," said Seraphine.

"*Like now*." Antho turned his hands over. Palms up. "*A door's different. A door has a threshold. You come to it, and you knock, and it asks who's there, and what you want, and then it opens, or it doesn't.*" He looked at his brother. "*A door between the world and the grey wouldn't drink. Not all the time. Not everything. It would let the room through when someone asked. A little. On purpose. And close again.*"

"*And the dead*?" said his mother.

Antho looked at her.

"*The dead could come home through it*," he said. "*If there were somewhere for them to go.*"

"*Mal*," said Seraphine. Softly.

"*Mal*. *If Mal were big enough. A door at the edge, opening onto Mal's room.*" Antho shook his head. "*I don't know how. I don't know what that would look like. A door in the west opening onto a pool in the east. But that's what doors are, isn't it? That's what I am. A thing that stands in a doorway and decides.*" He looked down at his hands. "*Two places that shouldn't touch. And a way between them. That asks.*"

---

It was very quiet in the hall.

Then Grandmother Asha laughed.

---

She was sitting on the edge of the quench-trough with her bare feet in the cold water and her three blankets round her shoulders. Very small. Very old. Brown and wrinkled as a walnut. She'd stood in the Ashari ring on the night the Blightmarch bloomed and poured desert fire into the ground until her sister came apart into sparks beside her.

She laughed now. A dry crackling sound. Like twigs in a fire.

"*Seven*," she said. "*He says seven.*" She shook her head. "*Ninety years I have lived, and I have met three people who could hear two Wells at once. One of them is dead.*" She pointed a crooked finger. At Lex. "*And he wants all seven. From one smith.*"

"*I've got two*," said Lex. "*Commanding.*"

"*And the rest*?"

He didn't answer. He didn't need to. He reached for it.

*Status*.

---

He didn't read it out. He didn't need to read it out. He just held it up in front of himself in the firelight of the Forge, where only he could see it, and looked at it.

---

---

Two.

Seven years since a stranger walked into this town and the seal on his blood cracked open. Seven years of Malachi laughing at him in the Stillwater. Of the Wellworlds. Of the deep. Of the Blightmarch and the grey country and the bottom of the world.

Two Wells he could command. Three he could speak to. Two that would only answer.

*A smith who can hear every one*.

---

"*Two*," he said. Out loud. "*Three Speaking. Two Answering.*"

Grandmother Asha nodded slowly.

"*And how long*," she said, "*did the first two take you?*"

"*Seven years*."

"*And you have six months*."

---

Nobody said anything.

He closed the panel.

He looked at the corner of the page. At the two lines. At the cramped angry glyphs of a man who'd died singing in the deep with his arms round a starving Well.

*When there is a smith who can hear every one*.

"*Then I'd better start*," said Lex Vey.

---

The hall didn't cheer. Nobody cheered for anything anymore. But something changed in it. He felt it. The way a forge changes when the bellows go in and the coals come up from red to orange.

Teo Brask stood up. Dripping.

"*Tide*," he said. "*I can take you to Tide. The Undertow. It remembers you. It liked you. You kept your vow.*" He went red. "*It said that. In the autumn. It said, tell the smith he kept his vow.*"

"*Gale*," said Wren. From the wall. With Lark on her hip. "*The First Breath. I can take you. I've been.*" A pause. "*It'll want something. It always does.*"

"*Shade*," said Antho. "*Obviously. Me.*"

"*Radiance*," said his mother. Very quietly. From the bench by the door.

Everyone looked at her.

She didn't look up. She was looking at her folded hands. "*I gave it once. At the Heart. When you were forging the net. I was a lullaby in the dark and I didn't know what I was doing.*" She lifted her eyes. Brown. Steady. "*I know now. I can teach you. If you'll let me.*"

Lex looked at her for a long time.

"*Yes*," he said.

---

"*And Hollow*?" said Brannoc.

They all looked at the pool. Not the pool. There was no pool here. They looked east. Toward a green circle under snow, three hundred miles away.

"*Mal*," said Lex. "*Mal will teach me Hollow. When Mal's big enough to teach anyone anything.*" He looked down at the Codex. "*Which means the Crowned. Which means asking.*"

"*And the third thing*?" said the Queen. "*Seraphine said two things and then a third.*"

---

Seraphine looked at Seren.

Seren looked back.

"*The lanterns*," said Seren Harrow.

She stood up. Slowly. In her grey shawl. And came down the hall between the anvils, past Brannoc and Tilda and Hedda and Teo's puddle, to the table at the end. And put her hand flat on the Codex beside Lex's.

"*Moths can't drink a light that's given*," she said. "*Did you know that? Not one. It's the oldest thing the Harrow women knew. A lamp you keep for yourself, they'll come to. A lamp you give away—they can't touch it. It's not theirs to take. It's not anyone's.*" She looked at her son. "*We lit eleven hundred of them on Lantern Night. In the Blightmarch. And gave every one away. And the moths burned.*"

"*It's not moths*," said Kestrel. "*It's grey.*"

"*It's empty*," said Seren. "*The same as she was. Maelis. Empty and drinking. The grey country's no different. It drinks what it can reach.*" She looked round the hall. "*And I don't think it can reach a light that's been given. I think a given light makes its own room. I think that's what giving is.*"

Silence.

"*You think*," said the Queen.

"*I think*," said Seren Harrow. "*I'm a hedge-witch, Majesty, not a scholar. I don't know anything. I only know what my mother knew and her mother before her.*" She took her hand off the book. "*But I'd like to try. I'd like to light every lantern in Caldor and carry them west and give them away. And see if the grey goes round.*"

---

"*Eleven hundred and thirteen*," said Antho. Very quietly. "*At the Keep.*"

His mother looked at him.

"*They've been lit since Lantern Night*," he said. "*On nothing. Every one. They don't need oil.*" He swallowed. "*They're given already. All of them. They've been given for years.*"

"*Then that's where we'll start*," said Seren.

---

The Queen of Caldor looked round her council.

At the smith with two Wells out of seven and six months to find the rest. At his brother with a coin of gold on his hand. At a scholar with eleven books, a fire-witch with a singed coat, a boy who dripped, a grandmother with her feet in the quench, an old smith with gold fingertips and a young smith with a gold hand. At a woman with a sleeping baby and three gold fingers. At a widow in a grey shawl who wanted to give away every lantern in the kingdom.

She sighed.

"*My grandfather*," said Lysandra, "*had a council of forty lords and a hundred clerks and an army.*"

"*Did it help*?" said Kestrel.

"*Not once*," said the Queen. She stood up. "*All right. A door. Six months. Seven Wells. Ask the Crowned. Lanterns.*" She looked at Lex. "*What do you need from me?*"

He opened his mouth.

He didn't get to answer.

---

The door of the Forge banged open.

Snow blew in. Cold. And a boy. Small. Ten. One of the Pruett cousins from the mill, red-faced, out of breath, with his cap gone and his hair full of snow.

"*Smith*—" He stopped. Saw the Queen. Went white. Saw everyone else. Went whiter. "*Smith—sorry—Your Majesty—sorry—*"

"*What is it, Jory*?"

"*On the green*," said the boy. He was shaking. "*On the green, smith. By the Stone.*" He swallowed. "*There's a gold giant sitting on the green.*"
