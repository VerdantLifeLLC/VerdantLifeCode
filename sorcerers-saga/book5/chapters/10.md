# The First Breath

"*There's no glass in the sky*," said Wren.

They were in the kitchen at Harrow. At midnight. With every shutter closed over every horn window and a lid on the water barrel and a cloth over the milk jug and Lark asleep in the cradle in the loft with Kestrel sitting on the ladder beside her, cross-legged, with a little flame in her palm, watching the dark.

Lex looked at her.

"*There's no water in the sky*," said Wren. "*Not still water. Not anything flat or bright or polished. Not up there. Not in a storm.*" She put her hands flat on the table. Her right one. And her left, with its three gold fingers. "*There's nowhere she can look out of. Nowhere she can reach through.*"

"*You want to take her into a storm*."

"*I want to take her where the Glass Queen can't*," said Wren. "*And you need Gale. And I know the way.*"

---

It had been three days since Seren came across the stepping stones in the dark.

Without a lantern. Without her shawl. Barefoot, in the snow, with her hair coming down and her face like chalk. She'd hammered on the door with both fists. And when Lex opened it, she'd pushed past him and gone straight up the ladder to the loft and stood over the cradle, looking down, breathing, for a long time, until she was sure.

Then she'd come down and told them.

*A Vey hand*. *A small one.*

*I didn't say yes. But I didn't say no.*

---

Lex had gone to the green that night. To find the King.

The King was gone.

His circle was there, in the snow by the cracked Stone. His gold path across the green to Old Mag's gate. His footprints, a step wide, going away from the gate and out along the mill road. East. And then north. Toward the hills.

He'd gone. In the dark. Without a word. With his gold cup in his hand and his sister's face in it.

Lex had followed the footprints for six miles in the snow, by the light of his own fire, before Antho came down on the dragon and stopped him.

*He's walking*, Antho had said. *That's all. He's not coming here. He's walking away from here.*

*How do you know*?

*Because he's walking away*, his brother said. *Look at the prints. Look how far apart. He's not creeping, Lex. He's running.*

---

So they'd waited.

Three days. Every window horn. Every bucket lidded. Kestrel on the ladder at night with her fire. Seren sleeping on the settle by the hearth with the grey book under her pillow. Brannoc in the lean-to with a hammer. Antho on the roof, sometimes, with his flute, not playing it.

And the stream outside running black under the ice.

And every night, Wren lay awake and listened to it.

---

"*You were going to go anyway*," she said. "*Seraphine says. Gale next. After Tide. You were going to go to the Teeth.*"

"*Without you*." Lex didn't look at her. "*I was going to go with Antho. Without you. Without her. I was going to leave you here with Kestrel and Brannoc and my mother and every bucket lidded, and go and get it over with, and come back.*"

"*I know*."

"*Because it's safer*."

"*Is it*?" Wren looked at him. "*Lex. There's a stream outside the door. There's a pond in the birch wood. There'll be puddles in every rut when the thaw comes, and there's no lid in the world big enough for a thaw.*" She shook her head. "*It's not safer. It's just closer to where you can see.*"

He didn't answer.

"*She can't reach into the sky*," said Wren. "*She can't reach into the wind. She can't reach into the eye of a storm four miles up over the Teeth.*" She reached across the table and took his hand. Her right one. In his. "*It's the safest place in the world. For the three of us. Right now.*"

He looked at their hands.

"*It's a storm*," he said. "*Wren. She's fourteen months old. It's a storm.*"

"*I was born in one*," said Wren Ashdown.

---

They left at dawn.

Ashka took them. With Antho in front, his long hair tied back and his face set. And Lex behind him. And Wren behind Lex, with Lark strapped to her chest in a sling of oiled wool lined with fleece, bundled so thick that only her round face showed, and her fluff of dark hair standing up out of the top of the hood like a feather.

Seren stood at the gate to see them off.

She didn't say *be careful*. Harrow women didn't. She just stood there in her grey shawl with her arms wrapped round herself and looked at her granddaughter's face for a long time.

Then she reached up and put her hand on the baby's cheek.

"*Be loud*," she said.

Lark considered this.

"*No*," she said.

Seren laughed. Short and surprised. And stepped back. And Ashka opened her wings with a crack like a ship's sail filling, and the yard went away under them, and the birch wood, and the stream, and the grey stone with its twenty lanterns and its one red rose.

---

It was cold up there.

Wren had known it would be. She'd been up there. Three years ago. With Lex and Antho and the others, on Vharuun's back, going up into the storm above the Teeth to find the First Breath.

She'd forgotten how cold.

It went through the oiled wool and the fleece and the three layers of wool under that and into her skin and into her bones. Her gold fingers ached with it. Not hurt. *Ached*. The way a stone aches in frost. The way a bell aches before it's struck.

Lark didn't seem to notice.

She was awake. Strapped to Wren's chest. With her face out of the hood and her eyes wide open. Grey-green. Enormous. Looking at the sky.

She didn't cry. She didn't squirm. She didn't say *no*. She just looked.

At the clouds going by underneath them, white and grey, like a field of snow. At the sun coming up behind them, red, then gold. At the mountains ahead, white and sharp against the blue, the Teeth, a row of fangs along the northern sky.

At the wind.

She couldn't see the wind. Nobody could see the wind. But Wren felt her daughter's small body go still against her chest, and felt her turn her head, slowly, into the cold rushing air, the way a flower turns to the sun.

And breathe it.

---

*She knows*, thought Wren.

*She knows what it is*.

---

They came to the storm at noon.

It sat over the Teeth the way it had always sat over the Teeth, the way it had sat there for nine hundred years and nine hundred before that. A tower of cloud. Black at the base. Grey in the middle. White at the top, so high it caught the sun and burned. Turning. Slowly. Like a wheel. Like a great slow millstone grinding the sky. With lightning walking in it, blue and white, silent at this distance. And under it, the mountains. And over it, nothing. Blue.

The wind came off it like a wall.

Ashka flinched. Wren felt her flinch. Felt the great grey body bank and slow and hang in the air a mile off the edge of the cloud, wings beating hard, holding.

"*She won't go in*," shouted Antho. Over the roar. "*Not without her mother.*"

"*Then call her mother*," shouted Wren.

---

Antho put two fingers in his mouth and whistled.

Long. High. Then low. Then high again. A sound like the wind in a chimney. Like a storm in a gully.

The cloud answered.

---

She came out of it like a mountain falling.

Grey. Silver-edged. Enormous. Bigger than Ashka. Bigger than a barn. Bigger than a hall. A storm-dragon, old as the Teeth, with wings that spread across half the sky and eyes like lightning in a well. Scarred along the flanks with old white scars, healed. Healed in a storm on a night twenty-seven years ago, when a woman in a cottage in Thistledown screamed and pushed, and a baby girl came out into the world, red-haired and furious, at the exact moment a dying dragon over the Teeth breathed in the first breath of a new storm and lived.

Vharuun.

She came down out of the cloud and hung in the air beside her daughter and looked at them. At Antho. At Lex. At Wren.

At the baby on Wren's chest.

And went very still.

---

Wren felt it. The great old eyes. Lightning in a well. Looking at Lark.

Lark looked back.

She didn't cry. She didn't hide. She lay against Wren's chest with her round face out of the hood and her eyes wide and her mouth a little open, and looked at the biggest thing she'd ever seen. Grey. Silver. Lightning walking in its eyes.

And her face changed.

Wren saw it. The way a child's face changes when it sees something it has no word for yet. Wide. Open. Delighted.

"*Ash*," said Lark.

---

Vharuun bent her head.

Down. Slowly. Through the screaming wind. The great grey wedge of her head, as big as a cart, scarred, ancient, coming down out of the sky toward the small bundled figure on Wren's chest. Until her nostrils were a yard away. Until Wren could feel her breath. Hot. Wet. Smelling of rain and lightning and cold high places.

She breathed in.

Smelling the baby. The way a mare smells a foal.

Lark reached out. Both square fists. Out of the sling. Toward the nostrils. Grabbing at the air.

"*Ash*," she said again. Firmly.

And Vharuun—old as the Teeth, mother of storms, who had lain dying in the high cold for a hundred years before a red-haired baby was born under her—breathed out.

Very gently.

Over the child.

---

It was warm.

Wren felt it go past her. Over her. Through her. Over Lark. A breath. Warm and wet and enormous, smelling of spring rain and summer lightning and the first green coming up through snow. It lifted Lark's hair. It lifted Wren's. It went past them and round them and on, out into the cold air, and was gone.

Lark laughed.

She'd laughed before. Of course she had. She laughed at Lex's faces and at the goat and at Brannoc's hymns and at falling down. But not like this.

This came up out of her from somewhere very deep. Big. Loud. Delighted. A laugh like the first rain on a hot day. Like a door flung open.

She laughed and laughed.

And Vharuun lifted her head. And looked at Wren.

And turned. And went back into the storm.

And Ashka followed her.

---

It was dark inside.

Dark and loud and wet and full of wind. Rain like stones. Hail like gravel. Lightning, so close it didn't flash, it *was*—a white light everywhere at once, and a sound like the sky being torn in half, and then dark again, and the afterimage burning. Ashka bucking and plunging under them. Antho shouting something nobody could hear. Lex's arm round Wren's waist, iron-hard, holding her on.

Wren had her arms round Lark. Both of them. Her good arm and her gold-fingered one. Curled over her. Holding her against her chest under the oiled wool with her whole body.

Lark was still laughing.

Wren could feel it. Through the sling. Through the fleece. Through her own ribs. The small body shaking with it. Every time the lightning came. Every time Ashka dropped. Every time the wind hit them like a fist.

*She's not frightened*, thought Wren. *She's not frightened at all.*

*She's home*.

---

They came out into the eye.

All at once. The way you come out of a dark wood into a clearing. One moment wind and rain and hail and lightning, and the next—

Still.

Not still. *Calm*. A great round open space in the middle of the storm, a mile across, two miles, with the walls of cloud going up on every side, black and grey and white, turning, turning, like the inside of a well. And overhead, at the top, a circle of blue sky. And in the blue, the sun.

And in the middle of the calm, hanging in the air, a stillness that wasn't still.

---

Wren had seen it before.

You couldn't see it, exactly. That was the thing. It was air. It was nothing. A place in the middle of the eye where the air was clearer than the rest, the way water is clearer in a spring than in a pond. Bright. Quick. Moving all the time, in every direction at once, and never going anywhere.

The First Breath.

The first wind there ever was. The breath the world breathed in when it was made. Gale's own heart. Or its Wellworld. Or both. Seraphine had written forty pages about it and still wasn't sure.

Wren had stood in front of it three years ago. And it had asked her what she was holding.

And she'd been holding Lex's hand.

*Let go*, it had said. *You cannot carry a wind and hold a hand. Not at the same time. Choose.*

She'd let go.

It had nearly killed her. Not the wind. The letting go. She'd stood in the eye of the storm and opened her fingers and felt his slide out of them and thought *I'll never get it back*.

She had. Of course she had. He'd been right there. He'd taken it again ten minutes later, on Vharuun's back, on the way down, with the First Breath in her lungs and her whole body singing.

But she'd *let go*. Not knowing.

That was the price.

---

Ashka hung in the calm.

Vharuun circled. Slowly. A long way off. At the edge of the eye. Watching.

And the First Breath turned toward them.

---

Wren felt it turn. The way you feel someone in a room turn to look at you. The air in front of them going bright and quick and *attentive*.

*Wind-child*, it said.

Not words. A rush. A gust. A whisper of air past her ear, with meaning in it.

*You came back*.

"*I brought my husband*," said Wren. "*He needs to talk to you.*"

The air moved. Round Lex. Round his face. His hands. Lifting his hair. Tasting him.

*Smith*. *Fire and stone. And water now. New water. Salt.* The air brightened. *You let something go. In the sea.*

"*Yes*," said Lex.

*I can smell it on you*, said the First Breath. *Grief. Gone out. Good.* The air moved on. Round Antho. *Shadow. I know you. Brother of the wind-child's man. You hold a door.* It moved on. Quickly. Restlessly. Never stopping. Down. To Wren's chest. To the sling.

And stopped.

---

It had never stopped. Wren had never felt it stop. Not three years ago. Not in all the stories. The First Breath was the thing that never stopped. It was moving. It was always moving. It was the first movement there ever was.

It stopped.

Over Lark.

The air in front of the sling went very bright and very still. Like a held breath. Like the moment before a gust.

And Lark looked up at it.

---

*Oh*, said the First Breath.

---

Wren didn't move.

She sat on Ashka's back in the eye of the storm with her daughter on her chest and the first wind there ever was hanging in front of them, *stopped*, and didn't move at all.

*Oh*, said the First Breath again. Very softly. A whisper. Like wind in grass. *Oh. New.*

Lark reached out.

Both fists. Out of the sling. Into the bright still air.

And laughed.

---

The First Breath went *wild*.

All at once. Like a cork out of a bottle. Like a bird out of a cage. The still bright air in front of them burst outward in every direction, and the calm wasn't calm anymore, it was a *whirl*, a spinning, a dance, gusts going up and down and round and round, lifting Wren's braid straight up off her shoulders, lifting Lex's coat, lifting Ashka's wings so that the dragon lurched and grabbed at the air.

And lifting Lark.

---

Out of the sling.

Wren felt it happen. Felt the straps go loose—not cut, not broken, just *loose*, the knots untying themselves, the wool falling open—and felt the small warm weight on her chest *lift*.

Up.

Out of her arms.

Into the air.

---

"*Lark*—"

Lex was moving. She felt him move. Felt him lunge past her, his arm going out, his hand open, grabbing—

"*Don't*," said Wren.

---

She didn't know why she said it. It came out of her the way the mist came out of her. From somewhere under her breastbone. From the place where the wind lived.

She caught his arm. With her right hand. And held it.

"*Don't*."

He stared at her. His face white. His eyes wild.

"*Wren*—*she's*—"

"*Look*," said Wren.

---

Lark was flying.

Not falling. *Flying*. Hanging in the air above them, in the middle of the eye, in the middle of the whirling bright wind. Bundled in fleece and oiled wool, with her hood fallen back and her dark hair standing up like a feather. Turning. Slowly. Round and round. The way a leaf turns in an eddy.

Laughing.

The wind had her. Wren could see it. Not *see*. Feel. The First Breath. All round her. Under her. Holding her up. The way water holds up a swimmer. The way a hand holds a bird. Gently. Absolutely.

Spinning her. Lifting her. Dropping her a foot and catching her. Lifting her again.

And Lark laughing and laughing and laughing, with her square fists out and her face turned up to the blue circle of sky at the top of the storm.

---

"*It's got her*," said Lex. Hoarse. "*Wren. It's got her. It's going to—*"

"*It's not going to do anything*," said Wren.

"*You don't know that*."

"*No*," said Wren. "*I don't.*"

---

She didn't. That was the truth. She sat on the dragon's back with her husband's arm in her hand and her daughter turning in the air above them, and she didn't know anything at all.

She didn't know if the wind would bring her back. She didn't know if it would take her up into the blue and away. She didn't know if it knew what a baby was, or how small, or how easily broken.

She didn't know.

She held on to Lex's arm.

And let go of everything else.

---

*Smith*, said the First Breath.

It was everywhere. In the whirl. In the gusts. In the roar of the storm walls turning round them. Laughing. It was *laughing*. Wren could hear it. The first wind there ever was, laughing with a baby.

*Smith. What are you holding?*

---

Lex didn't answer.

His arm was rigid in Wren's grip. Every muscle in it locked. Straining. Toward the child. She could feel it. If she let go, he'd lunge. He'd throw himself off the dragon's back into the open air after his daughter and fall four miles to the Teeth.

*What are you holding*? said the wind again.

"*Her*," said Lex. Through his teeth. "*I'm holding her.*"

*No*, said the First Breath. *I am holding her.*

The wind spun Lark round again. She shrieked with joy.

*You are holding your fear*, said the wind. *Since the rose. Since the old woman's cottage. Since the night the grandmother came barefoot through the snow.* The air brightened round his face. *You have been holding it so tight you cannot breathe. Like your grief, in the sea.*

"*She's my daughter*."

*She is*, said the First Breath. *And she is the wind-child's daughter. And the wind's.* A gust. Round Lark. Lifting her higher. *She was born on rubble, under lanterns, in a storm of light. She breathed me in her first breath. All children do. I am the first breath. I am in every one.*

The air came close to Lex's face. Very close. Very bright.

*But she remembered*, said the wind.

---

Wren felt Lex's arm shake.

"*I can't*," he said. "*I can't let go of it. She's—the Glass Queen wants her. The King. They want her hand. They want—*" His voice broke. "*If I let go, I'm not holding her. If I'm not holding her, anyone can—*"

*Can what*?

"*Take her*."

*You cannot hold her forever*, said the First Breath. *Smith. You cannot. No one can.* The air was very gentle now. *You can hold her hand while she learns to walk. You can hold her when she falls. You can hold the door open so she can go through it. But you cannot hold her.*

A pause.

*That is what the King does*, said the wind. *That is what keeping is.*

---

Lex went still.

Wren felt it. All at once. The rigid arm in her grip going slack. Not giving up. *Stopping*. The way a man stops when he's walked to the edge of a cliff in the dark and someone lights a lantern.

He looked up.

At his daughter. Turning in the bright wind above them. Laughing. Her dark hair blowing. Her square fists out. Her face turned up to the sky.

Not afraid.

Not held.

*Carried*.

---

He breathed out.

Wren felt it go out of him. Through his arm. Through his whole body. A breath he'd been holding since a rose came up through the snow by his father's grave. Since an old woman's cottage and a flame in a lantern that didn't know him. Since his mother hammering on the door in the dark.

It went out of him. Into the wind.

And the wind took it.

---

*There*, said the First Breath.

Wren saw his face. In the bright air. As the panel came and went in front of him, where only he could see it. Saw it change.

He didn't look triumphant. He looked like a man who'd put down something very heavy after carrying it a long way. Light. Strange. Unsteady on his feet. Not sure what to do with his hands.

He looked up at Lark.

And, for the first time in three days—for the first time, Wren thought, since his father died—

He laughed.

---

The wind brought her back.

Slowly. Spinning. Down out of the bright air like a leaf coming down in autumn. Turning. Laughing. Into Wren's arms. Wren caught her. Held her. Felt the small warm weight land on her chest and the square fists grab her collar.

Lark looked up at her.

Grey-green eyes. Enormous. Shining. Her cheeks red with cold and her hair standing straight up and her whole face lit like a Lantern Night window.

"*More*," said Lark Vey.

---

Wren stared at her.

"*More*," said Lark again. Firmly. Pointing up. At the wind. With one square fist. "*More. More.*"

Beside her, Lex made a sound. A choked, cracked, astonished sound.

"*That's new*," he said.

"*That's new*," said Wren.

Lark beamed at them both. Proud. As if she'd known the word all along and had simply been waiting for something worth saying it to.

Then she put her fist in her mouth. And fell asleep. Instantly. Against Wren's chest. The way babies do. As if someone had blown out a candle.

---

The First Breath was laughing.

Wren could hear it. All round them. In the eye. In the turning walls of cloud. A sound like wind in a thousand chimneys. Like a summer gale through a field of barley.

*More*, it said. *More. She said more.* The bright air came close again. Over the sleeping baby. Very gently. Lifting a strand of dark hair. *Nine hundred years they have said that to me, every child, in their first breath, without knowing. More. And I give it.* A pause. *She is the first one who ever said it out loud.*

Then it turned. To Wren.

*Wind-child*.

"*Yes*."

*You let go*, said the First Breath. *Of his arm. Of hers. Of knowing.* The air moved round her. Through her braid. Over her gold fingers, where they lay against the sleeping baby's back. *You did it again. Like before.*

"*I didn't know if you'd bring her back*," said Wren. Her voice was shaking now. Now it was over. "*I didn't know.*"

*No*, agreed the wind. *That is what makes it letting go.*

They went down at dusk.

Out of the eye. Through the wall of the storm. Lightning and hail and wind and dark, Ashka plunging, Vharuun beside them all the way down like a grey cliff falling. And out into the open air over the Teeth, with the sun going down red behind the mountains and the cloud below them like a field of fire.

Lark slept the whole way.

Lex had his arm round Wren's waist. Not iron-hard now. Just there. Holding her on. The way you hold someone on a horse.

She leaned back against him.

"*More*," he said. In her ear. Over the wind. Wondering. "*She said more.*"

"*I heard*."

"*Two words*." She felt him smile against her hair. "*No and more. That's all she needs. That's everything.*"

Wren laughed. And didn't say anything. And watched the mountains go by underneath them in the red light, and felt her husband breathe against her back, slow and easy, in and out, the way he hadn't breathed in a long time.

---

Vharuun left them at the edge of the Teeth.

She banked. Huge. Grey. Silver. Across the red sky. And for a moment hung beside them, wing to wing with her daughter, and looked across at Wren with her lightning eyes.

At the sleeping child.

Then she turned. And was gone. Back into the north. Into the storm.

---

They came home in the dark.

Over the Vale. Over the Greyspine. Over the Forge at Ashbourne, with its twenty chimneys smoking, and the green, and the chapel, and the churchyard with a new grave under the yew.

And Wren, looking down, saw the Stone.

---

On the green. In the middle. Pale. Tall. Cracked from top to bottom down the middle.

Lit.

Not silver-blue. Not the dim steady glow it gave at a Kindling, when a child put her hand on it and it looked all the way through her. Not that.

*White*. Cold. Hard. Like frost. Like ice with the moon on it. Shining up into the dark from the middle of the green. And in the crack down the middle of it—

Wren leaned over Ashka's shoulder. Stared.

In the crack, faint, where the two halves of the Stone didn't quite meet—

An eye.

Ice-grey. Wide open. Looking up.

At them.

---

"*Antho*," said Wren. Very quietly.

He'd seen it. She felt him see it. Felt the dragon falter under them as he pulled her round.

And beyond the green. Beyond Ashbourne. Out across the dark Vale, south and west and north. On every hill. At every crossroads. In every village square.

Lights.

White. Cold. Hard. Like frost under the moon. One after another. Coming on. Like lanterns being lit in windows. Like eyes opening.

Every Lattice Stone in Caldor.

Looking up.
