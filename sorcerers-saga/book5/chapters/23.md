# The Iron of the Key

The Key didn't want to burn.

Lex put the bow of it in the fire at dawn. The ring. The first of the three pieces. Black. Not black like soot. Black like the space between stars. He set it in the heart of the great hearth at the end of the Forge, where the coals were white, where he'd melted Sunreach steel and Stone-Well iron and a dragon's tooth, once, on a bet with Kestrel.

He worked the bellows.

The coals went from white to blue-white. The air over the hearth went thick and wavy. The heat came off it like a wall. Tilda, at the next anvil, stepped back with her arm over her face.

The bow of the Key sat in the middle of it.

Black.

Cold.

---

He let it sit an hour.

Then he pulled it out with the long tongs and laid it on the anvil and put his bare hand an inch over it.

Cold. Not cool. *Cold*. Like a stone at the bottom of a winter river. Like the black glass at the bottom of the world. An hour in the heart of a fire hot enough to make iron run like water, and the bow of the Key of Shadows lay on the anvil as cold as the night it was made.

"*Well*," said Brannoc. From the doorway. With his arms folded. "*That's a first.*"

---

It had been forged from a shadow.

Lex knew that. Everyone knew that. Nine hundred years ago, the night the net was woven, a frightened young man with a little hammer had stood in front of a starving Well with his little brother watching, and cut his own shadow off at the ankles, and beaten it into a key.

*The Key of Shadows*. It unwrote. That was what it was for. Whatever it was turned against, it unmade. Names. Locks. Ceilings. The edge of the world.

It had been barred in the Knot for three years with a forge-thing called the Lockward and an old man's thread wound round it. It had been turned one last time by that old man, in a dead girl's crown, against the Veil.

And it had come apart in his hand. Into three. The way it had been made.

Bow. Bit. Blade.

---

*Hinges: iron. Something that has turned before*.

Seraphine had written it. Down the side of the drawing. In her tiny hand. Before she'd known what she meant.

Lex had known. The moment he read it. He'd looked under the bench at the basket and known.

He hadn't told anyone.

He'd thought it would be easy. Iron's iron. You heat it, you beat it, you shape it, you quench it. He'd been doing it since he was eleven.

He hadn't thought about what it was.

---

"*It won't take heat*," said Tilda. Standing at his elbow. With her gold hand in her apron. Looking at the black ring on the anvil. "*Why won't it take heat?*"

"*Because heat's a change*," said Lex. Slowly. Working it out as he said it. "*Heat makes iron something else. Soft. Red. Ready.*" He looked at the bow. "*And this was made to unmake things. Not to be made.*"

"*So it's stuck*," said Tilda.

"*So it's stuck*." He looked at it. "*It's the most finished thing I've ever seen.*"

---

He tried for three days.

Fire. All the heat he had. Flame Commanding. He stood in front of the great hearth and called it up out of the ground, out of himself, round the net, the way he'd learned to on the night the net went still. White-gold. Roaring. Until the Forge was full of it and everyone had to go out into the yard and the slates on the roof over the hearth started to crack.

The bow didn't warm.

Black heat. Fire with no light. Brannoc's lesson. Fire so hot it burned past glowing into dark. He held the bow in black heat for an hour and felt his own hands begin to blister through the tongs.

Cold.

Stone. He tried to *weigh* it. The way Stone Commanding let him feel the heft of a thing, the grain of it, where it would bear and where it would break. He put his hand over it and felt for the grain.

There wasn't any.

It was black all the way through. Like a hole. Like a hole in the shape of a ring.

---

On the third night, he sat on the floor of the Forge with his back against the long anvil and the three pieces of the Key on the flags in front of him and didn't do anything at all.

The Forge was dark. The hearths banked. Everyone gone home. Tom Pruett and the Arley boys had left the leaf of the door on the saw-bench, half-made. Eleven apple planks, edge to edge, pegged, with two ledges across the back and the brace still to cut. Blooming. Petals drifting off it in the dark onto the flags.

The frame stood at the end of the hall. Empty. Seven feet. Waiting.

Lex sat on the floor and looked at the Key.

---

Bow. A ring of black iron, the size of his palm. Where a hand would go. Where the hand that turned it would hold it.

Bit. A short square bar, notched like a ward. The part that went into the lock. The part that knew the shape of what it was opening.

Blade. A long slim shaft, as long as his forearm. That had held the other two together. That carried the turn from the hand to the lock.

He'd forged a thousand hinges. He knew how a hinge was made. A strap, nailed to the leaf. A knuckle, curled round at the end of the strap. A pin—a pintle—driven into the frame, upright, for the knuckle to sit on. Turn.

*Something that has turned before*.

He could see it. As clear as if it were already made. The bow, drawn out flat into a strap, curled at the end into a knuckle. The upper hinge. The bit, the same. The lower. And the blade, cut in two, and each half driven into the frame for a pintle.

He could see it.

He couldn't make it.

---

*[...smith...]*

---

He went very still.

Not from the hearth. From the floor. From the flags under him. From the little hollow under the long anvil, where the scale and the cinders fell and nobody ever swept. A dark round space the size of a cupped hand.

*[...smith...you're sitting on the floor...]*

*Mal*.

*[...you've been sitting on the floor for a long time...Ivy said...she can feel you...through me...she says you're stuck...]*

He almost laughed.

*I'm stuck*.

*[...on what...]*

He looked at the three pieces of black iron on the flags.

*On something that doesn't want to be anything else*, he thought.

---

The Well was quiet.

Lex felt it there. Under him. Through the floor. Through the ground. Three hundred miles off. A pool in a green field with a ring of five hundred and fifty-six lanterns round it and a woman's crown of blooming thorns on its rim.

Bigger, now. He could feel it. Not much. But bigger. The way a child is bigger after a winter, without anyone seeing it happen.

*[...show me...]* said Mal.

---

So he showed it.

He didn't know how. He just did. The way you'd hold something up in front of a friend's face. He looked at the bow of the Key on the flags in front of him, and let the Well look through him.

And felt it go still.

*[...oh...]*

*You know it*?

*[...I know it...]* The voice was very small. *[...it's the thing that was in the lock...in the Knot...at the bottom...]* A shiver. Through the floor. *[...it's the thing that made me empty...]*

---

Lex didn't move.

*[...he made it...]* said the seventh Well. *[...the little man...with the hammer...the one who sat with me...at the end...]* A pause. *[...he made it to lock me...to shut the lid...to keep the hunger in the dark...]* Another pause. Longer. *[...it was made out of the dark...the same as me...]*

*What*?

*[...his shadow...]* said Mal. *[...a shadow is a place where there isn't any light...it's a hollow...a hollow in the shape of a man...]* The voice was very slow now. Working it out. *[...he cut off his own hollow...and made it into a key...to lock the biggest hollow there was...]*

*To lock you*.

*[...yes...]*

---

Lex looked at the bow of the Key.

Black. Like the space between stars. Like a hole in the shape of a ring.

*A hollow in the shape of a man*.

He thought about Shade's dark. The stars. The gaps between them. *Every mould is a dark*. He thought about his father's shape, sitting in a chair, made of no stars.

He thought about Aurelian. Nine hundred years ago. A frightened young man, not much older than Lex had been when the seal cracked. Cutting off his own shadow. His own hollow. The room in himself where something new could have grown.

And beating it into a lock.

---

*It's you*, he thought. *It's made of the same stuff as you.*

*[...yes...]*

*Room*. *Hollow. A place where something isn't.*

*[...yes...]* said Mal. *[...but pointed the wrong way...]*

Lex stared at the floor.

*What do you mean*?

*[...I'm room that waits...]* said the seventh Well. Slowly. As if it had never said this before. As if it had never known it before. *[...room for something to come...a baby...a bud...a loaf...a door...I'm the space in front of things...]* A pause. *[...that's room that unwrites...it's the space where something WAS...it takes things away and leaves the hole...]*

*So it's the opposite of you*.

*[...no...]* said Mal. *[...it's ME...just turned round...]*

---

Lex sat on the floor of the dark Forge for a long time.

*Turned round*.

The Key. Made of a hollow. Room. The same as Mal. But pointed the wrong way. Not *waiting* for something. *Taking* something. Making room by unmaking. Leaving holes.

*It can't take heat*, he thought. *Because heat would change it. And it's never been allowed to change. It's only ever been allowed to change other things.*

*It's been pointed one way for nine hundred years*.

*Nobody ever turned it round*.

---

He reached out.

He picked up the bow of the Key. In his bare hand. Not with tongs.

Cold. So cold it burned. Like holding a handful of snow, if snow could bite. He held it anyway. In his palm. The black ring. The place where a hand went.

He didn't reach for fire.

He reached for room.

---

The seventh Well came up through the floor.

Through the flags. Through the hollow under the anvil where the cinders fell. Through Lex. Up his back. Into his arm. Into his hand. Soft. Dark. Patient. Enormous. The space in front of things. Room that waits.

And met the Key.

---

He felt it meet.

Like two currents meeting in a river. Like warm water meeting cold. The Key's cold. Black. Hard. A hollow in the shape of a ring. Pointed one way for nine hundred years. *Unwrite*. *Unmake*. *Take away*. And Mal's room coming up into it. Soft. Dark. Waiting. *Make room*. *Make room*. *Make room for something new.*

They didn't fight.

That was the thing. Lex had braced for it. For the two of them to grind against each other like millstones. Like the Gloam and the Root. Like fire and water.

They didn't fight.

They *recognised* each other.

---

He felt it. In his palm. In the black ring. A shiver. A start. Like someone who's been alone in a dark house for nine hundred years hearing a voice in the next room that sounds like their own.

*[...oh...]* said Mal. Softly. To the Key. Through Lex. *[...oh...it's you...hello...]*

The Key didn't answer. Keys don't.

But it moved.

Not in his hand. *In* itself. In the black. A turn. A slow turn. Like a key turning in a lock. Like a lock turning round its key. Like something that had been facing one way for nine hundred years turning, very slowly, very carefully, to face the other.

---

And the bow got warm.

---

Not hot. Warm. Like a stone in the sun. Like a cup of tea held in both hands. Like his daughter's cheek when she'd been asleep. Warm. In his palm. The black ring. Warm.

And not black.

Not quite. Still dark. Still the dark of the space between stars. But not *empty* dark. Not a hole. The dark of a mould before the pour. The dark of a womb. The dark of a hearth before the fire. The dark of the inside of a cupped hand.

*Room that waits*.

Then another panel. Under it. Without him calling it.

---

He looked at it for a long time.

Seven.

*A smith who can hear every one*.

Malachi had written that. In a cramped angry hand. On the back of a page. Nine hundred years ago. *You could not hear all seven, brother. No one can, yet.*

*Yet*.

---

He didn't feel triumphant.

He sat on the floor in the dark and looked at the seven lines. Flame and Stone, seven years. Tide, in the sea, letting go of his father. Gale, in the storm, letting go of his daughter. Shade, in the dark, with his hand on the shape of a coal. Radiance, on a grey field, with a lantern he'd wanted to keep. Hollow, on a forge floor, with the Key of Shadows turned round in his palm.

He thought of everyone who'd been there.

Teo. Wren. Antho. His mother. Nan Ashby. Mal.

He hadn't done any of it alone.

Not one.

---

*[...smith...]* said Mal. Faintly. *[...smith...that's seven...isn't it...I felt it...]*

*That's seven*.

*[...is that good...]*

Lex laughed.

He didn't mean to. It came out of him cracked and wet and sudden. Alone. On the floor. In the dark. With the warm ring in his palm.

*Yes*, he thought. *Yes, Mal. That's good.*

*[...oh...]* A pause. *[...I'm glad...I'm very glad...]* Another pause. *[...smith...I think I'm going to sleep now...it was very far...]*

*Go to sleep*.

*[...good night...]*

*Good night*, *Mal.*

And the Well went back down through the floor. Through the ground. Three hundred miles. To a pool in a green field. And Lex felt it go. Soft. Tired. Like a child going upstairs to bed.

---

He forged the hinges the next day.

---

The bow first. Into the fire. Not the great hearth this time. The small one at the end of his own anvil. Ordinary coals. Ordinary bellows. Tilda working them.

It took heat.

It went red. Not cherry. Not orange. A deep dark red, like the heart of a coal at night, like wine held up to a candle. It glowed. From inside. And Lex pulled it out and laid it on the anvil and struck it.

*Clang*.

It rang.

Not like iron. Not like anything he'd ever struck. A long low note. Deep. Clear. Like a bell rung at the bottom of a well. It went out through the Forge and up into the rafters and out through the open door into the yard, and everyone stopped.

Tom Pruett at the saw-bench. The Arley boys. Hedda with her chisels. Kestrel at the hearth. Brannoc in the doorway.

Everyone stopped. And listened.

The note went on for a long time.

---

He drew it out.

Stroke by stroke. Heat by heat. The ring of black iron opening. Straightening. Flattening. Into a strap. Long. Flat. Dark. The width of three fingers, the length of his forearm. With a curl at one end that he turned round the beak of the anvil, slowly, carefully, into a knuckle. A tube. Round. Hollow.

A hinge.

He held it up in the tongs.

Dark. Not black. Dark red, cooling to dark grey, to the dark of a winter evening. With a sheen on it like the sheen on a crow's wing. Every stroke of the hammer showing in it, faintly, like the grain in wood.

And warm.

---

"*Quench*," said Brannoc. From the doorway. Gruffly.

Lex didn't move.

He looked at the hinge in the tongs. Then he looked at the quench-trough. At the water in it. Ordinary water. From the well in the yard. Brannoc's, nailed down at the edges.

He put the tongs down.

He reached into his coat.

---

The shell was where it had been since the winter. In the inside pocket. Against his ribs. Small. Pale. Spiral. Like the shells children hold up to their ears on the shingle at Saltreach.

*For your door*, the Undertow had said. *The turn. The moment between out and back. When the tide stands at the bottom of the shore, and stops, and remembers, and begins to come in again.*

He held it over the quench-trough.

*Quench your door in that. And it will know how to open. And how to close again*.

He tipped it.

---

Nothing came out.

Not water. Not anything you could see. He held the shell tipped over the trough, and nothing poured from its mouth. Nothing splashed. Nothing fell.

But the water in the trough—

*Stopped*.

Not still. Not like the grey. Not finished. *Stopped*. The way the sea stops at the bottom of the tide. Every ripple in it going flat at once. Every eddy. Every movement. The whole long trough of water, from end to end, holding its breath.

Waiting.

And then—very slowly, very gently, from the far end of the trough—

*Turning*.

---

A ripple. Coming back. From the far end toward him. The way the first wave of the flood tide comes back up a mile of flat sand. One. Then another. Then the whole trough. Moving again. Toward him.

Coming in.

Lex picked up the tongs. And plunged the hinge.

---

It didn't hiss.

Iron always hisses. Hot iron in cold water. It screams. It boils. Steam goes up in a white cloud.

It didn't hiss.

It went *in*. Into the turning water. Quietly. Like a stone into a pond at the exact moment the pond is still. And the water closed over it. And held it.

And Lex felt it. Through the tongs. Through his hands. The hinge, in the water. Taking it. The turn. The stop and the start. The out and the back. Going into the dark iron the way the tide goes into the sand, filling every grain.

*Out*. *And back*.

He drew it out.

---

It was dark. Still. The dark of a winter evening. With the sheen like a crow's wing.

And it moved.

Not in the tongs. *On* itself. The knuckle. The round hollow curl at the end of the strap. He held it up to the light from the door, and he could see it. Faintly. A turn. Inside the curl. Slow. Like a breath. Like something rocking very gently in a cradle.

Waiting to turn.

---

The Forge was silent.

Everyone was looking at it. Tom Pruett. The Arley boys. Hedda. Kestrel. Tilda, with her gold hand over her mouth. Brannoc, in the doorway, with his arms folded and his face very still.

"*Well*," said Brannoc Holt. At last. Very quietly. "*That'll hang a door.*"

---

The bit took the same.

Heat. Strike. The long deep note, like a bell at the bottom of a well. Draw it out. Flatten it. Curl the knuckle round the beak. Quench in the turning water.

The lower hinge.

It lay beside the first on the anvil. Dark. Warm. Breathing.

---

Then the blade.

---

He'd been dreading the blade.

He didn't know why. It was the simplest. A long slim shaft of black iron. Cut it in two. Drive each half into the frame. Pintles. Pins for the knuckles to turn on.

But it was the part that had carried the turn.

Nine hundred years. From the hand on the bow to the bit in the lock. Every time anyone had ever turned the Key of Shadows—Aurelian, on the night it was made, locking the lid; Elric, with the Unnamed, cutting names out of the world; Elric again, at the very end, in a dead girl's crown, against the Veil—the turn had gone down this shaft.

It was the part that remembered.

---

He put it in the fire.

It took heat. Red. Deep. Like the others.

He drew it out. Laid it on the anvil. Lifted the chisel to cut it in two.

And stopped.

---

Because it was speaking.

---

Not a voice. Not words. Not like Mal, or the Undertow, or the First Breath. Lower. Fainter. A *rasp*. Like a dry laugh. Like an old man clearing his throat in the next room.

Coming up out of the red iron on the anvil.

Lex knew it.

He'd heard it at the bottom of the world. In the Knot. On the black glass. A smudge in the dark, wound round and round a key and a bar with a grey thread. *Three years, lad. Give or take. I've been counting threads.*

*Elric*.

---

Not him. Lex knew it wasn't him. Elric had gone. With the Veil. With the last thread. Unwritten. He'd put on Hesper's crown and turned the Key one last time and gone with what it unwrote.

But he'd *turned* it.

His hand on the bow. His turn going down the blade. The last turn. The one that unwrote the edge of the world.

And the blade remembered.

---

*Always second*, said the iron. Faintly. In the rasp. *Wasn't I, lad. Always second. To Arion. To Aurelian. To Morvane. To the Concord. To you.*

Lex didn't move.

*You were first*, he thought. *Father said. At the Knot. In the spring. You were the first friend he had in two hundred years.*

*Aye*. The rasp. Very faint. *He said that. Beside, he said. When he came, after. Not first. Not second. Beside.*

---

Lex looked down at the red blade on the anvil.

He looked at the hinges beside it. Dark. Warm. Breathing. Waiting to turn.

He thought about a door.

A door gets looked at. A door gets knocked on. A door gets painted blue and hung with a lantern and remembered. People say *the door*. *Go through the door*. *Come home through the door*.

Nobody ever says *the hinge*.

The hinge holds the door. The whole weight of it. Every time it opens. Every time it closes. The hinge holds it up and lets it swing and carries the turn. And nobody ever looks at it. Nobody ever paints it. It's just there. On the side. At the back.

Second.

Beside.

---

*It's a hinge*, thought Lex. To the blade. To the rasp in it. To whatever was left of an old man in a grey cloak with brass storm-charms who'd turned a key one last time. *That's what you'll be. A hinge. For a door at the edge of the world.*

The rasp was quiet.

*You'll hold it*, thought Lex. *Every time it opens. Every time it closes. You'll carry the turn. Nobody'll see you. Nobody'll paint you.* He swallowed. *But nothing goes through without you.*

---

Silence.

Then the rasp. One more time. Very faint. Very dry. Like an old man laughing at something he's only just understood.

*Beside*, it said.

*Aye. That'll do.*

---

And the iron on the anvil went quiet.

Not dead. Quiet. Red. Warm. Waiting.

Lex lifted the chisel.

He cut it in two.

---

They hung the door eleven days later.

On the frame. At the end of the Forge. With the leaf finished—Tom Pruett had cut the brace himself, and pegged it, and planed every edge, and stood back and looked at it with his son beside him and his mouth working, and said nothing at all.

Eleven planks of apple wood. Two ledges. One brace. Seven feet high. Three and a half wide. Pale gold at the heart, darker at the edges.

Blooming.

Lex nailed the hinges to it. Two straps. Dark. With the sheen like a crow's wing. He drove the pintles into the frame. Two pins. Dark too. And he and Brannoc and Tom Pruett lifted the leaf between them, and settled the knuckles down onto the pintles, one, two—

And let go.

---

It swung.

On its own. Not far. A few inches. Out. Slowly. Like a breath going out.

And then back.

Slowly. Like a breath coming in.

And stopped. Shut. Against the frame.

Waiting.

---

Nobody in the Forge said anything.

They stood round it. Everyone. All of them. Brannoc and Tilda and Hedda and Kestrel and Asha and Teo. Tom Pruett and his son. The Arley boys. Hollis Penn, who'd come up with bread. Wren, with Lark on her hip. Seraphine, squinting, with Antho's arm round her. Seren and Rhosyn, from Harrow, with the pot of blue.

A door. In a frame. At the end of a forge.

Apple wood. Blooming. On hinges made from the Key of Shadows.

With no threshold under it. No bell above it. No paint on it.

And no latch.

---

Lex put his hand flat on the leaf.

Warm. Alive. Growing. He felt it under his palm. Making room. Over and over. Every moment. For the next thing.

He pushed.

It swung. Open. Easily. On its hinges. Without a sound.

And through it—

The back wall of the Forge. Grey stone. Soot. A rack of tongs.

That was all.

He let go. It swung back. Shut.

---

"*It's not a door yet*," said Seraphine. Very quietly. From behind him. "*Not really. Not without the threshold. Not without the latch.*"

"*I know*."

"*It's a door-shaped thing*," she said. "*In a frame. In a forge.*" She squinted at it. "*It doesn't go anywhere.*"

"*I know*, *Sera.*"

"*It needs the Stone*," said Seraphine. "*And it needs a latch.*" She looked at him. "*And I still don't know what the latch is.*"

---

Nobody said anything.

The door stood at the end of the Forge. Shut. Blooming. With petals coming off it onto the flags.

And then someone knocked on it.

---

Not on the door. On the *other* door. The great double doors of the Forge. Behind them. At the far end of the hall. The ones that opened onto the yard.

*Knock*.

Once. Soft. Careful. Very careful. The way a very big man knocks when he's afraid of breaking something.

Everyone turned.

---

The great doors were open. They were always open. It was a forge. Light and air came in. People came in.

He was standing just outside them. In the yard. In the spring dusk. Not coming in.

Gold. Enormous. Glowing like metal in a crucible. Taller than the doors, nearly. With his head bowed under the lintel. With a lopsided gold cup in one hand, with a crack across the tea in it. With his other hand still raised, where he'd knocked on the open door.

The Gilded King looked down the long hall of the Forge.

Past the anvils. Past the hearths. Past all of them, standing frozen.

At the door at the end.

And Lex saw his face.

He'd seen it at the bottom of Old Mag's step. At the bottom of the yard at Harrow, in the rain. On the floor of his mother's cottage, at the end.

He'd never seen it look like this.

---

"*Smith*," said the King. Very quietly. "*Is that it?*"

Lex didn't answer.

"*The door*," said the Gilded King. He was looking at it. At the apple wood. At the dark hinges. At the petals. "*The one you're going to put at the edge. Where Hes's curtain was. For the dead to go through.*"

"*Yes*."

The King looked at it for a long time.

"*May I come in*?" he said.
