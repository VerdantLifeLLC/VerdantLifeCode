# The Undertow

Lex didn't want to go.

He stood in the yard at Harrow on the morning after the rose, with his pack on his back and Teo Brask dripping patiently at the gate, and didn't want to go anywhere at all.

"*Go*," said Wren.

"*She's in the stream*."

"*She's in every stream*. *And every pond and puddle and window in Caldor.*" Wren had Lark on her hip and her bow over her shoulder and her face set in the way it set when she'd made up her mind and you could argue with it the way you could argue with a cliff. "*You can't stand in front of all of them.*"

"*I can stand in front of this one*."

"*And then what*?" She looked at him. Green eyes. Steady. "*Six months, Seraphine says. Seven Wells. You've got two.*" She shifted the baby. "*You stand in front of this stream for six months, and at Midsummer the grey comes up the Vale and over the hill and into this yard, and you're still standing here with your arms folded, guarding a stream.*"

He didn't answer.

"*Go*," said Wren. "*Your mother's here. Brannoc's here. Kestrel's sleeping in the lean-to and she says if anything comes out of the water she'll boil it.*" A pause. "*I'm here.*"

"*I know you're here*."

"*Then go*," said his wife. And kissed him. On the handprint. "*And come back with three.*"

---

He went.

He looked back once. From the top of the track, where it came out of the birch wood onto the Ashbourne road. At the cottage. At the yard. At Wren, at the gate, with the baby. At the grave by the stream with its twenty lanterns and its one red rose.

Lark waved.

She'd learned it the week before. She did it at everything. Horses. Crows. The goat. She did it with her whole arm, like someone trying to flag down a cart.

He waved back.

Then he turned and walked to the coast.

---

It was four hundred miles.

They didn't walk it. Ashka took them to the edge of the Vale, and Antho said *I'll be at the Keep, with the lanterns*, and flew back north. And from the edge of the Vale they went by cart, and by river barge, and by a fishing boat out of Saltreach with a skipper who looked at Teo's dripping hair and Lex's handprint and charged them double and then refused to take their money at the other end.

It took eight days.

Lex didn't sleep much. He lay on the deck of the fishing boat at night with his coat over him and watched the stars and thought about a rose in the snow.

*YSY IN STONES*.

*NEEDS VEY HAND*.

*SMALL ONE*.

---

They'd taken it to the Forge. The same day. Wren had carried it there herself, in the dawn, running, with Lark on her back and the silk in her fist. And the council had sat on their anvils again and passed it from hand to hand, and nobody had said anything for a long time.

Then Seraphine had said, very quietly: "*The Root.*"

The Root of the net. The gold centre at the bottom of everything, where every thread came in and crossed and met. Where Aurelian had sat for nine hundred years. Where Lex had stood, once, at the Heart, with a hammer in his hand and the whole world in front of him, and opened his hand and let it go.

*No one at the centre*, he'd thought. *Everyone holding everyone*.

*You left a crown lying in the road*, Elric had said. At the bottom of the world. A year later. *And they're coming to pick it up.*

"*The Stones are glass*," Seraphine had said. "*To her. Every Lattice Stone in the world. She walks in glass. And the Stones are glass all the way down to the net.*" She'd looked at the silk in her hands. "*She's going in. Through the Stones. Down the threads. To the centre.*"

"*And the centre won't take her*," said Lex. "*It's Vey. It's always been Vey. Aurelian built it that way. Only his blood.*"

"*Yes*," said Seraphine. "*So she needs a Vey hand.*"

They'd all looked at the last line. Pricked in thorn-holes. Shaking.

*SMALL ONE*.

---

Lex lay on the deck of the fishing boat and watched the stars and thought about his daughter's hands.

Square. Too big for the rest of her. Broad in the palm. Strong. A smith's hands. *Vey hands*, his father had said once. Holding her by the pool on the night she was born. *Aurelian had hands like that. In the paintings. Too big for him.*

He thought about the Glass Queen in the bathwater. *She looked at me. As if there were something there.*

He didn't sleep.

---

Teo's village was called Gull's Reach.

It sat in a notch in the sea cliffs on the long grey western coast below Saltreach, where the land ran out in a tumble of black rock and the Sundering Sea came in green and white and enormous from the edge of the world. A harbour wall. Thirty houses. Boats drawn up on the shingle. Nets on poles. Smoke. Gulls. A smell of salt and tar and fish.

And a small round tower of grey stone at the end of the harbour wall, with a lantern on top, that was Teo's.

"*The Keepers gave it to me*," said Teo. Climbing the steps ahead of him. Dripping on every one. "*After the net. After the Undertow. They said a Tide Speaker ought to have a tower.*" He went red. "*I didn't want one. I wanted to stay at the Forge. But the sea wanted me here.*"

"*The sea told you*?"

"*The sea tells me everything*," said Teo Brask. Simply. "*All the time. It never stops talking.*" He opened the door at the top. "*It's quite tiring, actually.*"

---

The room at the top of the tower was round and bare and very wet. Not damp. *Wet*. Water ran down the inside of the walls in thin bright sheets, all the way round, from the ceiling to the floor, and ran away through a channel in the flags and out through a hole in the wall. Like standing inside a waterfall that had been folded into a circle.

In the middle, a table. A chair. A bed with an oilskin over it. And on the table, in a big glass jar full of seawater, a fish.

Small. Silver-blue. Fat. Swimming in slow circles.

"*That's Barnaby*," said Teo.

"*Hello, Barnaby*."

The fish looked at him.

"*He came to the wedding*," said Teo. "*At the Hollowmaw. In his jar. Seraphine says he's the only fish that's ever been to a royal wedding.*" A pause. "*He liked it. He liked the music.*"

Lex looked at the fish. The fish looked at Lex. It had a very steady stare for a fish.

"*Teo*," said Lex. "*You've got a lot of water on your walls.*"

"*I know*." Teo looked at it. Mournfully. "*It follows me. It always has. Since the Undertow. I tried to stop it once and the sea was offended for a week. No fish anywhere. The whole village nearly starved.*" He sat down on the bed. On the oilskin. Water ran off his hair and down his neck. "*I don't mind it really. You get used to being wet.*"

---

He told Lex about the tide at Morrow.

They sat at the table in the round wet room with Barnaby circling in his jar, and Teo told it. Simply. The way he told everything. In a soft hesitant voice, with his long hands flat on the table and the water running down the walls all round them.

"*The cliffs at Morrow*," he said. "*Sixty miles south. Where the coast runs out at the old edge. Where the Veil came down to the sea.*" He swallowed. "*The tide didn't come in. Last week. On the Tuesday.*"

Lex stared at him.

"*It went out*," said Teo. "*The way it always goes out. Twice a day. And then it didn't come back. It's still out. A week. The fishing boats at Morrow are lying on their sides in the mud a mile from the water, and the water's just—*" He made a small helpless gesture. "*Lying there. Flat. Like a pond. Not going anywhere. Not coming back.*"

"*The grey*," said Lex.

"*I think so*. *The sea thinks so.*" Teo looked at the walls. At the water running down them. "*The sea's frightened, Lex. I've never felt it frightened. Not even at the Undertow. Not even when the net was on it and it hated the stillness so much it tried to drown me for coming near.*" He looked back at Lex. "*It says there's something at the edge of the world that's drinking it. Out past Morrow. Where the Veil came down to the water. It says it can feel itself going. Out. And not coming back.*"

---

Lex thought about the cut on Davy Pruett's hand.

*Not festered. Not healed. Just staying*.

He thought about the Undertow.

He'd met it once. Three years ago. In the Wellworld of Tide. Teo had taken them down—Teo, and Seraphine, and Antho—through the green and the cold and the dark to the bottom of the sea, where the current was a voice and the voice was a god, or something like one. Enormous. Ancient. Angry.

Angry at the net. At the Ceiling. At the Tithe. At the stillness. *I am the thing that moves*, it had said, in a voice like every wave that had ever broken. *Your net has held me still for nine hundred years. I hate it. I hate what holds.*

And Lex had made it a vow. On his knees, in the dark, at the bottom of everything, with the water pressing on his ears. *I'll break it. The net. The Ceiling. All of it. I'll weave a new one with nothing in it that holds.*

And he had. A year later. At the Heart.

*Tell the smith he kept his vow*, it had said. Teo told him. In the autumn.

---

"*It'll want something*," said Lex.

"*It always wants something*." Teo looked at his hands. "*Last time it wanted Seraphine's fear of the sea. She gave it. She's never been afraid of water since.*" He looked up. "*I don't know what it'll want from you.*"

"*Neither do I*."

"*It liked you*," said Teo. "*Last time. Did you know? It said you were the only one who didn't beg.*" A shy smile. "*It said you swore at it.*"

"*I don't remember swearing at it*."

"*You swore at it quite a lot*," said Teo. "*When it was drowning you.*"

---

They went down at the turn of the tide.

At dusk. On the shingle below the harbour wall. In the cold. With the sea coming in green and grey and enormous out of the west and the gulls going home and the lantern on Teo's tower lit above them.

Teo walked into the water.

Just walked. In his clothes. In his boots. Up to his knees. Up to his waist. Up to his chest. The waves broke over him and he didn't flinch. He stood in the sea with the water to his shoulders and the swell lifting him and setting him down, lifting and setting him down, like a cork. And turned round.

"*Come on*," he said. "*It's not cold. Not after the first bit.*"

It was the coldest thing Lex had ever felt.

---

He'd been cold before. Snow in the Greyspine. Ice on the Frostvault. The black glass at the bottom of the world. But this was different. This was cold that went *in*. Through the skin. Through the muscle. Into the bone. Into the marrow. Into the place under his breastbone where the Wells lived, where Flame was, where the heat came from.

Flame flinched.

He felt it. The fire in him, that had been in him since he was nineteen and a seal cracked open in a storm of gold, *flinched* from the water. The way a cat flinches from a bath. Pulled back. Small. Deep. A coal in a grate in a draughty room.

*Easy*, he thought. To the fire. To himself. *Easy.*

He walked in.

---

Up to the waist. The swell lifting him off his feet and setting him down. Up to the chest. His breath going out of him in great gasps, white, smoking. Teo ahead of him. Calm. Patient. Waiting.

"*Now*," said Teo. "*Breathe in. All the way. And hold it.*"

"*And then*?"

"*And then let it take you*," said the Tide Speaker.

He went under.

---

Green.

Green and dark and cold and loud. Not loud. *Full*. Full of sound. The roar of the swell overhead. The grind of shingle on the bottom. The click and hiss of a thousand small living things in the weed. And under all of it, deeper, bigger, so big he felt it in his teeth before he heard it—

A voice.

Not words. Not yet. A *pull*. Down. Out. West. Like a hand round his ankle. Gentle. Enormous. Absolutely certain.

He let it take him.

---

Down.

Through the green into the grey. Through the grey into the blue. Through the blue into the dark. Faster than falling. Slower than sinking. The light going out overhead like a candle snuffed. The cold going from cold to something past cold, to a pressure, an embrace, the whole weight of the sea folding round him like a fist closing.

He couldn't breathe. He'd known he wouldn't be able to breathe. He'd done this before. He held it. He held it. He held—

*Don't*, said the sea.

---

It was everywhere.

In the dark. All round him. Under him. Over him. Through him. A voice like every wave that had ever broken on every shore since the world was water. Slow. Vast. Patient. Angry, still—he could hear it, the anger, deep down, like a riptide under a calm surface—but not at him.

*SMITH*.

*You came back*.

---

He couldn't answer. He was holding his breath.

*Let it go*, said the Undertow.

He didn't.

*You are holding your breath*, said the sea. Very slowly. *You have been holding it a long time. Longer than this. Longer than the dive.* The dark pressed on him. Not hard. Steady. Like a hand on a door. *Let it go, smith. You cannot drown here. You are mine here. Let go.*

He let go.

---

The air went out of him in a rush of silver bubbles. Up. Away. Into the dark overhead.

And the water came in.

Into his mouth. His throat. His lungs. Cold. Salt. Heavy. Filling him. Every part of him. The way water fills a jar pushed under.

He didn't drown.

He'd thought he'd drown. Every part of him that had been a boy in a village with a millpond had known for certain he'd drown.

He breathed it.

---

*There*, said the Undertow. *There. That is better.*

He hung in the dark. Breathing the sea. Slowly. In and out. In and out. The cold of it going down into him and coming back out. Like a tide.

*You kept your vow*, said the sea.

*Yes*. He didn't speak. He didn't need to. Here, at the bottom of the world, under the sea, in the Wellworld of Tide, he only had to think it.

*You broke the net that held me. The Ceiling. The stillness. You gave the Root away. You wove a new one with nothing in it that holds.*

*Yes*.

*I said I would remember*. The dark moved round him. A current. Slow. Vast. Circling. *I remember. I am the sea. I remember everything that has ever fallen into me.*

---

*Something's drinking you*, thought Lex.

The current stopped.

Not slowed. *Stopped*. For one terrible instant the whole dark sea round him went still. Like the bread in the bowl. Like the boy in the field. Like the snow on the hedge with the wind going past it.

Then moved again. Faster. Harder. Angry.

*YES*.

The word went through him like a wave breaking on rock.

*At the edge*, said the Undertow. *In the west. Where the grey curtain met me. Nine hundred years it hung there, down into my water, and I hated it. I hated it the way I hated your net. A wall. A thing that held.* The current circled. Faster. *And now it is gone. And behind it—*

The dark shuddered.

*Stillness*, said the sea. *A country of stillness. Nine hundred years without a single wave. Without a single drop of rain. Empty. Dry. The driest thing I have ever touched.* The voice went low. Very low. Like the drag of the tide going out over a mile of shingle. *And it is drinking me, smith. At Morrow. I can feel myself going. Out. Into it. Not coming back.*

*I know*. *I've seen it. It's drinking everything.*

*I am the thing that moves*, said the Undertow. *I am the turning. I go out and I come back. Out and back. Twice a day since the moon was young.* The current slowed. Faltered. *And at Morrow I have gone out. And I cannot come back.*

Lex hung in the dark and didn't know what to say.

*It is the worst thing*, said the sea. Very quietly. *It is worse than the net. The net held me still. This takes me away.*

---

*I'm going to make a door*, thought Lex.

The sea was quiet.

*At the edge*. *Where the Veil was. Not a wall. A door. Out of every Well. All seven.* He didn't know how to say it better. He was a smith. He didn't make speeches. *A door that asks. That lets the room through when someone knocks. A little. On purpose. And then closes.*

He felt the sea listening. The whole of it. Every wave on every shore.

*Like a tide*, said the Undertow.

*Like a tide*.

*Out*. *And back.*

*Yes*.

---

The sea was quiet for a long time.

Then the current came round him again. Slow. Circling. Not angry now. Thoughtful. The way water circles in a pool below a fall.

*You cannot command me*, said the Undertow. *You know that. You are Speaking. You hear me and I hear you and we talk, like this, at the bottom of the world. That is Speaking.* A pause. *To command the tide is a different thing.*

*I know*.

*It is not a thing you take*, said the sea. *It is a thing you give. To command fire, you give it your heat. You did that. In the hearth, three years ago. You gave it yourself and it nearly kept you. To command stone, you gave it your patience. You sat all night on a mountain and waited.* The current tightened round him. Very slightly. *What will you give the tide?*

*What do you want*?

*I do not want*, said the Undertow. *I am the sea. I take what falls in. I give it back on the shore.* The dark pressed closer. *What have you been holding, smith?*

---

He didn't answer.

*You were holding your breath*, said the sea. *When you came down. You held it and held it. I had to tell you to let go.*

*Everyone holds their breath when they go under*.

*Not like that*, said the Undertow. *Not for that long. That was not a breath for a dive.* The voice was very slow now. Very deep. Like the long low roll of a swell a thousand miles from any land. *That was a breath you have been holding since the night your father died.*

---

Lex hung in the dark.

He didn't move. He couldn't. The whole sea was holding him. Gently. Absolutely. The way you'd hold a child who'd fallen and hadn't started crying yet.

*I haven't*—

*You knelt by him*, said the Undertow. *You held his hand. You did not cry. You walked down through the pool two nights later to the bottom of the world with his name in your mouth and the Key in a basket. You did not cry. You came up. You cut his name in a stone with your hands shaking. You did not cry.*

*How do you*—

*I am the sea*, said the Undertow. *Every tear that has ever been wept runs to me in the end.* A pause. *Yours have not come.*

---

He thought about a bed by a west window.

He hadn't thought about it. Not properly. Not once. In seven weeks. He'd thought about the grave and the lanterns and the stone and the horn lantern on the sill and the basket under the bench. He'd thought about the King and the bread and the dead at the rim.

He hadn't thought about the bed.

He thought about it now.

His father. On three pillows. Under a boarded window, with one board off, and the evening coming in through it. Thin. So thin. White hair on the pillow. His grey eyes open. Looking at Seren. At his wife. Who remembered him. All of him. At last.

*Leave a light on for me*.

*Too late*.

And his hand. In Lex's. Thin and dry and light as paper. Tapping. Two fingers. On Lex's knuckles. The rhythm Lex had heard in the back of his head for seven years, in a dry small voice, before he knew whose it was.

*Tap*. *Tap*.

And then not.

---

He thought about Ember.

In the lantern. In the Ferryman's lantern. A gold flame. Small. Steady. Going away across the grey water in the dark, in the bow of a long grey boat, with his father sitting in the stern.

Ember hadn't looked back.

It couldn't. It didn't love him anymore. It had given that away. All of it. Every scrap of seven years. To buy his mother thirty years of memories for one hour.

It had chosen that.

*I want to put that on the record*, it had said. Once. In the forge. About something else. *In a footnote. Ember did not like it.*

Lex hadn't liked it either.

He'd never said so.

---

He thought about forty-nine mornings.

Sixty-six, now. Kneeling on the hearthstone. *Good morning*. Waiting. Half a breath.

Nothing.

---

He opened his mouth.

The sea came in.

And something came out.

---

He didn't know what it was at first. It didn't feel like crying. Crying was a thing you did with your face, with your eyes, with your throat. This came from lower down. From the place under his breastbone. From wherever it was he'd been holding his breath for sixty-six days.

It came up out of him like a tide coming in over a mile of flat sand. Slow at first. Then all at once. Then more. Then more than he'd known he had.

He couldn't see it. He was at the bottom of the sea in the dark. He couldn't hear it. The sea was too loud.

But he felt it go. Out of him. Into the water. Warm into the cold. Salt into the salt.

And the sea took it.

---

*There*, said the Undertow.

Very softly. All round him. Like a hand on his hair.

*There*.

---

He didn't know how long it lasted. A minute. An hour. Long enough that when it stopped, he was hanging in the dark with his arms wrapped round himself and his forehead pressed against something he couldn't see, and he was so tired he could hardly think.

And the sea was moving round him.

Not circling. *Breathing*. In and out. In and out. The whole weight of the dark water drawing back from him and coming in again, drawing back and coming in, like a tide, like a chest rising and falling.

And he was breathing with it.

He hadn't meant to. He just was. In when it came in. Out when it drew back. In. Out. The way you fall into step with someone walking beside you without knowing you've done it.

*Out*, said the Undertow. *And back.*

*Out*, thought Lex. *And back*.

---

He saw it. In the dark. Silver-blue. Small. Hanging in the black water in front of his face where only he could see it.

He didn't feel triumphant. He didn't feel anything. He felt like a beach after the storm. Wet and flat and washed clean and very, very tired.

*You gave me something*, said the sea.

*I didn't mean to*.

*Nobody means to*, said the Undertow. *That is how you know it was given.*

---

The current came round him one last time.

And something pressed into his hand.

Cold. Smooth. Round. Small enough to close his fingers over. He couldn't see it. He felt it. A shell. A spiral. Like the shells children hold up to their ears on the shingle at Saltreach to hear the sea.

But heavier. And *full*.

*For your door*, said the Undertow.

*What is it*?

*The turn*, said the sea. *The moment between out and back. When the tide stands at the bottom of the shore, and stops, and remembers, and begins to come in again.* The current tightened round his fingers. Gently. *It is the only stillness I have ever loved. Because it is not still. It is waiting to turn.*

He closed his hand over it.

*Quench your door in that*, said the Undertow. *And it will know how to open. And how to close again.*

---

He came up.

Fast. Faster than he'd gone down. Through the black into the blue into the grey into the green. The light coming back overhead. The roar of the swell. The cold.

He broke the surface twenty yards off the shingle at Gull's Reach in the last of the dusk, and the sea came out of his lungs in a great choking gush, and the air went in, and he was coughing and gasping and floundering in the waves like a boy who'd fallen off the mill dam.

Teo got him by the collar.

Hauled him in. Through the surf. Onto the stones. And let him lie there, on his back, on the wet shingle, gasping, with the waves running up round him and drawing back, running up and drawing back.

---

He lay there a long time.

Teo sat beside him. Dripping. Saying nothing.

The stars came out.

---

"*Did it*—" said Teo. At last. Shyly.

"*Yes*."

"*What did it want*?"

Lex didn't answer for a while.

He lay on the shingle with the sea running up round him and drawing back, and the shell clenched in his right fist, and his face wet. Not with the sea. He could tell the difference now. He'd never been able to tell the difference before.

"*Nothing*," he said. "*It didn't want anything.*" He swallowed. "*It just took what I'd been carrying.*"

Teo looked at him.

Then he nodded. Slowly. As if that made perfect sense. As if that were the most ordinary thing in the world.

"*It does that*," said the Tide Speaker. "*It's the sea.*"

---

They sat on the shingle in the dark.

And far off, out past the harbour wall, out on the black water toward the edge of the world, the tide turned. Lex felt it turn. He'd never felt that before either. The whole sea, from Saltreach to the Teeth, standing for one long breath at the bottom of the shore, and stopping, and remembering—

And beginning to come back in.

He opened his hand. Looked at the shell. In the starlight. Small. Spiral. Pale. Humming, very faintly, against his palm, like a held breath about to be let go.

---

That was when the bell rang.

---

Under his shirt. Against his breastbone. On its cord. Where it had hung for three years. Small. Silver. The kind of bell you'd tie round a goat's neck. With a clapper bent a little, crooked, so that when it rang it rang very slightly off.

*Ting*.

Malachi's bell. Hesper's, before that. A girl had given it to a barefoot boy on a grey shingle nine hundred years ago and said *then I'll know. Wherever I am. If you're sad.*

It rang when someone was sad.

*Ting*.

---

Lex sat up.

He put his hand flat over it. Through his wet shirt. And felt it ringing. Not once. On and on. Soft. Steady. Off-key. Like a heartbeat with a stumble in it.

He'd heard it ring before. On the rubble of Duskhold. On the night the edge fell. In the forge, sometimes, at night, when he didn't know who it was ringing for.

He'd never felt it ring like this.

As if someone very far away, someone very big, was sitting somewhere in the dark, and couldn't stop.

---

And then, from the cliffs above the harbour, a sound.

A crack. Like a ship's sail filling.

A shape against the stars. Huge. Grey. With silver along the edges of its wings like frost along the edges of slate. Coming down. Fast. Over the harbour wall. Over the tower with its lantern. Onto the shingle twenty yards off in a roar of wind and a spray of stones.

Ashka.

And on her back, his long black hair loose and wild and his face white in the starlight—

"*Lex*," said Antho. "*Get on.*"

Lex was already running.

"*Who*?"

His brother looked at him. Down from the dragon's back. With the bell still ringing, ringing, off-key, under Lex's wet shirt.

"*Old Mag*," said Antho. "*Tilda sent word. She says it's her feet.*" He swallowed. "*She says she's asking for you.*"

A pause.

"*And for him*."
