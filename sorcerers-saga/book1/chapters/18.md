# The Inquisitor

The man who came through the splintered door of Hedda's shop looked like a shadow that had learned to stand up straight.

He was tall and very thin, all length and no width, in a long black coat that hung to his ankles and buttoned to his throat. His hair was black, slicked flat against a narrow skull. His face was long and pale and smooth, so smooth it looked polished, with a thin mouth and a thin nose and eyes the color of wet slate. He wore no armor. He carried no sword. Around his left forearm, wrapped from wrist to elbow, he wore a length of chain.

It was the strangest chain Lex had ever seen. Not iron. Not steel. Something silvery and black at the same time, like oil on water, and every link was carved with tiny glyphs. Wellscript. Lex could see them from across the room: *Sever. Sever. Sever.* Over and over.

"Hedda Vorne," said the man in black. His voice was the cold, thin, precise voice they'd heard through the wall. "Harboring wildcasters. Harboring a fugitive thief. Obstructing the Spire." He didn't look at Hedda. He was looking past her, down the narrow shop, toward the curtained doorway to the back room. "I'm disappointed. You had a good pension."

Hedda Vorne stood in the middle of her shop with her cudgel in her one hand and a cut across her forehead streaming blood into her eyes. Behind her, through the broken door, Lex could see grey cloaks crowding the lane outside. Torches. Crossbows.

"Get out of my shop, Vane," said Hedda.

"Stand aside."

"I was making blades for the Spire when you were still wetting your bed in the novice dormitory." Hedda raised the cudgel. "*Get out of my shop.*"

Vane sighed.

He moved his left arm. Just slightly. A flick of the wrist. And the chain *uncoiled*. It shot off his forearm like a striking snake, silver-black, glyphs glowing, and wrapped itself three times around Hedda's arm and cudgel and chest before she could move.

Hedda staggered. Her face went grey. She didn't fall. She was too big and too stubborn to fall. But the cudgel dropped from her fingers, and she sagged against the counter, and Lex, watching through a gap in the curtain, saw a silver panel flicker over her head and go dark.

"Severance chains," Elric whispered beside him. "Lady of Lanterns. He's brought Severance chains." His voice was very tight. "They cut you off from the Lattice. All of it. Your skills, your stats, your class. Everything the Lattice gives you." He swallowed. "If he gets those on you, you're nothing. You're a level-one nobody with no class and no skills and no magic."

"Is there a back way out?" hissed Garrick.

"The cellar." It was Hedda's voice. Weak and rough, from the front of the shop. She hadn't turned around. She was still slumped against the counter, wrapped in silver-black chain. But she'd raised her voice just enough to carry. "Under the forge. Trapdoor under the slack tub. Old smugglers' tunnel. Goes to the drains."

Vane's slate-grey eyes went to her. For the first time, he looked almost interested.

"Thank you, Hedda," he said. "That's very helpful."

And he started down the shop toward the curtain.

---

Garrick met him in the doorway.

The old knight came through the curtain with Oathkeeper drawn and frost blooming along the blade, and swung for Vane's head without a word. It was a beautiful swing. Lex had watched Garrick fight for weeks now and had never seen him make a better one.

Vane stepped back half a pace, and the blade passed an inch from his nose.

The chain moved. It came off Hedda as if it had a mind of its own, uncoiling, whipping back to Vane's arm, and then out again, straight at Garrick. Garrick got Oathkeeper up in time. The chain wrapped around the blade instead of his body, three times, glyphs flaring.

And Oathkeeper *rang*.

It was the same long, clear, singing note it had made in the burned smithy when Garrick swore his oath on it. The runes along the blade flared gold, all three of them at once. And the chain *slipped*. Its coils loosened. Slid. Fell away from the steel like a snake sliding off a hot stone.

Vane's eyes widened.

"What," he said softly, "is *that*?"

"Masterwork," said Garrick, and swung again.

---

The back room exploded into chaos.

Wardens came pouring in through the front of the shop behind Vane, crossbows raised. Garrick held the doorway, Oathkeeper flashing, frost and gold. Wren was loosing arrows through the gap over his shoulder, the new steel-headed arrows Lex had made her, and where they struck crossbows and sword-hands, the rune *True* guided them home. Seraphine had her new crossbow up and was firing too, slower, but steadier than she'd ever been.

And Lex was hauling the slack tub off the trapdoor.

It was full. Twenty gallons of water. He got his arms around it and *heaved*, thirty-seven Might, and it slid across the floor in a great sloshing wave, and there under it, set into the flagstones, was a square wooden trapdoor with an iron ring.

"*Go!*" Garrick roared from the doorway. "*Everyone down! I'll hold them!*"

"Garrick—"

"*I'm standing on the steps, boy! Go!*"

Antho was first down. He dropped through the trapdoor into darkness with the Keeper's Lantern glowing at his belt, and the little flame lit a narrow stone stair going down. Seraphine went after him. Then Wren.

Then the chain came through the doorway past Garrick.

It came so fast Lex barely saw it. A silver-black blur, past Garrick's guard, past Oathkeeper's ringing blade, across the forge, straight at Lex. He threw up Ironskin. He swung Anvilsong. Neither of them mattered. The chain wrapped around his left arm, three times, from wrist to elbow, and the glyphs on its links flared silver.

And the world went *quiet*.

Not silent. Lex could still hear Garrick shouting, the crossbows twanging, the crash and clatter of the fight. But something inside him went quiet. The silver panel at the edge of his vision went dark. His skills flickered out like candles in a wind. Ironskin. Ember Strike. Heat Shaping. Gone. All of it gone. He felt the strength drain out of his arms like water out of a bucket. His legs went weak. The hammer in his right hand went from weightless to heavy, terribly heavy, the weight of twelve pounds of iron in the hand of a young man who'd been hammering it for eight years and was, suddenly, only that.

"There," said Vane softly, from the doorway. Garrick was still blocking it, still fighting, but Vane had reached past him with the chain the way a fisherman reaches past a rock with his line. "There. Now. Let's have a look at you."

He began to reel Lex in.

Lex dug in his heels. It didn't matter. The chain pulled him across the forge floor, step by step, toward the doorway, toward Vane. He tried to call the fire. It didn't come. He tried to call anything. Nothing came.

*[Lex,]* said Ember.

It was very faint. Faint as a whisper from the bottom of a well. But it was there.

*Ember? I thought—the chain—*

*[The chain cuts the Lattice,]* said Ember, faint and fast. *[Silver threads. It's very good at it. It's cut every silver thread you have.]* A pause. *[But you're not only silver, Lex. You haven't been since the night of the gloamhounds. Your class isn't in the Lattice. It's under it. In the Root. Gold.]*

*I can't feel it.*

*[Because you've been reaching for it through the silver. Out of habit. Stop reaching through the silver. Reach* down*. Past it. To the gold.]*

The chain pulled him another step. He was halfway to the doorway. Vane's thin pale face was smiling slightly.

Lex closed his eyes.

He stopped reaching for the fire the way he'd always reached for it, through the silver panel and the silver threads and the skill names Ember had taught him. He reached *down*. Past all of that. Down into the place under his breastbone where, on the night of the gloamhounds, a door had broken. Where the fire had been banked for fifteen years.

It was there.

It had always been there. Not in the Lattice. Not given by the Lattice. *His*. Under everything. The way the forge is under the iron.

He let it out.

---

It came up through him like a furnace door swinging open.

The silver panel at the edge of his vision stayed dark. But behind it, under it, something else flared into life. Gold. Not a panel. Just *gold*. A heat so fierce that the flagstones under his boots cracked and the water from the overturned slack tub flashed into steam all around him.

The chain on his arm screamed.

The glyphs on its links had been glowing silver. Now they glowed red. Then orange. Then white. The chain was *heating*, link by link, from the place where it touched his skin outward, the way an iron bar heats in the forge. *Sever. Sever. Sever.* The glyphs were trying to cut him off from the Lattice. But there was nothing left to cut. He wasn't using the Lattice.

He was using what was under it.

The chain *melted*.

It went soft and bright and liquid, from his wrist to his elbow, and dripped off his arm in molten silver-black drops that sizzled on the wet flagstones. The rest of it, the part still connecting him to Vane's arm, went white-hot along its whole length in a single flash, faster than any heat Lex had ever made, and Vane shrieked and let go and staggered back clutching his forearm, his black sleeve smoking.

Lex stood in the middle of the forge with steam pouring off him and his eyes blazing gold, and the silver panel at the edge of his vision flickered, and flickered, and came back on.

Vane was staring at him from the doorway. His face, for the first time, wasn't smooth. It was twisted with pain and shock and something else. Something almost like fear.

"*Impossible*," he said. "Nothing breaks the chains. *Nothing*. They cut everything the Lattice—"

"I know," said Lex. "I'm not using the Lattice."

Garrick, still in the doorway, still holding back the Wardens with Oathkeeper, turned his head and grinned at Lex through the blood on his face.

"*Go*, boy," he said. "Before he works out what that means."

---

Lex went.

He dropped through the trapdoor into the dark. A moment later, Garrick came down after him, backing down the stairs with Oathkeeper still raised, and slammed the trapdoor shut over their heads. Lex heard something heavy hit it from above. Heard Vane's voice, cold and furious, shouting orders.

And then he heard another voice. Gravelly. Defiant.

*Oh, I'm so sorry, Inquisitor. Was that your foot? I'm terribly clumsy since I lost the arm.*

Hedda.

"She's free," said Garrick, very low, at the bottom of the stairs. "The chain came off her when he threw it at you. She'll stall them." He paused. "She knew she'd have to. When she let us in."

Lex stood in the dark at the bottom of the stairs and felt something tear in his chest.

"We have to go back for her—"

"No," said Garrick. "We don't." He put his hand on Lex's shoulder. "She chose. Like I did, on the steps of that chapel. Like your mother did, Antho told me, in your kitchen." His voice was very quiet. "You honor that by running, lad. Not by going back and making it mean nothing."

Above them, through the trapdoor, came the sound of breaking glass. And Hedda Vorne's voice, raised in a roar of pure unrepentant fury. *My BEESWAX! That's a hundred crowns of beeswax, you—*

Lex ran.

---

The smugglers' tunnel was narrow and low and stank of old water. It ran down from the cellar under Hedda's forge in a long sloping passage of rough stone, and then joined a larger tunnel, arched and brick-lined, with a channel of black water running down its middle. The Old Drains, Seraphine called them. Built three hundred years ago when Highcrest still had a wall around the Lower Town, abandoned when the new sewers went in, used now by smugglers and thieves and rats.

They ran. Antho in front with the Keeper's Lantern lighting the way. Seraphine and Wren behind him. Lex and Garrick at the back. And Elric in the middle, leaning on his staff, breathing hard.

Behind them, far off, they heard shouting. Splashing. The Wardens had found the trapdoor.

"Where does this come out?" Wren panted.

"The Rivergate," said Elric. "At the bottom of the city. Where the river comes in under the walls." He coughed. "There'll be a grating. Old. Rusted. We can break it."

They ran on. The tunnel sloped down and down. The water in the channel grew deeper and faster. The shouting behind them grew closer.

And then Seraphine stumbled.

She went down hard on the wet bricks, and her pack burst open as she fell, the strap snapping. Books spilled out across the tunnel floor. Papers. Her notebook. Her spare spectacles. And something else, something small and bright that bounced on the bricks with a high metallic *ting* and rolled to a stop at Lex's feet.

He looked down at it.

It was a token. Silver, the size of a coin, thick and heavy. Stamped on one side with the lattice of the Wardens. And on the other side, a seal. A tower, with a star above it.

"*Leave the books!*" Garrick was hauling Seraphine up by the arm. "*Leave them! No time!*"

"My notes—"

"*Leave them!*"

Lex bent and picked up the silver token.

"Later," Elric said, very low. His face in the lantern-light was grey and strange. "Later, Lex. Run."

The seal of the Office of the High Warden.

They reached the Ferrow landing an hour before dawn. A rickety wooden jetty on the riverbank, a ruined boathouse, and a muddy track leading away north through the dark fields. ---

Lex walked at the front with Wren, who had bound up a cut on her arm with a strip of her shirt and was limping slightly. Lex didn't look at him. "I saw it."

"Then you know what it is." Elric held it out. The silver coin with the lattice on one side and the tower-and-star seal on the other. "A passkey. From the Office of the High Warden. It opens every sealed door in the Spire. Every vault. Every ward." He turned it over in his knotted fingers. "There are perhaps a dozen of these in existence. The High Warden keeps them very close."

"She said it wasn't hers."

"She did." Elric's voice was very gentle. "And perhaps it isn't. Perhaps someone planted it on her. Perhaps someone slipped it into her pack in Hedda's shop, while she slept." He looked at Lex. "Or perhaps that's how a junior archivist got past the sealed vault wards, two years ago. Perhaps someone in the High Warden's office gave it to her. Perhaps she's been carrying it ever since. Perhaps she's still using it."

Lex stopped walking.

He turned and looked back along the muddy track. Seraphine was walking beside Antho, thin and pale and exhausted, her spectacles askew, her half-empty pack clutched to her chest. As he watched, she stumbled, and Antho caught her arm, and she looked up at him and said something, and he smiled.

"She stood next to my brother all night in the Barrows," Lex said. "She wrote three hundred and forty-six names into the Lattice."

"She did," Elric agreed. "And it was very moving. And very convincing." He put the token back in his cloak. "And it made all of you trust her completely. Which is exactly what a very good spy would do."

Lex didn't say anything.

"I'm not asking you to do anything," said Elric. "Not yet. Just watch. That's all I've ever asked." He put a hand on Lex's shoulder. "We're close now, Lex. Mirrormere is less than two weeks north. Whoever the Barrow-King was warning us about, they'll have to move soon." He squeezed Lex's shoulder. "Be ready when they do."

He walked on ahead.

Lex stood on the muddy track in the morning sunlight and watched him go.

*[Lex,]* said Ember slowly.

*I know.*

*[It* could *have been planted.]*

*I know.*

*[Anyone could have put it there. In Hedda's shop. While she slept.]* A long pause. *[Anyone who had access to the High Warden's office. Anyone who knew how the Spire worked. Anyone who'd been* not-being-noticed *by the Spire for longer than she's been alive.]*

Lex looked up the track at Elric's back. At the grey cloak, the brass charms glinting in the sun, the lightning-scarred staff tapping on the stones.

Then he looked back at Seraphine.

*I don't know who to believe,* he thought.

*[Neither do I,]* said Ember quietly. *[So let's keep watching. Both of them.]* A pause. *[And let's get to your father. Quickly. Before whoever it is gets there first.]*

Lex shifted Anvilsong on his shoulder, and turned his face to the north, and walked on.
