# Interlude: The Second Man

Duskhold did not have doors.

Elric Thorne had noticed that on his first night there, and he noticed it again every night after. The fortress had corridors, and halls, and stairs, and great vaulted chambers with ceilings lost in darkness. It had windows, narrow slits in walls of black glassy stone that looked out over the grey country. It had a throne room, and a great hall, and a chasm at its heart.

But it had no doors. Nothing could be closed. Nothing could be shut. Every room opened into every other room, and every corridor ran into every other corridor, and everywhere, in every one of them, the Quiet stood.

They lined the walls. Hundreds of them. Thousands, perhaps. Men and women and children, grey-skinned, white-eyed, standing perfectly still in rows along every corridor and around the edges of every hall, their hands at their sides, their faces blank. They didn't move. They didn't speak. They didn't blink. When Elric walked past them, their white eyes didn't follow him. They simply stood, the way the statues in the Oracle's gorge had stood, waiting.

And it was silent. That was the worst of it. Duskhold was utterly, perfectly silent. No wind. No echo. Even Elric's own footsteps on the black stone floor made no sound, as if the fortress swallowed them. He'd tried, the first night, to hum to himself. Something. Anything. An old marching song from the eastern march. The sound had come out of his mouth and simply stopped, an inch from his lips, as if it had walked into a wall.

He didn't try again.

---

He walked to the chasm every evening.

It was in the center of the fortress. A great round hall, the largest in Duskhold, open to the grey sky at the top. And in the middle of its floor, the floor simply ended. A pit. Not round. Ragged. Torn. Like a wound in the earth that had never healed. Black, utterly black, so black that the eye slid off it. Cold air rose out of it in slow invisible waves. And the smell, wet ash and rot and nothing, so thick here that it coated the inside of Elric's mouth like oil.

The Vhessa breach. The first. The worst. Sixteen years old.

Morvane had built his fortress around it, the way a pearl is built around a grain of sand.

And on the edge of the pit, on a great black plinth that had grown up out of the floor like a fungus, stood the crystal.

Elric stopped at the edge of the hall and looked at it.

It wasn't clear anymore. Not entirely. Black veins ran through its faceted walls now, branching, spreading, like cracks through ice. Like the veins under Morvane's white skin. They were thicker every evening. Every evening, a little more of the crystal had gone dark.

And inside it, half out of it, frozen, Arion.

His head and one shoulder and one arm were outside the crystal, as they had been when the Hollow-knights lifted it off the cellar floor at Caldris. Reaching. His face was grey and still. His eyes were open, fixed, staring at nothing. The rest of him was still inside, upright, his other arm at his side, his long dark coat hanging straight.

Elric walked toward him.

He did it every evening. He didn't know why. He told himself it was to watch Morvane's progress. To see how far the black veins had spread. To know how long it would be before the graft was complete and the net ran through Morvane and Morvane kept his promise.

He stood at the foot of the black plinth and looked up at his oldest friend's frozen face.

Arion's eyes were looking at him.

Elric's heart stopped.

They hadn't been. They'd been fixed on nothing, staring past him. He was sure of it. Every evening for three weeks, he'd stood here, and Arion's eyes had stared at nothing.

Now they were looking at him.

Grey. Unblinking. Still. But *looking*.

"Arion?" Elric whispered.

The sound stopped an inch from his lips. Duskhold swallowed it.

But the grey eyes moved. Just slightly. Just a fraction. Focused. On his face.

And somewhere, faint as frost forming on glass, faint as a voice heard through a wall, not in the air but in the place under the air, where the Root was, where the stolen gold still burned in Elric's chest like a coal swallowed whole, something spoke.

*Was it worth it, old friend?*

Elric stood very still at the foot of the plinth.

He opened his mouth. Nothing came out. Duskhold took it.

So he answered the way Arion had asked. In the place under the air. With the stolen gold.

*Not yet,* he thought.

The grey eyes held his for a long moment.

Then they slid away again. Back to nothing. And the crystal was only a crystal, black-veined, with a frozen man in it.

Elric stood there until his legs ached. Then he turned and walked out of the hall, past the silent rows of the Quiet, into the corridors without doors.

---

He'd been eighteen when he met Arion Vey.

He thought about it a great deal, in Duskhold. There was nothing else to do. No books. No conversation. No sound. Just walking the silent corridors, and standing at the edge of the chasm, and remembering.

Eighteen. A first-year novice at the Spire, Kindled two years before as a [Stormcaller], Epic grade, the best class anyone in his village had ever received. He'd been proud. So proud. A blacksmith's son from a fishing town on the Saltreach coast, who'd put his hand on a cracked village Stone and seen *Epic* unroll in silver, and wept with joy in front of everyone.

He'd come to the Spire thinking he was the most gifted novice in a generation.

And then, in his second month, a boy had arrived from nowhere. A thin quiet boy with a forgettable face. And on his first day, the boy had walked into the great chamber at the top of the Spire to be presented to the Great Stone, and laid his hand on it the way every novice did. And the Great Stone had lit up like a bonfire. Every Warden in the building had felt it. Elric had been in the refectory, eating porridge, and his spoon had fallen out of his hand.

Arion had been nineteen. He'd looked nineteen for the next forty years.

They'd become friends. Elric had never been entirely sure how. Arion was quiet and clever and strange and didn't seem to want friends. But he'd sat down next to Elric in the refectory the next morning, the morning after the bonfire, and said *I'm told you're the best novice in the Spire, and I'd like you to teach me the Circles, because nobody else will tell me anything.* And Elric, who was eighteen and proud and furious and jealous, had looked at him and said *Why should I?* And Arion had said, with perfect simplicity, *Because I think you'd be good at it.*

He had been good at it.

He'd taught Arion the Circles, over a month. And on the last day, Arion had looked at the seven nested rings of light spinning over his own palm and said *Oh, I see, it's like folding steel*, and done it perfectly. And Elric had smiled, and clapped him on the shoulder, and said *well done*.

And gone back to his cell that night and lain awake until dawn.

That was the first time.

---

There had been so many times.

Arion making Adept two years before Elric. Arion making Master four years before. Arion being given Root access to the Great Stone, by blood, by right, on the day he turned twenty-five, when Elric had applied for the same access three times and been refused. Arion being named Warden-Architect at thirty, the youngest in the Order's history.

Arion finding the forbidden records. Arion going east. Arion coming back with a face like a grave. Arion meeting a freckled hedge-witch with flour on her cheek in a market square in Saltreach, and falling in love in an afternoon, and walking away from the Spire and everything in it to marry her, as if it were nothing. As if none of it had ever mattered.

And through all of it, Elric beside him. Teaching him. Helping him. Covering for him. Riding with him into the eastern march. Standing on the walls at Varrow Ford with lightning in his hands while Arion worked his impossible Root-magic in the river below. Earning the title *Tempest of Varrow Ford*, which was the only thing anyone ever remembered him for, and which he'd only earned because Arion needed someone to hold the walls while he saved the day.

Always beside. Always second. Always *well done*.

And Arion had never aged.

That was the thing Elric couldn't forgive. Not the Root. Not the gifts. Not the effortless first-try brilliance. He could have forgiven all of that. He *had* forgiven all of that, every day, for forty years, the way you forgive a storm for raining.

But Arion had never aged. And Elric had.

Every year, Elric had looked in the mirror and seen a little more grey. A few more lines. A little less strength in his arms, a little less sharpness in his eyes. And every year, Arion had looked exactly the same. Thirty. Thirty-five, maybe, by the end. Silver at the temples, a few faint lines around the eyes. A man in his prime. Forever.

*The Root doesn't let you age*, Arion had told him once, almost apologetically, when Elric finally asked. *Not the way the rest of you do. I don't know why. It's in the blood.*

*The rest of you.*

Elric had spent forty years beside a man who would never grow old. He'd poured his youth into helping that man become what he was. And at the end of it, he'd be a bent, toothless, half-blind old wreck in a chair by a fire somewhere, and Arion would come to visit him, still thirty-five, still brilliant, still kind, and pat his hand and say *well done, old friend*.

He'd asked for Sanction eleven years ago. Tier Four. Warden-Architect. Not because he needed it. Because it was the only thing left. The only thing that might, maybe, let him stand beside Arion as an equal for once. Just once. Before the end.

And Arion had written back: *Not yet. Not because you aren't strong enough. Because you want it too much.*

Elric had read that letter in a rented room in Saltreach, by the light of a single candle, and something inside him had broken. Quietly. Like a bone breaking in sleep.

He'd gone to the Spire the next week and stolen the High Warden's passkey.

He hadn't known why. Not then. He'd only known that he needed to have something. Something that was his. Something Arion didn't know about.

---

Morvane had found him three years later.

Or he'd found Morvane. He was never quite sure which. He'd been on the eastern border, alone, looking for Arion, following some rumor. And he'd camped one night at the edge of the grey country. And in the morning, there had been a man standing at the edge of his camp. Tall and thin and white, in a long white coat.

*Elric Thorne*, Morvane had said. Softly. Reasonably. *Arion's friend. I remember you. From the eastern march. From Varrow Ford.*

He'd had a cup of tea with him. That was the absurd thing. They'd sat by Elric's campfire on the edge of the Blightmarch and drunk tea, and Morvane had talked. About order. About peace. About quiet. About the terrible noise of the world, all the wanting and hating and grieving and *grasping*, and how much better it would be if it simply stopped.

And then he'd said: *I know what Arion did to you. I know what he denied you.* And: *I could give it to you. When I hold the net. Whatever you want.*

Elric had told him to go to the Lady's own hell.

Morvane had smiled and gone.

But a year later, Elric had sent a raven east. Just a small thing. Just a rumor he'd heard, about a Warden patrol, about where they'd be. He'd told himself it was a test. To see if Morvane could be trusted. To find out what Morvane knew about Arion. He'd been gathering intelligence. That was all.

And a raven had come back, with information about breaches that Elric couldn't have gotten anywhere else. Useful information. Information that might help him find Arion.

So he'd sent another.

And another.

Eight years of ravens. Small things. Always small things. Never anything that mattered. Never anything that would really hurt anyone. He'd told himself that every time. *I'm using him. Not the other way around.*

Until the night on the causeway in the Drowned Barrows, when he'd stood with Quill on his wrist and written *the waters that hold the sky*, and known, even as he tied the paper to the raven's leg, that it was not a small thing. That it was the biggest thing he'd ever done.

And Lex had seen him do it.

And he'd lied.

---

"You're thinking about him again."

Morvane's voice. Soft. Pleasant. It was the only voice in Duskhold that made a sound. As if the fortress allowed its master to speak and no one else.

Elric turned. He was standing at one of the narrow window-slits in a corridor high in the fortress, looking out over the grey country. He hadn't heard Morvane come up behind him. Nobody ever heard Morvane come.

The white figure stood a few paces away. His long white coat. His long white hair. His face like porcelain, the black veins running under the skin. Fainter than they'd been before Avaloria. But spreading again. Every day a little thicker.

"Arion," said Morvane. "You're thinking about Arion. You always have that look."

Elric didn't answer. He couldn't. Duskhold wouldn't let him.

Morvane smiled. He lifted one white hand, a small gesture, and something in the air around Elric loosened. Like a hand being taken off his mouth.

"There," said Morvane. "You may speak. For a little while."

"How long?" said Elric. His voice sounded strange. Rusty. He hadn't heard it in days. "Until the graft is done?"

"Two months. Perhaps less." Morvane came and stood beside him at the window. "The crystal is resisting. Arion is stubborn, even in stasis. Even with nothing left. He's holding on to the knot with his bare hands." He shook his head, almost admiringly. "He always was the most stubborn man I ever knew."

"And then you hold the net."

"And then I hold the net. And then I rewrite it." Morvane's white eyes looked out over the grey country, toward the west, toward the green. "Every thread. Every knot. Every life. All of them running through me. One will. One voice." He smiled. "One quiet."

"And my Sanction," said Elric. "My Tier. What you promised."

Morvane turned his head and looked at him. For a long moment, he didn't say anything at all.

"Elric," he said gently. "Don't you understand yet?"

Elric's skin went cold.

"When the net runs through me," said Morvane, "there won't *be* Sanctions. There won't be Tiers. There won't be levels or classes or Ceilings. There won't be anything to want." He spread his white hands. "That's the gift. That's the whole gift. Not to *have* what you want. To stop *wanting* it."

"You promised me Warden-Architect."

"And you'll have it. You'll have everything. You'll have Root, and Authority, and Tier Four, and all the power you ever dreamed of." Morvane's voice was very soft and very kind. "And you won't care. Because you'll be quiet. Like them." He nodded at the corridor behind them. At the rows of grey white-eyed figures standing silent along the walls. "Isn't that what you really want, Elric? Isn't that what you've wanted for forty years? Not the Sanction. Not the Root. To stop *hurting*. To stop standing beside him and feeling it. To stop *wanting*."

Elric stared at him.

He thought about the mirror on the silver road. The young man on the white throne, crowned with gold, smiling. Alone. In an empty room.

"No," he whispered.

"Yes," said Morvane. "You'll see. When it's done, you'll see. You'll thank me." He put one white hand on Elric's shoulder. It was very cold. "You'll be at peace, old friend. At last."

He turned and walked away down the corridor without doors, between the silent rows of the Quiet. And as he went, the air around Elric tightened again, like a hand closing over his mouth, and the silence came back.

---

Elric stood at the window for a long time.

Then he went to the small bare room that Morvane had given him, high in a tower, and sat down on the hard narrow bed, and took out a piece of paper and a pencil.

Quill was on the windowsill. The raven had been very quiet since Caldris. He didn't speak. He didn't croak. He just sat on the sill and looked at Elric with his black bright eyes.

Elric wrote.

He didn't know who he was writing to. He wrote *A.* at the top, and then stopped, and crossed it out, and wrote *L.*, and stopped, and crossed that out too. Then he just wrote.

*I'm sorry.*

He looked at it.

He wrote it again. *I'm sorry.* And again. And again. Down the whole page. *I'm sorry. I'm sorry. I'm sorry. I'm sorry.* His hand shook. The pencil tore through the paper in places. He kept writing.

When the page was full, he stopped. He sat looking at it for a long time.

Then he held it in the candle flame until it burned. And dropped the ashes in the cup of water by the bed. And stirred them with his finger until they were gone.

---

Later, much later, in the darkest hour of the silent night, Elric Thorne reached inside himself, into the place where the stolen gold burned like a swallowed coal.

Sixty-four points of Authority. Arion's Authority. Torn out of him at the moment of waking, through the black graft-glyph Morvane had inked on Elric's palm.

Morvane thought he had it all. Morvane had asked for it, on the first night in Duskhold. *Give it to me, old friend. I'll keep it safe. I'll use it for the graft.* And Elric had given it. Had stood at the edge of the chasm with his marked palm on the black plinth and let the gold flow out of him into the crystal, into the black veins, into Morvane's working.

Most of it.

Not all.

He'd kept back a shard. A sliver. Nine points. Hidden down deep, under the rest, the way Arion had once hidden a sliver of himself in a little boy's seal. He hadn't decided to do it. His hand had simply... held on. The way a drowning man's hand holds on to a piece of driftwood.

He held it now. In the dark. Nine points of gold, warm in the cold silence of Duskhold.

It wasn't much. It wasn't enough for anything. Not enough to free Arion. Not enough to stop Morvane. Not enough to undo what he'd done.

But it was his.

And as long as he held it, some small, stubborn, unquiet part of him couldn't be made silent.

---

On the windowsill, Quill shifted his feet and ruffled his feathers.

And from very far away, so faint that Elric thought at first he'd imagined it, a World Notice unfolded across the inside of his eyes. Silver. Plain. The kind of thing the Lattice only said once in a generation.

But not this kind. This was smaller. A private notice. The kind that went only to those with Root.

:::root
ROOT SIGNATURE DETECTED
Location: SALTMERE FENS (approx.)
Signature: VEY (locked)
Status: —LISTENING—
:::

Elric sat up on the narrow bed.

*Listening.*

Not the Lattice. Not the Root. Something else. Something he'd only ever read about in footnotes.

He stared at the word for a long time.

Then, slowly, impossibly, in the silent dark of Duskhold, where no sound was permitted and no hope was allowed, Elric Thorne began to smile.

"*He'll find a way*," he whispered. The words died an inch from his lips. He didn't care. "*He'll find a way. They always do. His father always did.*"

He lay back down on the hard narrow bed and closed his eyes and held his nine stolen points of gold, and for the first time since Caldris, he slept.
