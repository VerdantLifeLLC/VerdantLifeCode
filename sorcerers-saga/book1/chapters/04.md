# The Enigmatic Brother

They camped that first night in a fold of the foothills, where a stand of wind-bent pines broke the worst of the cold coming down off the Greyspine, and Lex lit the fire without touching it.

He didn't mean to. He'd gathered the kindling and stacked it the way Brannoc had taught him, and he'd crouched with the flint and steel in his hands, and he'd thought, idly, *catch*, the way you'd think it at any fire. And the kindling caught. All at once, with a soft *whump*, every twig flaring orange from the inside, as if it had been waiting.

He stared at it.

*[You'll want to be careful with that,]* Ember said mildly. *[In a dry forest you might set fire to a county.]*

"I didn't do anything."

*[You did. You just did it without noticing. That's what happens when a power's been bottled for fifteen years. It leaks.]* A pause. *[It'll settle as you learn to hold it. For now, try not to think "catch" around anything you're fond of.]*

Across the fire, Elric Thorne lowered himself onto a flat rock with a small groan and stretched his legs out toward the flames. Quill hopped down from his shoulder and began picking at a heel of Mother Orla's bread with the concentration of a jeweler.

"Talking to it again?" Elric asked.

Lex hesitated. He hadn't told Elric much about Ember. Only that there was a voice, and that it had helped him in the fight, and that it seemed to know things about the Lattice. He hadn't told him about the courteous dry tone, or the jokes, or the way the voice sometimes paused before it spoke, as if choosing words with care.

He wasn't sure why he hadn't. It just felt private. Like a letter you hadn't finished reading.

"Sometimes," he said.

"Arion talked about constructs once. Long ago. A kind of working you could leave inside something to watch over it." Elric was looking into the fire. "I didn't know he'd ever made one. He never told me." He was quiet for a moment. "He never told me a great many things, it turns out."

Lex broke a piece of bread off the loaf and didn't eat it.

"You said you'd tell me about my brother," he said. "On the road. We're on the road."

Elric didn't look up from the fire. "So we are."

"So tell me."

---

"Eight years ago," Elric said, "the Spire's Lattice Watch recorded an event at the Ashbourne Stone. I wasn't there. I'd left the Order by then. But I still had friends in the Spire, people who'd pass me a letter now and then, and one of them sent me a copy of the record because the name on it was *Harrow*, and she knew I'd been asking after a woman named Seren Harrow for years."

"You knew my mother?"

"I knew *of* her. Arion talked about her. Not often. When he did, he'd get a look on his face like a man warming his hands at a fire." Elric smiled faintly. "He never told me where she lived. Never told me her name was Harrow. I worked that out myself, years later, from a few things he'd let slip." His smile faded. "By then it was too late. By then the Lattice Watch had already filed its report."

"What did it say?"

Elric reached into his cloak and drew out a small leather folder, worn soft at the edges, tied with a strip of faded ribbon. He untied it carefully and took out a single sheet of paper, folded in thirds, and held it out across the fire.

Lex took it.

It was a copy, made in a tidy, cramped hand. The ink had faded to brown. At the top was the silver lattice sigil of the Wardens, drawn in pen.

:::system
LATTICE WATCH — INCIDENT RECORD
Station: Ashbourne (S. Vale, Caldor)
Event: KINDLING — ANOMALOUS

Subject: ANTHOREN HARROW, 16
Class assigned: ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓
Grade: ERROR
Level at Kindling: 1 → 9 (!!)
Root signature: DETECTED (!!)

Secondary event, same night:
  Breach (minor), duration 4 min.
  Location: Harrow residence.
  Fire. One fatality presumed.

Disposition: Subject fled. Classed
WILDCASTER / UNSTABLE.
Retrieval team dispatched.
:::

Lex read it twice. The second time, his hands had started to shake, and he had to hold the paper against his knee to keep it still.

*One fatality presumed.*

His mother. Reduced to three words in a clerk's handwriting.

"Retrieval team," he said. His voice came out hoarse. "They sent people after him."

"They sent a team of six Wardens up into the Greyspine three weeks after the fire." Elric's voice was very even. "None of them came back. They sent another team the next spring. Two came back out of eight. They said the warlock of the Hollowmaw had been waiting for them. That he'd come out of the dark like a piece of the night tearing loose. That the dark *moved* for him." He paused. "They sent a third team the year after that, and a fourth, and then they stopped sending teams, because it was getting expensive, and because some bright clerk in the Spire realized it was cheaper to let the mountains keep him. The Hollowmaw went on the maps as forbidden ground. The traders at Thistledown started telling stories. And the mad warlock of the Greyspine became the sort of thing mothers frighten children with."

Lex stared at the paper. *Root signature: DETECTED.*

"He's like me," he said slowly. "Isn't he? He's got it too. The gold. The Root."

"He's exactly like you," said Elric. "That's the whole tragedy of it."

*[Lex,]* Ember said quietly in his head. *[Breathe.]*

He hadn't realized he'd stopped. He made himself do it. In. Out. The fire beside him, which had leaped up a foot higher in the last minute, settled slowly back down.

"Why?" he said. "If he's like me, if Father sealed him like he sealed me, why did his break? Why did it happen to *him*?"

Elric was quiet for a long moment.

"I've thought about that a great deal," he said at last. "I think it's because your seals weren't the same. Arion would have had to make them differently. You were four; your brother was nine. He was older, and his power was older, settled deeper. I'd guess your father put a lid on him. A good lid, a strong one, but a lid." Elric turned his hands over in the firelight, looking at them. "And a lid is only as strong as the hand holding it down. When your brother turned sixteen and put his hand on that Stone, the Lattice reached into him to give him his class, and it found Root under the lid, and it *pulled*. And the hand that should have been holding the lid down wasn't there."

"Because Father was gone."

"Because your father was gone. Away, somewhere, pouring himself into whatever it was he went to do. Feeding your seal, and your brother's, from a great distance, and getting weaker every year." Elric looked up. "Your seal was made to hold for a long time. Your brother's was made to hold until your father came back. He didn't come back."

"So it broke."

"It broke. All at once. At the worst possible moment, in front of a Lattice Stone, with the whole weight of the Lattice pulling on it. And whatever came through..." Elric shook his head. "I don't know what happened to him, Lex. I don't know what he saw or what he heard or what it did to his mind. But I know the Lattice didn't know what to make of him. *ERROR*. That's not a class. That's the Lattice throwing up its hands."

Lex folded the paper. He folded it very carefully, along the old creases, and handed it back across the fire.

"He killed her," he said.

"Did he?"

"*You read it*. One fatality. The fire was in our house. He was standing in the middle of it with his eyes black and that sound coming out of him and she was calling his name, and then she was gone, and he *walked away*." Lex's voice cracked. He clenched his jaw until it stopped. "He walked away and he didn't look back. He left me there."

"I know," said Elric softly.

"So don't ask me *did he*."

"I'm sorry. You're right." Elric tucked the folder back into his cloak. "But I'll tell you something, and you can do what you like with it. I've known a great many people who did terrible things, Lex. Some of them meant to. Most of them didn't. And I've learned that what a person does in the worst moment of their life is not always the whole of who they are." He met Lex's eyes across the fire. "Your father taught me that, actually. Ironic, given everything."

Lex looked away into the dark.

Up on the mountains, very far away, a wind was blowing across the snowfields. He could hear it, faintly, a long cold sighing.

"Why are you telling me this?" he said. "Why now? Why does it matter what happened to him?"

Elric was silent for a moment. Then he reached into his cloak again, deeper this time, into some inner pocket against his chest. And he drew out a second paper.

This one wasn't a copy. It was a letter, written on thick cream stock gone yellow with age, sealed once with blue wax, the seal long since broken. He held it for a moment in both hands, looking at it, before he passed it over.

"Because of this," he said.

---

The handwriting was small and neat and slanted slightly backward, as if the writer had been left-handed and fighting it. The ink was black.

*E.—*

*I've no right to ask anything more of you. I'm asking anyway. I always do.*

*I can't come back. Not yet. Perhaps not ever. What I found in the east is worse than I told you, and the only way to hold it is from inside, and the only way inside is a door I can't come back through once it's closed. I've made my peace with that. I've made my arrangements. The boys are hidden as well as I can hide them, and S. knows what to do.*

*But if the seals fail, if the boys wake, then I'll be failing too, and you'll know it, and so will everything else that's hunting. If that happens, find them. Both of them. Bring them together. The way to me runs through Avaloria, and the Way opens only to two of the blood. One of them alone can't reach me. Both together can.*

*I know what I'm asking. I know what it means that I'm asking you, and not someone else. I've never been fair to you, old friend. I'm sorry for that too.*

*Mind the quench.*

*—A.*

Lex read it three times.

"Mind the quench," he said at last.

"It's a smith's phrase, I think," said Elric. "He used to say it. *Mind the quench.* Whenever he meant *be careful*, or *don't rush*, or *the important part is at the end*. I never knew where he got it."

*[It's what you say to an apprentice,]* said Ember in Lex's head, and its voice was very odd, very quiet. *[When the iron's shaped and you're about to put it in the water. The quench is where the iron gets its hardness. It's also where it cracks, if you're careless. You don't rush the quench.]* A pause. *[I don't know how I know that. I mean, I know it as metallurgy. But I know it as a* saying*. As something someone said to someone. Over and over.]* Another pause. *[That partition's locked.]*

Lex looked at the letter in his hands. At the small, neat, backward-slanting writing.

He'd never seen his father's handwriting. Not once. His mother had kept nothing. Or if she had, it had burned with the cottage.

He found that he was touching the letters with his thumb. Tracing them. He made himself stop.

"When did you get this?" he asked.

"Eleven years ago." Elric's voice was flat. "It was the last time I heard from him. He'd sent me letters before that. Four years of them, after he disappeared. Short ones. Never saying where he was. Just that he was alive, and working, and that I shouldn't look for him." He held out his hand for the letter, and Lex, after a moment, gave it back. "Then that one came. And then nothing."

"And you never found him."

"I never found Avaloria." Elric folded the letter along its old creases and slipped it back into the pocket against his chest. "It's a legend. A Founders' temple, one of the great ones, older than the Spire. The old texts say it stands on an island in a lake of glass, in the far north, beyond the mountains. I've walked the far north. I've been to every lake in it. I've found nothing." He looked at Lex. "Because the Way opens only to two of the blood. I'm not of the blood."

"So you need me."

"I need you. And I need your brother." Elric's storm-grey eyes held his. "That's why it matters, Lex. That's why I'm telling you all this. Because your father is dying somewhere north of those mountains, and the only way to reach him is together. You and Anthoren. Both."

The fire crackled. Quill had finished the bread and was cleaning his beak against a rock, very thoroughly.

"I'm not going to find my brother," Lex said.

Elric said nothing.

"He's a murderer. He's mad. He's killed—how many Wardens? Twenty? Thirty? He's been living in a cave in the mountains for eight years doing Lady knows what. Everyone in the Vale thinks he's a monster. And you want me to walk up to him and say, *Hello, Antho, it's me, your little brother, let's go on a trip together to find the father who abandoned us*?"

"Yes," said Elric. "That's exactly what I want."

"You're mad."

"Very possibly." Elric leaned back against his rock and closed his eyes. "But your father isn't. And your father told me to bring you together. So I'm going to try."

---

He didn't sleep much that night.

He lay by the fire wrapped in Brannoc's spare blanket with the heat of the coals on his face and the cold of the mountain night at his back, and he looked up through the pine branches at the stars, and he thought.

He thought about his mother calling his brother's name through the smoke. *Antho, Antho, sweetheart, look at me.*

He thought about Antho's eyes.

He thought about the last real memory he had of his brother before all that, before the weeks of silence and the strange late nights on the woodpile. Antho at fifteen, sprawled on the riverbank below the mill, teaching him to skip stones. Antho had been endlessly patient about it. *No, flatter. Flick your wrist. Low, low, like you're trying to make it fly under a door.* And when Lex had finally made one skip four times, Antho had whooped so loud he'd frightened a heron off the far bank, and picked Lex up and swung him round, and said, *Lex, that was brilliant, you're brilliant, you're going to be better at this than me.*

He thought about that for a long time.

*[You're awake,]* Ember said quietly, some time after midnight.

*I can't sleep.*

*[I noticed.]* A pause. *[Would it help to talk?]*

*About what?*

*[Anything. Your brother. Your father. The relative merits of charcoal versus coke as a forge fuel.]* Another pause. *[I'm told I'm a good listener. I'm not sure who told me. That partition's locked too.]*

Lex almost smiled.

*Do you think he's right?* he thought. *Elric. Do you think Father's really dying?*

*[I think the seal is failing,]* said Ember carefully. *[I can feel it. It's like... the walls of a house, getting thinner. Something that was being fed isn't being fed as well as it was. Whether that means the one feeding it is dying, or trapped, or simply very far away and very tired...]* A pause. *[I don't know. I'm sorry. I'd tell you if I knew.]*

*Would you?*

*[Yes,]* said Ember, without hesitation. *[I would. I don't think I was made to lie to you.]*

Lex lay looking up at the stars.

*If I go,* he thought slowly, *if I go up there and find him, and he's what everyone says he is. What happens then?*

*[Then we'll deal with it.]*

*He could kill me.*

*[He could try.]* Ember's voice was very dry. *[But I'd like to point out, with all due respect, that you've already been nearly killed once this week, and it didn't take.]*

Despite himself, Lex laughed. A short huff of breath in the cold air.

*And if he's not what they say?*

Ember was quiet for a long moment.

*[Then you'll have a brother again,]* it said at last. *[And I think you might like that more than you're willing to admit.]*

---

When he woke in the grey light, the Lattice had left him a message.

It was waiting at the edge of his vision when he opened his eyes, a silver panel with a faint gold border, glowing softly in the dawn mist like a lantern left burning.

:::system
QUEST OFFERED

THE LOST FATHER  (Main Quest)
Your father, Arion, is alive — for
now. Find him.

  ◇ Find the Temple of Avaloria
  ◇ The Way opens to two of the blood

Reward: ???
Failure: ???

ACCEPT?   [ YES ]   [ NO ]
:::

He lay there looking at it for a long time.

*[The Lattice offers quests sometimes,]* Ember explained, unprompted. *[When it recognizes that you're on the edge of something significant. Accepting it won't change anything about the task itself, but it will track your progress, and it may grant rewards along the way. Experience. Items. Occasionally, knowledge.]* A pause. *[Failure conditions are listed as unknown. That's unusual. Usually the Lattice is very precise about what happens if you fail.]*

*What does that mean?*

*[It means either the Lattice doesn't know what will happen if you fail. Or it does know, and has decided not to tell you.]* Another pause. *[I'm not sure which I find more comforting.]*

Lex looked at the line. *The Way opens to two of the blood.*

He thought of a boy on a riverbank, whooping at a stone that skipped four times.

"Yes," he said aloud.

The panel flickered, and folded itself up, and something new unrolled in its place.

:::system
QUEST ACCEPTED: The Lost Father

CHAIN QUEST UNLOCKED
THE BROTHER IN THE MOUNTAIN
Anthoren Harrow dwells in the
Hollowmaw, high in the Greyspine.
Find him. Survive the meeting.

  ◇ Reach Thistledown
  ◇ Find the Hollowmaw
  ◇ Speak with your brother
:::

*Survive the meeting*, Lex thought.

"Comforting," he said.

---

Elric was already up, standing at the edge of the pines with his staff in one hand and Quill on his shoulder, looking north at the mountains. The Greyspine rose up before them in the dawn like a wall at the end of the world: grey rock and white snow and black forest, ridge piled on ridge, the high peaks lost in cloud. Somewhere up there, Lex knew, was a cleft called the Hollowmaw, and in the Hollowmaw was a cave, and in the cave was his brother.

"I'm not coming with you," Elric said without turning around.

Lex stopped buckling his pack. "What?"

"Not up the mountain. Not yet." Elric turned. His face in the dawn light was lined and tired and very serious. "There are people I need to see. Old friends. Some of them may know things about Avaloria, or about the Gloam, or about what the Order is planning. Some of them may be willing to help us, when the time comes. And some of them are..." He paused. "Delicate. They won't talk to me if I have a wildcaster at my heels."

"You're leaving me to go and find my murderous brother *alone*?"

"You won't be alone. You'll have your—" Elric gestured vaguely at Lex's head. "Your construct."

*[Ember,]* said Ember, sounding faintly offended.

"And I'll send Quill to you when I can. He'll find you, wherever you are. He's very good at that." Elric scratched the raven under the beak, and Quill closed his eyes in bliss. "Go to Thistledown. It's two days' walk north and east, on the lower slopes. A trading post, mostly trappers and miners. Hire a guide there. Tell them you want to go to the Hollowmaw, and pay them twice what they ask, and don't take no for an answer."

"And then what? I walk up to his cave and knock?"

"Something like that." Elric's mouth twitched. "Lex. Listen to me. I don't believe your brother will hurt you."

"You don't *know* that."

"No. I don't know it. But I believe it." Elric stepped closer. "Whatever's happened to him, whatever he's become, he's still Arion's son, and your father put something into those seals, something of himself. I could see it in you, last night, when your power came through. It came through *clean*. It came through like something that knew you. If there's anything of that left in your brother..." He shook his head. "I'm gambling, I'll admit. But I think it's a good gamble."

"And if it isn't?"

"Then hit him with your hammer," said Elric, "and run."

Lex stared at him.

Then, despite everything, he laughed. It came out of him unexpectedly, the first real laugh since the night of the hounds, and Elric smiled too, crookedly, and for a moment the two of them stood there in the dawn mist like old friends instead of strangers who'd met two days ago.

"There's one more thing," Elric said. "You need to grow."

"Grow?"

"Levels. Skills. Strength. You're level five, Lex. You're a fire in a lamp, and you need to be a fire in a forge." Elric's eyes were very bright. "The mountains are full of things that will try to kill you. Let them try. Every one you put down will make you stronger. By the time you reach the Hollowmaw, I want you strong enough that your brother has to take you seriously. And by the time we reach Avaloria..." He paused. "By the time we reach Avaloria, I want you strong enough to stand next to your father."

Something in the way he said it caught at Lex. The faintest edge in the old man's voice. Like the dull note under the ring of good iron.

He almost used True Sight. He caught himself in time.

"Fine," he said.

Elric nodded. He reached out, unexpectedly, and gripped Lex's shoulder. His hand was thin and knotted and surprisingly strong.

"Mind the quench," he said quietly.

Then he turned and walked away down the slope, west and south, his grey cloak blending into the grey mist, the brass charms on it chiming faintly until the sound faded and was gone. Quill circled once over Lex's head, croaked something that might have been "*Good luck*," and flew after him.

Lex stood alone at the edge of the pines with his hammer and his pack and a voice in his head, looking up at the mountains.

*[Well,]* said Ember after a moment. *[That's that, then.]*

"That's that."

*[Shall we?]*

Lex shouldered Anvilsong. It was warm against his neck, humming faintly, like a cat purring.

"We shall," he said, and started to climb.

---

Two days later, and thirty miles south, Warden-Captain Daxian Roole stood in the middle of Ashbourne's village green and looked at a cracked old Lattice Stone that was glowing gold.

It wasn't glowing much. You had to look closely, and at the right angle, in the early morning when the light was low. But it was there. A faint warm shimmer along the edges of the crack, like light leaking from under a door. He'd seen a great many Lattice Stones in his twenty years with the Order. He'd never once seen one glow gold.

He'd been told he might.

He was a tall man, lean and weathered, with close-cropped iron-grey hair and a face like a hatchet: all angles and planes, as if someone had carved it in a hurry and never gone back to smooth it. He wore the grey cloak and silver lattice-pin of the Order, and a sword at his hip that had seen more use than ornament. His eyes were pale blue and missed nothing.

Behind him, his squad was finishing with the village. Twelve Wardens, moving from house to house with the brisk efficiency of people who'd done this a hundred times. Knocking on doors. Asking questions. Using Inspection on anyone who looked nervous, which in Roole's experience was everyone, because people always looked nervous when the Wardens came, whether or not they'd done anything.

He'd gotten the story in an hour. It wasn't hard. The whole village was talking about it.

Monsters made of black mist, coming down the north road in the night. A stranger in a grey cloak with lightning on it. And Lex Harrow, the blacksmith's apprentice, the quiet one, the one whose mother died and whose brother went mad, *glowing* like a coal in the fire and smashing monsters into gold sparks with a hammer.

*Gloamhounds*, Roole thought, looking at the scorch marks still faint on the cobbles. *This far west. This far south.* That was going to be in his report. That was going to make some people at the Spire very unhappy.

"Captain."

He turned. Corporal Fenn was coming across the green toward him: a short, wiry woman with a tracker's squint and a [Tracker] class to match. She jerked her head back toward the smithy.

"The big one won't talk," she said. "The smith. Says the boy went south. To Saltreach. Says he took the coast road two days ago, and if we hurry we'll catch him at Millbridge." She spat. "He's lying."

"I know," said Roole. "Bring him."

Brannoc Holt came out of the smithy without needing to be brought. He walked across the green with the slow, heavy tread of a man who had made up his mind about something, and stopped in front of the Warden-Captain, and looked at him.

He was very large. Roole had been told that. He hadn't been told how *steady* the man was. Like a wall.

"Master Holt," Roole said.

"Captain."

"Your apprentice went south, you say. To Saltreach."

"He did."

"Two days ago."

"Two days, aye."

Roole looked at him for a long moment. Then, without looking away, he called up his Inspection. It didn't take long. It never did with commoners; there wasn't much to read.

:::system
BRANNOC HOLT
Class: Smith (Common)
Level: 24 (CAP)
:::

Level twenty-four. Capped. Roole had seen ten thousand panels like it, and he felt, as he sometimes did and would never have admitted, a small tired twist of something that wasn't quite pity.

"Master Holt," he said, "I've been a Warden for twenty years. In that time I've had more people lie to me than I could count. Most of them were lying to protect themselves. Some of them were lying to protect someone else." He paused. "You're the second kind. I can always tell. The second kind look you right in the eye."

Brannoc said nothing. He looked Roole right in the eye.

"I'm not going to arrest you," Roole said. "I could. Harboring a wildcaster's a hanging offense in some provinces, and Caldor's not as soft about it as it used to be. But I'm not going to. Do you want to know why?"

"I expect you'll tell me."

"Because I'd do the same." Roole's pale eyes didn't waver. "If it were my son. I'd stand where you're standing and lie to my face and I'd sleep sound after." He let that sit for a moment. "But I want you to understand something, Master Holt, and I want you to understand it very clearly. Whatever came down that road two nights ago, whatever was hunting your boy, I'm not the worst thing that's going to come looking for him. Not by a long way. And I'd rather find him before *they* do."

Something flickered in the big smith's face. Then it was gone.

"He went south," Brannoc said. "To Saltreach."

Roole sighed. "Of course he did."

"Captain!"

It was Fenn again. She'd gone off to the north edge of the green while they talked, where the road ran up out of the village toward the mountains, and she was crouched on the packed earth there with her fingers spread over a patch of grey, dead grass.

"Tracks," she called. "Two days old. Two men, heading north. One heavy, carrying something big, one light, with a staff." She stood and dusted off her hands. "They go up into the foothills. Toward the Greyspine."

Roole didn't look at Brannoc. He didn't need to. He could feel the big man go still beside him, the way a wall goes still, which is to say, more so.

He turned and looked north. Up the road, up the foothills, to the great grey wall of the mountains standing up against the morning sky, snow on the peaks, cloud on the ridges, and somewhere up there in the high cold dark, a cleft that had been marked on the Order's maps for eight years as forbidden ground.

"The Greyspine," he said quietly. "The Hollowmaw." He let out a long breath through his nose. "Of course. Where else would a Harrow go?"

"Captain?" said Fenn. "Orders?"

Roole was silent for a long moment, looking up at the mountains. He thought about the report he'd have to write. He thought about the four retrieval teams that had gone up into those mountains over the years, and the handful of men who'd come down. He thought about the gold light leaking from the crack in the Stone behind him.

And he thought about the letter in his pocket, sealed with the private sigil of the High Warden herself, that had found him on the road five days ago, before any flare, before any monsters, and ordered him to Ashbourne with all possible speed. *A Root signature has been detected flickering in the southern Vale. Locate the source. Retrieve it. Alive, if possible.*

*If possible.*

"Mount up," he said. "We're going hunting."
