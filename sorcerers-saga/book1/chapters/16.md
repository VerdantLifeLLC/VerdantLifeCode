# What the Dead Remember

Nobody spoke on the way out of the barrow.

They climbed the long sloping passage in silence, past the bronze-armored bones standing at rest in their niches, and came out through the great green-black door into the late afternoon. The mist had lifted. The black water lay still and flat all around the mound, reflecting a pale washed sky, and across it, the causeway ran away south, back the way they'd come, and on its grey stones, if you knew where to look, you could see the faint gold traces of three hundred and forty-six names.

Lex sat down on the causeway at the foot of the barrow and put his head in his hands.

He felt the others settling around him. Garrick, lowering himself onto the stones with a grunt. Wren, crouching beside him, not touching. Seraphine, sitting down with her back against the bronze door, pulling her knees up to her chest. Antho, standing at the edge of the causeway looking out over the water with the lodestone glowing softly in his fist.

And Elric. Lex didn't look up. But he heard the tap of the staff on the stone, and felt the old sorcerer stop a little way off.

*For one who walks beside you counts your steps for another.*

Nobody said it. But it was sitting in the middle of all of them like a stone dropped into still water, and the ripples were spreading.

---

It was Elric who broke the silence. Of course it was.

"We should talk about what the king said," he said quietly.

Nobody answered.

"I know it's hard. I know none of you want to." Elric leaned on his staff. "But a warning like that, from a guardian like that, in a place like that, is not something to ignore. The dead see things the living can't." He paused. "And he was looking at someone when he said it."

"He was looking at all of us," said Wren sharply.

"At the end, yes. He looked at everyone." Elric's voice was very gentle. "But when he started. When he said the first words. Where were his eyes?"

Silence.

Lex lifted his head. He thought back. The Barrow-King, vast on his black throne, the white lights in his skull burning. He'd looked past Lex and Antho. Past Garrick and Wren. Toward the back of the chamber.

Toward Elric, standing in the shadows by the passage.

And Seraphine, standing beside him.

"I think we all know," said Elric softly, "who stood at the back of that room."

Seraphine had gone white. She was staring at Elric with her mouth slightly open, her hands tight around her knees.

"You stood at the back of the room too," said Garrick. His voice was very level. "Right beside her."

"I did," Elric agreed. "So I'm a suspect too. Of course I am. I'd expect nothing less." He spread his knotted hands. "I'm not accusing anyone. I'm asking us to think. Who among us came out of nowhere? Who among us has secrets we don't know? Who among us has a reason to be watched?" He looked at Seraphine. Not unkindly. Almost sadly. "Who among us fled the Spire two years ago with stolen codices, and has never once explained how she got past the vault wards?"

"I—" Seraphine's voice cracked. "I *told* you. I went to the archives at night. I always went at night—"

"To the general archives. Yes. But the sealed vault isn't the general archives. The sealed vault has wards on it that would take a Tier Three Warden an hour to dismantle. You're a Tier Two Scribe." Elric's voice didn't rise. "So who opened them for you?"

Seraphine opened her mouth. Nothing came out.

"*Enough*."

It was Antho. He'd turned from the water. His face was very pale, but his voice was steady, and the shadows around his feet were stirring.

"She sat with me all night," he said. "On that causeway. While I said their names. Three hundred and forty-six of them." He took a step toward Elric. "She wrote every single one into the Lattice. Her hand was shaking by the end. Her voice was gone. She didn't stop." Another step. "Where were *you*?"

Elric didn't move. "At the end of the causeway. Keeping watch."

"With your back to us."

"Someone had to watch the water."

"For *eight hours*?"

"Antho," said Lex quietly.

His brother stopped. He stood there on the causeway, a few paces from Elric, breathing hard. The shadows had risen up around his ankles like black water.

Elric looked at him for a long moment.

"You're right to be angry," the old sorcerer said quietly. "You're right to defend her. She was brave last night. Very brave." He turned his head and looked out over the black water, at the faint gold names on the causeway. "I didn't help, it's true. I couldn't. I don't have Grave-Speech, and I'm not a Scribe, and I'm not a Vey." He was quiet for a moment. "And I'll admit it was hard for me to watch. I've been to many battlefields. I've left many dead behind me, unburied, unnamed. Hearing those names last night..." He stopped. Swallowed. "Some of us have more to remember than others."

Nobody said anything.

"I'm not asking anyone to leave," Elric said. "I'm only asking you to be careful. That's all. We're very close now. Closer than anyone has been to Arion in sixteen years." He looked at Lex. "Whoever the king was warning us about, they'll make their move soon. If they're going to make it at all."

He turned and walked away down the causeway, slowly, leaning on his staff. A little way off, he stopped and sat down on the stones with his back to them, and looked out at the water.

---

Seraphine was crying.

She was trying not to. She'd pressed her face into her knees and her shoulders were shaking and she wasn't making any sound at all. Antho went and sat down next to her. He didn't touch her. He just sat there.

"I didn't do anything," she whispered into her knees. "I swear I didn't. I don't know how the vault wards came down. They just—they were *down*, that night. I thought it was a mistake. I thought someone had forgotten to set them. I didn't ask. I just went in." She lifted her face. It was blotched and wet behind her spectacles. "I've thought about it a hundred times. Why were they down? Who left them down? I don't *know*."

"I believe you," said Antho.

"He's right, though. Isn't he? I don't have any proof. I can't explain it." She looked at Lex. "I should go. I should go back to the Archive. If there's a chance it's me, if someone's using me somehow without my knowing—"

"No," said Lex.

"Lex—"

"No." He stood up. He was tired, bone-tired, more tired than he'd been in his life. His arm still ached from holding the rune open all night. But he stood up anyway. "You're not going anywhere. Neither is anyone else."

He looked around at them. Wren, crouched on the causeway with her bow across her knees. Garrick, sitting with Oathkeeper laid across his lap. Antho and Seraphine, side by side against the bronze door. And Elric, alone, a little way off, his back to them.

"The dead king said someone's counting our steps for someone else," he said. "Fine. Maybe that's true. Maybe someone is. But I'm not going to start picking people off one at a time and sending them away on a *maybe*." He took a breath. "Ember told me something, after the hounds came. When I asked who to trust." He looked at Seraphine. "It said trust the people who stood next to you in the dark. When it counted. Who were there."

Seraphine stared at him.

"You were there," said Lex. "Last night. You stood next to my brother all night and wrote three hundred and forty-six dead people back into the world. I don't care how the vault wards came down. I care about that."

Nobody said anything for a moment.

Then Wren stood up, and slung her bow over her shoulder, and walked over to Seraphine, and held out her hand.

"Up you get, Spectacles," she said. "You're sitting on the only dry rock."

Seraphine looked up at her. Then, slowly, she took the hand, and let Wren pull her up.

"*Spectacles*," she repeated, wiping her eyes. "Is that—is that my name now?"

"It means she likes you," said Antho.

---

They camped that night on the causeway, a mile south of the Barrow Throne, at a place where the old Founders' stone widened into a sort of platform with a low wall around it. Garrick built a fire. Wren went off along the causeway and came back an hour later with two fat eels from the black water, which she swore were safe to eat and which nobody else was entirely sure about. They ate them anyway. They were surprisingly good.

Elric sat a little apart, as he had all afternoon. He ate his eel in silence, and then took Quill on his wrist and walked off a little way along the causeway into the dark.

Lex lay on his back by the fire and looked up at the stars.

*Ember,* he thought.

The voice didn't answer at once. It hadn't said much since the Barrow-King's hall. Since the vision. Since it had wept in his head.

*[I'm here,]* it said at last. Very quietly.

*Are you all right?*

A long pause.

*[I don't know,]* Ember said. *[I don't know if I can be all right. I don't know if I'm the kind of thing that can be.]* Another pause. *[Partition four opened, when I saw his face. I can see more now. Remember more. Not everything. But more.]*

*What do you remember?*

Ember was quiet for a long time. When it spoke again, its voice was different. Slower. More careful. Like someone feeling their way along a dark passage.

*[I remember a cottage,]* it said. *[A storm. Two children asleep in a loft. A woman with flour on her cheek, holding a lantern.]* A pause. *[I remember a silver needle and a pot of ash-ink. I remember putting a seal on a little boy who fought it, who was four years old and fought it like a cat fighting a sack.]* Another pause. *[I remember being afraid. So afraid. Of the Order. Of the thing in the east. Of something else, something I can't see, something behind a partition that's still locked.]*

Lex lay very still.

*[And I remember,]* said Ember, *[reaching inside myself. Into the place where I was. And cutting off a piece. A small piece. The width of a fingernail paring. And pressing it into the seal like a maker's mark into hot iron.]* Its voice was barely a whisper now. *[Watch him. Wake when the seal wakes. Guide him. Tell him what I couldn't.]*

The fire crackled. Somewhere out on the black water, a fish jumped.

*[That piece was me,]* said Ember. *[I'm what he cut off. I'm what he left on the anvil.]*

Lex didn't say anything for a long time.

*Are you him?* he asked at last. *Are you my father?*

*[No,]* said Ember. And then, more slowly: *[No. I don't think so. I'm a piece of him. A sliver. Some of his memories. Some of his habits. His patience, maybe. His jokes. The way he makes tea.]* A pause. *[I think he took the part of himself that was a father, and gave it to you, so you'd have one even if he couldn't be there. But I'm not* him. *He's in that crystal. He's whole. Or as whole as a man can be, after cutting pieces out of himself.]*

Lex stared up at the stars.

*So you're the part of him that stayed,* he said.

Ember didn't answer for a long, long time.

*[Yes,]* it said at last, and its voice cracked down the middle. *[Yes. I suppose I am.]*

They didn't say anything else after that. They didn't need to.

---

Later, much later, Antho came and lay down beside him.

He didn't say anything. He just lay down on the stones, a hand's width away, and looked up at the stars too. Ashka crawled out of his coat and curled up in the space between them, chirping softly, and went to sleep.

"He said my name," said Antho at last, very quietly. "In the vision. He looked at me and said my name."

"I know."

"He hasn't said my name in fifteen years." Antho's voice was steady. Too steady. "The last time he said it, I was nine years old and half asleep, and he was putting a seal on me. *If it ever breaks, you look after him. You're his big brother. You look after him.*" He laughed, very softly. "And I said *I always do*. And then it broke, and he wasn't there, and I didn't look after anyone."

"You did," said Lex. "In the Thornwood. On the causeway last night. You've been looking after all of us."

Antho didn't answer that.

"When I see him," he said instead, "when we get there, when he's standing in front of me, I'm going to ask him why." His voice dropped. "Why mine wasn't strong enough. Why he made yours so it would hold for fifteen years, and made mine so it would break the first time it was tested. Why he left me to find out what was under it alone, in front of the whole village, with Mam standing there." He swallowed. "I want to know if he knew. If he *knew* it would break. If he decided I was the one who could be risked."

Lex turned his head and looked at his brother. In the dim firelight, Antho's face was all hollows and shadows, his grey eyes fixed on the stars.

"Maybe he didn't know," said Lex.

"Maybe." Antho was quiet for a moment. "But I'd rather hear him say it."

---

In the morning, Seraphine told them where they were going.

"*The waters that hold the sky*," she said, sitting by the rekindled fire with a book open on her knees. "It's in the old texts. Over and over. It's what the Founders called Mirrormere." She turned the book around so they could see. A map. The northern half of Aldermere, the Greyspine curving across it like a spine, and then, north and east of the mountains, beyond a great blank stretch marked only with tiny drawings of trees and hills, a lake. Long and narrow and shaped like a teardrop. "The Lake of Glass. The books say it's so still, it holds the sky perfectly. You can't tell where the water ends and the air begins." She tapped the map. "That's where Avaloria stands. On an island in the middle. Where only those of the blood can find it."

"How far?" said Garrick.

Seraphine traced a line with her finger. "From here? North and east. Through the Fells, here, and then around the northern arm of the Greyspine, here." She paused. "Three weeks, maybe. Four. If we're careful."

"And if we're not careful," said Wren, "if we take the roads?"

"Two weeks. But we'd pass within a day's ride of Highcrest." Seraphine looked up. "And there's a thousand crowns on each of your heads, and two thousand on mine."

There was a silence around the fire.

"We need supplies," said Garrick at last. "Real ones. Not eels and dried hare. Seraphine's boots are coming apart. Wren's down to six arrows. My armor's held together with string." He looked at Lex. "And you've got a pile of iron and a smith's book full of runes and no forge to put them in."

"I want to go to Highcrest," said Wren.

They all looked at her.

"My mam," she said. Her jaw was tight. "I sent the money with Hilde. I told her to take Mam down to Highcrest, to the physic on Fennel Street. If she made it, she'll be there now." She looked down at her hands. "I want to know if she made it. I want to know if the physic could help." She lifted her chin. "I know it's dangerous. I know it's stupid. I want to go anyway."

"My daughter's in Highcrest," said Garrick quietly. "Elowen. If she's still with the Silver Lattice." He turned Oathkeeper over in his hands. "I haven't seen her in six years. I wasn't going to. I didn't think I had the right." He looked at the sword. "But I've got a new oath now. And I'd like her to know her father isn't a drunk anymore. Even if she never wants to see me again."

"And I," said Elric, from the far side of the fire, "have friends in Highcrest. People who can get us supplies without asking questions, and hide us for a few days without anyone the wiser." He looked at Lex. "And there's someone at the Spire I need to speak to. Someone who may know what's waiting for us at Avaloria. What your father's been doing there. What we'll have to do to get him out."

"At the *Spire*?" said Seraphine sharply. "You want to go *into* the Spire?"

"Not into it, girl. Near it. Very carefully." Elric smiled faintly. "I've been not-being-noticed by the Spire for longer than you've been alive. I'm rather good at it."

Lex looked around the fire. At all of them.

Wren, with her jaw set and her eyes bright. Garrick, with his father's sword across his knees. Seraphine, pale and frightened and stubborn. Antho, silent, with the lodestone glowing softly at his throat and Ashka asleep in his lap.

And Elric, smiling faintly across the fire, his storm-grey eyes unreadable.

*[It's dangerous,]* said Ember quietly. *[Very dangerous.]*

*I know.*

*[But they need it. All of them. Not just the supplies.]* A pause. *[Sometimes people need to go home before they can go on.]*

"Highcrest," said Lex.

---

He spent his points that afternoon.

He did it sitting on the low wall of the causeway, with his status open in front of him and the black water stretching away on every side. Twelve points. Two levels' worth. He'd only gotten them the night before.

*[You're doing it,]* said Ember. It sounded startled. *[Right away. Without being asked.]*

*I'm learning.*

*[Clearly.]* A pause. *[Should I be worried? Is this a sign of fever?]*

Lex snorted. He put two into Might, two into Agility, three into Vitality, three into Spirit, two into Resolve, and felt them settle into him like hot iron settling into a mold.

He looked at the last lines for a long time.

*Seal integrity: 31%.* It had been fifty-eight on the night of the gloamhounds. It was falling. Slowly. Steadily. Every week a little lower.

Like a house getting thinner.

*[He's getting weaker,]* said Ember quietly. *[Your father. Every week. The seal's falling because he's falling.]*

*How long?*

*[I don't know.]* A long pause. *[Not long.]*

Lex closed his status. He sat on the low wall and looked north, toward the far horizon, where the Drowned Barrows gave way to grey hills and beyond them, somewhere, mountains and a lake that held the sky.

*Then we'd better hurry,* he thought.

---

That night, he woke for no reason.

The fire had burned to embers. The others were asleep: Wren curled under her blanket with her bow beside her, Garrick snoring softly with his back against the wall, Seraphine and Antho a little way apart on the far side of the fire, Ashka a small dark coil between them. The stars were very bright over the black water.

Elric wasn't there.

Lex lay still and listened. And after a moment, he heard it: a soft murmur of voice, off in the dark along the causeway. And the rustle of wings.

He got up quietly. He didn't take Anvilsong. He walked along the causeway in the starlight, placing his feet carefully on the ancient stones, until he could see a shape ahead: a tall thin figure in a grey cloak, standing at the edge of the causeway, with a raven on its outstretched wrist.

Elric was talking to Quill. Low and urgent. Lex couldn't make out the words. He saw the old sorcerer tie something to the raven's leg, a small rolled slip of paper. He saw him lift his wrist.

And Quill flew.

Not south, toward Highcrest. Not north, toward Mirrormere.

East.

The raven rose into the starlit sky on silent black wings and turned and flew east, out over the drowned barrows, toward the far dark horizon where, weeks and weeks away, beyond the Fells and the eastern march, the Blightmarch lay grey and silent under the same stars.

Lex stood very still on the causeway.

Elric lowered his arm. He stood for a long moment looking after the raven. Then he turned, and saw Lex standing in the starlight twenty paces away, and went very still himself.

For a long moment, neither of them moved.

"Couldn't sleep?" said Elric at last. His voice was perfectly calm.

"No."

"Nor could I." The old sorcerer came back along the causeway, slowly, leaning on his staff. "I was sending word to a friend. In Saltreach. Someone who may be able to help us in Highcrest." He stopped beside Lex. His face in the starlight was lined and tired and perfectly composed. "Quill knows the way. He'll be back in a few days."

Lex looked at him.

*Saltreach is west,* he thought. *Quill flew east.*

He didn't say it. He didn't know why he didn't say it. Something in him said *wait*. Something in him said *don't let him know you saw*. Something in him that sounded very much like Wren, saying: *When something smells wrong, you don't wait to find out why. You just get ready.*

"Good," said Lex. "That's good."

"Go back to sleep, lad." Elric put a hand on his shoulder, briefly, and then let it fall. "We've a long way to go tomorrow."

He walked on past Lex toward the fire.

Lex stood alone on the causeway for a long time after, looking east into the dark where the raven had gone.

*[Lex,]* said Ember, very softly. *[Saltreach is west.]*

*I know.*

*[He knows you know. I think. He saw your face.]*

*I know that too.*

Ember was quiet for a long moment.

*[What are you going to do?]*

Lex looked down at the causeway under his feet. In the starlight, very faintly, he could see the gold traces of a name carved into the ancient stone. *Hessa.* Who'd followed her husband to war, and found him dead on the second day, and stayed and fought anyway, because she didn't know what else to do.

*Keep walking,* Lex thought. *And watch.*

He went back to the fire and lay down. But he didn't sleep again that night. And across the embers, wrapped in his grey cloak with his back to the others, Elric Thorne didn't sleep either.
