# The Unbound Forge

They all came to watch.

Lex hadn't asked them to. He'd told Malachi, the night before, that he was going to forge at dawn, and he'd gone to sleep by the eternal fire in the hut, and when he woke in the grey light before sunrise, they were all already up. Wren, sitting cross-legged by the cold hearth with her bow across her knees. Antho, leaning against the wall with Ashka draped over his shoulders like a blue-grey scarf the size of a pony. Seraphine, with her pen and her stack of paper and her cracked spectacles. Malachi, peeling an apple.

"We're coming," said Wren. "Don't argue."

"I wasn't going to argue."

"You were going to say *it's dangerous* and *I don't know what will happen* and *you should stay back*." She stood up. "You can say all that on the way."

---

The forge in the cave behind the ivy was burning when they got there.

Lex had left it burning the night before. Gold and steady and clear, on the bare white stone of the hearth, without fuel or spark. It had burned all night. It leaned toward him the moment he ducked under the ivy.

He went to the anvil. Aurelian's anvil. Black iron, pitted and scarred. He laid Aurelian's little hand-hammer on it, beside Anvilsong. Two hammers. One nine hundred years old. One three months old. Both with an open hand and a flame struck into their heads.

"How?" said Seraphine quietly. She'd sat down on a rock near the mouth of the cave with her paper on her knees. "How do you forge yourself? I've been thinking about it all night. You can't put yourself on an anvil."

"No," said Lex. "But I can put the anvil in myself."

She stared at him.

He didn't know how to explain it. He'd been thinking about it all night too, lying by the eternal fire, listening to the others breathe. Thinking about the place under his breastbone where the seal was. Where the fire had been banked for fifteen years. Where Elric had slammed an iron door and bolted it.

It was a forge. He'd always thought of it that way, without knowing he did. *A fire with a forge-door bolted over it*, Elric had said, that first night in Ashbourne. And Ember had said it too, in the Frostvault. And Arion, in the prologue of everything, kneeling over a four-year-old boy who fought the seal like a cat in a sack, had thought *it has to be a forge-door. Iron, banded, locked, with the fire still roaring behind it.*

Everyone had always known it was a forge. Except him.

"I'm going to go in," said Lex. "To the place where the lock is. The way I go into the fire when I listen to it. Down. Under." He looked at the gold flame in the hearth. "And I'm going to take this with me."

---

He knelt in front of the hearth.

He laid his left hand flat on Aurelian's anvil. He picked up Aurelian's little hammer in his right. He looked into the fire.

And he went down.

It wasn't like Listening. Listening was quiet. Listening was sitting by the pool with his eyes closed until the running thoughts stopped and he could hear the fire on the other side of the wall. This was the opposite. This was *going through the wall*. Down past the quiet, past the place where the Wells were, down into the place where *he* was.

He'd never been there before. Not on purpose.

It was dark. Then it wasn't. It was a forge.

He knew it at once. Every stone of it. It was Brannoc's forge, in Ashbourne. The big brick hearth. The rack of tools. The slack tub. The brick ledge where Slag slept. Except it wasn't quite Brannoc's forge. It was bigger. Much bigger. The ceiling went up and up into darkness, and the walls went back and back, and there were things on the racks that Brannoc had never owned: the *Book of Making*, open; Wren's bow; a little clay lamp with a flame burning in it; a bone flute; a rune-struck lantern, shattered.

And the hearth was dark.

There was no fire in it. There was a door.

A great iron door, set into the brick of the hearth where the fire should be. Black iron, banded with gold and black, riveted, studded. Covered all over in glyphs. And across the middle of it, holding it shut, a lock. A massive iron lock, the size of a man's chest, shaped like a fist. And carved into the fist, in letters of black and stolen gold:

*LOCKED. — E.T.*

Lex stood in front of the iron door in the dark forge in the middle of himself, with Aurelian's little hammer in his hand, and looked at it.

He could feel the fire on the other side. His fire. Roaring. Trapped. Hammering at the inside of the door like a fist on a coffin lid.

And under the roar, faint, very faint, so faint he almost didn't hear it, something else.

A voice. Dry. Calm. Courteous. Muffled, as if speaking through a pillow.

*[…Lex? Lex, is that you? I can hear you. I can hear you walking around out there. Lex?]*

He put his free hand flat on the iron door.

*I'm here,* he thought. *I'm here, Ember. I'm going to get you out.*

*[Oh,]* said the muffled voice. And it sounded, for the first time since he'd known it, like it was crying. *[Oh, thank goodness. It's so dark in here, Lex. It's so dark and I couldn't find you.]*

---

In the cave behind the ivy, kneeling in front of the gold fire with his eyes closed and his hand on the anvil and the little hammer raised, Lex began to strike.

The others saw it. Seraphine wrote it down, afterward, every detail, in her neat cramped hand.

He struck the empty air above the anvil. Again and again. *Tink. Tink. Tink.* The little hammer coming down on nothing, stopping a finger's width above the black iron, rising again. And every time it came down, the gold fire in the hearth *flared*. And every time it flared, something went across Lex's face. Pain. Effort. Strain. Sweat stood out on his forehead. His jaw clenched.

"*It's not working*," he said. His eyes were still closed. His voice was hoarse, strange, coming from very far away. "*The fire's not hot enough. I can't get it hot enough. The lock's too cold. It's Root-cold.*"

"What do you need?" said Malachi quietly.

"*Air. I need air. Like bellows. It needs more air.*"

Wren was already moving.

She went and knelt beside him at the hearth. She didn't ask what to do. She closed her eyes, and lifted her face, and *listened*. Lex felt her do it. Felt it through the fellowship, through whatever was between them. Felt her reach for the wind.

And the wind came.

It came down through the chimney carved into the rock. It came in under the ivy curtain. It came from everywhere, a rushing roaring gust, and poured into the hearth like air from a giant's bellows.

The fire *roared*.

It went from gold to white. It went up in a column that filled the hearth and licked up the chimney and set the ivy at the cave mouth smoking. And in the dark forge in the middle of himself, Lex felt the heat come up behind him like the sun rising. Felt the iron door in front of him begin to glow.

Red. Then orange. The glyphs on it burning. The gold-and-black bands beginning to soften.

*Strike.*

He struck. The little hammer came down on the iron fist of the lock. *CHANG.* The sound went through the dark forge like a bell. A crack ran across the fist.

---

"*Where*?" he gasped, in the cave. "*Where do I hit it? There are glyphs on it. On the lock. I can see them but I can't read them. They're Root-script. Elric's script. I don't know where the weak place is.*"

Seraphine dropped her pen.

She came and knelt on his other side. She leaned close to his face, close to his closed eyes, as if she could see what he was seeing.

"Describe them," she said. "The glyphs. Exactly. Shape by shape. I'll read them."

He described them. His voice was cracked and strained. *A shape like a fist. A shape like a key turned backward. A shape like a coiled snake.* Seraphine listened with her eyes shut and her lips moving, translating, as fast as he spoke.

"*Lock*," she said. "*Bind*. *Root-held*. *Stolen*—there. Lex. The fourth glyph, the snake. That's the Hollow-graft. That's how he tied stolen Root to his own will. It's the weakest part. It's not *his*. It's borrowed." She opened her eyes. "Hit the snake."

*CHANG.*

The snake glyph cracked. The crack spread through the iron fist like a fracture through ice.

The lock screamed.

It wasn't a sound. Not in the cave. But in the dark forge, it was a shriek like metal tearing, and the iron fist *writhed*, and black smoke poured out of the crack, and in the smoke, a face. Elric's face. Grey and old and twisted with grief and rage.

*You can't,* it said. *You can't. I locked it with your father's own Root. Nothing in the Lattice can—*

*I'm not using the Lattice,* said Lex.

He struck again.

---

And Antho.

Lex felt his brother before he saw him. Through the Bloodbond. The cold deep pool, stirring. Coming. Reaching.

The iron door was glowing white-hot now. The lock was cracked through and through, black smoke pouring from it, Elric's face screaming silently in the smoke. And on the other side of the door, Lex's fire was hammering, hammering, the whole door shaking on its hinges.

But it was too hot.

He could feel it. The door was going to *burst*. Not open. Burst. Shatter. Like a cracked crucible in the forge. And when it burst, everything behind it would come out at once. Fifteen years of fire. All at once. And it would take him with it. And Ember. And the cave. And maybe the valley.

*[Lex,]* said Ember, through the door, faint and frightened. *[Lex, it's too hot. It's too fast. You're rushing the quench.]*

*Mind the quench.*

He knew. He knew, and he couldn't stop. The fire was too big and too hungry and it had been locked away too long, and it wanted *out*.

And then something cool touched his back.

A hand. Long-fingered. Scarred. Cold as a winter night. It lay flat between his shoulder blades in the dark forge in the middle of himself. And from it, flowing into him, came *dark*. Not the Gloam. Not the hunger. *Shade*. Soft and deep and peaceful, like the sky between the stars. Like the night holding a fire safe until morning.

*I keep the door*, said Antho's voice. In his head. Through the Bloodbond. *That's what I do. Let me keep this one.*

The cool flowed into the white-hot iron. Not putting the fire out. *Holding* it. Steadying it. The way the night holds a banked hearth, the way cold water holds hot steel in the quench: not rushing, not fighting, just *there*, drawing off the excess, letting the metal settle into its temper.

The door stopped shaking.

The roar behind it dropped. From a scream to a growl. From a growl to a deep steady thrum. The sound of a forge with its bellows working in rhythm. The sound of a fire that knows it's going to be let out, and is willing to wait for it.

*Now*, said Antho. *Now, Lex. Gently.*

Lex raised Aurelian's little hammer.

*Tink.*

Not a blow. A tap. The lightest touch. The way you'd tap a finished blade to hear it ring. On the very center of the cracked iron fist, on the snake.

The lock fell apart.

It didn't shatter. It didn't burst. It simply *came apart*, the way a well-made thing comes apart when you find the right place to touch it. The iron fist split along its cracks into a dozen pieces, and the pieces fell, and dissolved in the air before they hit the floor of the dark forge, and Elric's face in the smoke went with them, and was gone.

And the iron door swung open.

---

The fire came out like dawn.

Not in a blast. Not in a column. It came out of the open hearth like the sun coming up over the edge of the world: slow, steady, unstoppable, gold. It filled the dark forge. It filled the high dark ceiling and the deep dark walls. It lit every tool on every rack. The *Book of Making*. Wren's bow. Seren's little lamp. The bone flute. The shattered lantern.

And it filled *him*.

He felt it come back. Every scrap of it. The heat in his chest. The strength in his arms. The quickness in his legs. All the points he'd spent and all the levels he'd climbed and all the skills he'd learned. Ember Strike. Heat Shaping. Ironskin. Slag Shot. Kindle. Runic Forging. Forge Sense. Pouring back into him like water into a dry riverbed.

But not the same.

*Different.*

Because it wasn't coming through the wheel anymore. Not only. It was coming from the river *and* the wheel. Together. Braided. The Lattice and the Well, gold and gold, so tangled together that he couldn't tell where one ended and the other began.

*[…Lex?]*

The voice was right there. Not muffled. Not through a door. In the back of his head, where it had always been. Dry. Calm. Courteous. Shaking.

*[Lex? Is that—are you—why is it so* warm*? It's never been this warm before.]*

He laughed. It came out of him like a sob.

*Ember*.

*[Hello, little smith,]* said Ember. *[I'm so sorry I wasn't there. I couldn't find you. It was so dark.]* A pause. *[I could hear you, you know. Through the door. Every time you said my name. Every single time.]* Another pause, and the voice cracked. *[You said it a great many times.]*

*I missed you*, Lex thought.

*[I know,]* said Ember softly. *[I heard that too.]*

---

He opened his eyes.

He was kneeling in front of the hearth in the cave behind the ivy. The gold fire was burning steady and clear. Aurelian's little hammer was in his right hand, resting on the black iron anvil. His left hand was flat beside it.

Wren was kneeling on his left, her eyes still closed, her face lifted, her fox-red braid streaming in a wind that was slowly dying. Seraphine was kneeling on his right, her cracked spectacles askew, her pen forgotten on the floor. And Antho was behind him, on his knees, with his scarred hand flat between Lex's shoulder blades.

And they were all staring at him.

"*Your eyes*," whispered Wren.

He didn't need to ask. He could feel it. The gold behind them. The heat.

His panel came up on its own. It didn't wait to be called. It just *opened*, the way a door opens when someone's been waiting behind it, and the light that came off it was brighter than it had ever been. Gold and gold. Root-gold and something else. Something warmer. Something older.

And beside the cave mouth, leaning on his staff in the ivy, Malachi was looking at them. All four of them. Kneeling in a row in front of a nine-hundred-year-old hearth that hadn't burned in nine hundred years until last night.

His shifting eyes were wet.

"Bellows," he said softly. "And a reader. And a quench." He shook his head slowly. "He tried to do it alone, you know. My brother. Every single thing. He tried to do every single thing alone." He wiped his eyes with the back of his gnarled hand. "Took you four of you. And a smith." He laughed, shaky. "That's the answer to the Oracle's question, smith. That's how you mend a net from inside. Not one pair of hands." He looked at the four of them. "*All* of them."

---

Lex found Wren on the cliff that afternoon.

The one at the rim of the valley, where Malachi had made her step off into the wind. She was sitting on the edge with her legs dangling over the forty-foot drop, looking out over the white salt plain to the horizon. She'd been there since noon. She'd gone very quiet after the forge.

He sat down beside her.

"You were the bellows," he said.

"I was the bellows." She didn't look at him. "I didn't know I could do that. Call the wind like that. Make it go where I wanted." She was quiet for a moment. "It felt like climbing. Like the best climb I've ever done. Like reaching for a handhold you can't see and finding it there."

"It *was* there."

"I know." She looked down at her hands. "It's been there my whole life. Malachi says so. I just never knew." She laughed, very softly. "Twenty-four. Capped. *Further experience will not be recorded.* And the whole time, there was a whole other thing under it. A whole other mountain."

Lex looked at her.

"Wren," he said.

She turned her head.

"I've got thirty-one Authority," he said. "It's coming back fast. Four times faster than it used to, Ember says. Because the Wells are feeding it." He took a breath. "And Ember says Unbind's not locked anymore."

Wren went very still.

"It said Tier Three and sixty Authority, before," said Lex. "In the temple. But that was the wheel's rule. The wheel's lock for the wheel's key." He looked at her. "Ember says the Wells change it. It says someone who's Answering a Well is already halfway out of the cage. The Ceiling's only on the wheel. And you're not only on the wheel anymore. You've got Gale."

"How much?" said Wren. Her voice was barely there.

"Thirty," said Lex. "It costs thirty."

She stared at him.

"You've got thirty-one," she said.

"I know."

"You'd have one left. One. If anything happened. If something came out of the salt at us tonight, if Morvane sent something—"

"I know."

"*Hammer*—"

"You told me not to make promises for you," said Lex. "On the moor. Before the storm." He held her gaze. "You said make them for me. Not for you." He swallowed. "So here's one for me. I want to see you fly. Not for a heartbeat off a cliff. *Fly*. Like in the mirror. That's what I want." He reached out and took her hand. "And I want it *with* you. Not for you. With you. Is that all right?"

Wren looked at him for a long, long time. Her green eyes were very bright. Her jaw was very tight.

"You're an idiot, Hammer," she said. Her voice cracked down the middle. "You're the biggest idiot I ever met."

"I know."

"Do it," she said.

---

He did it standing on the rim of the cliff, in the late afternoon light, with the wind coming off the white salt plain and stirring her braid.

He didn't know how. Nobody had ever told him. Ember had only said *Unbind breaks a Ceiling*. That was all. So he did it the way he did everything. Like a smith.

He put his palm flat on her chest, over her heart. The way he'd put it on Antho's in the Thornwood, the way Antho had put it on his in the dark forge that morning. He felt her heart under it, hammering.

He reached down past the silver. Past the gold. Down to the place where the river ran.

And he looked at her.

Not with True Sight. Deeper. With the Wells. With Flame and Stone, the way he'd look at a bar of iron before he decided what to make of it. And he saw her. Not her panel. *Her*. A girl made of wind and stubbornness and freckles, who'd climbed every mountain she'd ever seen and never once looked down. And around her, like a band of iron around a barrel, like a hoop around a wheel, a Ceiling.

Grey. Plain. Indifferent. A flat line of silver-grey, drawn tight around her at the level of twenty-four. Holding her in. Holding her down.

It wasn't the Lattice's. He could see that now. The Lattice under it was gold, Founders' gold, Aurelian's gold, and it went up and up forever, the way the Founders had meant it to. The grey line had been drawn *on top* of it. Later. By someone else. A Warden, three hundred years ago, with a pen and a ledger and a reason.

*It's just a band*, he thought. *A hoop. Someone hammered it on. I can hammer it off.*

He used Unbind.

He felt the thirty points of Authority go out of him in a single rush. Felt them go down his arm, through his palm, into her. Felt them meet the grey band around her, the Ceiling, the hoop of iron.

And he *struck*.

Not with a hammer. With himself. With Flame, answering. With his Root, unlocked. With every bit of fire he had and every bit of love, the way he'd struck the iron door that morning, the way he'd struck the snake glyph on Elric's lock.

The grey band *broke*.

---

Wren gasped.

She arched back, her eyes flying open, her hands coming up to clutch at his wrist. A sound came out of her that wasn't a word. And the wind, the wind that had been stirring her braid, *roared*. It came up off the white salt plain in a great gust and swept up the cliff face and around them, lifting her braid straight up, billowing her coat, pulling at both of them like a giant's hand.

Over her head, a panel exploded open. Silver at first. Then cracking. Then something else showing through. Not gold, not Root. *Pale*. Pale blue-grey, like the sky over a mountain at dawn.

Not stepped. *Jumped*. Out. Off the rim. Arms spread wide, braid streaming, into the roaring wind.

It didn't drift her down this time, like a leaf. It took her *up*. Lifted her. Carried her. *For the first time in three hundred years.*

*[And Morvane,]* said Ember. *[And Elric.]*

---

Wren came down at sunset.

She came down slowly, spiraling, the wind lowering her gently, until her boots touched the grass on the rim of the cliff beside him. Her face was wind-burned and wet and glowing. Her braid had come completely undone, and her fox-red hair was a wild tangle around her head.

She didn't say anything. She just walked up to him and put her arms around his neck and kissed him. Long and slow. The way he'd kissed her on the stair at Avaloria.

When she pulled back, she was smiling. The real one. The one that snuck up on her.

"*With* you," she said.

"With you."

"Not for me."

"Not for you."

"Good." She leaned her forehead against his. "Because I'm going to pay you back, Hammer. Every single point. I'm going to fly so high and fight so hard and find so many ways where there's no way that you'll be sick of me."

"I'll never be sick of you."

"You will. I'm very annoying. Ask anyone." She laughed. It was shaky. "Thirty-one. Lady of Lanterns. *Thirty-one.*"

Behind them, someone coughed.

They broke apart. Malachi was standing a little way off, leaning on his staff. Antho and Seraphine were behind him. Antho was grinning, a real grin, the crooked one that was exactly like Lex's. Seraphine had her pen out and was writing furiously.

"Very touching," said Malachi. "Very romantic. I'm delighted for you both." He was smiling too. But his shifting eyes had gone a color Lex didn't like. Grey. Storm-grey. "You do know what you've just done, though. Don't you."

Lex looked at him.

"You've just told every person in the world that the Ceiling can be broken," said Malachi quietly. "Every capped smith and every capped miner and every capped farmer from here to the sea. And the High Warden. And the thing in the east with the white face."

Nobody said anything.

"They'll be coming," said Malachi. "All of them. Some to beg you to do it for them. Some to kill you before you can." He looked at Lex. "You've done a very good thing, smith. A very brave thing. A thing my brother should have done nine hundred years ago." His voice was quiet. "And now you'd better go. Because this valley isn't hidden anymore. Not from anyone."

Lex looked east. Over the white salt plain. Toward the far horizon where, weeks and weeks away, a black fortress stood around a wound in the world, and a man in a crystal was being slowly unpicked by a thing with a white face.

"Then we go," he said.

"Where?"

He thought about it.

He thought about Morvane's army, massing. About the three hundred at Fort Caldris who'd become one hundred, scattered across the hills. About Teodra Hale saying *get your fire back, then send for me*. About every capped smith and miner and farmer in Aldermere, looking at a World Notice that said *a Ceiling has been broken*.

"Home," he said. "To Ashbourne." He looked at Wren. At Antho. At Seraphine. "And every village between here and there."

"To do what?" said Antho.

Lex looked down at his hands. At the burn scars. At the calluses. At the faint gold glow that came off his skin now, all the time, steady, like a hearth that would never go out.

"To raise an army," he said.
