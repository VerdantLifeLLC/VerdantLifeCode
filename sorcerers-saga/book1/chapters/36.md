# Duskhold

Sorrowmaw saw them halfway down.

Lex felt it before he saw it. A change in the air. A cold that came up from below like water rising in a well. And then the vast grey shape that had been circling lazily over the black towers *turned*. Its great dead wings tilted. Its long neck swung round. And its blank white eyes, each the size of a cartwheel, fixed on the blue-grey dragon diving out of the dusk.

It opened its jaws.

"*Ashka—*" Antho shouted.

The Gloam came out of Sorrowmaw's mouth in a torrent. Black and oily and endless, a river of it, pouring up through the darkening air toward them like smoke up a chimney. Lex threw up the gold shell of heat around them. Felt it take the first of the black flood. Felt it *strain*.

And Ashka didn't dodge.

She went straight into it.

She folded her wings tight and plunged into the black torrent like a diver into a lake, and Lex felt the gold shell shudder and buckle around them, felt the Gloam hissing and boiling against it on every side, felt Seraphine screaming into his back and Garrick's good hand clamped on his shoulder like a vise. Black all around. Black and cold and roaring. He couldn't see. He couldn't breathe.

And then they were through.

Out the other side of the black river, into clear air, with the vast grey shape of Sorrowmaw directly in front of them. So close Lex could see the cracks in its grey scales. The black seeping from them. The white eyes, blank and enormous, staring.

Ashka screamed.

Not a battle cry. Not a roar. The high thin plaintive cry Lex had heard at Avaloria. The cry of a hatchling calling.

*Father. Father. Father.*

And Sorrowmaw *stopped*.

It hung in the air above the black towers, its great wings beating slow and silent, and its white eyes fixed on the little blue-grey dragon in front of its face. And something moved in them. Deep in the white. A flicker. Gold. A deep molten amber, slit-pupiled like a cat's.

The way it had at Avaloria. For one heartbeat.

But this time it didn't go away.

It *flickered*. Gold, then white. Gold, then white. Like a candle in a draft. Like a man trying to wake from a nightmare.

*[Lex,]* said Ember. *[He's fighting it. The Gloam. He's fighting it. Like Antho fights the thief.]*

"*Ashka*," said Antho. His voice was very quiet. He was leaning forward on her neck, his hands buried in her crest. "*Ashka. Put us down. On the tower. The one by the hall. Then go to him.*"

The dragon twisted her long neck around and looked at him. Her golden eyes were enormous and wet.

"*Go to him*," said Antho. "*He's in there. He's still in there. I can hear him. Shade can hear him.*" His voice cracked. "*Go and remind him who he is. The way Lex reminded me.*"

---

She put them down on the top of a black tower at the edge of the great round hall.

There was no parapet. No battlement. Just a flat glassy top of black Gloam-stone, slippery and cold, leaning out at a sickening angle over the hall below. They slid off her back one by one: Antho, Lex, Seraphine, Garrick. Wren stepped off the air beside them.

And Ashka leaped back into the sky.

Lex watched her go. Up toward the vast grey shape hanging over the fortress. A small blue-grey dragon, barely a tenth its size, flying straight up at it with her silver-framed wing beating hard and her cry going out across the grey sky. *Father. Father. Father.*

Sorrowmaw turned to meet her.

Lex didn't see what happened next. Antho grabbed his arm.

"*Lex*." His brother's voice was a whisper. "*Lex, look down.*"

He looked down.

---

The great hall of Duskhold was round, and vast, and had no roof.

From the top of the tower, Lex could see all of it. The black walls curving away on every side, glassy and smooth. The floor, far below, black too. And standing on the floor, in rows, in rings, filling the whole hall from wall to wall: the Quiet. Thousands of them. Grey and white-eyed and motionless. All facing inward.

Toward the center.

In the center of the hall, the floor simply ended. A pit. Ragged. Torn. Black. So black that the eye slid off it and wouldn't stay. Cold air rose out of it in slow waves that Lex could feel even from the top of the tower. And the smell. Wet ash. Rot. Nothing.

The Vhessa breach.

And on the edge of the pit, on a great black plinth that had grown up out of the floor like a fungus, stood the crystal.

It wasn't clear anymore. It was almost entirely black. Black veins had spread through it so thickly that only a few faint faceted patches of clear crystal still showed, like windows in a burned house. And through them, Lex could see his father. Still frozen halfway out. Head and shoulder and one arm outside the crystal, reaching. The rest inside. His face grey. His eyes open.

His eyes were closed.

They'd been open. At Caldris, and in every report since. *Open and fixed*. Now they were closed.

"*Ninety-four*," whispered Seraphine. She was lying flat on the glassy tower-top beside Lex, peering over the edge through her cracked spectacles. "Lex. The graft's at ninety-four. When it hits a hundred—"

"I know."

"We have minutes. *Minutes*."

Lex looked down at the hall. At the thousands of Quiet. At the black pit. At the white figure with his hand on the crystal.

There was no way down. The tower's sides were sheer, glassy black, two hundred feet to the hall floor. No stair. No ladder. No doors. Duskhold had no doors.

"Wren," he said.

She was already moving. She stepped off the edge of the tower into the air, and caught the wind, and held out her hand.

"One at a time," she said. "I can carry one at a time. Down to the floor." She looked at the thousands of Quiet below. "Somewhere near the crystal. Somewhere clear."

"There isn't anywhere clear."

"Then I'll *make* somewhere clear."

---

She took Garrick first. Because, she said, if there was going to be a fight at the bottom, someone should be standing on the steps when the rest of them got there.

The old knight didn't argue. He took her hand with his good one and stepped off the tower into nothing, and his face went the color of ash, and he swore very quietly and very continuously all the way down. Wren lowered him through the cold air in a long spiral, down toward the floor of the hall, toward a place near the black plinth where the Quiet stood thinnest.

And as they came down, the Quiet turned their heads.

All of them. At once. Thousands of grey faces with white eyes, turning upward, toward the two figures coming down out of the sky. In perfect silence.

And then the silence *broke*.

Not a scream. Not a roar. A whisper. From every one of them. Rising up out of the hall like steam out of a cauldron.

*[bright … bright … quiet them … quiet the bright ones … quiet …]*

They began to move.

---

Garrick hit the floor and drew Oathkeeper in one motion. Blue-white fire ran along the blade. Frost bloomed from it. And the first grey hands that reached for him shattered into splinters and didn't rise again.

*Bind.*

Wren went back up for Seraphine. Then Antho. Each time, the Quiet surged toward the place where they landed, and Garrick met them, and held.

And then she came back for Lex.

He took her hand. He stepped off the edge of the tower. And for one long moment he was falling through the cold dark air above the hall of Duskhold, with the black pit below him and the white figure at its edge and the thousands of grey faces turned upward, and nothing holding him up but Wren's small hard hand and the wind she was calling.

"*Don't look down*," she said. "*Climber's rule.*"

He looked at her instead. At her windburned freckled face, set and fierce. At her fox-red braid streaming upward in the air.

"*I love you*," he said.

He hadn't meant to. It just came out. The way the fourth rune had come out on the anvil in Ashbourne.

Wren looked at him. Her green eyes went very wide.

"*Now?*" she said. "*You're telling me NOW?*"

"*It seemed like a good time.*"

"*We're FALLING into a FORTRESS full of—*"

"*I know.*"

She stared at him. Then she laughed. Wild and helpless, in the middle of the dark air above Duskhold.

"*You're an idiot, Hammer*," she said. "*I love you too.*"

Their feet hit the black floor.

---

The silence came down on them like a lid.

Lex felt it the moment his boots touched the black stone. Duskhold's silence. The silence Elric had walked through for weeks. It pressed against his ears. It filled his mouth. When he opened it to shout to Garrick, nothing came out. The sound died an inch from his lips.

He could hear the whisper of the Quiet. Only that. Rising and falling around them like wind in dead grass. *[quiet … quiet … quiet …]* The fortress let its own voice through. Nothing else.

Garrick was fighting in silence. Oathkeeper rising and falling, blue-white and gold. No clang of steel. No grunt of effort. Just the whisper, and the cold, and the grey hands reaching.

Lex looked at Antho. His brother's mouth was moving. Nothing came out.

*Bloodbond*, Lex thought. And reached.

*[Can you hear me?]*

*[Yes.]* Antho's voice. In his head. Clear as a bell. *[Yes. Through the Bloodbond. It can't stop that.]*

*[Ember?]*

*[I'm here,]* said Ember. *[It can't stop me either. I'm on the inside.]*

Lex looked at Wren. At Seraphine. At Garrick. They had no Bloodbond. No voice in their heads. Only hands and eyes.

Seraphine was already moving. She had a stick of charcoal in her hand, she always had charcoal, and she was writing on the black floor. Big letters. *CRYSTAL. WARDS. NEED 5 MIN. COVER ME.*

Garrick read it over his shoulder. He nodded. He turned and planted his feet in front of her, between her and the Quiet, with Oathkeeper raised. *On the steps.*

Wren nocked an arrow. Kindled it with a thought from Lex, gold fire running down the shaft. She turned to face the other way.

And Lex and Antho turned toward the crystal.

---

Morvane hadn't moved.

He was standing exactly where he'd been, with his white hand on the black crystal, the black threads running from his fingers into it and the silver and gold threads running out of it into him. His back was to them. He hadn't turned around. He hadn't looked up when they came down out of the sky.

But he spoke.

His voice was the only voice in Duskhold that made a sound. Soft. Reasonable. Pleasant. Coming from everywhere at once, the way the whisper of the Quiet did.

"*Hello, boys*," said Morvane. "*I was wondering when you'd come.*"

He still didn't turn.

"*You're too late, I'm afraid*," he said. "*Ninety-five percent. Ninety-six. He's letting go, at last. Your father. He held on so long. Longer than anyone could. But he's letting go now.*" His voice was almost tender. "*He's very tired. He wants to rest. I'm going to let him rest.*"

Lex walked toward him across the black floor. Antho beside him. The Quiet parted before them. Not attacking. Just *stepping back*, the way they'd stepped back on Ashbourne's green from the remembering anvil. Making a path. A long straight path through the grey rows, from where they stood to the plinth at the edge of the pit.

As if Morvane *wanted* them to come.

*[It's a trap,]* said Ember. *[Lex. It's obviously a trap.]*

*[I know.]*

*[You're walking into it anyway.]*

*[He's my father.]*

*[I know,]* said Ember softly. *[I know. I'm just saying.]*

They were halfway down the path when the lightning hit them.

---

It came from the side. From the black wall of the hall, where an archway opened into one of the corridors without doors. Gold lightning. Root-gold. It struck the floor between Lex and Antho and the black stone *exploded*, and they were flung apart, Lex one way and Antho the other, rolling, sliding across the glassy floor.

Lex came up on one knee with Anvilsong in his hands.

Elric Thorne was standing in the archway.

He looked old. Older than Lex had ever seen him. Bent. Grey. Hollow-cheeked, as if he hadn't eaten in weeks. His grey cloak hung off him like a sack. The brass storm-charms on it were dull and tarnished. His lightning-scarred staff was in his hand, and its head was crackling with gold.

And his eyes.

His eyes were the eyes of a man who hasn't slept since he did something terrible.

His lips moved. No sound came out. Duskhold took it. But Lex could read them. He'd been reading lips since the vision in the Barrow-King's hall.

*I'm sorry.*

He raised his staff again.

---

Lex didn't want to fight him.

That was the strangest part. He'd imagined this, every night since Caldris. Lying awake in the ruined tower, in the Pale Wastes, in the Stillwater. Imagined finding Elric. Imagined the old man's face when Lex walked up to him with his fire back and his class unlocked and Ember in his head. Imagined hitting him. Burning him. Making him *pay*.

Now he was here. And Elric was standing in the archway, old and grey and hollow, with gold lightning crackling on his staff and *I'm sorry* on his lips.

And Lex didn't want to fight him.

The lightning came anyway.

He threw up Ironskin. Gold heat over his skin, Well-fed now, thicker than it had ever been. The lightning hit it and *splashed*, like water on a hot forge, gold on gold, and scattered across the black floor in crackling streams. He felt it through the heat. Felt the stolen Root in it. His father's Root.

He stood up.

Elric's eyes went wide.

He struck again. Harder. Lightning poured off his staff in a torrent, gold and blue-white, the stolen Root and his own Stormcaller power together. Lex took it on Anvilsong's head. The hammer *drank* it. The runes blazed. *Ardent*. Held heat. Held lightning. The hammer glowed white in his hands.

He walked toward Elric through the storm.

The old sorcerer backed away. Into the archway. Into the corridor. His face was white. His lips were moving. Lex couldn't read them now; Elric was talking too fast, too frantically.

Lex reached him.

He didn't swing the hammer. He reached out with his free hand and took Elric's staff, the lightning-scarred ash staff with the gold crackling on its head, and *pulled* it out of the old man's grip. As easily as taking a stick from a child.

Elric staggered back against the wall of the corridor.

Lex held the staff. He looked at it. At the lightning dying on its head. At the brass storm-charms on the cloak of the man in front of him.

Then he reached for Root. For the Lattice. For the place under the air where the stolen gold burned in Elric's chest like a swallowed coal.

*[What are you doing?]* said Ember.

*[Looking,]* said Lex.

He used True Sight.
