# The Ethereal Father

For a long moment, nobody said anything at all.

Lex stood on the black glass floor with his brother's hand crushed in his and looked at the shape of light standing in front of the crystal. At the long dark coat, pale gold and translucent. At the silver temples. At the grey eyes. At the face that slid off his attention like water off oiled leather, so that he had to keep looking, and looking, to hold it.

He had imagined this moment every night for fifteen years.

He had imagined what he would say. He'd had whole speeches, once. When he was small, they'd been speeches about how he'd been good, how he'd helped Mam, how he'd learned to read. When he was older, after the fire, they'd been speeches about how much he hated him. How he'd left. How he'd never come back. How Mam had died waiting. He'd rehearsed them in the loft above the forge, lying awake, until he could say them in his sleep.

He couldn't remember a single word of any of them.

"Hello," he said.

It came out very small.

His father's face did something complicated. The shape of light was faint, flickering at the edges like a candle in a draft, and it was hard to read its expression. But Lex saw the grey eyes fill. Saw the lips press together. Saw the long hands, translucent and gold, curl slowly into fists at his sides, the way Antho's did when he was trying not to feel something.

"Lexander," said Arion Vey. His voice was quiet and deep and a little rough. It was exactly the voice from the Thornwood, the voice Lex had imagined his whole life. It was nothing like it. It was better and worse. "Look at you. Look how tall you are." He swallowed. "You were so small. The last time. I could hold you in one arm."

Lex couldn't answer.

Arion's eyes moved to Antho.

"Anthoren," he said. And his voice broke completely on the name.

Antho didn't move. He was standing perfectly still beside Lex, his hand rigid in Lex's grip, his face white as the walls of the temple. Ashka was on his shoulder, very still too, her golden eyes fixed on the shape of light.

"Antho," said their father. "I'm so—"

"Don't."

Antho's voice was flat. Dead. A voice Lex hadn't heard from him since the Hollowmaw.

"Don't say it," said Antho. "Don't say you're sorry. Not yet. I'm not ready to hear it." His jaw worked. "I don't know if I'll ever be ready to hear it."

Arion closed his mouth.

The two of them looked at each other across the black glass floor. Father and son. The same jaw, the same long straight nose, the same grey eyes. One made of light. One made of eight years of darkness.

"All right," said Arion quietly. "All right. I won't say it." He bowed his head. "Not until you want me to."

The others had come to the archway behind them. Lex heard them. Felt them. Wren's quick light step, Garrick's heavy one, Seraphine's hesitant shuffle. They stopped in the archway and didn't come further.

And Elric.

Arion's grey eyes lifted past his sons to the archway. And found the old sorcerer standing there, leaning on his staff. And something went across his translucent face that Lex had never seen on anyone. Joy, and grief, and gratitude, and something like shame, all at once, the way a sunrise is many colors at once.

"*Elric*," he said.

Elric didn't move.

"Old friend," said Arion. "You found them. Of course you found them. I knew you would." He took a step forward, across the black glass, toward the archway. His form flickered. "I asked you. Eleven years ago. I asked too much. I always asked too much. And you did it anyway."

Elric made a sound.

It wasn't a word. It was something torn out of him. He let go of his staff. It fell to the floor with a clatter that echoed around the great dome. And the old sorcerer stumbled forward across the black glass and stopped in front of the shape of light that was his oldest friend, and his face crumpled, and he wept.

He wept like a child. Like a man who had been holding something in for twenty years and couldn't hold it anymore. His thin shoulders shook. His knotted hands came up and reached for Arion, and passed through the gold light, and closed on nothing, and he wept harder.

"*Twenty years*," he said. "*Twenty years, Arion. I looked for you. I looked everywhere. I stood on that ridge three times. I put my hand in that water.*" His voice cracked to pieces. "*You never told me. You never told me anything. You just left.*"

"*You always left. You always went first. You always—*" Elric stopped. He pressed his hands over his face.

And something in his chest that had been clenched tight since the silver road, since the mirror and the throne and the young man crowned with gold, since *not yet* whispered over the chasm, slowly began to unclench.

They sat on the black glass floor in a half-circle around the crystal, and Arion told them.

Not everything. Lex knew that even as he listened. There were places in the story where his father's voice would slow, and his translucent face would go still, and he would choose his next words with great care, like a man stepping between stones in a stream. Places where he said *I'll tell you more of that another time*. Places where he didn't say anything at all.

But he told them a great deal.

He told them about the House of Vey. About Aurelian, who had woven the Lattice nine hundred years ago, not as a gift but as a cage and a lid. A net over the will of men, so no one could ever again wield unchecked power. And a lid over the hunger beneath.

"The Hollow," said Arion. "That's what the Founders called it. The empty place behind the world. And in it, something that woke in the Sundering. Something that eats." His grey eyes went to Antho. "You know it. Better than anyone alive. You've been listening to it for eight years."

Antho didn't answer. His hands were clenched on his knees.

"The Lattice holds it down," said Arion. "Has held it down for nine hundred years. But the Lattice is *old*. Older than anything should be. And it's tired. It's fraying. Thinning. Like an old net left too long in the sea." He paused. "And where it thins, the hunger's breath seeps through. That's the Gloam. Not a blight. Not a disease of the land. The *breath* of the thing under the world, leaking up through the cracks."

"The breaches," said Seraphine.

"The breaches." Arion nodded. "There were three, when I was young. Small ones. Far away. The Order patched them and kept them quiet. Then, sixteen years ago, in Vhessa, there was a fourth." His voice had slowed. "A large one. The largest ever recorded. I was there when it opened."

"What happened?" said Lex.

His father looked at him. For a long moment, he didn't answer.

"Something went wrong," he said at last. "I'll tell you all of it. I promise. But not today." He looked away, at the crystal. "Not today."

*[He's hiding something,]* said Ember quietly. *[I can feel it. Like a locked partition. Like a door with no handle on this side.]*

*Do you know what it is?*

*[No. Whatever it is, he didn't give me that piece.]* A pause. *[He kept it for himself.]*

"Why did you leave?"

It was Antho. He hadn't spoken in a long time. He'd been sitting very still with his hands on his knees and his eyes fixed on the floor, while their father talked about Aurelian and the Hollow and the Lattice. Now he lifted his head.

"That's what I want to know," he said. "Not about Aurelian. Not about the Gloam. I don't care about any of that. I've been living with the Gloam for eight years." His grey eyes met their father's. "Why did you leave us?"

Arion was quiet for a long moment.

"Because of what you are," he said. "Both of you."

He looked from one to the other.

"Root blood is rare. There are perhaps three of us in the world, in any generation. Usually fewer. And Root is *bright*. To anyone who knows how to look, it shines like a lantern in a dark room." His voice was very steady. "The Order knows how to look. The senior Wardens. The High Warden. If they'd found you, they'd have taken you. Raised you in the Spire. Trained you. *Used* you. Made you into keys for their locks. You'd have been the High Warden's tools from the age of five." He paused. "I couldn't let that happen."

"So you sealed us," said Antho.

"So I sealed you. To hide you. From the Order." Arion's eyes flickered. "And from the hunger. The Gloam is drawn to Root the way moths are drawn to a flame. When the Vhessa breach opened, it learned my scent. I knew it would learn yours too, if you stayed near me. So I hid you, and I went away, as far as I could go." He paused again. "And from—"

He stopped.

The chamber was very quiet.

"From what?" said Lex.

His father didn't answer. His translucent face had gone very still, the way it did at the places in the story where he chose his words like stones.

"From other things," he said at last. "Things I'll tell you about another time."

*[Another locked door,]* murmured Ember.

"And then you came here," said Lex.

"And then I came here." Arion turned and looked at the crystal behind him. At the body inside it, standing upright with its eyes closed. "Because I'd found something else, in the east. Something worse than the breach. The Lattice wasn't just thinning, Lexander. It was *failing*. Unraveling. Thread by thread. And if it unraveled completely, the lid would come off, and the hunger would come through. All of it. Not a breath. The whole thing."

Nobody spoke.

"Avaloria is the Anchor," said Arion. "The place where the Lattice is tied to the world. If you hold the Anchor, you can hold the net. Not mend it. Not yet. But hold it together. Keep it from unraveling further." He spread his translucent hands. "So I came here, and I anchored myself into the heart of it. My body, my blood, my Authority. All of it. I became a knot in the net. And I've been holding it ever since." He looked at his sons. "Fifteen years."

"Fifteen years," whispered Seraphine. "Alone. In a crystal."

"Not entirely alone." Something almost like a smile touched his face. "I could feel you both. Through the seals. Faintly. Like hearing music from another room." He looked at Lex. "I felt it when you picked up your first hammer. When you were eleven. I felt it when you forged your first nail." He looked at Antho, and the smile faded. "And I felt it when your seal broke."
