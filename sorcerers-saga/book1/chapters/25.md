# Shadows of Betrayal

The weight came down on him like a mountain.

Lex had thought he was ready for it. His father had warned them. *It will be the heaviest thing you've ever carried.* He'd lifted anvils. He'd swung sledges for eight years. He'd held a rune open in an ancient road for a whole night while three hundred and forty-six names went through it.

He had never carried anything like this.

He laid his palm on the carved right hand of the Twin Stone, and reached down through it, through the Bloodbond, along the threads, all the way back across the mountains and the moors and the lake that held the sky to the white temple on its island, to the Anchor. And he *took hold*.

And the whole world came down on him.

He felt it. Every thread. Every knot. The vast silver net of the Lattice spread over all of Aldermere like a fishing net spread over the sea, and every thread of it *pulling*. Pulling at him. Pulling at the place in his chest where the seal was, where the fire was. Ten thousand million lives, every one of them a knot in the net, every one of them a weight. Brannoc at his anvil. Pell at the mill. Wren's mother on her step. The girl in Highcrest who wanted to be a Healer. All of them, all at once, hanging from his hand.

And the breaches.

He felt them like wounds. Like burns. Twenty-seven of them, scattered across the net, black holes in the silver where the threads had torn and frayed. He felt the cold coming through them. The hunger. The breath of the thing beneath the world, seeping up through every tear. The one in the Hollowmaw, held shut by his own ward-nails. The one under the Thornwood. And far to the east, vast and black and gaping like a mouth: Vhessa. The first. The worst. The wound that all the others radiated out from like cracks from a hammer-blow on glass.

His knees buckled. He caught himself on the Twin Stone. The breath went out of him in a sound that wasn't a word.

"*Lex*." Antho's voice. Beside him. Strained to breaking. "*Lex, I've got it. I've got the left side. Hold the right. Don't let go.*"

He felt his brother. Through the Bloodbond. Through the Stone. On the other side of the net, holding the left half of the weight the way Lex held the right. The cold deep pool of him, braced, straining, shaking.

*Together.*

Lex set his teeth. He set his feet. He *held*.

And in the crystal beside them, their father opened his eyes.

It was slow. Agonizingly slow.

The gold shape of light that had been Arion's ghost for fifteen years flowed back into the crystal like water flowing into a basin. It went in through the faceted walls and filled them, and the crystal glowed brighter, and brighter, until Lex had to narrow his eyes against it. And inside the crystal, the body that had stood upright with its eyes closed for fifteen years *breathed*.

Lex saw its chest rise. Saw it fall. Saw the fingers twitch at its sides. Saw the grey eyes open, and blink, and focus.

And the crystal began to soften.

Not crack. Not shatter. *Soften*, the way ice softens in spring, going from hard and clear to wet and pliant. The faceted walls blurred. Their edges rounded. Drops of something that wasn't water began to run down them and fall to the floor and vanish before they touched the stone.

Arion Vey took a step forward.

Inside the softening crystal, slowly, stiffly, like a man who had forgotten how to walk, he took a step. And another. His hand came up and pressed against the inside of the crystal wall, and the crystal gave under it, like a curtain of thick gel. His fingers came through. His hand. His wrist.

A real hand. Flesh and bone. Long-fingered, scarred across the knuckles, a smith's hand like Lex's own.

*Ten minutes*, Lex thought dizzily, through the crushing weight. *He said ten minutes.*

Above them, through the ceiling, through the black stone of the keep, through the walls of the fortress, he heard the horns. Heard the roar. Felt, through the fellowship bar at the edge of his vision, Wren's health flicker as something struck her. Felt Garrick's dip, and steady, and dip again.

*Hold. Just hold. Ten minutes.*

Arion's arm came through the crystal to the elbow. His shoulder. The side of his face, pressing through the softening wall like a man pushing through deep water, his grey eyes fixed on his sons. His lips moved. No sound yet. He'd forgotten how. But Lex could read them.

*Almost.*

---

Behind them, Elric moved.

Lex didn't see it at first. He couldn't see anything but the Twin Stone and his own hand on it and the crystal and his father pushing through it. The weight of the world was pressing down on him so hard that the edges of his vision had gone grey. But he heard the tap of a staff on stone. Footsteps. Coming from the door.

Coming *toward* the Stone.

"*Elric*?" That was Brisk. The old castellan's voice. Puzzled. "Elric, you said we'd guard the stair. What are you—"

Elric didn't answer.

He came around the Twin Stone, on the side between Lex and Antho, where the third carved hand lay waiting in the white stone. The center hand. The larger one. Palm up. The place meant for Arion. For the third of the blood.

He stood over it. His face was grey. His eyes were very bright.

"Elric," said Lex. It came out a croak. He couldn't move. He couldn't let go of the Stone. "Elric, what are you doing?"

The old sorcerer looked down at him.

"I'm sorry, Lex," he said quietly. "I truly am."

He lifted his left hand. And Lex saw, for the first time, that there was something on his palm. A mark. Not a scar. A *glyph*, inked into the skin. Black. Branching. Like the black veins under Morvane's white skin. Like cracks in porcelain.

Elric laid his marked palm on the third carved hand.

---

It was like a sluice gate opening.

Lex felt it through the Stone. Felt the river of gold that had been flowing out of the Anchor, out of the net, back into his father, the fifteen years of Authority that Arion had poured into holding the world together, finally coming home. He felt it turn. Swerve. Like water finding a new channel. And go *into Elric*.

Into the black glyph on Elric's palm. Up his arm. Into him.

The old sorcerer gasped. His back arched. His head went back. The brass storm-charms on his grey cloak flared, all at once, blazing, not blue-white this time but *gold*. Gold like Lex's eyes. Gold like Root. And over his head, a panel exploded open: silver at first, then cracking, the gold showing through, the way Lex's own panel had cracked on the night of the gloamhounds.

Garrick was crouched over her on the keep steps with Oathkeeper in one hand, its blade blazing blue-white, and every Gloamed thing that came within reach of it shattered and didn't rise.

He looked up as they came out of the smoke.

"*Lad*," he said. And his face, seeing Lex alive, did something Lex would remember for the rest of his life.

Antho dropped to his knees beside Seraphine.

"*Seraphine*," he said. "*Seraphine*. *Sera*." His scarred hands hovered over her, shaking, not knowing where to touch. "*Look at me*."

She didn't look at him. Her eyes were closed. Her lips were blue.

"She's dying," said Garrick quietly. "The lightning went into her. Into her chest. She's got maybe minutes." He looked at Lex. "Can you—you did it for me. On the stair at Avaloria. The hand thing. The healing."

*The Hand Held Out. Once per day.*

He'd already used it. On Antho. In the cellar.

Lex looked down at the hammer in his hand. At the runes, glowing faint and red-gold. At the fourth one. The open hand.

*Once per day.*

He'd given Antho half of what he had. He didn't have much left. A nineteen-year-old smith's body, locked out of everything that had made him strong. Bruised, battered, burned, shaking with exhaustion.

He knelt beside Seraphine on the keep steps. He laid the hammer across her chest. He put his own hand over it.

*Please,* he thought. *Please. I know it's only once a day. I know I already used it. I know I don't have anything left.* He pressed down on the hammer. *But I made you. I made you with my own hands. And you remember the hands you held.*

*Please.*

The fourth rune flickered.

And something went out of him. Not much. A thread. A last thin thread of warmth, from the very bottom of him, from the place under the locked door, the place even Elric's stolen Root couldn't reach. It went down his arm, through his hand, into the hammer, and through the hammer into the thin burned body lying on the steps.

He felt his own heart stutter. Felt the grey creep in at the edges of his sight.

Seraphine's eyes opened.

She gasped. A real breath. Deep. Ragged. Her lips went from blue to grey to faintly pink. Her eyes, unfocused without her spectacles, wandered across the smoky sky and found Antho's face bending over her.

"*Antho*?" she whispered.

Antho made a sound and put his forehead down against hers and didn't say anything at all.

Lex sat back on his heels on the keep step. The world was swimming. He couldn't feel his hands.

"*Lad*." Garrick's hand on his shoulder. Steadying him. "*Lad, stay with me.*"

"I'm here," Lex said. It came out a whisper. "I'm here."

"Good." Garrick's voice was rough. "Good. Because you need to *run*."

---

He didn't want to run. He'd never wanted to run less in his life.

But Teodra Hale's voice was screaming *RETREAT* across the burning courtyard. And the Gloam was still pouring through the shattered gate. And above them, in the smoky sky, a vast grey shape was circling. With something hanging beneath it, held in its great grey claws. Something faceted, glowing faintly, with a man's arm reaching out of it.

And on Sorrowmaw's back, standing upright between its vast shoulders, two figures. One tall and thin and white, in a long white coat. And one tall and thin and grey, in a long grey cloak, with brass storm-charms on it that blazed gold.

The dragon turned east. Its silent wings beat once, twice. And it flew away over the burning walls of Fort Caldris, toward the grey country and the bruise on the horizon, with their father hanging in its claws.

"*The sally port*," said Wren. She had Lex by one arm. Antho had Seraphine in his arms, carrying her, her head on his shoulder. Ashka had come back out of nowhere and was clinging to his back, shrieking. "*North wall. There's a sally port. Teodra's getting people out that way. Come on.*"

"*Garrick*—"

The old knight was standing up.

He stood on the keep steps with Oathkeeper in his hand, the blade blazing blue-white and gold, looking out across the courtyard at the shattered gate. At the black river pouring through it. At the Gloamed things coming.

"Go," he said.

"*Garrick, come with us*—"

"Somebody has to hold the gate, lad. Long enough for the rest to get out the sally port. Three hundred people came here because Thorne asked them." His jaw tightened. "Somebody has to stand on the steps."

"*No*—"

Garrick turned and looked at him. His scarred face was calm. Very calm. The calm of a man who has stood in a lot of doorways and knows exactly what this one is.

"I swore an oath on this sword," he said. "To you. To your brother. To the girl with the bow. To stand on the steps, wherever the steps are, for as long as I can stand." He smiled. It was a real smile, warm and crooked. "These are the steps, Lex. And I can still stand."

He turned back to the gate.

"*Go*," he said. "And find your father. And bring that traitor's head back to me in a sack. And tell my girl I kept my oath."

He walked down the keep steps into the courtyard, toward the shattered gate and the black river pouring through it, with Oathkeeper blazing in his hand. And he didn't look back.

Wren dragged Lex away.

---

They came out through the sally port in the north wall into cold dark air and a steep slope of rock and heather falling away below them. People all around them. Teodra Hale's Ashen Banners, what was left of them. Deserted Wardens, bloody and stumbling. A handful of Dame Brand's knights, carrying their wounded. All of them running north, away from the fortress, into the dark.

Lex looked back once.

Fort Caldris was burning. The keep, the towers, the walls. Grey-black fire climbing into a sky full of smoke. And at the shattered gate, small and far away, there was a light. Blue-white and gold. Flashing. Flashing. Holding.

Then the smoke covered it.

He turned and ran.

---

Much later, in the grey hour before dawn, they stopped in a fold of the hills a dozen miles north. What was left of them. Perhaps a hundred, out of three hundred. Lying in the heather, bleeding, weeping, too exhausted to move.

Lex sat with his back against a rock and Anvilsong across his knees and looked at nothing.

Sixteen. Nine. Fourteen. Thirteen. Four. Fifteen.

The numbers from the morning Elric walked into the forge. The numbers he'd looked at every day for three years. The numbers he'd thought were him.

He sat there in the heather and looked at it for a long time.

Then he closed his eyes and reached, one more time, for the place in the back of his head where a dry calm courteous voice had lived for three months. Where it had called him *Smith*, and then *Lex*, and then *little smith*. Where it had sung his mother's lullaby off-key in a Founders' waystation in the ice.

Nothing at all.

Beside him, Wren sat down in the heather. She didn't say anything. She just leaned her shoulder against his, the way she did.
