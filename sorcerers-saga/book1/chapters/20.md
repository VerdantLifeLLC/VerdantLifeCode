# Mirrormere

They went down to the shore in the dark.

There was a path. They hadn't seen it from the ridge, but it was there once they started looking: a narrow track of grey stone slabs, Founders' work like the Road, winding down the steep side of the valley in long switchbacks between the pines. It was old and worn and in places half-buried under fallen needles, but it was straight and true, and it led them down, down, through the cold dark trees, until they came out onto a beach of fine pale shingle at the edge of the water.

Up close, Mirrormere was even stranger.

The water lapped at nothing. There were no waves on the shingle, no ripples, no sound at all. It lay against the shore like a sheet of black glass laid on the ground. And in it, perfectly, impossibly, the night sky was reflected: every star, every faint smear of cloud, the thin new moon just rising over the eastern peaks. Lex stood at the edge and looked down and saw the sky beneath his feet, as deep and vast and endless as the sky above, and for one dizzy moment he felt that if he stepped forward he would fall into it and never stop falling.

He took a step back.

"Don't touch it," said Elric quietly. "Not yet."

"Why not?"

"Because I touched it. The first time I came here. Twenty years ago." The old sorcerer was standing a little way along the shingle, leaning on his staff, looking at the water. "I knelt down and put my hand in it, the way you'd put your hand in any lake." He was quiet for a moment. "It was very cold. And I saw something in it. A reflection. Not of my face." He shook his head. "I didn't touch it again."

"What did you see?" said Wren.

Elric didn't answer.

---

Antho stood at the very edge of the water with the lodestone in his fist.

It had stopped pulling. It hung perfectly still on its cord, pointing straight down at the black glass of the lake. Glowing. Grey and green and blue, twined together, pulsing slowly.

"So what do we do?" he said. "How do we call it?"

Seraphine had her green book open, the one with the drawing of Avaloria in it, and was reading it by the light of the Keeper's Lantern at Antho's belt. "*Two of the blood*," she read. "*At the shore of the waters that hold the sky. Let the stone be given to the water, and the blood be given to the stone, and the Way will rise.*"

"*Given to the water*," said Antho slowly. "You mean throw it in?"

"I think you mean *lay* it in. Gently." Seraphine looked up. "And then blood. From both of you. On the stone."

Antho looked down at the lodestone in his hand. At the little grey river-pebble his mother had worn every day for fifteen years. That he'd found in the ashes of their house, and worn every day for eight more.

"If it sinks," he said, "if it doesn't work—"

"Then we'll dive for it," said Lex.

Antho looked at him. Then, slowly, he lifted the cord over his head.

He knelt on the pale shingle at the edge of the black water. Lex knelt beside him. Antho held the lodestone out over the lake, on its cord, so that it hung an inch above the surface. Above its own reflection. Above the stars.

He looked at Lex.

Lex drew his knife and cut the ball of his thumb, the way Elric had cut it on the Ashbourne green, a lifetime ago. A bead of blood welled up. He held his hand out over the stone.

Antho cut his own thumb. Held it out beside Lex's.

Two drops fell. One from each of them. They struck the lodestone at the same instant.

And Antho let go of the cord.

---

The stone didn't sink.

It touched the surface of the lake, and the surface of the lake *took* it. Not like water taking a dropped pebble. Like a hand closing around something precious. The lodestone rested on the black glass, glowing, and the light from it spread outward. Grey and green and blue, rippling out across the water in rings, the first ripples Lex had seen on Mirrormere, spreading wider and wider, a hundred paces, a thousand, out toward the center of the lake.

And where the ripples passed, the reflection changed.

The stars in the water didn't vanish. But something rose up through them, as if from far below. A line. A path. A narrow road of pale silver light, glowing faintly, running out from the shingle at their feet across the surface of the lake, straight as a ruled line, toward the center. Toward the place where there was nothing.

And at the end of it, as the ripples reached the center of the lake, something *appeared*.

It came up out of the water the way a reflection comes into focus when ripples settle. An island. Small and steep and rocky, rising out of the black glass. And on the island, rising from its summit, white in the starlight, impossibly delicate, a building.

Columns. Domes. Towers. Arches. Spires so slender they looked like they'd been spun from glass. Walls of white stone that glowed faintly from within, the way the Lattice Stones glowed at dawn. A great central dome, crowned with a spire, and at the very top of the spire, a light. Gold. Steady. Like a lantern left burning in a window.

"*Avaloria*," breathed Seraphine.

Behind them, Elric made a sound. A small, broken sound, like a man who has been punched in the stomach.

Lex turned. The old sorcerer was standing on the shingle, staring out across the lake at the white temple. His face in the starlight was grey. His hand on his staff was shaking.

"Twenty years," Elric whispered. "Twenty years, I've been looking. I stood on that ridge three times. I walked this shore. I put my hand in that water." His voice cracked. "And it was here all along. Right there. And I couldn't see it."

"It opens to two of the blood," said Antho quietly. He'd risen from his knees. The lodestone had vanished; the lake had taken it, or it had become part of the silver road. He was looking at Elric with an expression Lex couldn't read. "You're not of the blood."

"No," said Elric. "No, I'm not." He laughed, very softly. It wasn't a nice sound. "I've never been of the blood. I've only ever been *beside* it."

He looked at the silver road. Then at Lex. Then at Antho.

"Will it carry me?" he said. "Will it let me walk it? Or will I step onto it and fall through into the water like a fool?"

*[I don't know,]* said Ember. *[The Way opens to two of the blood. That's all the old texts say. They don't say whether it carries anyone else.]*

Lex looked at the silver road. At the five people standing behind him on the pale shingle. Wren with her bow. Garrick with his sword. Seraphine with her spectacles and her green book. Elric with his staff, shaking. And Antho, beside him, with Ashka on his shoulder.

He held out his hand to Wren.

"Together," he said. "All of us. Hold on to each other. Whatever happens, don't let go."

---

They walked out onto the lake in a line, holding hands.

Antho in front. Then Lex. Then Wren, then Garrick, then Seraphine, and Elric at the back, his thin knotted hand gripping Seraphine's so hard she winced.

The silver road held them.

It was strange to walk on. Not solid like stone. Not soft like water. Something in between, faintly yielding, like walking on a taut sheet of silk. It glowed under their feet, pale silver, and on either side of it, close enough to touch, was the black glass of the lake with the stars in it. Below them. All around them. Lex looked down and saw the sky going down and down forever, and the narrow silver road suspended above it like a thread across an abyss, and felt his stomach lurch.

"Don't look down," said Wren behind him. Her hand was very tight in his. "Climber's rule. Don't look down. Look at where you're going."

He looked at where he was going. At the white temple on its island, glowing in the starlight, growing slowly closer.

They walked for a long time. Much longer than it should have taken. The island seemed to come no closer, and then all at once it was much closer, and then it seemed to drift away again. The stars wheeled overhead. The thin moon climbed. Nobody spoke.

And then, halfway across, the road stopped.

Not ended. *Stopped*. There was a place where the silver path widened into a broad round platform, like a landing on a stair. And standing around the edge of the platform, facing inward, there were mirrors.

Six of them. Tall, oval, taller than a man, standing upright on the silver road with nothing holding them up. Their frames were white stone, carved with Wellscript. Their glass was perfectly clear, perfectly black, like the lake itself.

And in each one, there was a reflection.

Not of the platform. Not of the stars. Of *them*.

Lex let go of Wren's hand. He didn't mean to. His feet carried him across the platform on their own, toward the mirror that was waiting for him, the way his foot had carried him toward the edge of the Road in the Thornwood.

It was him.

Not him now. Him *older*. Forty, maybe. Fifty. Broad and heavy and thick-bellied, with grey in his dark hair and deep lines around his eyes and burn scars all up and down his arms. He was standing in a forge. Brannoc's forge, in Ashbourne. Lex knew every stone of it. The same anvil. The same hearth. The same brick ledge by the fire, with a cat sleeping on it. A different cat. Slag would be long dead.

The man in the mirror was hammering a horseshoe.

He did it beautifully. Every stroke perfect. Lex could see it: the economy of motion, the ease, the decades of practice in every swing. When the shoe was done, the man lifted it in the tongs and looked at it and nodded, satisfied, and plunged it into the slack tub. Steam rose.

And a silver panel hung in the air beside him.

The man in the mirror looked up. Through the glass. At Lex.

"Hello," he said.

His voice was Lex's voice. Deeper. Slower. Tired, but not unhappy.

"You could have this," said the man in the mirror. "You know that, don't you? You could still have it. Turn around. Walk back across the lake. Go home." He gestured around the forge with the tongs. "Brannoc's old now. He'll need someone to take over. You'd be good at it. You *are* good at it." He smiled. "Pell's Kindled now. He came by the forge last week. Got [Miller], poor lad. He'll get used to it. Everyone does."

Lex couldn't speak.

"It's a good life," the man in the mirror said gently. "A quiet one. Nobody hunting you. No monsters. No Wardens. No voice in your head. No father lying in a crystal waiting for you to save him." He set down the tongs. "You'll marry, probably. Someone from the village. You'll have children. You'll make ten thousand horseshoes and every one of them will be good. And you'll never once have to find out whether you're strong enough." He tilted his head. "Isn't that what you wanted? When you were sixteen? Standing in front of that Stone with your heart going? You wanted it to tell you who you were, so you wouldn't have to decide."

The fire in Lex's chest was very low.

"Level twenty-four," said the man in the mirror. "It's not so bad, you know. Brannoc was happy. Mostly. You'd be happy too." He held out his hand. Through the glass. "Come home, Lex. It's easier."

Lex looked at the hand.

It was his own hand. Scarred and calloused and strong. A smith's hand. A good hand.

He thought of Brannoc, staring at his own hands in the forge. *Nineteen years.*

He thought of the girl in Highcrest, standing in front of the white Stone with her face going carefully blank. *Laborer. Common. Next.*

He thought of Wren in the clearing at the heart of the Thornwood, looking at a panel only she could see. *That's that, then.*

"It's not so bad," he said slowly. "You're right. It's not so bad for *me*." He looked at the man in the mirror. "I'd be fine. I'd be happy, probably. I'd make good horseshoes." He swallowed. "And the girl in Highcrest would still be a Laborer. And Brannoc would still be twenty-four. And Wren would still be stuck. And every kid in every village would still put their hand on a rigged Stone and be told what they are." He shook his head. "I can't. I can't go home and make horseshoes knowing that. Knowing I could have done something."

"You don't know that you can."

"No," said Lex. "I don't. But I'm going to find out."

The man in the mirror looked at him for a long moment. Then he smiled. Not sadly. Something else. Something almost proud.

"Good," he said. "That's the right answer. I always hoped I'd give it."

And the mirror *cracked*.

A single line, from top to bottom, through the center of the glass. Then a hundred lines, spreading out like frost. And then the whole thing shattered, silently, into a thousand glittering pieces that fell and dissolved before they touched the silver road.

The sky.

A great wide blue sky, and a figure in it. Wren. Not on a mountain. Not climbing. *Flying*. Arms spread, braid streaming, high above a grey-green world spread out below her like a map. The wind lifting her. Carrying her. She was laughing.

And beside her, in the air, a silver panel.

"*It could be*," said the Wren in the mirror, from the sky. Her voice was carried on the wind. "*If you let it.*"

Wren pulled her hand away from the glass. She stood there shaking.

"*Who says you can't?*"

Wren didn't answer. She turned away from the mirror. She looked across the platform at Lex, and her face was wet and furious and terrified.

And her mirror didn't crack. It just went dark. Quietly. Like a lantern going out. Waiting.

---

Garrick was kneeling before his mirror, the way he'd knelt in the burned smithy. Oathkeeper was point-down before him. In the glass, Lex saw a woman in a blue dress, standing in a garden. And a young woman beside her, in armor, with a captain's sash and a crooked nose. They were both smiling at him.

Garrick wasn't crying. He was just looking. His face was very peaceful.

"I'm on the steps," he told them quietly. "I'm standing on the steps."

His mirror shattered.

Seraphine stood before hers with her arms wrapped around herself. In her glass, she was sitting at a long table in a vast library, white and gold and full of light, surrounded by books, reading. People were coming to her. Asking her questions. Listening to her answers. Respecting her. Her reflection looked up from the book and smiled.

Seraphine bit her lip. She stood there a long time. And then she said, very quietly, "I'd rather be out here. Being useful. Even if nobody believes me."

Her mirror shattered.

---

And Antho.

Lex had been afraid to look. He looked now.

His brother was standing before the sixth mirror, very still, with Ashka on his shoulder and the Keeper's Lantern glowing at his belt.

And in the mirror was Antho.

But not Antho. His eyes were black. Fully black, edge to edge, like holes cut in the world. And his face was calm. Utterly, terribly calm. Peaceful. Serene. The face of a man who'd never once heard a voice in his head, because he *was* the voice.

"*Hello, Anthoren*," said the thing in the mirror.

Its voice was Antho's voice. And under it, a second voice. Wet and soft and hungry. The Murmur, wearing his face.

"*It's quiet in here*," the thing said. "*Did you know that? It's so quiet. No arguing. No fighting. No screaming at three in the morning. No lanterns. No little brother to look after. Antho stood very still in the falling glitter of the broken glass. Then his knees gave out, and he sat down hard on the silver road, and Ashka climbed down into his lap and pressed against his chest, rumbling.

Lex went and sat down beside him. Their shoulders touched.

Neither of them said anything.

---

It was only later, much later, after Wren had come and sat down on Lex's other side, and Garrick and Seraphine had joined them, that Lex realized someone was missing.

Elric.

He looked around. There were six mirrors on the platform. Five of them had shattered, or gone dark. Lex's. Wren's, dark and waiting. Garrick's. Seraphine's. Antho's.

The sixth was still standing.

Elric stood in front of it. He'd been standing there the whole time, Lex realized. All through the others. Silent. Motionless. Leaning on his staff with both hands, staring into the glass.

Lex got up. He walked across the platform, slowly, quietly. He didn't mean to look. He told himself afterward that he hadn't meant to look.

He looked.

In the mirror, there was a room.

A vast round room, white and high-domed, with a floor like black glass. The room Lex had seen in the vision in the Barrow-King's hall. And in the center of the room, where the crystal had been, there was a throne. A tall white throne, carved with Wellscript, glowing gold.

And Elric was sitting on it.

Not old. Young. Thirty, maybe. Tall and straight and handsome, with dark hair and bright storm-grey eyes and no scar on his face. He wore a long dark coat, like the coat Lex had seen on his father in the vision. And around his head, floating, a circlet of light. Gold. Root-gold.

Above his head, a panel.

The young Elric on the throne was smiling. Not kindly. Not warmly. The smile of a man who has finally, finally been given what he was owed.

And the room around him was empty.

Completely empty. No one else. No friends, no family, no voices, no faces. Just the vast white dome and the black glass floor and the throne in the middle and the one man sitting on it, alone, crowned with gold, smiling at nothing.

The old Elric stood in front of the mirror and looked at it. His face was grey. His hands on his staff were white at the knuckles.

He didn't shatter it.

He didn't turn away.

He just stood there and *looked*. The way a starving man looks through a window at a feast.

Then he turned his head, very slowly, and saw Lex standing beside him.

For one long moment, they looked at each other.

Lex saw the old sorcerer's face change. Saw something go across it, quick as a fish's tail in dark water. Shame. Fear. And under them, very deep, something hard and cold and bitter that Lex had heard once before. A dull note under the ring of good iron.

Then it was gone. Smoothed away. And Elric was only an old man again, tired and grey and leaning on his staff.

"Don't," he said quietly. "Don't ask me, Lex. Please." He looked back at the mirror. At the young man on the throne. "We all have things we want. Things we're ashamed to want." His voice was very soft. "That's all this is. A foolish old man's foolish dream."

He reached out with the end of his staff and touched the glass.

It didn't shatter. It went dark. Slowly. Like the mirror in front of Wren. Not broken. Just waiting.

"Come on," said Elric. He turned away from it. "We're nearly there."

He walked across the platform toward the silver road, toward the island, toward the white temple glowing in the starlight.

Lex stood alone in front of the dark mirror for a long moment.
