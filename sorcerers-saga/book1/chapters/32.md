# Redemption's Embrace

Malachi called a storm for Ashka on their last night in the Stillwater.

He did it standing at the edge of the still pool, at dusk, with his staff raised and his shifting eyes gone the pale grey of a winter sky. He didn't shout. He didn't chant. He just *listened*, for a long time, with his head tilted, the way a man listens for a voice in another room. And then he said something, very softly, in a language Lex didn't know. Not Wellscript. Older than Wellscript.

And the sky answered.

Clouds came up over the rim of the valley from nowhere. Black and roiling and lit from within by flickers of white. The wind rose. The goats on the slopes fled bleating for the shelter of the trees. The surface of the still pool, which had not moved in nine hundred years, shivered.

And the lightning came down.

Ashka was waiting for it. She'd been waiting all afternoon, ever since Malachi told her what he was going to do, pacing up and down the bank of the pool with her wings half-spread and sparks crawling along her spines. When the first fork came down out of the black sky, she leaped up to meet it.

It struck her in midair. And she *drank* it.

Lex watched it happen. Watched the lightning pour into her, blue-white, crackling, so bright he had to shield his eyes. Watched her scales blaze. Watched her wings beat once, twice, and *grow*. The silver frame on the left one stretched, and stretched, the runes he'd struck into it at Hedda's forge flaring gold as they let it grow. Her neck lengthened. Her tail. Her legs. Her whole body swelled, the way a fire swells when you throw dry wood on it, until the pony-sized drake who'd leaped off the bank was something much, much bigger.

The second fork came down. She drank that too.

The third.

By the time the storm passed, there was a dragon standing on the bank of the still pool. A real one. Horse-sized and more, lean and long and blue-grey and shimmering, with a crest of sharp spines running down her back and great storm-colored wings folded along her sides. The silver frame on the left one was almost invisible now, so fine compared to the size of her, like a spider's web on a sail. The torn membrane inside it had healed completely.

She turned her long narrow head and looked at Antho. Her golden eyes were the size of plates.

And a picture came into Lex's head, so loud it made him stagger. *Sky. Storm. Lightning in her belly like a feast. Antho on her back. FLYING. FLYING TOGETHER.*

Antho was staring at her with his mouth open.

He was a peddler. A thin, frightened, grey-faced man with a pack on his back and a mule with a cart, fleeing east, of all directions, toward the Blightmarch. He stopped dead in the road when he saw two hundred people and a dragon coming toward him, and went white as chalk, and tried to turn the mule around.

Wren dropped out of the sky in front of him and he screamed.

"*Easy*," said Wren. "Easy. We're not Gloamed. Where are you running from? Why east? Nobody runs *east*."

"The Vale," gasped the peddler. "The southern Vale. Under the Greyspine." He was shaking. "The black stuff came down off the mountains. A week ago. A whole river of it. Out of a hole in the Hollowmaw. The ward broke." He swallowed. "It's all gone grey. Thistledown. Coldwater. Millbridge." He looked at Lex. At the gold coming off his skin. At his face. "*Ashbourne*."

Lex went cold all over.

"The ward broke," he said. His voice sounded very far away. "In the Hollowmaw. The nails."

"I don't know about nails. I don't know about wards." The peddler was backing his mule away. "I just know it came down the north road like a flood, and everyone it touched went grey. And there's a thing in the middle of it. In Ashbourne. On the green. A tall white thing." He shuddered. "And it's taken the Stone."

Behind Lex, he heard Antho make a sound.

The nails. Twelve ward-nails, forged from Warden crossbows, struck with *Bind*. Ninety-four percent. *For a good long while. Months, at least.*

It had been months.

And the man who'd made the green fire around the breach, who'd been holding it shut for four years, had been away. Far away. With his brother.

"*Ashbourne*," whispered Antho.

Lex turned and looked west. Toward the green. Toward the Greyspine. Toward the Vale, and the village, and the forge, and an old smith standing at the edge of the cobbles with his hand raised.

*Hammer-oath. What's struck on the anvil holds.*

"Ashka," he said. His voice was very quiet. "How fast can you fly?"
