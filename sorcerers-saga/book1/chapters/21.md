# The Temple of Avaloria

The silver road ended at the foot of a stair.

It was white stone, like the temple above it, and it climbed the steep rocky side of the island in a single straight flight, a hundred steps, two hundred, so many that Lex stopped counting. Antho didn't stop counting. Antho never stopped counting. "Three hundred and sixty-five," he said, when they reached the top. "One for every day."

"Of course it is," said Seraphine faintly, leaning on Garrick's arm.

At the top of the stair was a door.

It was enormous. Taller than the bronze door of the Barrow Throne, taller than the gates of Highcrest. Two great leaves of white stone, smooth and seamless, glowing faintly from within. There was no handle. No hinge. No lock. In the center, where the two leaves met, there was a single carving, waist-high, deep-cut and perfect.

An open hand. And above the palm, a flame.

Lex looked at it. Then at Antho.

"Together," said his brother.

They put their hands on it. Lex's right, Antho's left. Side by side, palms flat on the white stone, fingers just touching.

The door didn't open. It *dissolved*. One moment it was there, solid and white and seamless. The next it was light. Pale gold, shimmering, like sunlight on water. And then it was gone, and there was only an archway, and beyond it, darkness.

And a voice. Not Ember. Not the Murmur. Something vast and old and impersonal, like the voice of the Lattice itself, speaking in Wellscript that Lex somehow understood.

Lex stood in the archway and couldn't move.

"It says *welcome home*," said Antho. His voice was very strange.

---

The inside of the temple was white.

White stone walls, white stone floors, white columns rising up into darkness overhead. Everything glowed faintly, the way the door had glowed, a soft pale light that came from the stone itself and cast no shadows. It was very quiet. Their footsteps echoed. Their breath smoked in the cold air.

And everywhere, on every wall, every column, every floor-slab, there was Wellscript. Carved deep and fine and precise, in lines and spirals and great concentric circles. Seraphine made a sound like someone who'd walked into a room full of treasure and didn't know where to look first.

"Don't touch anything," said Elric sharply.

Seraphine snatched her hand back from a column.

They walked. There was only one way to go: a long white corridor running straight into the heart of the island, wide enough for ten people abreast, high enough that the ceiling was lost in shadow. At the end of it, far ahead, there was a faint gold glow.

They'd gone perhaps a hundred paces when the light came out of the walls.

It came in shapes. Tall shapes, man-shaped, but taller. Made of light. Pale gold, translucent, shimmering, like figures seen through a heat-haze. They stepped out of the white walls on either side of the corridor as if the stone were water, and turned, and faced the six of them, and raised their hands.

They held swords. Swords of light, long and straight and blazing.

Thousands of them, woven together into the shape of a man. And at the center of the weaving, where a heart would be, a single glyph. Gold. Root-gold.

He felt the gold go out of him. A small draining. Like a thread pulled from a spool.

He hadn't known what it would *feel* like.

It felt like a door closing. A heavy door. Iron. With no handle on his side.

"Hammer?"

Wren had come up beside him. She was looking at his face. And she knew. He saw her know. Saw her see the grey panel in his eyes, or in his face, or in the set of his shoulders.

The corridor ran on. And at the end of it, there was a chasm.

The white floor simply stopped. Beyond it was nothing. Darkness. A great round shaft dropping away into the heart of the island, so deep that the faint light from the walls didn't reach the bottom. Cold air rose out of it. And something else. A smell Lex knew. Faint, very faint, but there.

Wet ash. Rot. Nothing.

"The Hollow," said Antho quietly. He'd stopped at the very edge, looking down. His face had gone pale. "It's down there. Not the Gloam. The *thing*. Under everything." His hand went to the Keeper's Lantern at his belt. "I can feel it. It's so far down. But it's there."

And as they stepped onto the black glass floor, the light inside the crystal *moved*. It flowed outward, through the faceted walls, into the air of the chamber. It gathered. It took shape. Pale gold, translucent, shimmering, like the Lumen Wardens but softer, warmer. The shape of a man. Tall. Lean. In a long dark coat. With silver at his temples and grey eyes and a face that was very hard to remember even while you were looking at it.

The shape looked at them. At Lex. At Antho. At their joined hands.

And it smiled. A tired smile, and sad, and so full of love that Lex couldn't breathe.

"Hello, boys," said Arion Vey.
