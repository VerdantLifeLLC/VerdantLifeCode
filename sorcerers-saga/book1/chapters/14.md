# The Stormcaller's Lessons

"Again," said Elric.

Lex held out his hand, palm up, and called the heat.

It came the way Antho had taught him: shape the channel, then let it flow. A thread of warmth rising from his chest, down his arm, pooling in his palm. A shimmer in the air. A small ball of heat, invisible except for the way it bent the light, the way the air above a summer road bends.

"No," said Elric. "Stop."

Lex let it go. The heat dissipated with a faint *whuff*.

They were standing in a clearing on the eastern edge of the Thornwood, where the black trees gave way to rolling grey country and the Road ran on toward a low, misty, marshy horizon. They'd left the Leafbound Archive two days ago. The Thorn Mother had let them go with a rustle of silver leaves and a promise that the forest would remember them kindly. Seraphine had brought a pack so full of books she could barely lift it, until Garrick had quietly taken half of them and put them in his own.

And every evening since, Elric had been teaching.

"You're doing it like your brother," the old sorcerer said now. He was leaning on his staff at the edge of the clearing, watching Lex with his sharp grey eyes. "*Letting* it. Like water finding a crack."

"It works."

"It works for him. His power is deep and still. It *wants* to pool. It wants to find the low places and settle there." Elric shook his head. "Yours doesn't. Yours is fire. Fire doesn't settle. It *climbs*. It leaps. It wants to go everywhere at once. If you only *let* it, it'll go everywhere at once, and most of it will be wasted." He lifted a hand. "Watch."

A spark leaped from his fingertips. Blue-white, crackling. It didn't fly off, the way lightning would. It *hung* there, in the air above his palm, a tiny knot of energy the size of a pea. And then it began to turn. Around and around, faster and faster, tracing a circle in the air, and then another circle inside the first, and another, until there were seven circles of blue-white light nested one inside the other above his palm like the rings of a gyroscope, spinning, humming, perfectly controlled.

"Circles," said Elric. "That's how the Spire teaches it. You don't pour your power out like water from a jug. You *structure* it. Give it a shape to run in. A path. A circuit. And it goes round and round that path, building, building, until you decide to let it out." He closed his fist. The circles collapsed inward with a sharp *crack*, and a single bolt of lightning leaped from his knuckles across the clearing and struck a dead tree, and the tree split down the middle with a sound like a cannon. "And then it goes where you *tell* it. All of it. Nothing wasted."

Lex stared at the smoking tree.

"Your brother's way is good for defense," said Elric. "For holding. For shields and bindings and walls. That's why it suits him." He came across the clearing. "Your way should be good for *striking*. For forging. For driving the fire exactly where you want it to go. Try it. Give the heat a circle to run in."

Lex held out his hand again.

He called the heat. He didn't let it pool this time. He *shaped* it. A circle. A ring of warmth in his palm. He tried to make it turn.

It wobbled. It flared. It collapsed in a puff of hot air that singed the hair off the back of his hand.

"Again," said Elric.

---

It took him four evenings.

Four evenings of wobbling, collapsing, singeing circles. Four evenings of Elric's patient, dry, relentless voice saying *again*. Four evenings of Antho sitting at the edge of the clearing with Ashka in his lap, watching, saying nothing, his face unreadable.

And on the fifth evening, it worked.

He held out his hand, and called the heat, and gave it a circle. And this time the circle held. It turned. Slowly at first, a single ring of shimmering air above his palm, and then faster, and then a second ring inside the first, and a third. He felt it building. Felt the heat gathering, concentrating, growing hotter and denser with every turn, like a forge with the bellows working, until the air above his palm was shimmering so fiercely it was hard to look at.

"Now," said Elric quietly. "Not out. *In*. Over your skin."

Lex didn't understand. And then, suddenly, he did. He didn't let the circles out. He let them *spread*. Down over his hand, his wrist, his arm, his shoulder, a thin shell of heat, not burning, just *there*, wrapping around him like a second skin. Like armor. Like the glowing surface of iron fresh from the fire.

Elric picked up a stone and threw it at him.

It struck Lex's shoulder and bounced off. He barely felt it. Where it had touched, the stone was glowing faintly red.

"There," said Elric. He looked, for a moment, almost proud. Then something else crossed his face. Lex couldn't name it. "Your father learned that in an afternoon," the old sorcerer said quietly. "I spent a month teaching it to him, and he looked at me on the last day and said *Oh, I see, it's like folding steel*, and did it on the first try." He smiled faintly. "I could have strangled him."

"It *is* like folding steel," said Lex. He let the Ironskin go, and the shell of heat sank back into him.

Elric laughed. It came out a little forced. "Of course it is," he said. "Of course it is. You're both smiths, aren't you. In your different ways."

He turned away and walked back to the fire.

*[Hm,]* said Ember.

*What?*

*[Nothing. He's a good teacher. He really is.]* A pause. *[I just wondered why it seemed to hurt him to say that.]*

---

Elric's news was bad.

He'd told them most of it that first morning in the Leafbound Archive, over tea and bread, while the light came up grey through the round windows. The rest came out in pieces over the following days, around the evening fire.

The Order was mobilizing. Not against the Gloam. Against *them*. Roole's report on the Hollowmaw had gone up to the Spire, and it had caused, Elric said, *a considerable stir*.

"He told the truth," Elric said, poking the fire with his staff. "That's what's caused the trouble. Warden-Captain Roole wrote in his official report that the Ashbourne warlock was holding a breach shut. That the Gloam in the Greyspine was coming from a hole in the Lattice, not from Anthoren Harrow. That Lex Harrow had disarmed fifteen Wardens and spared them all, including Roole himself." He shook his head. "The High Warden didn't like that. The High Warden doesn't like anything that contradicts the official story. And the official story is that the Gloam is a natural blight and the Ashbourne brothers are dangerous wildcasters."

"What happened to Roole?" said Lex.

"Relieved of command. Recalled to Highcrest. Under investigation." Elric shrugged. "He'll be fine, probably. He has a good record and friends in the Order. But they've sent someone else after you now. Someone less inclined to tell the truth in his reports."

"Who?"

"Inquisitor-General Vane. You don't want to meet him." Elric's face was grim. "And the bounties have doubled. A thousand crowns each."

Wren whistled. "That's more than Thistledown makes in ten years."

"It's more than most *towns* make in ten years. Every bounty hunter, mercenary and desperate farmer between here and Saltreach will be watching the roads." Elric looked around the fire at them. "We need to stay off them. Which means the Founders' Road is still our best path. Nobody uses it. Nobody knows it's there."

"And the Gloam?" said Garrick. "What about the black stuff? The breaches?"

Elric was quiet for a moment.

"Getting worse," he said at last. "Much worse. I went east, after I left Lex in the foothills. As far as the edge of the Blightmarch. I didn't go in. Nobody goes in." He stared into the fire. "It's grown. It's grown a great deal in the last year. There are villages along the border that were green last spring and are grey now. And there are things moving in the grey. Hounds. Wraiths. Worse. Whole companies of Gloamed, standing in the fields, not moving, just *waiting*." He looked up. "And they're organized. That's what frightened me most. They're not wandering. They're placed. Like soldiers on a map. Like someone is *arranging* them."

"Morvane," said Seraphine quietly.

Elric's head turned sharply. "What do you know about Morvane?"

Seraphine hesitated. Then she told him about the letter from the aide. Elric listened in silence. When she finished, he was quiet for a long time.

"I knew Morvane," he said at last. "A long time ago. When he was young. When we were all young." His voice was very soft. "He was a hard man. A rigid man. He believed in order above everything. He believed the Lattice was the greatest gift ever given to humanity, because it made the world *make sense*." He shook his head slowly. "If anything in the Hollow could find a way into a man like that, it would find it through that. Through the love of order. Through the longing for everything to just be *quiet*."

*[Quiet,]* said Ember. *[Be quiet. All be quiet.]*

Lex shivered, though the fire was warm.

---

Antho didn't like Elric.

He never said so. He was polite, in his strange, careful way. He answered when Elric spoke to him. He listened to Elric's stories around the fire. He watched Elric's lessons with Lex from the edge of the clearing. But he never sat next to the old sorcerer. Never walked beside him on the Road. And Lex, watching his brother's red panel out of the corner of his eye, noticed something else.

Antho's Clarity dropped whenever Elric was near.

Not much. A few points. Seventy-four to seventy. Seventy-one to sixty-seven. It always came back up once Elric moved away. But it was there, every time, as regular as a tide.

On the sixth night, Lex asked him about it.

They were sitting together at the edge of camp, a little way from the fire, watching the stars come out over the grey marshy country to the east. Elric had gone off into the dark to *scout*, he said, as he did most evenings. The others were by the fire. Wren was showing Seraphine how to string a bow, and Seraphine was doing it badly, and Garrick was laughing at both of them.

"It's the lightning," said Antho. "I think. The thing in my head doesn't like lightning. It's too bright. Too loud."

"Is that all?"

Antho didn't answer right away. He was turning the lodestone over in his fingers, watching it pulse grey and green in the dark.

"No," he said at last. "That's not all." He hesitated. "It *likes* him, Lex."

Lex turned his head.

"It wants him," Antho said quietly. "The way it wanted him at the Hollowmaw, when he came up and called my name. More than it wanted the Wardens. More than it wants most people." He frowned. "I asked it why. I don't usually ask it things. It's never a good idea. But I asked."

"What did it say?"

Antho was quiet for a long moment.

"It said he's hungry too," he said.

The night was very still. Somewhere off in the marshes, a bird called, low and mournful.

"Hungry," Lex repeated.

"That's what it said. *Hungry one. He's hungry too.* Like it recognized something. Like they were the same." Antho shook his head. "I don't know what it means. Maybe nothing. It says a lot of things. Most of them are nonsense. Most of them are just *want want want* over and over." He put the lodestone back inside his shirt. "But it's never said that about anyone else."

Lex sat there in the dark for a long time after that, thinking.

He thought about Elric's face in the lantern light on the Ashbourne green, the first night, staring at the Stone. That strange raw look. *Like a man looking through a window at a feast laid out for someone else.*

He thought about the panel he'd seen with True Sight, in the forge. *Tier IV — DENIED (×3).*

*Twice by the Spire. And once, someone else.*

*[Lex,]* said Ember quietly. *[I don't think he means you any harm.]*

*I didn't say he did.*

*[You were thinking it.]* A pause. *[I've been watching him too. He's teaching you well. He's protecting you. He walked a long way to bring you news. Those aren't the actions of an enemy.]*

*Then what are they?*

Ember didn't answer for a long time.

*[The actions of a man who wants something,]* it said at last. *[I'm just not sure what it is yet.]*

---

The gloamhounds came on the seventh night.

Lex woke to Ashka's shriek, and Wren's shout, and the smell: wet ash and rot and nothing, thick on the air, rolling in off the marshes. He was on his feet with Anvilsong in his hands before he was fully awake. The fire had burned low. The camp was dark. And out in the darkness, beyond the circle of dying firelight, white eyes.

Dozens of them.

"*Up!*" Garrick roared. "Everyone up! Backs to the fire!"

They came in a rush, low and fast and whispering. Lex met the first one with an Ember Strike that turned it to gold sparks. He threw up Ironskin as the second one leaped, and its teeth closed on his forearm and *scorched*, and it fell back shrieking with smoke pouring from its jaws. He swung. Another one burst. Beside him, Garrick's blade flashed blue-white, and every hound it touched shattered into black splinters and didn't re-form. Wren's arrows sang. Antho's shadows rose up in a wall around the fire, and where they touched the hounds, the hounds recoiled, and *went still*, and dissolved.

And Seraphine stood in the middle of them, by the fire, with her crossbow in her hands and the bolt in the right way up this time, and shot a gloamhound through the eye at ten paces, and then stared at it as it burst into smoke as if she couldn't believe what she'd done.

"*Good shot!*" Wren shouted.

"I—I was aiming at the other one!"

"*Still counts!*"

It was over in ten minutes. Twenty hounds, perhaps thirty. They came out of the marsh and broke against the five of them like waves against rocks and fell back into the dark and didn't come again. When it was done, Lex stood by the fire with Anvilsong smoking in his hands and steam rising off his skin and looked around.

Everyone was standing. Everyone was alive. Garrick had a gash across his cheek. Wren had a bite on her calf, already going grey at the edges, which Lex knelt and burned clean. Seraphine was sitting on the ground by the fire with her crossbow across her knees, shaking, laughing, crying, all at once.

"I shot one," she kept saying. "I shot one. I've never shot anything. I've never shot *anything*."

"You shot it very well," said Antho. He'd sat down beside her. Not touching. Just close. His shadows were sinking back into the ground around them. "Right through the eye. I couldn't have done better."

Seraphine looked at him. She was still shaking. "You could have," she said. "You did. I saw you. You did about fifteen of them."

"Yes, well. I've had eight years of practice."

She laughed, wet and shaky. And after a moment, without seeming to think about it, she leaned sideways until her shoulder touched his.

Antho went very still. But he didn't move away.

Elric came back an hour later.

He came walking out of the dark from the east, from the direction of the marshes, leaning on his staff, his grey cloak dark with mud to the knee. Quill was on his shoulder. He stopped at the edge of the firelight and looked around at the camp, at the scorch marks on the ground and the wisps of black smoke still curling up from where the hounds had died, and his face went still.

"What happened?"

"Gloamhounds," said Garrick. "Thirty of them. Maybe more." His voice was very even. "Where were you?"

"Scouting." Elric came into the firelight. "There's a ridge about two miles east. I went up it to look at the marshes. I saw—" He stopped. Looked around again. "Is everyone all right?"

"Everyone's fine," said Lex.

"Thank the Lady." Elric let out a long breath. He sat down heavily on a rock by the fire. "I didn't hear anything. I didn't see anything. The wind must have been wrong." He shook his head. "I'm sorry. I should have been here."

Nobody said anything.

Lex looked at him. At the mud on his boots. At the lined, tired face in the firelight. At the storm-grey eyes, which were looking into the fire and not at any of them.

He almost used True Sight.

He didn't. He didn't know why. Maybe because Elric had told him, that first night in the forge, that it was rude. Maybe because some part of him didn't want to know.

*[He's telling the truth about not hearing it,]* said Ember quietly. *[I think. There's a ridge to the east, two miles off. And the wind is from the west. He wouldn't have heard.]*

*Then why does it feel wrong?*

---

Later that night, Wren came and sat down beside Lex at the edge of camp.

She didn't say anything for a while. She'd been avoiding him, a little, since the silver path in the Thornwood. Not unkindly. Just carefully. As if they were both standing on a frozen pond and neither of them wanted to be the one to make it crack.

"I saw him burn something," she said at last, very quietly.

Lex looked at her.

"Elric. Two nights ago. When he said he was going scouting. I couldn't sleep, so I went to check the snares I'd set, and I saw him on a rock a little way off, with a candle." Wren's voice was flat. "He was writing a letter. Then he read it over. Then he held it in the candle flame until it burned, and dropped the ashes in a puddle, and stirred them with his staff." She paused. "He did it three times. Wrote a letter, read it, burned it. Three letters."

"Maybe he couldn't get the words right."

"Maybe." Wren didn't sound convinced. "People who can't get the words right crumple up the paper. They don't burn it and stir the ashes into a puddle."

Lex didn't say anything.

"I'm not saying anything," said Wren. "I'm just telling you what I saw."

"I know."

"He's been good to us. He found you. He's teaching you. He brought us news." She was quiet for a moment. "And I've lived on a mountain my whole life, Hammer, and the mountain teaches you one thing above everything else. When something smells wrong, you don't wait to find out why. You just get ready."

---

Elric found him the next morning.

The others were breaking camp. Lex had gone down to a stream to fill the waterskins, and Elric came down after him, slowly, picking his way between the reeds with his staff.

"A word," the old sorcerer said quietly. "If you have a moment."

Lex straightened up with a dripping waterskin in each hand. "What is it?"

Elric glanced back up the slope toward the camp. Seraphine was there, folding her blanket, her back to them.

"The archivist," Elric said, very low. "How much do you trust her?"

Lex blinked. "She's been honest with us. As far as I can tell."

"As far as you can tell." Elric nodded slowly. "I'm sure she has. I'm sure she seems very honest. She's very convincing." He leaned on his staff. "Lex, I've spent my life around the Spire. I know how the sealed vaults are warded. I know what it takes to get into them, and what it takes to get out with codices under your arm." He shook his head. "A junior archivist doesn't do that. Not alone. Not without help. Someone had to let her in. Someone had to let her out. Someone had to make sure the wards were down for an hour on the right night." He looked at Lex steadily. "So either she had a powerful friend inside the Spire, who has since vanished. Or she still has one. And she's still working for them."

Lex stared at him.

"I'm not saying she's a spy," said Elric. "I'm saying ask yourself the question. She knew a great deal about you very quickly. She knew about Avaloria. She knew about your father's requisition record. She knew about *Morvane*." He spread his knotted hands. "Where does a junior archivist hiding in a tree for two years learn all that?"

"From her papers. She told us. She copied them before she ran."

"Did she show you the papers?"

Lex opened his mouth. Shut it again.

She'd shown them the map. The aide's letter. The books. But the rest of it, the requisition record, the Kindling ledgers—she'd *told* them about those. She'd recited them from memory.

"I'm only asking you to be careful," Elric said gently. "That's all. Watch her. Don't tell her anything you don't have to. Especially about the lodestone. Especially about Avaloria." He put a hand on Lex's shoulder. His grip was thin and knotted and surprisingly strong. "Your father trusted too easily, sometimes. It's the one flaw he had." He smiled faintly. "Well. One of several."

He turned and went back up the slope toward the camp.

Lex stood by the stream with the waterskins dripping in his hands, and the cold water running over his boots, and a feeling in his chest like a slag pocket buried in good iron. A dull note under the ring.

*[Lex,]* said Ember slowly. *[I don't know what to think.]*

*Neither do I.*

*[He's not wrong that it would be hard to get out of the sealed vaults alone. And she* did *know a great deal.]* A pause. *[But Wren's not wrong either. About the letters. About things that smell wrong.]*

*So who do I trust?*

The silence went on for a long time.

*[I don't know,]* said Ember at last. *[But I think I know who your father would have told you to trust.]*

*Who?*

*[The people who stood next to you in the dark,]* said Ember quietly. *[When the hounds came. Who were there.]*

Lex looked up the slope at the camp.

Wren, slinging her bow over her shoulder. Garrick, buckling on Oathkeeper. Antho, crouched by the ashes of the fire with Ashka on his shoulder, helping Seraphine roll up a blanket that she had somehow tied in a knot. Seraphine, laughing at something Antho had said, pushing her spectacles up her nose.

And Elric, a little apart from the rest of them, leaning on his staff and looking east toward the grey marshes, his face unreadable.

---

They reached the edge of the Drowned Barrows that afternoon.

It was Seraphine who recognized it first, from her books. The ground had been getting wetter all day: grey grass giving way to sedge and reeds, firm earth giving way to soft, squelching peat. The Road ran on across it on a raised causeway of ancient stone, but on either side the land sank lower and lower, and pools of black water began to appear among the reeds, and then larger pools, and then lakes, still and dark and mirror-flat.

And rising out of the water, here and there, were mounds.

Great round grassy mounds, like upturned bowls, some no bigger than a haystack and some as large as a house. Dozens of them. Hundreds. Stretching away across the flooded country as far as the eye could see, half-sunk in the black water, wreathed in mist.

"Barrows," said Seraphine softly. "Burial mounds. From the Sundering. Nine hundred years ago, this was a battlefield. The greatest battle of the whole war. Forty thousand dead in a single day." She looked out over the drowned land. "They buried them where they fell. Kings and soldiers and sorcerers. And then the sea came in, and the rivers changed course, and the whole plain flooded. It's been underwater ever since."

The mist drifted between the mounds. The water was utterly still. Not a bird. Not an insect. Not a sound.

And the cold that came off the water was not the cold of a marsh. It was deeper than that. Older. It went into Lex's bones and stayed there.
