# A New Dawn

Elric was gone.

Lex went back to the corridor that night, after the rain had stopped and the stars had come out over the open roof of the great hall. After they'd laid Garrick down on his cloak at the edge of the frost-ring, with Oathkeeper on his chest and his hands folded over its hilt, and Seraphine had written his name on the black floor beside him in charcoal, in big plain letters, *SIR GARRICK DUNMORE, WHO STOOD ON THE STEPS*. After Wren had stopped crying, and Antho had stopped shaking, and the eleven thousand people who had been Quiet that morning had begun, slowly, to find each other in the dark.

He went back to the archway where he'd left the old sorcerer sitting against the wall with his burned hand pressed to his chest.

The corridor was empty.

The staff was still there. The lightning-scarred ash staff, lying on the black floor where Lex had dropped it. And beside it, folded small, a scrap of paper.

Lex picked it up. It was written in charcoal, in a shaking hand.

*Tell him I'm sorry.*

*Tell him I couldn't.*

*Tell him I kept nine.*

That was all.

Lex stood in the empty corridor for a long time, holding the paper.

*[He's gone east,]* said Ember quietly. *[Or somewhere. Into the grey. With nine points of your father's Authority and no staff and no raven and no one.]* A pause. *[Do you want to go after him?]*

Lex thought about it.

He thought about Brisk, folding to the cellar floor with a look of mild surprise. About Seraphine, burned black down one side. About the iron door slamming shut in the dark forge in the middle of himself, and Ember's voice going silent.

He thought about an old man sitting on the black floor of Duskhold, sobbing out loud for the first time in thirty-one days.

"No," he said.

He folded the paper and put it in his coat, beside Lysandra's letter and his father's letter to Elric.

And he picked up the staff, and carried it back to the hall.

---

Messages came through the night.

Quill brought the first one. The raven had stayed with them; nobody had seen him leave, and nobody had seen him come back, but around midnight he was simply there, perched on the edge of the black plinth beside the crystal with a scrap of paper tied to his leg. Lex didn't know how he'd gotten it. He didn't ask.

It was from Teodra Hale. Three lines, in a hand that looked like it had been written on a saddle in the dark.

*They stopped. All at once. Just after sunset. Twenty thousand of them, and they just STOPPED. Sat down in the road. Some got up and started crying. Some didn't get up.*

*We held, smith. We held the pass.*

*Pell's alive. So's your old smith. He wants to know if you're eating.*

Lex read it three times and then had to sit down.

The second came at dawn. From Elowen Dunmore. Delivered by a gull, of all things, a great grey herring gull that came down out of the sky over Duskhold and dropped a sealed letter on the floor at Lex's feet and flew away screaming.

*Saltreach holds. The Gloamed on the causeway collapsed at sunset. The Princess came by sea with the Royal Guard. I have never seen anything like her.*

*Tell my father.*

Lex looked at the letter for a long time.

Then he folded it very carefully and went and laid it on Garrick's chest, beside Oathkeeper, under the old knight's folded hands.

---

They woke their father at noon.

They'd waited. Lex and Antho had nothing left. No Authority. Barely any mana. They'd spent everything on the Reckoning and the breach, and their panels were dark and cold as a forge on a winter morning. But the Wells were still there, under the wheel. Ember said so. And the Wells fed the Root four times faster than the old way.

So they slept. In the great hall, at the foot of the black plinth, with the crystal glowing softly above them and their father's eyes open inside it, watching. Lex woke once in the night and looked up and saw those grey eyes looking down at him, and his father's reaching fingers curling, slowly, uncurling. As if he were trying to say something with them.

By noon, the Authority had come back. Not all of it. Forty points for Lex. Something unmeasurable for Antho. Enough.

They climbed onto the plinth. They stood on either side of the crystal, the way they'd knelt on either side of the Twin Stone at Caldris. Lex on the right. Antho on the left.

Wren and Seraphine stood at the foot of the plinth. Ashka lay curled on the black floor behind them, vast and blue and silent, her golden eyes half-closed, still grieving. All around them, in the great hall, people had stopped. Stopped weeping, stopped searching, stopped talking. They stood and watched. Thousands of them. The ones who'd been Quiet. Watching two young men stand on a black plinth beside a crystal with a man inside it.

"Ready?" said Lex.

Antho looked at him across the crystal. His face was grey and hollow and exhausted. His eyes were very dark.

"No," he said.

"Truly answered," said Lex.

Antho laughed, very softly. Then he laid his hand on the crystal.

Lex laid his.

---

It wasn't like Caldris.

At Caldris, they'd had to take the weight of the whole Anchor. The whole net. Every thread, every knot, every breach. It had come down on Lex like a mountain.

This time, it didn't come down at all.

Because their father was holding it.

Lex felt him. The moment his palm touched the crystal. Felt the net, the vast silver web of the Lattice spread over all of Aldermere, still frayed, still thin in places, but *held*. Held by a knot at the center. By a man. By fifty-five points of Authority that had been stolen and burned and returned. By a will that had held on, for fifteen years, with nothing at all.

And the man reached out to them through the crystal.

*Boys,* said a voice. Not in the air. In the place under the air. In the Root. Quiet and deep and a little rough. *Boys. I've got it. I've got the weight. You don't need to carry it this time.*

*Then what do we do?* Lex thought.

*Just hold my hand,* said his father. *Both of you. And pull.*

---

They pulled.

Not with their bodies. With their Root. With Flame and Shade. With the Bloodbond, and the place where fire touched dark, and something else, something that wasn't Root or Well or anything Lex had a name for. Something that was just *two sons reaching for their father*.

And the crystal softened.

It went from hard to pliant, the way it had at Caldris. The faceted walls blurring. Drops of light running down them and falling and vanishing before they touched the plinth. And the man inside it took a step.

Lex saw his father's face come all the way through. His shoulders. His chest. His other arm. Pressing out through the softening wall of light like a man pushing through deep water. His long dark coat. His legs. His feet, bare, coming down onto the black stone of the plinth.

He stood there. Outside the crystal. For the first time in fifteen years.

And the crystal behind him dissolved.

All at once. Like a soap bubble bursting. Like frost going out of a window in the sun. One moment it was there, tall and faceted and glowing. The next it was light, gold, rising up into the open sky over Duskhold in a slow shimmering column. And then it was gone.

Arion Vey stood on the black plinth at the edge of the narrowed pit, in the grey noon light, with his sons on either side of him.

He swayed.

His legs buckled. Fifteen years of standing in a crystal, and his body had forgotten how to stand on its own. He started to fall.

Lex caught him. Antho caught him. One on each side.

And held him up.

---

He was lighter than Lex expected.

That was the first thing. He'd always imagined his father as big. The way four-year-olds imagine their fathers. Tall and strong and enormous. A man who could carry a child in one arm.

He wasn't big. He was a lean man of middle height, thin from fifteen years without food, his long dark coat hanging off him. Lex was taller. Broader. Antho was taller too. Their father stood between them, held up by them, and his head came barely to their shoulders.

His face was the face from the Thornwood memory. From the vision in the Barrow-King's hall. From the shape of light in the heart of Avaloria. A lean face, lined, tired, with silver at the temples and a long straight nose and a jaw Lex had seen in still water on his brother's face. A face that was very hard to remember even while you were looking at it.

Except Lex could remember it now. He was looking at it, and it wasn't sliding away. It was staying. Sharp and clear and real.

His father's grey eyes moved from one of them to the other.

His lips moved. Nothing came out. He'd forgotten how. He swallowed, and tried again. And this time, faint and cracked and rough as a rusted hinge, his voice came out into the air of Duskhold. Not through the Root. Not through a ghost. Out of his own mouth.

"*Lexander*," he said.

Lex couldn't speak.

His father turned his head.

"*Anthoren*."

Antho's face did something terrible.

---

Arion Vey stood on the black plinth at the edge of the narrowed pit, held up by his sons. And he lifted his hands. Slowly. Shaking. As if they weighed a hundred pounds each. And laid one on Lex's face, and one on Antho's.

His palms were warm.

They were real. Flesh and bone. Long-fingered and scarred across the knuckles, a smith's hands, like Lex's own. Warm against his cheek.

"*Look at you*," whispered their father. "*Look at you both.*"

And then his face crumpled, and he wept.

He wept the way Elric had wept, at Avaloria. Like a man who has been holding something in for fifteen years and can't hold it anymore. His thin shoulders shook. His hands stayed on their faces, trembling. And the tears ran down his lined face and into the silver stubble on his jaw, and he didn't wipe them away.

Lex was weeping too. He didn't know when he'd started.

And Antho—

Antho stood very still with their father's hand on his face. His jaw was clenched. His grey eyes were fixed on the man in front of him, and they were dry. Perfectly dry. His whole body was rigid.

"*I'm sorry*," said their father. His voice cracked to pieces. "*Antho, I'm so sorry. I'm so sorry. I know you said don't. I know you're not ready. But I'm so—*"

"*I know*," said Antho.

It came out flat. Hard. Like a door closing.

Their father stopped.

"*I know you're sorry*," said Antho. "*You said it at Avaloria. With your eyes, if not your mouth.*" His voice didn't rise. "*I've been thinking about it for months. Whether I want to hear it. Whether I can.*"

Arion's hand was still on his face. Shaking.

"*You weren't there*," said Antho. "*When it broke. When Mam went into the dark. When the whole village called me a murderer and I walked up into the mountains barefoot in the snow because I couldn't think of anything else to do. You weren't there for eight years.*" His jaw worked. "*And then you were a ghost in a crystal. Telling us about Aurelian. About the Lattice. About how you'd been sure. So sure you'd come back.*"

"*I know*."

"*And Morvane said you broke the world.*" Antho's voice dropped. "*Right here. At this pit. He said you were trying to free everyone and you tore the net open and the hunger came through. He said every breach and every Gloamed and every grey village was you.*" His grey eyes held their father's. "*Was he lying?*"

The great hall of Duskhold was utterly silent. Eleven thousand people, holding their breath.

"*No*," said Arion Vey.

---

He told them. Standing on the plinth at the edge of the pit, held up by his sons, with eleven thousand people listening.

He'd found the records in the Spire's sealed vault, twenty years ago. The truth about the Ceiling. That the Founders hadn't made it. That the Wardens had added it, three hundred years ago, for money and power and fear. That every capped smith and farmer and miner in Aldermere had been capped by a decision made in a room in the Spire by men who would never have to live under it.

"*I couldn't bear it*," he said. "*I had Root. I could see it. Every grey band around every gold thread. Millions of them.*" His voice was very quiet. "*And I thought: I can fix this. I'm a Vey. I'm Warden-Architect. I have the Root. I can reach into the net and rewrite it. All at once. Break every Ceiling in the world in a single night.*"

He looked down at the pit.

"*Rewrite is forbidden*," he said. "*Even for us. Even for the blood. The Founders forbade it. Aurelian himself. Because the net is old and tired and holds something terrible down, and if you pull on it too hard, too fast, it tears.*" His mouth twisted. "*I knew that. I'd read every codex. I knew exactly what the risk was.*"

"*And you did it anyway*," said Antho.

"*And I did it anyway.*" Arion closed his eyes. "*I came here. To Vhessa. Because the net is thinnest here; it always has been. Corvin came with me. He didn't know what I was going to do. He thought we were investigating a fault. I didn't tell him.*" His voice cracked. "*I knelt here. At this spot. And I put my hands on the floor, and I reached into the net, and I pulled.*"

Nobody spoke.

"*It tore*," whispered Arion. "*Right here. Under my hands. Like a rotten sail in a gale. And the hunger came up through it. And Corvin was standing beside me. And it found him first.*" He opened his eyes. They were wet and red and very, very old. "*Every breach since has spread from this one. Every grey village. Every Gloamed. Every Quiet in this hall.*" He looked around at the eleven thousand faces watching him. "*Every one of you.*"

He looked at Antho.

"*And your seal broke because I'd been pouring everything I had into holding the net together after I tore it*," he said. "*Because I couldn't come home. Because I'd made it so I couldn't come home.*" His hand on Antho's face was shaking so badly it was barely touching. "*Your mother went into the dark because of what I did here. You spent eight years in a cave because of what I did here.*" His voice broke completely. "*I did this. All of it. I broke the world trying to free it. And then I hid in a crystal for fifteen years holding the pieces, because it was easier than facing what I'd done.*"

---

The silence went on for a long time.

Lex stood on the plinth with his father's hand on his face and didn't know what to say. He didn't know what to feel. He'd known. Some of it. Morvane had told them, in this hall, yesterday. *He broke the world.* He'd said *I don't care*. He'd meant it, yesterday.

But yesterday his father had been a face in a crystal with a tear on it. Today he was a thin shaking man with warm hands, standing between them, saying it out loud.

*[Lex,]* said Ember quietly. *[I didn't know. I swear I didn't know. He never gave me that piece.]*

*[I know.]*

*[He kept it. For himself. The guilt.]* A long pause. *[I think he thought it was his to carry. Alone.]*

*[Like Antho,]* Lex thought. *[Like Antho in the cave.]*

*[Yes,]* said Ember softly. *[Exactly like that.]*

Antho hadn't moved. He was standing rigid with their father's hand on his face, staring at him. His grey eyes were dry. His jaw was clenched.

And then, very slowly, he lifted his own hand. And put it over their father's. On his own face.

"*The Oracle asked me a question*," he said.

Arion looked at him.

"*On the steps. Before the first song.*" Antho's voice was very quiet. "*She asked what grows heavier the longer it's carried, and lighter the moment it's set down. What can only be set down by another's hand.*"

Their father's face went very still.

"*I said guilt*," said Antho. "*And she said: whose hand?*" He swallowed. "*And I didn't know. There wasn't anyone. And the stone started climbing up my legs. And Lex stepped up and said: mine. His hand. Because he was the one I'd left.*"

He held their father's gaze.

"*I'm the one you left*," he said. "*Me. And Mam, but she's—*" He stopped. Started again. "*I'm the one. So I suppose it's my hand.*"

"*Antho*—"

"*I'm not ready to say it's all right*," said Antho. "*It isn't. It won't be. Not for a long time. Maybe not ever.*" His voice cracked. "*But I'm ready to stop carrying it. Mine. My part of it. Eight years of it.*" He pressed their father's hand against his face. "*And I'm ready for you to stop carrying yours. Fifteen years of it. You don't have to hold it alone anymore. Not in a crystal. Not anywhere.*"

Arion Vey stared at his eldest son.

"*I forgive you*," said Antho. It came out cracked to pieces. "*Not because you deserve it. Because I'm tired. And because Mam would have. And because the Oracle's been thinking about Lex's answer for nine hundred years and she said nobody ever brought anyone who could answer for them.*" His face finally crumpled. "*I'm bringing you, Father. I'm answering for you.*"

Their father made a sound Lex had never heard a person make.

And then the three of them were holding each other on the black plinth at the edge of the pit, father and sons, arms tangled, faces wet, while eleven thousand people who had been Quiet the day before stood in the great hall of Duskhold and watched, and wept with them.

"*Your mother*," he said. "*She'd have hit me. With a loaf of bread. Hard.*" He wiped his face with the back of his shaking hand. "*She always said I was the cleverest fool she ever met.*"

*[Lex,]* said Ember. *[May I?]*

*[May you what?]*

*[Speak. Out loud. To him. Like at Avaloria.]*

Lex looked at his father.

"*There's someone who wants to say hello*," he said.

Arion's face went very still.

*[Hello,]* said Ember. Out loud. In the air. Faint and gold, like a voice heard through a wall, coming from the place on Lex's chest where the seal had been. *[Hello again. You're awake. You're really awake.]*

"*Ember*," he said softly.

*[You remembered my name.]*

"*I'll never forget it.*" Arion's voice was rough. "*I felt you, you know. Through the crystal. Every time he said it. Every time he called for you, in the dark, when the thief locked you away.*" He swallowed. "*I wanted to answer. I couldn't.*"

*[The seal,]* said Ember. *[It's almost gone. Seven percent. When it goes completely, when you lift it, I don't know what happens to me. I'm a piece of you. You cut me off and pressed me in. "*Ember*," he said gently. "*I cut you out of myself on the worst night of my life. I took my patience, and my courtesy, and my terrible jokes about smelting. "*I gave you away*," said Arion. "*I don't get to take you back.*" He pressed his palm flatter. "*You were always a gift. You were always his.*"
