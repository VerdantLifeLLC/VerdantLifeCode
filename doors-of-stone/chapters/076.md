# Denna's Knot

They came through the door like water through a broken dam.

Scrael first. Dozens of them. A black gleaming flood, pouring out of the nothing beyond the doorway, their knife-legs clicking and scrabbling on the stone floor. They spread out across the chamber in every direction, fast, faster than anything that size should move.

And behind them, the other things.

The wet leather thing with no face. It came through slowly, unfolding itself from the gap like a man climbing out of a well. It stood up on the chamber floor, tall and thin and glistening, and turned its faceless head from side to side. Then it went toward the nearest soldier. The soldier screamed. The leather thing wrapped its arms around him. And when it let go, the soldier was gone, and the leather thing had a face. His face. And it smiled with his mouth.

A skin dancer. I did not know the name then. I learned it later. A thing that takes men's skins and wears them, and walks among other men, and no one knows.

The great pale worm came through, coiling and uncoiling, and where it passed, the stone of the floor went soft and grey and crumbled.

The shadow that had learned to stand came through, and where it passed, the torches went out.

And more. Things I have no names for. Things that I have seen in my dreams every night since, and will see every night until I die.

---

The chamber became a battlefield.

I saw the Adem meet the scrael in a ring of red, their swords flashing, and I saw the scrael's knife-legs glance off the Adem's close-bound clothing, and I saw an Adem woman go down with a leg cut out from under her and two of her brothers stand over her and keep fighting.

I saw Devi at the top of the stair, pulling Surprises from her satchel and hurling them into the black flood, and I saw the white fire bloom among the scrael and hear them shriek. I saw her take out a fistful of iron nails and hold them up in one hand and do something with the other, some binding I could not see, and the nails glowed red-hot and then white-hot and then flew from her hand like arrows and punched through the scraels' shells like paper.

I saw Wil on the stair below her with his scarred hands raised, pulling the heat out of the dropped torches and the Surprises' fire and throwing it in great sheets across the floor, a wall of flame between the scrael and the stair.

I saw Dagon and his three soldiers fighting back to back at the foot of the stair, their swords rising and falling, and I saw the skin dancer wearing the soldier's face come toward them, smiling, and I saw Dagon turn to meet it, and I saw what happened to Dagon.

I do not want to tell you what happened to Dagon.

---

And I saw Sim.

He was still standing over Meluan at the foot of the door, with the sword he did not know how to use held out in front of him. He had not moved. The scrael were pouring around him, past him, on either side, and he had not moved. His face was white as milk and his hands were shaking so hard the sword blade rattled. But he had not moved.

A scrael came at him. It came very fast, its knife-legs flashing. He swung the sword at it, wildly, like a man swatting at a wasp. The blade struck the scrael's shell and glanced off.

The scrael's leg came up and across. It caught Sim across the face.

He cried out and fell back. His hand went to his face. Blood welled between his fingers.

The scrael reared over him.

And then Bast was there, with the iron knife in his burned hand, and he drove it down into the place where the scrael's legs met its body, and the scrael shrieked and smoked and collapsed.

Bast stood over Sim with the knife in his hand. His hand was blistered and blackened where it held the iron. His face was twisted with pain. He did not let go.

"*Get up*," he said to Sim. "*Get up. Get her. Get her away from the door.*"

Sim got up. His hand was still pressed to his face. Blood was running down his cheek. But he got up, and he bent down, and he took Meluan under the arms, and he dragged her away from the door, across the floor, toward the stair.

---

I saw all of this. In pieces. In flashes. Standing in the open doorway with the box in my hands and the nothing at my back and the Thief's voice in my ears.

*THANK YOU*, it kept saying. *THANK YOU. THANK YOU.*

I could feel it coming. Not yet. It was very far away, in the center of the nothing. But it was coming. Slowly, the way a great tide comes in. The things pouring through the door were only the edge of it. The foam on the wave. The wave itself was behind them.

And when it came through, it would be over.

I looked down at the box in my hands.

*Only one of the line, standing before the door, with the key in hand, speaking the door's name. Not to open it. To un-speak their own part of it.*

I had spoken the name. I had opened the door. I had done exactly what Bredon wanted.

But I was still standing in the doorway. With the key in my hand. And the name still in my mouth.

---

I closed my eyes.

I stopped listening to the Thief's voice. I stopped listening to the screams and the shrieks and the clash of swords. I stopped listening to everything, except the box.

Its long slow *no*. And the name braided through it. The name of the line. My mother's name. My name.

I listened to it the way I had listened to the stone in the round room at the top of the Rookery. The way I had listened to the Sleeper, and the king's hair, and the wind on Hessa Tern's ship. Not reaching for it. Not wanting it. Just listening.

And I found the place where the line's name was braided into the door's name. The place where the two strands were twisted together. Like two threads in a cord.

I found the end of my strand.

And I began to pull.

---

It was like pulling out my own spine.

I cannot describe it better than that. It was the worst pain I have ever felt. Worse than the Eolian, worse than Cinder's hand, worse than anything before or since. I felt my name coming out of the door, strand by strand. And as it came out of the door, it came out of me.

Not the name *Kvothe*. The other one. The deep one. The name that was the shape of me. It unwound from the door's name the way a thread unwinds from a spool. And as it unwound, it pulled out of me. Out of my chest, out of my bones, out of the place where names live.

I felt myself getting smaller. Thinner. Emptier. The way I had felt on the stage at the Eolian, at the end of the song. But worse. So much worse. Because at the Eolian, the song had pushed my name out, and it had come back. This was not pushing. This was pulling. This was tearing.

And it was not coming back.

I opened my eyes.

---

My name was hanging in the air in front of me.

I could see it. I do not know how. Names are not things you can see. But I could see mine. A thread of silver light, very thin, very bright, unwinding from the face of the door and from my own chest at the same time, the way a spider's thread unwinds from its body. It hung in the freezing wind between me and the open doorway, trembling, shining, getting longer and longer.

And it was drifting toward the nothing.

The wind from the doorway was pulling it. The Thief was pulling it. I could feel the hunger reaching out for it, the vast terrible want turning toward the thread of my name like a flower toward the sun. If it reached the nothing, the Thief would have it. The last key. The only key. The name of the line.

And then no one could ever close the door again.

I tried to pull it back. I could not. I had nothing left to pull with. I was too small. Too thin. Too empty.

The thread drifted toward the doorway.

---

A hand caught it.

A small hand. A woman's hand. Pale and thin, with long fingers and a thin dark cord tied around the wrist in a knot that was not glowing anymore.

Denna.

She was on her knees on the stone floor beside me. I had not seen her come. I had not seen anything. Her face was grey and wet with tears. She was gasping for breath, one hand pressed to her chest. But the other hand was up, in the air, in front of the doorway. And the silver thread of my name was caught in it. Wound around her fingers.

She looked up at me.

"I have a cord," she whispered. "In my sleeve. I told you."

She let go of her chest. She reached into her sleeve. She pulled out a cord. Thin and dark and braided from three strands. Her own hair.

And she began to tie.

---

I have seen Denna tie knots a thousand times. In the Grey Man, teaching me. In the garden at Severen. In the cords she wore in her hair. I had watched her hands move, quick and neat and precise, and I had thought I knew what she could do.

I did not know.

Her hands moved so fast I could not follow them. They moved through the air in front of the doorway like birds, like flames, like water. They wound the silver thread of my name around the dark cord of her hair and tied it there. Knot after knot after knot. Looping and twisting and doubling back, in that particular way she had, that I would have known anywhere.

And each knot held.

I felt it. Each one. I felt my name caught in the knot, and held there. Not pulled toward the nothing. Not drifting. Held. Safe. Kept.

She tied and tied. Her face was grey. Her lips were white. Blood was running from her nose, down over her mouth and chin. Her hands were shaking. But they did not stop.

The silver thread unwound from my chest and from the face of the door, and she caught it, and she tied it. Knot after knot. Until the last of it came free. Until there was nothing left in me but a hole where it had been. Until there was nothing left in the door but its own name.

She tied the last knot.

And the door closed.

---

It did not slam. It did not crash. It simply closed, the way a flower closes at dusk. The two halves of grey stone slid back together, silently, smoothly. The silver light narrowed to a line. The wind died. The Thief's voice went quiet, in the middle of a word.

Whatever was caught in the gap when it closed was cut in half. I saw it. Scrael, sliced through their shells as neatly as if by a knife. The great pale worm, cut in two, both halves writhing on the floor. A grey many-jointed hand, severed at the wrist, lying on the stone with its fingers still curling.

And the door was shut.

Not perfectly. I saw that, even then. There was a line down the center of it, where the two halves met. Hair-fine. Barely visible. A crack, where there had been no crack before. A place where the stone did not quite fit.

But it was shut. And it was only a door now. A name of stone, like the other six. It did not answer to the line anymore. It did not answer to anyone.

There was no line anymore. Not in the door.

It was all in the cord. In Denna's hands.

---

I did not see what happened to Bredon. Not all of it. I was on my knees on the stone by then, with the empty box in my lap. I had nothing left. I could barely see.

But I saw a little. I will tell you what I saw.

I saw him standing at the edge of the doorway, just before it closed. Leaning on his stick. With the dark cord that ran to Denna's wrist in his hand, slack now, forgotten.

He was looking into the nothing. At the vast terrible want in the center of it. And his face was not mild, or kind, or interested. It was full of something I had never seen on it before.

It was love.

"Master," he said softly. "I've waited so long."

And he let go of the cord. And he stepped through the doorway, into the nothing, just as the two halves of the door slid together.

I do not know if he made it through. I do not know if the door caught him, the way it caught the scrael and the worm. I do not know if he is on the other side now, with the Thief, in the place between the stars. I have wondered, every day since. I have dreamed of him, standing in the nothing, leaning on his stick, smiling.

But I know that the moment the door closed, the knot on Denna's wrist came undone.

It simply fell apart. The loops loosened and slipped and unwound, and the dark cord slid off her wrist and fell to the stone floor, and lay there. And she looked down at it. And she made a sound. Half a laugh and half a sob.

She was free.

---

The shadow came down the stair.

I saw it, dimly, through the grey haze in my eyes. A place where light went to be forgotten, shaped like a man. It came down the stair slowly, step by step, the way a pilgrim walks the last mile to a shrine. The fighting stopped as it passed. The Adem and the soldiers and the scrael all went still. Even the skin dancer, wearing Dagon's face, stood motionless and watched it go.

Haliax crossed the chamber floor. He came to the door. He stood in front of it.

He did not speak. He stood very still before the grey stone, with its hair-fine crack down the center. Then he raised his hand—or the shape of a hand, made of shadow—and laid it flat against the door.

The stone said no.

I felt it. Even empty as I was, I felt it. The long slow patient *no* of the door, saying itself to him. *No. No. No.* The name of a prison, answering no one.

Haliax stood with his hand on the door for a long time. Then he took it away.

He turned. He looked at me, kneeling on the floor with the empty box in my lap.

"Singer," he said. His voice was very quiet. Very tired. "What have you done?"

I could not answer. I had no voice left.

"It is locked," said Haliax. "Truly locked. Forever. The line is gone from it." The shadow was very still. "No one will ever open it again."

He was silent for a long moment.

"You have given me forever," he said at last. "Do you know that? You have taken away the only door that led to my ending. And you have given me forever." The shadow seemed to shift, very slightly. "I do not know whether to hate you or thank you."

He turned away.

"Ferule," he said.

Cinder was standing at the edge of the chamber, with his hand pressed to his ruined face. Black blood was still running between his fingers. His black eyes were fixed on Vashet, who stood a few paces away with her sword raised, watching him.

"Lord," said Cinder. His voice was thick.

"We are finished here," said Haliax. "Gather the others."

And then they were gone. All of them. The Seven, and the shadow, and the things that had come through the door. The scrael, the skin dancer, the pale worm's severed halves. Gone, like candles snuffed. The chamber was empty of them.

Only the dead remained. And the living.

---

I was lying on the floor. I do not remember falling. I was lying on my side on the cold stone in front of the door, with the empty box beside me, and I could not move.

Someone was holding me. Someone was saying my name. Not my true name. I had none. The other one. *Kvothe. Kvothe. Kvothe.* Over and over. I did not recognize it. It was only a sound. A word someone was saying.

I looked up. There was a face above me. Dark hair, and dark eyes, and blood on the lips.

I did not know who she was.

I knew I should. I knew there was a name for her, and a story, and a great deal of feeling, somewhere. But I could not find it. It was on the other side of the hole where my name had been. Everything was on the other side of it. I was empty.

She was crying. She was holding something in her hand. A cord, dark and braided, full of knots. It was glowing faintly, a soft silver glow, like moonlight on snow.

She took my left hand. My cold hand, with the white print of a dead man's fingers on its wrist. She wound the cord around the wrist, over the print. Around and around. And she tied it. Tight.

"There," she whispered. "There. It's close to you. It'll stay close. Until we find somewhere safe."

I looked at the cord on my wrist. At the knots, glowing softly.

And for just a moment—just one moment—something came back. Not much. A flicker. A single thread, reaching out from the cord on my wrist and into the hole in my chest.

And I knew her.

"Denna," I whispered.

She laughed. She was crying and laughing at the same time.

"Yes," she said. "Yes. It's me. I'm here."

I looked at her face for a long time. And I tried to hold onto it. The name, and the face, and the knowing.

Then the flicker went out. And I was empty again. And I did not know her.

But I remembered that I had. For a moment. And that was something.

That was something.
