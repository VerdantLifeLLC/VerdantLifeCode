# Interlude: Harl's Bread

The innkeeper stopped, and looked down at his hands, and did not go on.

Chronicler waited. He had learned, over two days and a half, that there were silences in this telling that were not pauses. Places where the story ran up against something too heavy to carry past, and had to stop, and set it down, and rest.

This was one of them. He could tell. The innkeeper was sitting very still with his hands flat on the table and his eyes on nothing, and his face had the look it got sometimes. Not Kote's look, mild and tired. Not Kvothe's, bright and hungry. Something between. Something older than either.

Bast was watching him too.

"Reshi," he said softly. "You never told me about Harl."

"No."

"Why not?"

The innkeeper was quiet for a long time.

"Because I didn't know him," he said at last. "That's the terrible thing, Bast. I didn't know him. Eleven days. He said maybe a hundred words to me. He gave his bread to a boy with a crossbow and he held a door so I could run, and I never learned where he was from, or whether he had a wife, or what he liked to eat." He looked down at his hands. "How do you tell a story about a man you never knew?"

"You just did," said Chronicler.

The innkeeper looked at him.

"You told me he gave his bread away," said Chronicler. "You told me he said most talk is noise. You told me he checked the horses' feet before his own supper. You told me he held a door for a quarter of an hour against a dozen men." He tapped the page in front of him. "That's knowing someone. That's more than most people get."

The innkeeper was quiet for a long moment.

"It's not enough," he said.

"It's never enough," said Chronicler. "That's why we write it down."

---

The innkeeper looked at him for a long time. Then something in his face eased. Very slightly.

"You're very wise, for a man who writes about dragons for a living," he said.

"Draccus," said Chronicler, with dignity. "And I don't write about them for a living. That was one book."

"It was a very good book."

"You've read it?"

"I had a copy at the University. Before." The innkeeper smiled faintly. "It was the first thing I ever read that made me think the world might be bigger than I'd been told."

Chronicler stared at him. He opened his mouth, and closed it again, and found that he did not know what to say.

Bast laughed. It was a sudden, surprised sound, and it broke something in the room, and the heaviness lifted a little.

"Look at his face, Reshi," said Bast. "You've struck him dumb."

"So I have." The innkeeper stood up. "I'm going to make some stew. We've been sitting too long. And then I'll tell you about Tarbean." His face changed, very slightly. "Which is worse than Harl, in its way. So you'll want to eat first."

He went into the kitchen.

---

Chronicler sat very still at the table. He looked down at his page. At the last line he had written. *He said maybe a hundred words to me.*

He picked up his pen. And in the margin, very small, in plain letters, not in his cipher, he wrote a single word.

*Harl.*

He did not know why. It was not how he recorded things. It was not his method. But he wrote it, in the margin, where it would be seen by anyone who ever read these pages. Plain, and small, and permanent.

Bast was watching him.

"What's that for?" he said.

Chronicler looked at the name for a moment.

"So someone will remember," he said.
