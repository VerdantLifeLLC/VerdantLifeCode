# A Letter from Imre

The letter came in the fourth spring, with the carter from Baedn, in a packet of other letters for other people in Newarre. It was addressed in a hand I did not know, large and untidy and full of blots, to *Kote, Innkeeper, the Waystone, Newarre*.

I did not know anyone who would write to me. I turned it over in my hands for a long time, standing behind the bar, before I opened it.

It was from Sim.

I did not know that. I did not know who Sim was. But Bast, coming in from the yard with an armful of firewood, saw the letter in my hands and the handwriting on it, and dropped the firewood on the floor.

"That's Sim's," he said. "That's Simmon's hand. I'd know it anywhere. Nobody else blots like that."

"Who is Sim?"

Bast looked at me for a long moment. Then he came around the bar and took the letter out of my hands, very gently, and unfolded it.

"Sit down, Reshi," he said. "I'll read it to you."

---

*Kote,* it began. *Or whatever you're calling yourself these days. Bast wrote to me, last winter, and told me where you were. He made me swear on my mother's grave not to tell anyone, which was awkward, because my mother is alive and well and living in Atur and would be very upset to hear she had a grave. But I swore anyway. Bast was very insistent.*

*He also told me you don't remember me. That you don't remember any of us. He said it's better if I don't come to see you, because it might stir things up, and things that are stirred up can be heard.*

*So I'm writing instead. I hope that's all right. Bast said a letter was probably safe, if I didn't put anything in it that mattered.*

*I'm afraid everything I have to say matters. But I'll try to make it sound as if it doesn't.*

---

Bast read it to me slowly, sitting at the table by the hearth, with the late afternoon sun coming through the window. I sat across from him and listened.

*The Eolian is open again,* Sim wrote. *Deoch finished it last autumn. It took him three years, and all of Threpe's money, and half the money of every musician in Imre, and a great deal of swearing. But it's done. It looks exactly the way it did before. Exactly. Deoch was very particular about it. He put the bar on the same side, and the stage at the same height, and he had the balconies carved by the same family of woodworkers who carved them the first time.*

*There's only one thing different. There's a plaque. On the wall behind the stage, where everyone can see it. It's bronze, and it says: IN MEMORY OF STANCHION, WHO BUILT A GOOD ROOM. And underneath that, smaller: AND OF THE NIGHT THE TRUE SONG WAS SUNG.*

*It doesn't say your name. Deoch said you wouldn't want it to. But everyone knows.*

*People sing it there, sometimes. Tariniel. Late at night, when the crowd has thinned out. Not on the stage. From the tables. Very quietly. Deoch doesn't stop them.*

---

*Fela and I were married last summer,* Sim wrote. *In the courtyard of the Mains, with Elodin officiating, which was not strictly legal, since Elodin is not a priest of any kind, but he said he was a master of names and a marriage was only two names being tied together, so he was perfectly qualified. Nobody could argue with him. Nobody ever can.*

*It was a lovely day. The sun was out. Fela wore blue. She held up the courtyard's paving stones, for a joke, so that they floated an inch off the ground while we said our vows. Kilvin was very upset about it afterward. He said it was a frivolous use of a serious gift. But I saw him smiling.*

*She's a master now. Did Bast tell you? Master Namer. Elodin stepped down, last spring, and gave her his chair. He said he was tired of it, and she was better at it than he was anyway, and he wanted to spend more time on the roof. The other masters didn't want to let him. But he threatened to set all their beards on fire, so they did.*

*She's the youngest master in two hundred years. She's terrifying. The students are all in love with her. I'm very proud.*

---

Bast stopped reading. He looked up at me.

"Reshi," he said. "Do you remember any of this? Any of them?"

I thought about it. I thought about a girl with dark hair and a pale face, putting her small cold hand on my chest over my heart, and saying *hold*. I did not know her name. I did not know when it had happened. But I remembered the hand, and the word.

"No," I said.

Bast looked at me for a long time. Then he looked back down at the letter.

---

*Wil is still at the University,* Sim wrote. *He's Kilvin's right hand now. He's building bridges. Real ones. Kilvin's teaching him the big work, the heavy work, the kind that doesn't need fine fingers. He built a new bridge over the Omethi last year, downstream from the old one, for the carts. It's very good. Everyone says so. There's a stone at one end of it with a carving of a pair of hands, and underneath it says FOR THE ONE WHO HELD THE FIRE. He won't tell anyone who it means.*

*Devi is back at the University too. Did Bast tell you? They let her back in. After the war. Herma said anyone who could make a dead man walk to the gallows in front of ten thousand people was too dangerous to leave outside the walls, where they couldn't keep an eye on her. So they gave her a bench in the Fishery and a room in the Mews and made her promise not to blow anything up.*

*She has blown up four things since. Kilvin says three of them were accidents.*

*She's still lending money. She says it's research. She says she's studying the psychology of debt. She says you still owe her, and she's charging interest, and she'll collect it someday whether you remember or not.*

*She says you still have her page. She says she gave it to you so you'd have to come back and return it. She says that's called collateral.*

---

*Kilvin still has the candle,* Sim wrote. *No, that's not right. You have the candle. Kilvin has the ledgers. He still keeps them. He says the candle is with you, and so the record must be too, and he writes to Bast every season to ask if it's burned blue.*

*Bast says it hasn't. Not in four years. Kilvin says that's either very good news or very bad news, and he doesn't know which.*

---

*Auri is well,* Sim wrote. *At least, I think she is. None of us see her much. She doesn't come up. But Fela says she goes down, sometimes, to the deep places, and she can hear her. Singing. Very softly. Down by the door. Fela says she sings to it every night. Fela says the door is quieter than it used to be. Calmer. She says it's almost as if it's listening.*

*Elodin says Auri is the bravest person he's ever known. He says she's doing what the Amyr used to do, only with songs instead of books. He says someone should write her name in a very important book somewhere. He won't say which book.*

---

*Threpe is old now,* Sim wrote. *Older than he was. He doesn't go out much. But he still goes to the Eolian every Felling night and sits at the table nearest the stage and listens. He says he's waiting. He won't say for what. But he always saves the chair beside him. Nobody's allowed to sit in it.*

*Denna—*

Bast stopped reading.

He sat very still at the table, with the letter in his hands, and looked at the next line for a long time. His face did something complicated.

"What does it say?" I asked.

Bast did not answer right away. Then he folded the letter, very carefully, along its creases.

"It says no one knows where she is," he said. "It says no one's heard from her since the bridge. It says Sim hopes she's well."

I looked at him.

"That's not all it says."

Bast looked at me for a long moment. His deep blue eyes were very steady.

"No," he said. "It's not." He put the folded letter inside his shirt. "But it's all you need right now, Reshi."

I did not argue. I did not know how. I did not know who Denna was, or why the name made the scar on my wrist ache, very faintly, like an old wound before rain.

---

I found the letter, years later, in the bottom of a drawer in Bast's room, when I was looking for something else. I was still Kote then. I read the rest of it standing in the doorway with the grey light coming through the window.

*Denna sent me a letter too,* Sim had written. *Last winter. From somewhere far away. It had no return direction, and it was very short. It said she was well. It said she was singing again, in a place where no one knew her. It said she had a new name, and she liked it, and she was going to keep it.*

*It said to tell you, if I ever found you, that she still has the knot. The small one. And that she knows what it's for.*

*And it said to tell you that she's sorry. For everything. And that she'd do it all again.*

*I don't know what that means. I hope you do. Or that you will, someday.*

*Your friend, always, whether you remember it or not,*

*Sim.*

*P.S. The rhymes in this letter are terrible. I know. I did it on purpose. So you'd know it was really me.*

---

I did not know what it meant. Not then.

But I read it three times, standing in Bast's doorway. And then I folded it along its creases and put it back in the bottom of the drawer where I had found it. And I did not tell Bast I had read it.

And that night, for the first time in years, I dreamed in color.
