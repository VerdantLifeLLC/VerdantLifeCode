# The Man Who Tapped

There was a man in the cell below mine, in the Tower of Thorns. I never saw his face. I never learned his name. But he talked to me every night for three months, and he is part of this story, and I will not leave him out.

He did not talk with his voice. The stone walls of the Tower were too thick for voices. He talked by tapping.

I noticed it first in my second month. I was lying on my pallet in the grey dark, not sleeping, not thinking, holding my left wrist in my right hand and running my fingers over the knots. And I heard it. Very faint, coming up through the stone floor. *Tap. Tap-tap. Tap.*

I did not think anything of it. I did not think anything of anything, in those months. But it went on. Every night, at the same hour, after the guards changed. *Tap. Tap-tap. Tap. Tap-tap-tap.* A pattern. The same pattern, over and over, for an hour, and then silence.

After a span of nights, I tapped back.

I do not know why. I had nothing in me to wonder with, and nothing to want. But my hand moved. It found a loose chip of stone on the floor beside my pallet, and it rapped on the floor with it. *Tap. Tap-tap. Tap.* The same pattern.

Below me, the tapping stopped.

Then it started again. Faster. Different.

---

It was a cipher. A simple one, the kind prisoners have used in every dungeon since there have been dungeons. Taps for letters, counted out in rows and columns. The kind of thing I would have worked out in an afternoon, before. As Kote, with nothing in my head, it took me three spans.

But I worked it out. Slowly. Tap by tap. Letter by letter.

The first thing he said, when I could finally understand him, was: *WHO.*

I did not know how to answer. I did not know who I was. So I tapped back the only thing I had.

*KOTE.* Though that name was not given me until later, on a roof in Imre, and I did not have it yet. What I actually tapped, as best I can remember, was *NO ONE*.

There was a long silence below.

Then: *ME TOO.*

---

He was old. That was the first thing I learned about him. He told me, over many nights, tap by tap. He had been in the Tower of Thorns for eleven years. Before that, in another prison in the south, for six. Before that, he did not say.

He did not know what he was in for. Or he said he did not. He said they had come for him in the night, in a town in the eastern hills, men in plain grey with no badges, and taken him, and he had been in one cell or another ever since. He said no one had ever told him the charge.

*WHY*, I tapped. It took a long time.

Below me, silence. Then:

*TATTOO.*

---

It took me a long time to understand. Three nights. He tapped it out patiently, letter by letter, while I lay on my pallet and listened and let the letters fall into me like stones into a well, and slowly, slowly, they began to make a shape.

He had a tattoo. On his chest, over his heart. It was very old. His father had given it to him, when he was a boy, and his father's father to his father, back and back. He did not know what it meant. His father had not known either. Only that it was important, and that it must be passed down, and that it must never be shown to anyone who was not family.

It was a tower. Burning.

*AMYR*, I tapped. I did not know how I knew the word. It came up out of the hole in my chest from somewhere very far down.

The silence below was very long. Longer than any silence before.

Then: *YES.*

And then, faster, tapping so fast I could barely follow:

*HOW YOU KNOW. HOW. NO ONE KNOWS. NO ONE LEFT.*

---

I did not know how I knew. I tapped that. *DONT KNOW. KNOW ANYWAY.*

He was quiet for a long time after that. Nights. I thought I had frightened him. Or that the guards had heard, and taken him away.

Then, on the fourth night, the tapping came again. Slow. Careful.

*TELL YOU SOMETHING. NEVER TOLD ANYONE.*

I waited.

*MY GRANDFATHER. HE KNEW. WHAT IT MEANT. THE TOWER.* A pause. *HE WAS THE LAST. THE LAST WHO KNEW. HE TOLD MY FATHER A LITTLE. NOT ALL.*

*WHAT.*

*THAT WE WERE SWORN. TO WATCH. A DOOR. UNDER A BIG STONE HOUSE. FULL OF BOOKS.* A long pause. *THAT A MAN CAME. A FRIEND. OLD. KIND. AND ONE BY ONE THE WATCHERS DIED. AND MY GRANDFATHER RAN. AND HID. AND NEVER WATCHED AGAIN.*

I lay very still on my pallet in the grey dark.

*A FRIEND*, I tapped. *OLD. KIND.*

*YES.*

*STICK.*

The silence below me went on and on.

*HOW YOU KNOW*, he tapped at last. Very slowly.

I did not answer. I did not know how to.

---

He told me the rest over the next month. Tap by tap. His grandfather had been one of the last of the Amyr. Not the great ones, the Ciridae, with their tattoos over their whole bodies and their swords and their terrible certainty. A lesser one. A watcher. One of those sworn to go down beneath the Archives, in their turn, and sit in the Chamber of Watches, and write *the door holds* in the book.

His grandfather had watched for twenty years. And then the watchers had begun to die. One by one. A fever. A fall. A knife in an alley. And his grandfather had noticed that before each death, the one who died had been visited by an old friend. Kind. Well-spoken. With a stick.

And his grandfather had run. With his wife and his son. East, into the hills, to a town where no one had ever heard of the Amyr. And he had never gone back. And he had told his son a little, and his son had told his son less, and all that was left, at the end, was a tattoo over the heart, and a story that no one understood.

*WHY THEY TAKE YOU*, I tapped.

*DONT KNOW. THINK SOMEONE SAW TATTOO. TOLD SOMEONE. OLD MAN MAYBE.* A pause. *THINK HE WANTED ALL OF US GONE. EVEN ONES WHO FORGOT.*

*HE IS GONE NOW*, I tapped. I did not know how I knew that either. But I knew. I had seen it, once. A man with a stick stepping through a door into nothing, just as it closed. I did not remember the door. But I remembered that.

The silence below was very long.

*GONE WHERE*, he tapped.

*THROUGH THE DOOR.*

---

He did not tap for three nights after that. On the fourth night, it came again. Slow. Weak.

*SICK*, he tapped. *VERY SICK. COUGH. BLOOD.*

I lay on my pallet and listened. I did not know what to do.

*WANT TO GIVE YOU SOMETHING*, he tapped. *BEFORE.*

*WHAT.*

*THE WORDS. ON THE TATTOO. UNDER THE TOWER. MY GRANDFATHER SAID NEVER FORGET THEM. I NEVER KNEW WHAT THEY MEANT.* A long pause. *YOU MIGHT.*

He tapped them out. Very slowly. Letter by letter. In a language I did not know, or thought I did not know. It took him the whole of that night, and he had to stop, many times, and I could hear, very faintly, through the stone, the sound of him coughing.

When he was done, I lay very still in the dark, with the letters sitting in me like stones.

*IVARE ENIM EUGE.*

I did not know what they meant. Not then. I learned later, much later, from Selitos's grey book, which I had read and did not remember reading. It is old Tema. It is the motto of the Amyr. The words Selitos gave them, among the ashes of the white city, on the night he founded the order.

*For the greater good.*

---

*THANK YOU*, I tapped. I did not know why. The words meant nothing to me. But my hand tapped them anyway.

Below me, very faint, a single tap.

Then silence.

He did not tap the next night. Or the next. On the third night, I heard the guards on the stair below, and a door, and the sound of something heavy being dragged.

After that, there was no more tapping.

---

I have told Chronicler about him. It is the first time I have told anyone. I asked Lady Tamsin, once, before I left Renere, if she knew who had been in the cell below mine in the Tower of Thorns. She asked. No one knew. There was no record. Only a note in a guard's ledger: *Prisoner, cell nine. Died of lung-fever. Buried in the lime pit.*

No name. No charge. Nothing.

He was the last of the Amyr, as far as I know. Or the last who carried the mark. He did not know what it meant. He did not know what his grandfather had watched, or why it mattered, or who had killed them all. He only knew that there was a door, under a big stone house full of books, and that an old kind man with a stick had come.

He died in the dark, tapping out the only thing he had to give to a man who could not remember his own name.

*For the greater good.*

I do not know if it was. I do not know if any of it was. But I have put him in this story. So that someone will remember. So that the last of the Amyr is not only a note in a guard's ledger and a body in a lime pit.

He was a man who tapped on the floor of his cell, every night, at the same hour, for eleven years, hoping someone would answer.

And at the end, someone did.
