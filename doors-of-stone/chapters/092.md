# Denna

She left me at the top of the stone bridge, three days later, at dawn.

I have told you that I always seem to leave places at dawn. It is true. But this was the first time someone left me. Or the first time I remember.

We had walked out together from the Grey Man in the grey hour before sunrise. Just the two of us. The others had said their goodbyes the night before, in the rooms at the top of the inn. I do not remember those goodbyes well. I remember faces I did not know, and hands on my shoulders, and voices saying things I did not understand. I remember the young man with the eye patch weeping openly and not caring who saw. I remember the small fair woman with no eyebrows pressing a folded paper into my hand and saying *you still owe me for this, don't you forget it*, and when I unfolded it later I found it was a page of old writing on something that was not quite paper, and I did not know what it meant.

I remember the big man from the Fishery gripping my hand in his great scarred paw and saying, very gravely, *Keep your hands busy. Even small things. Even nails.*

I remember the girl with dark hair who knew stone, putting her small cold hand flat on my chest, over my heart, and closing her eyes, and saying *hold*. Just that. *Hold*.

And I remember the lean man with restless eyes, sitting on the windowsill eating an apple, saying nothing at all. When I looked at him, he only raised the apple in a kind of salute, and smiled a strange crooked smile, and looked away.

---

The chest was in the back of a cart, in the inn yard, under a canvas cover. Bast was with it. He was sitting on the tailboard with his legs dangling, and he did not look at us as we came out of the inn. He was looking at the sky.

"He'll take it," said Denna. "The chest. And you. Somewhere quiet. Somewhere far away." She looked at Bast. "He knows. He's promised."

"Where?"

"I don't know." She looked at me. "I don't want to know. If I don't know, no one can make me tell."

We walked out of the inn yard and down the street toward the river. Bast did not follow. He sat on the tailboard of the cart with the chest behind him, and he watched us go, and his face was very still.

---

The streets of Imre were empty at that hour. Grey and quiet and damp with dew. A cat watched us from a doorstep. A baker's boy went past with a tray of loaves on his head, whistling. Somewhere a dog barked, and was answered, and fell silent.

We came to the bridge.

It rose up out of the grey morning mist in a great stone arch, curving up and over the river. The water beneath it was brown and slow, with little wisps of mist curling on its surface. On the far side, the University was a dark shape against the paling sky, all towers and roofs and the great grey windowless block of the Archives.

We walked up the arch of the bridge, side by side. Our footsteps echoed on the stone. At the top, where the arch was highest, Denna stopped.

"You used to stop here," she said. "Every time you crossed. You told me once. You'd stop at the top and look out at the river."

I looked out at the river. At the brown water and the mist and the trees along the banks, grey-green in the dawn.

"Why?" I said.

"You said it made you feel like the world was big enough." She was quiet for a moment. "Big enough for everything you wanted to do. Everything you wanted to know."

I looked at the river for a long time.

"It doesn't feel big," I said. "It feels grey."

Denna did not say anything. She stood beside me at the top of the bridge and looked at the river too.

---

"I'm going to go now," she said at last.

"Where?"

"I told you. I don't know. Far away." She did not look at me. "South, maybe. Across the sea. Somewhere no one's ever heard of me. Somewhere I can be no one."

"You've done that before."

She laughed softly. "Yes. I have. Many times." She was quiet for a moment. "But this time it's different. This time I'm not running away from something. I'm running away *for* something." She looked at me. "For you."

"I don't understand."

"I know." She reached out and touched my face, very lightly, with the tips of her fingers. The way she had touched my scar, a long time ago, outside a tavern that had burned. "That's all right. You don't have to."

She took her hand away. She reached into her sleeve, and took out a small cord. Barely as long as her finger. With a single knot tied in it.

"I'm keeping this," she said. "The twin knot. The one that opens the chest." She held it up between us. "I'll keep it safe. I'll keep it with me, always. I won't lose it, and I won't sell it, and I won't give it to anyone." She paused. "Unless it's time."

"How will you know?"

"I'll know." She closed her hand around it. "I'll feel it. The knot will tell me. When your name starts to stir. When it's getting ready to wake up." She looked at me. "It won't be for a long time. Years, probably. Maybe many years. But someday."

"And then?"

"And then I'll send it to you." She smiled faintly. "I won't bring it. I can't. If I come near you, someone might follow me. Someone might find you." She was quiet for a moment. "But I'll send it. Somehow. With someone I trust."

"Who?"

"I don't know yet." She put the cord back in her sleeve. "Someone who walks the roads. Someone who always pays their debts."

---

We stood at the top of the bridge for a long time without speaking. The sun was coming up now, behind us, over the hills to the east. The mist on the river was turning gold. The grey sky was turning pale.

I did not feel anything. I want to be honest about that. I stood beside her at the top of the bridge, and I knew that she was leaving, and that I would never see her again. And I did not feel anything at all.

But I knew that I should.

I knew that there was something in the hole in my chest, where my name had been. Something that would have felt this, if it were there. Something that would have broken, and bled, and cried out. Something that had loved her, once, more than anything in the world.

It was not there. It was in a cord, in a chest, in the back of a cart, in an inn yard in Imre. Locked away. Quiet.

But I knew it should have been.

"I'm sorry," I said.

Denna looked at me. "For what?"

"That I don't feel it." I looked at her. "I know I should. I know there's something I should be feeling. Now. Here. With you going away." I shook my head slowly. "I can't. It's not there. It's in the chest."

Denna's face did something very complicated.

"I know," she said. Her voice cracked. "I know it's not there. That's why I'm going." She took a breath. "It's easier. This way. If you felt it—if you were you—I don't think I could do it. I think I'd stay. I think I'd stay forever, and they'd find us, and it would all have been for nothing." She laughed, a broken sound. "It's the only time in my life I've been glad that someone didn't love me back."

"I did," I said. "Once. I think."

She looked at me for a long moment.

"You did," she said softly. "You did. More than anyone ever has. More than I deserved." She reached up and touched the thread around my neck, under my shirt. The one with the small knot on it. The one she had given me in the Grey Man. "And I did too. I do. I always will."

She pressed her hand flat over the knot, over my heart.

"Keep this," she said. "Promise me. Whatever happens. Keep it."

"I promise."

"Say my name."

I looked at her. At her dark hair, and her dark eyes, and her face in the dawn light.

"Denna," I said.

She closed her eyes.

"Again."

"Denna."

She stood very still, with her eyes closed and her hand on my chest. And then she opened her eyes, and they were wet, and she smiled.

"That's my name," she said. "That's the one that's real. Because you said it."

---

She kissed me. Once. Very softly. On the lips.

I did not feel it. Not the way I should have. It was a pressure, and a warmth, and a scent of pear blossoms. That was all.

But the knot on its thread, under her hand, over my heart, hummed. Just once. Very faintly. The way a string hums when another string nearby is plucked.

She felt it too. I saw it in her face. She pulled back and looked at me, and her eyes were very wide.

"You're in there," she whispered. "You're still in there. Somewhere."

"I don't know."

"You are." She laughed through her tears. "You are. I felt it."

She stepped back. She looked at me for a long moment. At my face, and my short brown-stained hair, and my empty wrist with its thin red mark.

"Goodbye, Kote," she said.

"Goodbye, Denna."

She turned. She walked down the far side of the bridge, toward the University, toward the road that ran along the river to the south. She did not look back. She walked very straight, with her head high, the way she had walked into the great hall at Renere to sing the true song.

I watched her until she was out of sight.

And then I stood at the top of the bridge for a long time, looking at the river, with the sun coming up behind me and the mist turning gold on the water.

It did not feel big. It felt grey.

But I stood there anyway. Because she had said I used to.

---

Bast found me there an hour later.

He came up the arch of the bridge from the Imre side, walking slowly, with his hands in his pockets. He stopped beside me and looked out at the river. He did not say anything for a long time.

"She's gone," he said at last.

"Yes."

"Did she say where?"

"No."

"Good." He was quiet for a moment. "I didn't like her, you know. Not ever. I thought she was bad for you. I thought she hurt you." He looked at the river. "I still think that."

I did not say anything.

"But she did it," said Bast quietly. "She caught your name. In the chamber. When it was drifting toward the door. She caught it and tied it and saved it." He swallowed. "And now she's walked away from you forever, so no one can use her to find you." He shook his head. "I don't think I could have done that. Walked away. I don't think I'm that strong."

"You're staying," I said.

"I'm staying." He looked at me. His deep blue eyes were very bright. "I told you I would. In the passage, under the Lackless house. Whatever you are. However much less. I'll stay until you're you again." He paused. "Or forever. Whichever comes first."

I looked at him. At his fine-boned face, and his dark hair, and his bandaged hands.

"I don't know who you are," I said.

"I know." He smiled. It was a small, crooked, sad smile. "That's all right. I'm Bast. I'm your student. You're my teacher. I call you Reshi." He paused. "You'll get used to it."

"Reshi."

"Reshi." He nodded. "It means teacher. Or master. Or fool who steps in front of arrows." His smile widened, just slightly. "It depends on the context."

I looked at him for a long moment. Then, for the first time since the chamber beneath the Lackless house, something happened to my face that I had not decided on.

It was not a smile. Not quite. But it was the shape of one. The ghost of one.

Bast saw it. His eyes went very wide.

"Reshi," he whispered. "Reshi, you—"

"Where are we going?" I said.

He looked at me for a long moment. Then he swallowed, and looked away, and wiped his eyes with the back of his bandaged hand.

"West," he said thickly. "For a while. With the Ruh. And then—" He shrugged. "Somewhere. Somewhere quiet. Somewhere no one's ever heard of you." He looked at me. "An old storyteller told me about a place. He said he'd help us find it."

"What storyteller?"

Bast was quiet for a moment.

"You'll see," he said.
